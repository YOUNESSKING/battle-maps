# FACT NOTES: Vicksburg 1863 (campaign 29 Apr - 4 Jul 1863, with context)

Written 2026-11-18 by the research agent. Extends `grant/research/FACTCHECK_B_vicksburg.md` + `grant/research/FACT_NOTES.md` (their "do not say" list still applies; two of their verdicts are corrected below, marked **CORRECTION**).
Status: **CONFIRMED** = two independent sources, or a primary text fetched and read here. **SINGLE** = one source only (say it with care or attribute it). **DISPUTED** = sources disagree (use the hedge given).
Every number in the script must come from this file. Places, roads and positions: `GEO.md` + `../data/geo.json`.

## 1. Stakes and the enemy plans
| Fact | Status | Safe wording / detail | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lincoln's "Vicksburg is the key" | CONFIRMED wording, SINGLE witness, late | Porter (1885) recalls Lincoln, pointing at a map while planning the New Orleans expedition (late 1861): "See what a lot of land these fellows hold, of which Vicksburg is the key. ... Let us get Vicksburg and all that country is ours. The war can never be brought to a close until that key is in our pocket." Recalled ~23 years later by Porter alone. Say "Porter later remembered Lincoln saying...". Not "Vicksburg is the key!" as a stand-alone exclamation (ABT/NPS paraphrase). | PI pp. 95-96; NPS quotes it as from Porter |
| Davis wanted both river forts held | CONFIRMED (primary) | Davis to Pemberton, 7 May 1863: "To hold both Vicksburg and Port Hudson is necessary to a connection with Trans-Mississippi." | OR1 (Pemberton's report, ~p. 327); WP agrees |
| Davis "nailhead that holds the South's two halves together" | UNSOURCED | Quoted by WP/ABT without a primary source; not in OR1, JN. Do not quote. | WP, ABT (no cite) |
| Johnston's plan | CONFIRMED (primary) | Arrived Jackson night 13 May; wired Richmond: "I arrived this evening, finding the enemy in force between this place and General Pemberton, cutting off the communication. I am too late." Same night ordered Pemberton to strike "Sherman" at Clinton: "If practicable, come up in his rear at once ... Time is all-important." (It was in fact McPherson's corps at Clinton.) | JN (telegram to Seddon, 13 May); GM ch. XXXV (dispatch text) |
| Pemberton's plan | CONFIRMED (primary) | Pemberton's base "was, and is, Vicksburg"; a council at Edwards preferred cutting Grant's supply line; he marched "say seventeen thousand five hundred" toward Raymond/Dillon's on 15 May, camped at Mrs. Ellison's. Grant: "I, however, had no base, having abandoned it more than a week before." | JN (Pemberton's letter of 17 May); GM ch. XXXV |
| What the Mississippi meant | CONFIRMED | With Vicksburg + Port Hudson the Confederacy kept the link to Texas/Arkansas/Louisiana (cattle, horses, men). After both fell, the river was open and the Trans-Mississippi was cut off. | Davis telegram (OR1); WP; NPS |
| Grant on the result | CONFIRMED (primary) | "The fate of the Confederacy was sealed when Vicksburg fell." | GM ch. XXXVIII |

## 2. Winter of failures (Dec 1862 - Mar 1863), one line each
| Event | Status | Line | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

| Chickasaw Bayou, 26-29 Dec 1862 | CONFIRMED | Sherman's assault on the bluffs north of the city failed; Union ~1,776 losses vs ~187 Confederate (Eicher/Smith via WP). Men of the 6th Missouri scooped caves in the bank with their hands under the parapet (SM). | SM; WP |
| Grant's (Williams's) canal, Jan-Mar 1863 | CONFIRMED | Begun by Williams in 1862, pushed by ~4,000 men; a rise broke the dam at the upper end on 8 March; a Confederate battery drove off the two dredges. Lincoln "set much store" by it. | GM ch. XXXII; NPS grants-canal |
| Lake Providence, Feb-Mar | CONFIRMED | Levee cut 30 Jan to open a 400+ mile bayou route to the Red River; Grant thought it hopeless, kept the men busy. | GM ch. XXXII |
| Yazoo Pass / Fort Pemberton, 11 and 13 Mar | CONFIRMED | Levee cut 2 Feb; gunboats failed twice before Fort Pemberton at Greenwood; 6 killed, 25 wounded. | GM ch. XXXII |
| Steele's Bayou, 14-22 Mar | CONFIRMED | Porter's gunboats trapped by felled trees and sharpshooters; Porter was ready to blow them up; Sherman's men marched through the night carrying candles in their hands and rescued the fleet. | GM ch. XXXII |
| Duckport canal, Apr | SINGLE | Last canal attempt (Duckport to Walnut Bayou), failed as the river fell. | WP |

## 5. Cut loose (3-7 May)
| Fact | Status | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision | CONFIRMED | At Grand Gulf 3 May Grant learned Banks could not reach Port Hudson before 10 May, with only 15,000: "I therefore determined to move independently of Banks, cut loose from my base, destroy the rebel force in rear of Vicksburg and invest or capture the city." He knew "Halleck's caution would lead him to disapprove". | GM ch. XXXIV |
| Grant's state on 3 May | CONFIRMED | No change of underclothing since 27 Apr, no tent; first a bath, borrowed underclothes from a naval officer, a meal on the flagship. | GM ch. XXXIV |
| Sherman's doubt | CONFIRMED | Sherman from Hankinson's Ferry: "stop all troops till your army is partially supplied with wagons ... for this road will be jammed, as sure as life." Grant: "make the country furnish the balance." Not a "protest". | GM ch. XXXIV; FACTCHECK_B #12-13 |
| Living off the land | CONFIRMED | Beef, mutton, poultry, forage in abundance; plantation corn mills (mule-powered) kept running day and night; bread and coffee short. Blair's 200 wagons (14 May) were "the only commissary supplies received during the entire campaign". | GM chs. XXXIV-XXXV |
| Strength | CONFIRMED | ~33,000 by 7 May once Sherman crossed (GM); Grant estimated Pemberton's movable force at 18,000; it was "nearly 50,000" (GM; DISPUTED: WP gives Pemberton ~23,000 in the field at Champion Hill). | GM |
| Strategy | CONFIRMED | Grant aimed for the railroad east of the city (not the south side), kept his left on the Big Black guarding the ferries, moved NE between Pemberton and Jackson "to fight him in detail". | GM ch. XXXIV |
| Halleck's countermand | CONFIRMED (primary) | Halleck's letter of 11 May ordering Grant back to Grand Gulf to join Banks reached him at the Big Black on 17 May, just as Lawler charged; Grant rode off and "saw no more of the officer who delivered the dispatch". | GM ch. XXXVI |

## 8. Champion Hill (16 May)
| Fact | Status | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Night before | CONFIRMED | Pemberton marched S from Edwards on 15 May but rain-swollen Baker's Creek and washed-out bridges sent him back to the Jackson road bridge; men marched until midnight. Early 16 May a repeat of Johnston's order made him countermarch, so his wagon train became his front. | GM ch. XXXV; WP |
| The tip | CONFIRMED (primary) | ~5 a.m. two railroad men told Grant they had passed through Pemberton's army: "eighty regiments of infantry and ten batteries; in all, about twenty-five thousand men." | GM ch. XXXV |
| Union columns | CONFIRMED | Hovey (then McPherson: Logan, Crocker) on the northern Jackson road; Osterhaus + Carr on the Middle road; A. J. Smith + Blair on the southern (Raymond) road. Pickets met ~7:00-7:30 a.m. | GM; WP; ABT |
| The hill | CONFIRMED (primary) | Grant: "one of the highest points in that section, and commanded all the ground in range"; the Jackson road turns south, climbs and follows the ridge about a mile, then turns west down to Baker's Creek. S. D. Lee on the hill saw the Union column coming at the open left flank. | GM; WP; ABT |
| Hill changes hands | CONFIRMED (Hovey's words) | Hovey + Logan reached the main line ~11:30 a.m., took the crest ~1 p.m. (ABT/WP); Bowen's counterattack drove them back nearly to the Champion house; Union batteries massed by Hovey ~2:30 p.m. "entirely enfilading the rebel columns"; the ground was "hotly contested for the third time during the day" (Hovey). Say "the hill changed hands three times" only as Hovey's "contested for the third time". | OR2 pp. 43-45 (Hovey); ABT; WP |
| Hovey's quote | CONFIRMED (primary) | "I cannot think of this bloody hill without sadness and pride ... It was, after the conflict, literally the hill of death; men, horses, cannon, and the debris of an army lay scattered in wild confusion." and "I never saw fighting like this." | OR2 p. 44 |
| Hovey's loss | **CORRECTION** (DISPUTED) | Hovey's own report: 4,180 effective, 211 k, 872 w, 119 m = 1,202, "more than 28.7 per cent ... in less than four hours"; 24th Indiana lost 201 of under 500. Grant says "more than one-third". Say "nearly a third" (Hovey: "nearly one-third"). | OR2 pp. 45-46; GM |

| Loring | CONFIRMED | Held the Raymond road citing Union strength in front; came late by a roundabout route; cut off, marched away to join Johnston; "never got back into Vicksburg" (GM). | GM; ABT; WP |
| Tilghman | CONFIRMED (primary) | Brig. Gen. Pemberton's own column ~17,500 (JN). Say "about 32,000 Union (half of them in the fight) against about 22,000". | WP; ABT; GM; JN |
| Losses | CONFIRMED (U) / DISPUTED (CS) | Union 410 k, 1,844 w, 187 m = 2,441 (GM) / 2,457 (Kennedy). | Grant's night | CONFIRMED (primary) | Grant pushed ahead of his army in the dark, then camped on the porch of a house turned rebel hospital "filled with wounded and dying"; "after the battle these scenes are distressing". | GM ch. XXXV |

## 9. Big Black River Bridge (17 May)
| Fact | Status | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| The position | CONFIRMED | The river turns west and hugs the bluff; on the low east bank a bayou with felled trees (a foot or two of water); parapet of cotton bales and earth behind it; the west-bank height commanded all. Two crossings: the railroad bridge and the steamboat Dot turned across the river as a bridge. | GM ch. XXXVI; WP |
| Defenders | CONFIRMED (WP) | Bowen's division (Cockrell S at the Jackson road, Green N) + Vaughn's East Tennessee conscripts (centre) + 4th Mississippi; 18 guns; held to cover Loring's expected return. ~5,000 (WP; GM "four thousand"). | WP; GM |
| Lawler's charge | CONFIRMED | Carr's division on the Union right, Lawler's brigade on the extreme right, in woods/a meander scar by the river; Grant "saw Lawler in his shirt sleeves leading a charge"; it hit Vaughn's line. About three minutes (Bearss via WP: SINGLE). "But little resistance was made." Capture "a little after nine o'clock A.M." | GM ch. XXXVI; WP |
| Result | CONFIRMED | Confederates burned the bridge and the Dot behind them; men left on the east bank captured; "Many tried to escape by swimming the river. Some succeeded and some were drowned." 18 guns, 1,751 prisoners. Union 39 k, 237 w, 3 m (~279, ~200 of them in Lawler's brigade). | GM; WP; ABT |
| Three bridges | CONFIRMED | Hains a raft bridge; McPherson a bridge on cotton-bale pontoons; Ransom felled trees from both banks so their tops interlaced in the river; all three done by 8 a.m. 18 May. | GM ch. XXXVI |
| Sherman at Bridgeport | CONFIRMED (primary) | Sherman crossed upstream at Bridgeport on Blair's India-rubber pontoons; "After dark, the whole scene was lit up with fires of pitch-pine. General Grant joined me there, and we sat on a log, looking at the passage of the troops"; "the bridge swayed to and fro under the passing feet". | SM; GM |
| Walnut Hills, 18 May | CONFIRMED (paraphrase only) | Sherman, looking down from the bluff where he had been beaten in December, told Grant this "was the end of one of the greatest campaigns in history". Grant: "I do not claim to quote Sherman's language; but the substance only." So paraphrase, no quote marks. | GM ch. XXXVI |
| Haynes' Bluff | CONFIRMED | Found abandoned 18 May with guns partly disabled; cavalry turned it over to a gunboat. | SM; GM |

## 10. Johnston's evacuation order and Pemberton's choice
| Fact | Status | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Johnston's order (17 May) | CONFIRMED (primary) | "If Haynes's Bluff is untenable, Vicksburg is of no value and cannot be held; if, therefore, you are invested in Vicksburg you must ultimately surrender. Under such circumstances, instead of losing both troops and place, we must, if possible, save the troops. If it is not too late, evacuate Vicksburg and its dependencies, and march to the northeast." | Pemberton a Northerner | CONFIRMED (standard) | Born in Philadelphia; served in the same division as Grant in Mexico (GM). WP suggests fear of being called a traitor shaped his choice (opinion, attribute). | GM; WP |
| Johnston by June | CONFIRMED (primary) | To Seddon, 15 June: "Without some great blunder of the enemy we cannot hold both. ... I consider saving Vicksburg hopeless." | OR1 p. 227 |

## 16. The 15 best verified vivid details
1. Henry Clay burning to the water's edge, drifting past the fleet; houses fired on both banks "lighted up the whole river" (GM, SM).
2. Grand Gulf: 5.5 hours of shelling "without silencing a single gun" while 10,000 men sat packed on the transports (GM).
3. A Black man walks into camp that night and tells Grant of the landing at Bruinsburg (GM).
4. Grant's motley ammunition train: fine carriages stacked with cartridge boxes, mules in plough harness and straw collars (GM).
5. Grant rides a borrowed horse on a bare saddle-tree; his 12-year-old son Fred follows the battles (GM).
6. Port Gibson country "stands on edge": roads on ridges, ravines choked with cane (GM).
7. "Make the country furnish the balance": plantation corn mills grinding day and night (GM).
8. Jackson: Grant and Sherman watch girls weave "C. S. A." tent cloth, then the mill goes up in flames (GM).
9. The spy courier: a man staged as a Memphis exile carries Johnston's order straight to McPherson (GM).
10. Champion Hill: Hovey's "literally the hill of death"; ground "contested for the third time during the day" (OR2).
11. Tilghman killed at 5:20 p.m. while aiming a gun of his rearguard battery (OR2).
12. Lawler "in his shirt sleeves", leads the charge at the Big Black while a staff officer argues that Grant must obey Halleck's order to turn back (GM).
13. Sherman's pontoon bridge at night lit by pitch-pine fires; Grant and Sherman on a log watching the bridge sway (SM).
14. "Hard tack! Hard tack!" chanted along the line at Grant, turning to cheers (GM).
15. Siege: mule meat "very palatable" (Pemberton); the city paper printed on wallpaper, "First ketch your rabbit" ... "caught our rabbit" (OR1, GM); families in clay caves (GM).
Also good: the stunted oak carried off "as many cords of wood ... as The True Cross" (GM); the 25 June mine of 2,200 lb under the 3rd Louisiana Redan (OR2); Union soldiers handing bread to the surrendered garrison (GM).

## 17. Do NOT say (this film)
- Do not quote Lincoln as saying "Vicksburg is the key!" as a known line; use Porter's 1885 recollection, attributed.
- Do not quote Davis's "nailhead that holds the South's two halves together" (no source found).
- Do not say Lincoln exclaimed "The Father of Waters again goes unvexed to the sea" when he heard the news; it is in his 26 Aug 1863 letter to Conkling.
- Do not quote Sherman's "one of the greatest campaigns in history" with quote marks (Grant: substance only).
- Do not say "Sherman protested" the plan (see FACTCHECK_B #12-13); his own later line is "I opposed it. I wrote him a letter about it."
- Do not name the Bruinsburg guide or the spy courier; do not invent their words.
- Do not give a Confederate loss figure for the 19/22 May assaults; do not give a Union figure without "NPS".
- Do not say Grant had 41,000-44,000 when he crossed, or 24,000 landed (use ~17,000 first day; "not more than twenty thousand"; ~33,000 by 7 May; <43,000 by 19 May).
- Do not say the hill "changed hands three times" as flat fact unless framed as Hovey's report ("contested for the third time").
- Do not say Hovey lost "more than a third" (Grant) as fact: Hovey's figure is 28.7 %, "nearly one-third".
- Do not say Johnston fought to relieve the city (he never attacked); do not say Johnston's relief army "arrived" (it reached the Big Black 3 July and turned back).
- Do not say "29,491" or "only general in history"; do not say the toothbrush/no-baggage legend.
- Do not say the 22 Apr run was on 26 Apr (Sherman's slip).
- Do not call the Port Gibson-to-Big Black march "200 miles"; Grant's figure is "an average of about 180 miles" marched by 19 May.
- Do not call the 3rd Louisiana Redan "Fort Hill" on screen (Union reports did; it confuses with the river fort).
- Do not say Port Gibson was "too beautiful to burn" (later legend, not checked).
- Do not invent weather or moonlight for 16 April.
