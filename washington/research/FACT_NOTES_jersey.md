# FACT_NOTES: New Jersey, Dec 1776 - Jan 1777 (Moves 2 + 3, hook, ending)

Scope: Trenton, Assunpink (Second Trenton), Princeton, aftermath. Written for "George Washington's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves".

**How to read the tags.** `[V]` = I read this in a source fetched this session (URL given). `[K]` = standard in the literature but I did NOT re-read it this session; treat as "check before it goes on screen" (mostly Fischer, *Washington's Crossing*, OUP 2004 = "WC"). `[X]` = do not use. ## A. The stakes, December 1776

**A1. Army strength before Trenton: "fewer than 3,000 fit for duty".** SCREEN: "Under 3,000 fit for duty." Washington's own words, letter to his brother, 18 Dec 1776: "less than 3,000 men fit for duty owing to the dissolution of our force by short enlistments" `[V, quoted by ERW]`. Same letter: enemy "exceeding ten, and by others 12,000 men". Context line: the army had been 23,000+ in Aug-Sep 1776 and was about 5,000 on paper by Dec (ERW, same page). Total on the Pennsylvania side by 24 Dec was ~6,000 after Lee's/Gates's/militia joined (Fischer `[K]`; Stryker). Confidence H for the quote, M for the "6,000".
- Source: https://emergingrevolutionarywar.org/2021/08/31/i-think-the-game-is-pretty-near-up-george-washington-at-the-precipice/

**A2. "I think the game is pretty near up."** SCREEN (exact): *"I think the game is pretty near up."* Letter of 18 Dec 1776 to his brother John Augustine Washington, written from near Trenton Falls (Pa.). Longer lines `[V via ERW]`: "between you and me I think our affairs are in a very bad way." and "if every nerve is not strained to recruit the new army with all possible expedition".
- Founders Online: the letter to John Augustine is NOT retrieved by me (FO pages are JS-rendered, WebFetch returned empty). What I verified: the letter to **Samuel Washington of the same day** is https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Washington/03-07-02-0299 and its editorial note says the GW-to-John Augustine draft of 18 Dec is in DLC:GW and the two letters "differ in wording at only a few places" (WebSearch summary of FO). The John Augustine Washington letter has a FO doc ID in the 03-07-02-029x/030x range; the next letter to John Augustine in FO is 03-08-02-0467 (24 Feb 1777), which refers back to the 18 Dec letter. ACTION for the build: open founders.archives.gov and search "John Augustine Washington 18 December 1776" and copy the permalink + confirm "near up" vs "nearly up" (a secondary site, Revolutionary War Journal, prints "pretty nearly up, owing, in a great measure, to the insidious arts of the enemy"). HEDGE: say "wrote to his brother" and show only the 5-word core "the game is pretty near up".
- Confidence: M-H (phrase widely cited; exact FO permalink missing).

**A3. Enlistments expire 31 Dec 1776.** SCREEN: "Most enlistments end 31 December." Many remaining soldiers' terms ran out 31 Dec / 1 Jan 1777 (ERW; ABT). Washington to Congress's committee 1 Jan 1777 (FO 03-07-02-0395: https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Washington/03-07-02-0395) reports the six-week extension for $10. Confidence H.

**A4. Congress left Philadelphia.** SCREEN: "12 Dec: Congress flees to Baltimore." Congress left Philadelphia and moved to Baltimore (it reached there 20 Dec); on 12 Dec it gave Washington "full power to order and direct all things relative to the department and the operations of war" (ERW `[V]`). Philadelphia placed under martial law (ERW). Note: the 12 Dec date is the day Congress adjourned/passed the powers resolution; arrival in Baltimore is later `[K]`. Say "Congress fled Philadelphia in mid-December". Confidence H on the powers quote and the flight, M on exact days.

**A5. Paine, The American Crisis.** `[K]` First "Crisis" essay published 19 Dec 1776 in the Pennsylvania Journal; Washington had it read to the troops, reportedly 23 Dec (Fischer). Famous line "These are the times that try men's souls." Optional; the 19-vs-23 Dec date differs by source (19 Dec publication; 23 Dec read to the army). Use only "read to the troops before the crossing". Confidence M.

## B. British/Hessian posts and Trenton

**B1. British winter line.** `[K]` Howe's cantonment chain across NJ: Perth Amboy, New Brunswick, Princeton, Trenton, Bordentown, Burlington, with Hessian brigades at Trenton and Bordentown/Mount Holly. Maj. Gen. James Grant (New Brunswick) was the overall NJ commander; on 13 Dec 1776 Howe put Col. Carl von Donop (Bordentown) in command of two Hessian brigades incl. Rall's `[V, ATL: https://allthingsliberty.com/2023/02/johann-gottlieb-rall-tactical-negligence-or-personal-negligence-at-trenton/]`. Cornwallis was packing for England and was at New York on 25 Dec (recalled after Trenton) `[K]`. Black Watch (42nd) were at Bordentown, moved toward Maidenhead on 29 Dec `[V, Princeton Univ. Forbes finding aid]`. Confidence M-H.

**B2. The Trenton garrison.** SCREEN: "About 1,400 Hessians, Col. Rall." Three regiments: Rall (grenadier regiment), von Lossberg, von Knyphausen, plus a small detachment of Jaegers (35 under Lt. Grothausen left over the Assunpink bridge, WP order-of-battle) and about 20 British 16th Light Dragoons and a few gunners `[K, Fischer]`. Strength sources: ABT 1,500; MV/History.com 1,400; WP infobox 1,500, text 1,400. HEDGE: "about 1,400". Rall = age 50, 36 years in service (ABT `[V]`). Rall quartered at the Stacy Potts house (King St) per most accounts; some say merchant Abraham Hunt's house (see B6). Confidence M-H.
- Sources: https://www.battlefields.org/learn/revolutionary-war/battles/trenton ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trenton_order_of_battle

**B3. No fortifications.** `[V WP]` Rall refused his engineers' advice to build redoubts; reportedly said "Let them come ... We will go at them with the bayonet." (ABT quotes "Let them come"). His request for a garrison at Maidenhead was denied. SCREEN: "No earthworks. 'Let them come.'" Confidence H (tradition of the quote is old but ABT/WP both give it).
- Gen. Grant warned Rall 22 Dec: "be on your guard" after a spy report (WP). Rall publicly dismissed deserters' reports but privately wrote he could be "attacked at any moment" (WP; Fischer).

**B4. The Hessian outposts (the first contact).** `[V WP]` At the Pennington Road, about one mile northwest of Trenton, the outpost at a cooper's shop was commanded by Lt. Andreas Wiederholdt; a plan in the Bloomsburg archive shows one officer and 24 men (http://library.bloomu.edu/Archives/Maps/map047.htm). Other pickets covered the River Road (Calhoun's crossroads, Sullivan's side) `[K]`. On the night of 25-26 Dec the Hessians sent no patrols because of the storm (WP). The Hessians were exhausted by weeks of militia raids (ABT). Confidence M-H.

**B5. "Drunk Hessians" is a MYTH.** SCREEN: "Not drunk. Exhausted." Fischer cites patriot fifer John Greenwood, who supervised the Hessians after the battle and saw no liquor drunk that night (WP, citing Fischer). Edward Lengel calls the helplessly-drunk legend untrue (WP). WCP: "a well-circulated myth" https://www.washingtoncrossingpark.org/hessians-drunk/ (page returned 403 to me; claim from search summary). A Washington staff officer predicted before the battle the Germans would be drunk after Christmas (WP). The truth: they were exhausted, sleepy, soaked, harassed for weeks, with no patrols in the storm. Confidence H.

**B6. The note under Rall's pillow / the Loyalist warning.** NOT documented the way the legend tells it. What exists `[V WebSearch]`: a Loyalist (usually named around Abraham Hunt's house, with a card game or chess, depending on retelling) is said to have handed Rall a warning note; Rall put it unread in his vest/waistcoat pocket and it was found there when he died (some versions "under his pillow"). Sources for the retelling are 19th-century Trenton histories and popular books; ERW has a piece "That Chess Story" (https://emergingrevolutionarywar.org/2026/06/03/that-chess-story/) treating the chess story as legend. Abraham Hunt was tried for treason and exonerated by a grand jury (WP Abraham Hunt). Rall spoke no English; the note's language is unclear. VERDICT: use "a warning reached Rall; he did not act on it" ONLY as "reportedly"; better to use the documented Grant warning (B3). Do not show a card table or pillow. Confidence L on the note, H on Grant's warning.

## C. The plan of 25 Dec and the crossing

**C1. The three-prong plan.** SCREEN: "Three crossings." Washington crossed at McConkey's Ferry with ~2,400 (Greene and Sullivan divisions + Knox's guns); Gen. James Ewing (~700 Pa. militia) was to cross at Trenton Ferry below the town and seize the Assunpink bridge; Col. John Cadwalader (~1,500-1,900 Philadelphia Associators, Pa. militia and Marines) at Bristol/Dunk's Ferry to attack Donop at Bordentown / pin him `[V WP, Cadwalader numbers K]`. Ewing was stopped by ice and wind (WP); Cadwalader got some men across then recalled them when he could not land his artillery (WP). Two of the three crossings failed because of the ice/nor'easter (ABT). SCREEN: "Two of three crossings failed." Confidence H.

**C2. Timing of the crossing.** SCREEN: "Dark by ~5 p.m. Crossing: 6 p.m. to ~3 a.m." `[V ATL]` Troops marched to the landing around 5 p.m. Sunset 4:40 p.m., moon rose 5:31 p.m., ~99% full. Temperature 29 degF at 3 p.m. Washington hoped to be over by midnight; Knox said all troops were in NJ by 2 a.m.; Washington said the artillery was over by 3 a.m.; march began about 4 a.m. (ATL; WP gives 4 p.m. arrival, most troops at the crossing by ~6 p.m.). Delay about 3 hours (ABT, ATL), "nine to ten hours" for the crossing (ATL). Confidence H.
- Source: https://allthingsliberty.com/2020/12/christmas-night-1776-how-did-they-cross/

**C3. Weather on the night of 25-26 Dec.** SCREEN: "Nor'easter: rain, sleet, snow." `[V ATL]` Around 11 p.m. a nor'easter obscured the moon. Knox: it "hailed with great violence"; fifer John Greenwood: rain, hail, snow and freezing at once, "hurricane" wind; Ludlum: a cyclonic storm beginning as snow, turning to snow and sleet; Sgt. Thomas McCarty on the 26th: "the worst day of sleet rain that could be". Ice floes in the river (Knox; ABT: river 300 yards wide there). Confidence H.

**C4. Boats and loads.** `[V ATL]` Durham boats 40-66 ft (avg ~60 ft, 8 ft beam); flat-bottomed ferries about 40-50 ft for horses and guns. Greene's 19 Dec order: "sixteen Durham Boats & four flats" (ATL's estimate: ~16 Durhams + 4-5 flats used; whole Durham fleet 40 to 100 by various). Knox reported 18 guns and 64-105 horses (estimates). SCREEN: "18 guns, 2,400 men." Troops probably stood in the boats (Fischer). Each man 60 rounds, three days' rations (ATL). Confidence H on 18 guns, M on boat counts. ABT says "Durham cargo boats 40-60 feet" and ferries for guns/horses (https://www.battlefields.org/learn/revolutionary-war/battles/trenton).

**C5. Glover and Knox.** `[V ATL, WP]` Col. John Glover's 14th Continental (Marbleheaders): 177 officers and men crewed the boats with Jersey and Pennsylvania rivermen. Knox directed the crossing; Wilkinson described Knox's voice carrying above the storm (ATL) and Knox wrote it was done "with almost infinite difficulty" (WP). Confidence H.

**C6. "Victory or Death".** SCREEN: password "Victory or Death". Dr. Benjamin Rush said he saw Washington write it on a scrap of paper (ATL `[V]`); WP says troops guarding the landing used it. Some accounts give the watchword for the attack itself. Confidence M-H.

**C7. The march: 9 miles, two columns, Birmingham split.** SCREEN: "9 miles in sleet." `[V WP]` Column left ~4 a.m., marched about 9 mi (14.5 km). At Birmingham (about 4-5 mi in) the army split: Greene's division (with Washington) took the northern route via the Scotch and Pennington Roads; Sullivan's took the River Road (WP). Sleet and snow; many men lacked boots and wrapped feet in rags (WP). Two men died of exposure on the march (WP, ABT-cited "2 frozen to death"). Planned 5 a.m. dawn attack slipped to ~8 a.m. (WP). Fischer says Washington's famous exchange with Sullivan about wet powder: "Tell General Sullivan to use the bayonet and penetrate into the town" `[K]`. Conflict: ABT's own page says "nine miles" and elsewhere "ten more miles"; use "about 9 miles". Confidence H.

## D. Battle of Trenton, 26 Dec 1776

**D1. Timing.** Attack opened ~8 a.m. (picket skirmish at the cooper shop first, then Greene's guns at the head of King/Queen Streets). Whole fight about 45 min-1 hr `[K]`. Wind and sleet in the Hessians' faces `[K, Fischer]`. Source: WP, Fischer.

**D2. Guns at the head of the streets.** `[V WP]` American artillery set up at the heads of King and Queen Streets (today N. Warren and N. Broad); Knox oversaw the King St guns. The Trenton Battle Monument stands at the spot. Sullivan's column blocked the Assunpink crossing to cut off the retreat (WP). Confidence H.

**D3. Rall.** Mortally wounded (two wounds), carried to his HQ, died that night; he formally surrendered to Washington (ABT `[V]`). Knyphausen regiment marched the wrong way and surrendered separately; its acting commander Maj. von Dechow was also mortally wounded (WP). Entire Hessian officer corps killed/captured (5 killed, 5 wounded, 23 captured, WP order of battle).

**D4. Casualties (the conflict).** Best table:

| | ABT | WP text | History.com / MV | Fischer (via Simple WP, p.254 `[K]`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hessians killed | 22 | 22 | 22 | 21-23 |
| wounded | 83 | 83 | 92 | 80-95 |
| captured | 800 (incl. missing) | 800-900 (body: 896) | 918 | 890-920 incl. wounded |
| escaped | - | "just over a third" | ~400 | ~400-500 |

SCREEN: "~900 captured, 22 killed." HEDGE: "about 900 captured". Escaped: "roughly 400 slipped away over the Assunpink bridge and the south road" (History.com; WP "just over a third"). Confidence H for 22 killed; M for the rest.
American losses: 2 died of exposure on the march; 5 wounded (ABT 5 wounded, 0 killed; WP "2 dead from exposure, 5 wounded"). Wounded included Capt. William Washington (hands) and Lt. James Monroe (left shoulder; musket ball severed an artery, clamped by Dr. John Riker, WP `[V]`). SCREEN: "Americans: 0 killed in the fight. 5 wounded." Confidence H (note the old "4 wounded" figure in 19th-century books).
Spoils `[K]`: 6 cannon, ~1,000 small arms, flags, ~40 horses, rum/stores. Back across the Delaware the same day (26 Dec) with the prisoners: the main army recrossed on the night of 26-27 Dec; prisoners marched to Newtown and Philadelphia `[K]`. Confidence M.
- Sources: https://www.battlefields.org/learn/revolutionary-war/battles/trenton ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trenton ; https://www.history.com/articles/battles-of-trenton-and-princeton

## E. Second crossing, bounty, 30-31 Dec

**E1. Second crossing.** `[K, V partial]` Washington re-crossed at Trenton on 29-31 Dec (Cadwalader and Mifflin joined; ~5,000-6,000 total by 2 Jan, Fischer). WP Trenton page: sources differ whether Washington appealed to the troops on 30 or 31 Dec (search summary of ABT/PBS1777). Confidence M for dates.

**E2. The $10 bounty and six weeks.** SCREEN: "$10 to stay six more weeks." `[V]` Washington's 30 Dec 1776 letter (FO) describes Eastern regiments agreeing to stay "Six Weeks beyond their Term of Inlistment which was to have expired the last day of this Month", with a bounty of "Ten dollars per Man, besides their pay running on" (WebSearch summary of FO; the 1 Jan 1777 letter to the Congress committee repeats it: https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Washington/03-07-02-0395). The scene of the appeal (Washington on horseback, "My brave fellows, you have done all I asked you to do ... "; at first no one stepped forward, then a few, then most) is from soldiers' later recollections as retold by Fischer `[K]`: treat as tradition (I did not verify the speech text). ABT's page variant: "not a single soldier stepped forward at first". Estimate: about half stayed (WebSearch summary). Confidence H on the offer, L on how many ("about half" as hedge).
- Source (offer): https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Washington/03-07-02-0395

## F. Assunpink / Second Trenton, 2 Jan 1777

**F1. Cornwallis.** SCREEN: "~5,500 British, 28 guns." `[V WP]` Cornwallis, recalled from his departure for England, reached Princeton 1 Jan and left on the morning of 2 Jan with 5,500 men and 28 guns in three columns, after detaching 1,200-1,500 under Maj. Gen. Alexander Leslie at Princeton/Maidenhead (WP says 1,500 under Leslie at Maidenhead; the Princeton garrison under Lt. Col. Charles Mawhood was ~1,200-1,400, 17th and 55th Foot and 40th Foot, ABT: ~1,200 with eight cannon). Washington's force: about 5,000-6,000 (WP, Fischer `[K]`). Confidence M.

**F2. Hand's delaying action.** `[V WP]` Col. Edward Hand's Pennsylvania riflemen (and Hessian/English dragoons pushing them) fought at Five Mile Run (outer line, around Maidenhead-Trenton midpoint), then the south bank of Shabakunk Creek (about 3 miles from Trenton), then Stockton Hollow (about half a mile out; British reached it around 3 p.m.), then fell back through the town to the Assunpink. Fermoy's outer line was abandoned after Fermoy returned to Trenton drunk (WP). The delay cost the British about 6 hours of a 10-mile advance (traditional: they left Princeton ~9 a.m. and reached Trenton ~4 p.m.). Roads were thawed mud (see Section K). Delay = time for Washington's artillery to dig in. Confidence H on places, M on clock.

**F3. The bridge.** SCREEN: "Three British assaults repulsed." `[V WP]` The British charged the Assunpink bridge three times; the first two were driven back, the second by cannon fire; the third met canister. Fischer's detail on the third charge is not verified here; The attackers' unit names are not verified here. Some men forded below the bridge and were driven off. The American line ran about 3 miles along the south bank (WP), with Washington on the south heights near the bridge. Dusk began ~4:30-5 p.m. Confidence H on three assaults.

**F4. Casualties 2 Jan (conflict).** `[V WP]` Peckham: Americans, 6 killed, 10 wounded, 1 deserted at Stockton Hollow, none at Five Mile Run; Stryker: 1 killed and 6 wounded at the end; Fischer: ~100 American killed and wounded in all. British: Peckham 1 Hessian killed at Five Mile Run and "at least 10 killed, 20 wounded, 25 captured" at Stockton Hollow; Lowell: 4 Hessians killed and 11 wounded; Fischer: 365 British killed, wounded or captured `[V as quoted by WP]`. HEDGE: "Hundreds of casualties on the British side, tens on the American." Confidence L-M. SCREEN: avoid a number or use "at least 100 and far fewer for the Americans".

**F5. "Bag the fox in the morning."** The line is attributed to Cornwallis (WP cites Ketchum p. 291). Its attestation: it is reported in secondary histories from the 19th century on; Fischer treats the story as tradition and also gives a version where Cornwallis' officers (Sir William Erskine) urged an immediate attack and Cornwallis refused because his men were tired `[K]`. The Erskine exchange ("If Washington is the general I take him to be, he will not be found there in the morning") comes from the memoirs of Gen. Thomas Erskine/Stryker's collection and is also traditional `[K]`. SCREEN: say "Cornwallis, it is said: 'We've got the old fox safe now. We'll go over and bag him in the morning.'" HEDGE: always write "it is said" (traditional). Confidence M for his decision to wait, L for the exact quote. The recorded fact is Cornwallis waited until the morning of 3 Jan.

## G. The night march, 2-3 Jan

**G1. Council of war.** `[K Fischer]` Evening 2 Jan Washington held a council with Greene, Knox, Mercer, Mifflin, St. Clair, etc.; the options were to cross the river (rejected, ice), fight on the south bank, or slip out and hit Princeton. The Princeton plan was reported as Arthur St. Clair's suggestion and Washington's decision; about 5 hours of preparation. Confidence M.

**G2. Fires and noise.** SCREEN: "500 men left to keep the fires burning." `[V WP]` About 500 men and two cannon kept the camp fires burning and dug with picks and shovels loudly so the British would think the army was digging in. By morning these men had also left. Knox's artillery fired into Trenton during the night. Confidence H.

**G3. Baggage and route.** `[K]` Heavy baggage was sent to Burlington (Fischer, Stryker) (conflict: some say to Burlington and the main stores crossed at Burlington; 400-500 men; I did not verify). Army moved by 1-2 a.m. (WP: 2 a.m., Quaker Bridge Road in modern Hamilton Township; ABT: "on the road by 2 a.m. ... Quaker Road, then a disused farm lane, Sawmill Road" `[V ABT https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/ten-crucial-days-princeton]`). Route: Trenton - Sand Town (via the road southeast across the Assunpink) - Quaker Bridge - Quaker Road north - Sawmill Road - toward Princeton, about 12 miles (WP `[V]`: ~12 miles via Quaker bridge). Wheels wrapped in rags: Fischer mentions muffling wheels/stopping all talk `[K]`; WP: "Troops were ordered to march in silence" `[V]`. The "rags on the wheels" detail is repeated in popular accounts; I have NOT verified a primary source. Use "in silence" and show wrapped wheels only as ambient (or skip). Confidence L for rags, H for silence.

**G4. Weather: the freeze.** SCREEN: "The mud froze." `[V WP, ABT/PAW]` The ground froze overnight; roads that had been bog became hard enough for guns to move without sinking (WP). PAW (Princeton Alumni Weekly): "the temperature dropped about twenty degrees in a few hours on the night of January 2, which froze the muddy roads" `[V via search summary: https://paw.princeton.edu/article/battle-princeton]`. A wind shift to northwest at dusk is in Fischer `[K]` (not confirmed by me). Horses slipped and men broke through ice on ponds (WP). A panic rumour that the army was surrounded sent some militia running toward Philadelphia (WP). Confidence M-H.

## H. Battle of Princeton, 3 Jan 1777

**H1. Forces.** WP infobox: Americans 4,500 and 35 guns; British 1,200 and 6-9 guns (lead says 1,400). ABT: Mawhood's garrison "about 1,200 men and eight cannon", the Continentals outnumbered it about 5 to 1 with 35 cannon; Mercer's brigade about 300 men (ABT, https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/ten-crucial-days-princeton `[V]`). SCREEN: "Mawhood: 1,200. Washington: 4,500." Confidence M.

**H2. The clash.** `[V WP, ABT]` At dawn the army reached the Stony Brook (about 2 miles short of Princeton) already behind schedule; before 8 a.m. the army turned onto an unused road across Thomas Clarke's farm. Mercer's column (broken off to wreck the Stony Brook bridge) was walking through William Clarke's orchard when British light troops appeared; Mawhood turned the 17th Foot, the rest of the 55th, 50 cavalry and two guns against Mercer (WP/MV). British bayonet charge broke Mercer's men; Mercer was bayoneted and died 12 Jan, nine days later (ABT: "died nine days later"). ABT: fight about an hour. Cadwalader's Philadelphia militia wavered. Confidence H.

**H3. Washington rides forward.** SCREEN: "30 yards." `[V WP, ABT]` Washington rode among the rallying men ("Parade with us my brave fellows!"), led them to within about 30 yards of the British, called "Halt!" then "Fire!" (WP, ABT). His aides covered their eyes expecting him to be shot (traditional via Fischer `[K]`). Distance conflicts: some give 30 yards, Fischer approximately 30 yards; ABT 30 yards. Confidence H for 30 yards in the three sources I read.

**H4. "It's a fine fox chase, my boys!"** `[V WP]` WP: "Washington reportedly shouted 'It's a fine fox chase my boys!' as the British fled." Attestation: I found it only in Wikipedia and secondary retellings and could not trace the earliest source; treat as traditional, not a documented quote (check Fischer's endnote before use). SCREEN: use with "reportedly" or skip; the "bag the fox" line already supplies the fox joke (pair them: "Cornwallis said he'd bag the fox. At Princeton Washington joined the fox chase."). Confidence L-M.

**H5. Nassau Hall.** `[V WP]` The 40th Foot and 55th fell back; some British took refuge in Nassau Hall; Hamilton brought up cannon; after Americans broke down the front door, 194 British surrendered (WP). The story of a cannonball destroying a portrait of George II is "possibly apocryphal" (WP). Hamilton's role with the guns is tradition (Fischer credits Capt. Alexander Hamilton's artillery company at Nassau Hall; the door was broken by infantry). SCREEN: "Nassau Hall: ~200 surrendered." Confidence M.

**H6. Casualties (ranges).** American: 25-44 killed (WP), about 40 wounded (WP, MV: "about 25 killed and 40 wounded"); ABT estimates about 80 American casualties total. British: 18-100 killed (WP), 58-70 wounded, 194-280 captured; ABT estimates about 230 killed or wounded and 250 captured (ABT article) vs 270 British, 75 Americans on the ABT summary sidebar. So British total: 270-440 depending on source; American 40-80. SCREEN (safest): "British: about 270 lost or captured. Americans: about 75." Use "roughly" and the three-source range. Notable American dead: Brig. Gen. Hugh Mercer (d. 12 Jan), Col. John Haslet (Delaware), Capt. Daniel Neil (artillery) and Capt. William Shippen, also killed `[K, verify]`. Confidence M (the ranges are real disagreement).

## I. Aftermath

**I1. Morristown.** `[V WP, ABT]` The army reached Somerset Court House on the night of 3 Jan, Pluckemin by 5 Jan, and Morristown on the evening of 6 Jan (WP). ABT says "arriving January 5" (conflict). SCREEN: "6 January: Morristown." HEDGE: "within three days". Confidence M-H.

**I2. British withdraw.** SCREEN: "Almost all of New Jersey regained." ABT: the British pulled most forces out of New Jersey; WP/MV: they kept only New Brunswick and Perth Amboy (plus Amboy/Paulus Hook), i.e. ~the Raritan line `[K]`. In the "Forage War" that followed through March, small actions at Millstone/Drake's Farm etc. Confidence H.

**I3. Recruiting and morale.** `[K]` The "ten crucial days" (25 Dec-3 Jan) revived enlistment after the army had nearly dissolved; Howe's loyalty-oath/pardon program in NJ collapsed after the British abuses and the Hessians' defeat (Fischer, Ketchum). Frederick the Great reportedly called it "the most brilliant campaign of the century" **[X DO NOT USE]**: traces to a footnote of Benson Lossing, no direct quotation, and Fischer and Kidder do not mention it (https://boston1775.blogspot.com/2021/01/what-frederick-great-thought-of.html, plus the Mount Vernon Frederick page). What IS documented: Calvin Coolidge, 1925: "Distinguished military critics have described Washington's campaign of Trenton and Princeton as a military exploit of unparalleled brilliancy." (same blog). Confidence H that Frederick is unsourced.

## K. Packaging facts: thumbnail-word candidates

Rule: contradicts a viewer belief, literally true, not "HE LOST." (copied), not "lost almost every battle", "won by running away", "Ten Days", "invaded his own country".

1. **"AT NIGHT." / "IN THE DARK."** True: two of the three greatest moves were made at night: the 25-26 Dec crossing (dark by 5 p.m., moon obscured ~11 p.m., artillery over by 3 a.m.) and the 2-3 Jan night march (on the road by 2 a.m. in silence). Belief it flips: "great generals win in daylight on a clear field." Sources: ATL, WP, ABT. STRONG.
2. **"HE LEFT FIRES."** True: 500 men and two cannon kept the fires burning and dug noisily at the Assunpink while the army marched away (WP, ABT). Belief it flips: "a trapped army has no moves left." STRONG for Move 3, visually clean (campfire line against Cornwallis).
3. **"HE SNUCK OUT."** True: the army left Trenton 2 Jan in silence; the British found the camp empty at dawn (WP, ABT). Hedge: "snuck" is informal but accurate. MEDIUM.
4. **"HE HID."** Weaker: he did not hide; he deceived. Avoid.
5. **"NO ONE WAS DRUNK."** True (Fischer, Greenwood) but about the Hessians not Washington. Better as an inside-video myth card.
6. **"LESS THAN 3,000."** True ("less than 3,000 men fit for duty", Washington's own letter). Flips "he had a big army." Good number overlay with the "game is near up" quote.

Top 3: (1) "AT NIGHT." (2) "HE LEFT FIRES." (3) "HE SNUCK OUT." Title-A idea compatible with the recipe: "George Washington's Trenton and Princeton Campaign | The Army That Vanished" is NOT proposed (do not title off a word the note does not support).

## L. Myths to avoid (summary)

- Drunk Hessians (B5): documented against it.
- The note under Rall's pillow/chess game (B6): legend.
- Frederick the Great "most brilliant campaign" (I3): unsourced; DO NOT USE.
- Cornwallis's exact "bag the fox" words (F5) and Washington's "fine fox chase" (H4): "it is said".
- Leutze's painting: a standing Washington in a rowboat with the Stars and Stripes (not adopted until June 1777, and the Durham boats were 40-60 ft). Do not show the painting as fact.
- American "no losses at Trenton" is wrong: 2 died of exposure, 5 wounded (D4).
- 4,500 men crossed at McConkey's: the commonly cited 2,400 for the assault force; counts run to 4,500 (ATL); Knox wrote "about 2500 or three thousand" `[V ATL]`.
