# George Washington's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves (script v1, 2026-11-25)
Format: Top 3 commander film (hook + 20 s background + 3 moves + short ending), ~13-14 min, owner voice clone (exactly as Jackson).
Look: the Jackson v4 film (3D Vicksburg engine, line-block troops, muzzle flashes on every shot), 3 cloud dives (S01 the Delaware, S03 Brooklyn, S05 the Christmas crossing at night). Winter ground on the Delaware / Trenton / Princeton worlds only.
Facts: `research/FACT_NOTES.md` (every number, date and quote below is logged there). Tags: [Sxx | shot]. No "]" inside a tag. Nothing on screen before it is said.
Hook (1a + 1c): the stakes of THIS story (an army with a week left to live: the enlistments end on New Year's Eve), one continuous dive onto the Delaware at McConkey's Ferry, the crossing + the number at Trenton, the twist (the same army had nearly been trapped for good at Brooklyn), back into the danger (trapped again at the Assunpink), the general enters as the turn with ONE human detail (his letter: "the game is pretty near up"), name slam + 3 snap cuts.
Devices (1d): each move opens on the enemy plan + "in other words"; "But... instead" twist; set-up -> pay-off: Glover's Marblehead boatmen (S03 -> S05), the campfires left burning (S03 -> S07), "the game is pretty near up" (S01 -> S08); day-by-day dates in moves 2-3; cliffhanger at each move end.
Engagement (1b): subscribe bridge after move 1; ONE "What would you do?" on the night of 2 January (S06); final 1-2-3 question + "which commander next" (18th century: no family line).
Sides: Americans BLUE, British RED, Hessians RED (labelled by regiment). Flags: Grand Union flag (1776); British Union Flag 1707-1801 (no St Patrick's cross).
Method card ("THE WASHINGTON METHOD"): 1. SAVE THE ARMY FIRST · 2. HIT THEM WHERE THEY REST · 3. GO WHERE THEY ARE NOT.

## S01 HOOK (0:00 - ~1:05)

[S01 | colonies wide from altitude, winter light: red blocks spread in posts across New Jersey, a small blue army behind the Delaware on the Pennsylvania bank, separate blue boats drawn up along the bank; caption NEW YEAR'S EVE on its words; DELAWARE river name on its word]
In December seventeen seventy-six, the American Revolution was running out of time. On New Year's Eve, most of its beaten army could go home. What was left of it waited behind the Delaware.

[S01 | cloud dive 1 down onto McConkey's Ferry at night in a storm: sleet streaks, separate dark boats pushing across the icy river, lanterns; date plate 25 DECEMBER 1776 on its words; number 2,400 on its words]
On Christmas night, about two thousand four hundred of them crossed the icy river in a storm.

[S01 | dissolve to Trenton at a grey dawn: two blue columns enter the town from the north and the river road; guns at the head of the two streets fire (flash + sound each shot); red blocks spill out of the houses; number card 900 HESSIANS CAPTURED on its words, then a small line NO AMERICAN KILLED IN THE FIGHT on its words]
At dawn they fell on the Hessians at Trenton. In about an hour, some nine hundred of them were prisoners, and not one American had been killed in the fight.

[S01 | hard cut to the colonies wide map, late summer light: Brooklyn Heights pulse, a red ring closing round it, the East River glowing behind; date plate AUGUST 1776 on its words]
And the strangest part? Four months earlier, this same army had nearly been trapped for good, on a hill in Brooklyn, with a river at its back.

[S01 | back to Trenton by day, 2 JANUARY 1777 on its words: a long red army arrives from the north-east; the blue army behind a small creek, the Delaware behind them; one stone bridge glows]
And a week after Trenton, it was trapped again, with a British army in front and the river behind.

[S01 | the general enters: banner with no portrait yet on the Pennsylvania bank; a dark letter card on "brother": handwriting typed out "I THINK THE GAME IS PRETTY NEAR UP", small date 18 DECEMBER 1776]
A week before Christmas, its general had written to his brother: I think the game is pretty near up.

[S01 | NAME SLAM: GEORGE WASHINGTON (gold 3D title on its words), the banner portrait revealed; then 3 snap cuts, each on its words: the East River at night with boats shuttling, the Delaware in the storm, the orchard at Princeton with smoke]
His name was George Washington. He would steal his army out of a trap in a single night. He would cross a frozen river in a storm and fall on Trenton at dawn. And he would slip away from a British army in the dark, and strike behind it. These are George Washington's three greatest tactical moves.

## S02 BACKGROUND: NEW YORK, 1776 (~1:05 - ~1:45)

[S02 | colonies wide, summer: red ships as separate vessels fill the harbour below Staten Island; number 400+ SHIPS then 32,000 SOLDIERS on their words; date plate 1776]
Four months earlier, in the summer of seventeen seventy-six, Britain had sent the largest force it had ever sent overseas to take New York: more than four hundred ships and about thirty-two thousand soldiers, among them thousands of Germans hired from their princes, the Hessians.

[S02 | brooklyn region by day (dissolve): blue blocks behind the forts on Brooklyn Heights and out on the wooded ridge to the south; red blocks land and form on the plain; date plate 27 AUGUST 1776]
On the twenty-seventh of August, on Long Island, the British marched through the night around the American left, through a pass no one was guarding, and fell on the Americans from behind.

[S02 | red arrow sweeps round behind the ridge; blue blocks on the ridge about-face and fall back toward the forts; number card ~1,000 CAPTURED on its words; the blue survivors file into the works on the Heights, the East River behind them]
The American line broke. About a thousand men were captured, and hundreds more killed or wounded. The rest fell back into the forts on Brooklyn Heights, with the East River at their backs.

## S03 MOVE 1: THE NIGHT ON THE EAST RIVER (~1:45 - ~4:00)

[S03 | cloud dive 2 down onto Brooklyn Heights: the forts, the blue army packed inside, the British camp line facing them, the East River and the town of New York across it; date plate 28 AUGUST 1776]
The British plan was simple. Their commander, General William Howe, would not waste men storming the forts. His engineers would dig toward them, trench by trench, and his warships would sail into the East River and cut the Americans off from New York.

[S03 | the camera rises: red siege trenches creep forward; red ships at the mouth of the river; the river between Brooklyn and New York glows; caption 9,000 on its word]
In other words, some nine thousand men, about half of the army, were inside a sack, and the British were about to pull the string.

[S03 | rain: grey veil; the red ships hold at the river mouth, bows into a north-east wind (wind streaks); WIND caption small]
For two days, rain and a north-east wind kept the warships out of the river. It could not last.

[S03 | Washington's banner on the Heights; date plate 29 AUGUST; the twist: a bold blue arrow from the forts down to the ferry and across the river to New York]
But on the twenty-ninth of August, the general did not wait to be besieged. Instead, he decided to take his whole army across the river, in one night, under the enemy's nose.

[S03 | the ferry landing: separate boats arrive from up and down the river and gather at the landing, one by one; label GLOVER'S MARBLEHEAD REGIMENT on "Marblehead"]
Every boat that could be found was gathered that afternoon. And to row them, he had a regiment of fishermen and sailors from Marblehead, Massachusetts, under Colonel John Glover, men who had handled boats all their lives.

[S03 | night falls (night 1), rain: one blue block at a time steps out of the works, turns, walks down to the landing, the men break rank and stream onto the boats; the boats cross separately and come back empty]
After dark, the regiments slipped out of the lines one at a time and walked down to the ferry through the mud. Most of the men were told only that fresh troops were coming over to relieve them. A covering force stayed in the front line, so the British would see nothing change.

[S03 | the river at night: many separate boats crossing and recrossing, loaded low; at first they row against the wind and the tide; then sails appear on a few larger boats]
All night the boats crossed and came back, loaded almost to the water. At first the wind and the tide were against them. Then, late in the night, the wind died down and changed, and the sailboats could cross too.

[S03 | near dawn, grey: one blue block (the covering party) leaves the line too early, walks toward the ferry, stops, turns, walks back into the line; the line stands empty for a moment in between]
Then, in the middle of the night, the covering force was ordered out too early, by mistake, and the front line stood empty. The general met them on the road himself and sent them back. The British noticed nothing.

[S03 | dawn: fog rolls in thick over Brooklyn and the river (fog veil); the last boats push off into it; the banner moves to the last boat; caption FOG]
At sunrise, thousands of men were still waiting on the Brooklyn shore. Then a thick fog came down over the river. Under it, the last boats pushed off. The general crossed in one of the last of them.

[S03 | the fog lifts over empty forts; red blocks walk into the works; across the river the blue army is in New York; result card: ABOUT 9,000 MEN CROSSED IN ONE NIGHT; LEFT BEHIND: A HANDFUL OF STRAGGLERS]
When the fog lifted, the British walked into empty forts. About nine thousand men, with nearly all of their guns, had crossed the river in a single night. Only a handful of stragglers were left behind.

[S03 | method card: THE WASHINGTON METHOD, 1. SAVE THE ARMY FIRST on its words]
That was the first part of his method. As long as the army lives, the cause lives. Save the army first.

[S03 | colonies wide: the blue army falls back up Manhattan; label NEXT: CHRISTMAS NIGHT; subscribe button clicks on "subscribing"]
Brooklyn was only the beginning. So how do you win a war with an army that is about to go home? That's the kind of question we answer here, and subscribing is how you catch the next one. Here's how Washington did it.

## S04 THE LONG RETREAT (~4:00 - ~5:15)

[S04 | colonies wide, autumn: red arrows chase the blue army north off Manhattan; stamps on their words: WHITE PLAINS, FORT WASHINGTON; number 2,800 PRISONERS on its words]
The British plan was to end the war before winter. They drove Washington off Manhattan and beat him again at White Plains. On the sixteenth of November, they stormed Fort Washington, on the Hudson, and took nearly three thousand Americans prisoner.

[S04 | the blue army retreats across New Jersey (a long blue arrow south-west from Fort Lee to Trenton), red arrows close behind; date plate rolls DECEMBER 1776]
Then they chased what was left of his army across New Jersey, all the way to the Delaware River.

[S04 | jersey wide (dissolve), winter: the blue army crosses to Pennsylvania; separate boats along sixty miles of river pulled to the far bank (boat markers move across, one by one); date plate 8 DECEMBER]
On the seventh and eighth of December, he crossed into Pennsylvania, and took every boat along sixty miles of the river with him, so the British could not follow. Congress fled Philadelphia.

[S04 | red posts settle along the New Jersey bank: Trenton, Bordentown, Princeton; Trenton glows; label HESSIANS: RALL, LOSSBERG, KNYPHAUSEN on its words; Rall banner with a red pennant]
The British settled into winter posts across New Jersey. At the river town of Trenton they left three regiments of Hessians, about fourteen hundred men, under Colonel Johann Rall.

[S04 | the blue camp behind the river: blocks thin out, a few fade away; caption ENLISTMENTS END 31 DECEMBER on its words; the date plate stays]
On the other bank, Washington's army was melting away. Thousands were sick, many had no shoes, and the enlistments of most of the rest ran out on the thirty-first of December. He had one week to do something.

## S05 MOVE 2: CHRISTMAS NIGHT AND TRENTON (~5:15 - ~7:45)

[S05 | jersey wide, winter day: the chain of red posts across New Jersey (Trenton, Bordentown, Princeton, New Brunswick); Trenton glows; caption NO FORTIFICATIONS on its words]
The British plan was to sit out the winter in a chain of posts across New Jersey, and finish the rebels in the spring. At Trenton, the post closest to the enemy, Colonel Rall did not even build fortifications.

[S05 | the plan as three blue arrows across the river: McConkey's Ferry (main), Trenton Ferry, Bristol; labels small on their words]
In other words, nobody expected the beaten Americans to attack before spring. But Washington did not wait for spring. Instead, he planned to cross back over the river on Christmas night, at three places, and attack Trenton at dawn.

[S05 | cloud dive 3 into the crossing at night: McConkey's Ferry, the Pennsylvania bank lined with blue blocks; the big boats; the storm begins (sleet streaks); date plate 25 DECEMBER 1776]
After dark on Christmas Day, his main force of about two thousand four hundred men began to cross at McConkey's Ferry, nine miles above Trenton.

[S05 | the river: long black Durham boats as separate vessels, each its own path, poled and rowed through ice floes; label GLOVER on its word; ferry flatboats carry guns and horses]
The boats were long, black Durham boats, built to carry iron ore down the river. And at the oars were the same fishermen from Marblehead who had saved the army at Brooklyn.

[S05 | storm stronger: sleet slanting across the lanterns; ice floes knock the boats; the guns land on the New Jersey bank one by one; caption 18 GUNS on its words]
Around eleven, the storm broke over them: snow, sleet and hail, driven by a freezing wind. Ice floes knocked against the boats. It took until three in the morning to get every man, horse and all eighteen cannon across.

[S05 | the other two crossings: grey arrows that stop at the river with ice symbols; caption THE OTHER TWO CROSSINGS FAILED on its words]
They were hours behind schedule. And downriver, the other two crossings failed in the ice. Washington would attack alone.

[S05 | jersey wide (dissolve), night storm: the blue army marches south in a column of blocks; at a fork it splits into two columns (Greene on the upper road, Sullivan on the river road); a distance ruler 9 MILES on its words]
His men marched the nine miles to Trenton through the storm, in the dark. At a crossroads, the army split in two, so it could hit the town from two sides at once.

[S05 | trenton (dissolve), grey dawn: the Hessian picket on the upper road fires; blue blocks deploy; date plate 26 DECEMBER 1776]
At about eight in the morning, on the twenty-sixth of December, they burst out of the storm onto the Hessian outposts.

[S05 | the guns at the head of King and Queen streets fire down both streets (flash + sound each shot); red blocks try to form in the streets and are driven back; blue blocks enter the town from the river road side]
The American guns raced to the top of the town's two main streets and fired straight down them. The Hessians tried to form up in the streets, but they were raked by cannon fire and shot at from the houses.

[S05 | Rall's banner rides out, falls (fades grey) on its words; red blocks fall back to an orchard east of the town; blue blocks close around them]
Colonel Rall led a counterattack and was shot from his horse. His men were driven out into the fields east of the town, surrounded, and they laid down their arms.

[S05 | the Assunpink bridge: some red blocks stream across before blue blocks close it; label ASSUNPINK on its word]
Several hundred got away across a bridge over the creek south of the town before the Americans closed it. The rest were trapped.

[S05 | result card over the dimmed town: HESSIANS ABOUT 900 CAPTURED, 22 KILLED, 83 WOUNDED; AMERICANS NONE KILLED IN THE FIGHT, 5 WOUNDED; small line: among the wounded, Lt. James Monroe]
About nine hundred Hessians were captured, and more than a hundred killed or wounded. Not one American was killed in the battle. Among the handful of wounded was a young lieutenant named James Monroe, a future president.

[S05 | method card: 2. HIT THEM WHERE THEY REST]
That was the second part of his method. When the enemy settles in for the winter, hit him where he rests.

[S05 | colonies wide: a long red arrow from New York south across New Jersey toward Trenton; label CORNWALLIS on its word with banner]
But the British were not going to let it go. In New York, General Charles Cornwallis was about to sail home to England. Instead, he was sent racing to Trenton, to finish Washington for good.

## S06 MOVE 3: THE TRAP AT THE CREEK (~7:45 - ~9:30)

[S06 | jersey wide, winter: the blue army crosses back over to Trenton (separate boats); caption 6 MORE WEEKS on its words; date plate 31 DECEMBER]
Washington had crossed back into New Jersey. On the last day of the year, he begged his men to stay six more weeks, for a bounty of ten dollars each. Many of them stayed.

[S06 | date plate 2 JANUARY 1777: the red army marches down the road from Princeton toward Trenton; small blue blocks fire and fall back along the road, again and again (muzzle flashes + sound)]
On the second of January, Cornwallis marched on Trenton with about five thousand five hundred men. Cornwallis's plan was simple: trap Washington against the river and destroy him. But all day, small groups of American riflemen fought him from the woods and creeks along the road, and fell back, and fought again.

[S06 | trenton (dissolve), dusk: the blue army in a line along the south bank of the Assunpink Creek, the stone bridge in the middle, guns behind it; red blocks come through the town to the bridge]
It was nearly dark when the British reached Trenton. Washington's army stood behind the Assunpink Creek, south of the town, with its guns covering the stone bridge.

[S06 | the British attack the bridge three times: red blocks advance onto the bridge, the American guns and muskets fire (each with its sound), the red blocks about-face and fall back; caption 3 ATTACKS on "three"]
The British tried to storm the bridge three times. Each time they were thrown back by cannon and musket fire.

[S06 | night: red campfires spread across the town and the fields north of the creek; blue campfires along the south bank; the Delaware glows behind the blue line]
Then Cornwallis stopped for the night. His men were tired, and the Americans had nowhere to go. In other words, he would finish them in the morning.

Now stop for a moment, and put yourself in Washington's place. The British army in front of you is bigger and better trained, and more troops are coming. Behind you is the river, and no boats. ## S07 MOVE 3: THE NIGHT MARCH TO PRINCETON (~9:30 - ~11:45)

[S07 | trenton, night: the decision reveal; a few blue blocks stay at the creek and keep the fires burning (campfires), men digging; the rest of the army turns and walks away east and south into the dark]
He slipped away. But not back across the river. Instead, he marched around the British army, and behind it. About five hundred men stayed at the creek to keep the campfires burning and to dig noisily, a covering force, just as at Brooklyn.

[S07 | jersey wide, night: the blue army column moves east and then north on a back road (the Quaker Bridge road) around the British left; frost spreads over the ground; caption THE MUD FROZE on its words]
The rest of the army marched off into the dark, by a back road the British were not watching. That night the wind changed, the weather turned bitter cold, and the muddy roads froze hard, just in time for the guns to roll.

[S07 | jersey wide, dawn: the blue column approaches Princeton from the south; a red column leaves Princeton marching south-west toward Trenton on the post road; the two converge; date plate 3 JANUARY 1777]
At sunrise on the third of January, the Americans came up on Princeton, twelve miles behind the British army. And a British brigade was just marching out of the town, on its way to join Cornwallis.

[S07 | princeton (dissolve): an orchard by a farm; blue blocks (Mercer) and red blocks (Mawhood) wheel to face each other; volleys (flash + sound); the red line charges with the bayonet]
An American brigade under General Hugh Mercer ran into them in an orchard. The British fired a volley and charged with the bayonet. Mercer's horse was shot from under him. He was surrounded, bayoneted again and again, and left for dead.

[S07 | Mercer's banner falls (fades grey); blue blocks about-face and fall back; fresh blue militia blocks arrive behind them, waver]
His men broke, and the militia coming up behind them began to waver.

[S07 | Washington's banner rides forward between the two lines, close to the red line; both lines fire (flashes + smoke); the smoke clears, the banner still stands; caption 30 YARDS on its words]
Then Washington rode forward himself, into the open between the two lines, about thirty yards from the British. Both sides fired. When the smoke cleared, he was still in the saddle.

[S07 | blue blocks advance on both sides of the banner; red blocks break and run south-west; a red group falls back into the town to a large building (Nassau Hall), a gun fires at it, a white flag (the block turns grey)]
The Americans rallied behind him and drove the British from the field. Some of them fled into the college building in the town, and surrendered when the Americans broke down the door.

[S07 | result card over the dimmed field: BRITISH 270-440 KILLED, WOUNDED OR CAPTURED (SOURCES DIFFER), 194 PRISONERS; AMERICANS ABOUT 75 (GENERAL MERCER MORTALLY WOUNDED); source line]
The British lost hundreds of men, killed, wounded or captured, almost two hundred of them taken prisoner. The Americans lost fewer than a hundred, but General Mercer died of his wounds nine days later.

[S07 | trenton, dawn: the British blocks wake facing empty works, the fires burning out; Cornwallis banner; a red arrow turns back north-east toward Princeton; no gun sound here (the guns are off screen)]
At the creek, Cornwallis woke up to empty lines and dying campfires, and heard cannon behind him, at Princeton. Afraid for his base at New Brunswick, he rushed his army back north.

[S07 | method card: 3. GO WHERE THEY ARE NOT, completing the method]
That was the third part of his method. When the enemy has you trapped, don't go where he expects. Go where he is not.

## S08 ENDING (~11:45 - ~12:45)

[S08 | colonies wide, winter: the blue army marches north into the hills to Morristown; red posts across New Jersey pull back to New Brunswick and Perth Amboy; the red ground shrinks; date plate JANUARY 1777]
Washington led his army into winter quarters in the hills at Morristown. And the British pulled back from almost all of New Jersey.

[S08 | the letter card returns briefly: I THINK THE GAME IS PRETTY NEAR UP, then a slow push over the map]
Three weeks after he wrote that the game was pretty near up, the Revolution was alive again. The war would last almost seven more years, but the army that would win it had been saved in those nights. The method had three parts: save the army first, hit them where they rest, and go where they are not.
