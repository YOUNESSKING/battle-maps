In December 1776 the American Revolution was running out of time. On New Year's Eve most of its beaten army could go home. On Christmas night about 2,400 of the men who were left crossed the icy Delaware in a storm, and at dawn they fell on the Hessians at Trenton: in about an hour, some nine hundred were prisoners, and not one American had been killed in the fight. Four months earlier the same army had nearly been trapped for good on Brooklyn Heights, and a week after Trenton it was trapped again, with a British army in front and a river full of ice behind it.

This is how George Washington kept the Revolution alive, told on animated 3D maps: the night his whole army slipped across the East River from Brooklyn under the noses of the British; the Christmas-night crossing of the Delaware and the attack on Trenton; and the night he left his campfires burning at the Assunpink Creek, marched around Cornwallis, and struck behind him at Princeton.

Notes: Casualty figures differ between sources, especially for Princeton (British losses are given as roughly 270 to 440 killed, wounded or captured; Americans roughly 40 to 80). Two Americans died of exposure on the march to Trenton; none were killed in the fighting. The fog at Brooklyn came only at first light, after more than half the army was across. Some details (Washington meeting the covering force on the road at Brooklyn) rest on later accounts. Popular stories not used: drunk Hessians, wheels wrapped in rags, and the "fine fox chase" line.
