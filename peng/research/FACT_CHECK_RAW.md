## 0. Biggest corrections (read first)
1. **"Longest retreat in US military history (275 miles)" is a real, sourced phrase but needs care.** It is the Eighth Army's own history page ("Before it could organize an effective line of defense, Eighth Army had withdrawn a total of 275 miles (which qualifies as the longest retreat in US military history)"), and Wikipedia says "often referred to as" it (citing Alexander, Malkasian, Varhola) but gives the withdrawal to the 38th parallel as about 120 mi. Mossman never uses the phrase. Safe wording: "what the US Army itself calls the longest retreat in its history"; do not say "275 miles" for the Chongchon-to-38th-parallel leg. Gemini's "Dec 1-14, flees 275 miles south past the 38th Parallel" is wrong: 275 mi is the total down to Line D / beyond (Jan 1951), not "past the 38th parallel" in two weeks.
2. **Eighth Army "abandoned heavy equipment and fled" is overstated.** Walker deliberately withdrew in delaying steps; MacArthur's HQ told him he could leave behind materiel as long as it was destroyed (EF ch. VIII). Mossman: "bugout fever" is "unwarranted" as a charge of cowardice; the main reason was the "constant threat of envelopment from the east."
3. **"300,000 men undetected" CORRECTED.** The first wave (XIII Army Group) was about **200,000** (Mossman: "Some 200,000 Chinese constituting the XIII Army Group ... six armies"); the 9th Army Group (~120,000, "approaching 120,000") entered the east in early November, giving **"above 300,000" by 23 Nov**. So 300,000 is the 23 Nov total, not the Oct crossing. And the UN did detect Chinese: they were identified in prisoners from 25 Oct; what was missed was the SCALE (UNC acknowledged 12 infantry divisions on 23 Nov when there were 30).
4. **Crossing date: 19 Oct (Wikipedia, widely quoted) but Peng's own memoir says he crossed "at dusk on October 18, 1950"**; Peng's 1953 report says the Volunteers "left China on October 25, 1950" (the official entry/combat date). Present as "night of 18-19 Oct".
5. **Quote 1 ("The enemy is advancing in separate columns, and his flanks are exposed...") UNSUPPORTED.** Not in Peng's memoir; could not be found in any source. Do not put in quotation marks or attribute to Peng.
6. **Quote 2 (artillery on the coast) is real but Gemini's wording is a paraphrase.** The real 1953 English text: "the days have gone forever when a Western aggressor was able - as for several hundred years past - to occupy a country by merely setting up some cannons along a sea coast in the East." Delivered 12 Sep 1953 (Central People's Government Council, 24th meeting), published 1 Oct 1953.
7. **Quote 3 ("Hide by day, march by night. If aeroplanes approach, every man must freeze") UNSUPPORTED.** Not in any source read. The facts (night march, concealment) are sourced; the "quote" is not.
8. **"Home by Christmas" was NOT MacArthur's scripted promise to troops "just days before ... dismissing intelligence".** Sourced reality: he flew to Korea on 24 Nov, said the attack "should for all practical purposes end the war"; the "home by Christmas" line is a press/remark-level quote ("if they go fast enough maybe some of them can be home by Christmas"), and Mossman describes it only as "speculation ... to end the Korean War by Christmas". Also the Chongchon River offensive began 24 Nov, the Chinese attacked the night of 25 Nov (one day, not "days").
9. **Third Phase start: Gemini "5:00 PM" is roughly right but wrong in detail.** Mossman: 116th Division (39th Army) crossed the Imjin "just after full dark" behind a 30-minute artillery preparation on 31 Dec; around midnight 116th and 113th (38th Army) hit the ROK 6th Div. (Wikipedia: shelling began 16:30.)
10. **Third Phase: ROK 1st Division was the one that broke open, not "ROK 1st and 6th broke on Jan 1".** Mossman: 116th Div "shoved the 12th Regiment out of position"; the ROK 6th "fared better, giving little ground" on 1 Jan, and only disintegrated during its own withdrawal on 2 Jan (6,000 men, half strength, gathered by 0900 on 3 Jan).
11. **"Seoul entered without engaging the heavy US formations" is mostly right, but there WAS a fight:** the British 29th Brigade's rear guard (Royal Ulster Rifles) was surrounded the night of 3-4 Jan; "between two hundred fifty and three hundred men and at least ten tanks, were lost" (EF ch. X). Also 4 Jan: Chinese attacked the US 27th Infantry covering force.
12. **Unsan: "8th Cavalry virtually destroyed" CORRECTED.** The 1st and 2nd Battalions escaped (heavy equipment lost); the **3rd Battalion** was destroyed ("less than 200 men of the 3rd Battalion managed to reach UN lines", Wikipedia). Not the whole regiment.
13. **Second Phase strength figures are DISPUTED, "130,000 Eighth Army combat effectives" UNSUPPORTED.** UNC ground forces in Korea on 23 Nov were 423,313 (223,950 ROK, 178,464 American, incl. X Corps), EF ch. I. Wikipedia infobox gives 254,571 UN troops on the Chongchon front.
14. **2nd Division loss: "over 4,000 men and all its artillery" CORRECTED.** 4,940 battle casualties 15-30 Nov (about 4,500 since 25 Nov), one third of its 15,000 strength; **64 artillery pieces** lost, not all (EF ch. VI, p. 127).
15. **"Previous video on Ridgway": the channel has no full Ridgway video** (already noted by main session).
16. **Mossman is itself outdated on Chinese names:** it says Lin Piao was the Chinese commander in Korea until Peng "replaced" him in Jan/Feb 1951 and names the XIII Army Group commander "Li Tien-yu". Modern sources (and Peng's own memoir) have Peng commanding from the October 1950 crossing; XIII Army Group was under Li Tianyu/Deng Hua. Use Peng's memoir and Wikipedia for who commanded; do not cite Mossman for this.

## 1. Bio
- Born **24 Oct 1898**, Shixiang village, Xiangtan County, Hunan: OK (Wikipedia). Peng's memoir: born "on the 10th day of the 9th moon on the lunar calendar in 1898" (consistent). PB ch. I.
- Died **29 Nov 1974**, Beijing, aged 76: OK (Wikipedia; Britannica; Laogai Research Foundation).
- **Age in Oct 1950: 51 on crossing night (18-19 Oct); turned 52 on 24 Oct 1950.** Gemini "aged 52 during these battles" is OK for Unsan (1 Nov) onwards; at the crossing he was 51.
- Death: CORRECTED/DISPUTED. He died in custody after Cultural Revolution persecution. Wikipedia: "probably from tuberculosis, thrombosis, or both" (cancer is NOT sourced; some Chinese accounts say rectal/intestinal cancer, I found no solid English source). Medical neglect: Domes cites scattered evidence Mao ordered no substantial medical aid; Shambaugh says Jiang Qing directed denial of treatment. Safe line: "died in custody in 1974, denied proper medical care". Rehabilitated posthumously Dec 1978 (3rd plenum 11th CC; Deng said the 1959 decision was "entirely wrong").
- Purge: OK. Lushan Plenum (8th Plenum, 8th CC) opened 2 Jul 1959; Peng wrote a private letter criticising the Great Leap Forward (dated 14 Jul 1959, in his memoir appendix); Mao circulated it on 17 Jul and denounced him as head of an "anti-Party clique". Arrested by Red Guards 1966; tortured / struggle sessions; tried 1970, sentenced to life (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peng_Dehuai).
- Long March: OK. Of 18,000 men he commanded at the start, about 3,000 remained on reaching Shaanxi (Wikipedia).
- Appointment: Mao first asked Lin Biao, who declined citing health. Peng was summoned to Beijing on **4 Oct 1950** (memoir: "At noon on October 4 ... an airplane arrived in Xi'an"), backed intervention on 5 Oct, then named commander and political commissar of the PVA (held until the 1953 armistice). OK. Mao's 8 Oct 1950 telegram to Stalin already names Peng.
- Nicknames: CORRECTED. "Old Chief Peng" (Peng Laozong, 彭老总) and "Grand General Peng" (彭大将军, from Mao's poem) are sourced (Wikipedia). **"Generalissimo Peng" UNSUPPORTED** and wrong in kind (he was a Marshal, 1955). "Lion of the Red Army" (红军之狮) also appears in Wikipedia.

## 2. Crossing
- Date: **night of 18-19 Oct** (memoir: "At dusk on October 18, 1950 I crossed the Yalu River with vanguard units"; they reached Ragocho power station on 19 Oct morning; Wikipedia Korean War: PVA 13th Army Group crossed "secretly" 19 Oct). Mossman: XIII Army Group "had entered Korea during the last half of October, crossing the Yalu at Sinuiju and Manp'ojin."
- First wave: Mossman ch. I: "Some 200,000 Chinese constituting the XIII Army Group of the Fourth Field Army ... six armies (38th, 39th, 40th, 42nd, 50th, 66th), each with three infantry divisions and about 30,000 men, two artillery divisions ... a cavalry regiment, and two truck regiments." Four armies (38, 39, 40, 42) fought the First Phase. Gemini's list (38/39/40/42 + artillery, ~260,000) UNSUPPORTED as a total; use ~200,000 for the group. (Wikipedia Korean War: "200,000 ... crossed".)
- IX Army Group (Third Field Army): 3 armies, each reinforced by a 4th division, about 40,000 each, "approaching 120,000"; crossed at Manp'ojin and Lin-chiang during the first half of Nov and went to Changjin/Chosin. "The total Chinese commitment in Korea by 23 November thus had risen above 300,000 men." (EF ch. I, pp. 54-55). OK for ~300,000 as the 23 Nov total.
- Concealment: Mossman: "a concerted effort to avoid aerial observation through a rigid march and bivouac discipline, movements under the cover of darkness, and substantial use of secondary roads"; "Other than Mosquito control aircraft ... the Far East Air Forces had no planes committed to visual reconnaissance"; "very little evidence of the Chinese entry was from the air." OK. FEAF's Yalu bridge attacks began 8 Nov, too late to hit the crossing.
- Code-name deception: "Perhaps most deceptive was a battalion designation for a division"; "By 23 November the UNC had acknowledged the presence of twelve Chinese infantry divisions when in fact there were nine armies with thirty infantry divisions." (EF ch. I.) Strong, safe fact for the video.
- UN estimates (EF): before the 24 Nov advance Eighth Army G-2 expected ~**48,000** Chinese along the likely defence lines (ch. IV); the "latest UNC estimate" was **~70,000** Chinese in Korea ("Over four times that number were massed in the mountains", ch. I); after the attack, on 26 Nov the G-2 raised the figure opposite Eighth Army **from 54,000 to 101,000**; by 29 Nov the highest estimate of total enemy strength was about 167,000, Chinese about 70,000 (ch. IV). Willoughby (FEC G-2) on 26 Nov: "should the enemy elect to fight in the interior valleys, a slowing down of the United Nations offensive may result." Gemini's "Willoughby ~30-70k" is the right range for the pre-attack estimate but I could not tie "30,000" to a page; use the sourced 48,000 / 70,000 / 54,000-101,000.
- "Hide by day, march by night, every man must freeze": UNSUPPORTED as a quote (see section 3). Peng's memoir (1st-3rd campaigns) says only that the troops "could not move about in the daytime" because of the bombing (ch. XIV).

## 3. Quotes
1. "The enemy is advancing in separate columns..." Peng to Mao Oct 1950: **UNSUPPORTED.** Not in Peng's memoir. Closest real text: memoir, Second Campaign: "We employed the tactic of purposely showing ourselves to be weak, increasing the arrogance of the enemy, letting him run amuck, and luring him deep into our areas." (PB p. 476). That is Peng's own wording and safe to use, with attribution to his memoir.
2. 1953 report: **REAL, source found.** "General Peng Teh-huai's Report on the Achievements of the Chinese People's Volunteers in Resisting U.S. Aggression and Aiding Korea", delivered at the 24th meeting of the Central People's Government Council, **12 Sep 1953**; *People's China* supplement, 1 Oct 1953. Verbatim (OCR corrected): "This is a lesson of great international significance. It conclusively proves that the days have gone forever when a Western aggressor was able - as for several hundred years past - to occupy a country by merely setting up some cannons along a sea coast in the East; that today any form of imperialist aggression can be defeated by relying on the strength of the people." Gemini's wording ("The time has passed when a Western aggressor could conquer a country by merely placing a few artillery pieces along the coast") is a paraphrase: use the real one. URL: https://www.marxists.org/subject/china/peoples-china/1953/PC1953-19-Sup-OCR-sm.pdf
3. "Home by Christmas": **CORRECTED.** Facts: MacArthur flew to Korea on the morning of **24 Nov 1950** to watch the Eighth Army step off (EF ch. IV). Mossman: he called the advance a "massive compression envelopment" and said "if successful, it should for all practical purposes end the war"; and "even some speculation that UNC forces would reach the border and end the Korean War by Christmas, the date on which the war would be exactly six months old" (EF ch. IV opening). The oft-quoted line, attributed to MacArthur, is "Well, if they go fast enough, maybe some of them can be home by Christmas" (Wikipedia, Battle of the Ch'ongch'on River; I could not confirm the original press source). Peng's memoir (Chinese view): MacArthur's headquarters "said in a broadcast to the troops: 'get ready, push to the Yalu River, and be home before Christmas'" (PB p. 476; note Peng wrongly puts it in "mid-November"). Safe on-screen: "He flew in on 24 November: 'This should for all practical purposes end the war.'" (communiqué No. 12). Do NOT present "home by Christmas" as an official promise to the troops.

## 4. First Phase / Unsan
(Appleman unreadable; see source note. Everything here is Mossman summary or Wikipedia unless marked.)
- First Phase dates: Mossman: Chinese discovered 25 Oct; "continued to attack until 6 November and then ... abruptly broke contact" (EF ch. I: "the voluntary disengagement of the XIII Army Group on 6 November"; Chinese phrasing: 25 Oct-6 Nov). Wikipedia Unsan: 25 Oct-4 Nov. Gemini "25 Oct - 4 Nov" OK for Unsan; the whole campaign is 25 Oct-5/6 Nov. Peng's memoir: "The First Campaign ended in victory for us on October 25" (his dating differs; ignore).
- ROK 6th Division: reached the Yalu at **Ch'osan** ("South Korean reconnaissance troops with an interior Eighth Army column reached the Yalu at the town of Ch'osan", EF ch. I) on 26 Oct per standard accounts; I could not confirm 26 Oct from a fetched source. Destroyed at **Onjong** 25-29 Oct by the 40th Army (118th/119th/120th Divs): 2nd Regt dispersed 26 Oct, 7th Regt ambushed 29 Oct (Wikipedia, Battle of Onjong). "ROK 6th Division was destroyed" OK.
- ROK 1st Division at Unsan: captured Unsan 25 Oct, surrounded, road reopened 27 Oct, 11th Regt withdrew by 29 Oct (Wikipedia Unsan). Gemini "Oct 25 PVA strikes ROK 1st Div ... exposes the 1st Cav flank" roughly OK (ROK 1st Div held the 40th Army's 120th Div at Unsan; 1st Cav replaced it on ~29 Oct).
- Chinese units at Unsan: **39th Army (115th, 116th, 117th Divs)** attacked the Unsan garrison 1 Nov; 40th Army's 120th Div held the ROK 1st Div; 38th and part of 42nd attacked ROK 6th/8th east of Unsan (Wikipedia). 116th Div: 347th Regt entered through the gap between battalions; 348th Regt road-junction ambush; 115th: 343rd Regt at "Bugle Hill" ambush of a 5th Cav patrol, 345th squeezed the 3rd Bn. **Gemini "39th and 40th Armies, 50-60,000" UNSUPPORTED** as a figure; a 39th Army was ~30,000 (Mossman Table 2 range 21,000-30,000 per army).
- 8th Cavalry attack: night of **1 Nov** (Wikipedia: Chinese in Unsan and ambushing at the road junction by 02:30 on 2 Nov). 1st and 2nd Battalions escaped by infiltrating in small groups and reached UN lines by 2 Nov; **3rd Battalion** held out in a 200-yard perimeter until survivors broke out by 4 Nov; "less than 200 men of the 3rd Battalion managed to reach UN lines" (Wikipedia). Gemini "Nov 2, 03:00" OK; "by Nov 4 virtually destroyed" OK for the 3rd Bn only.
- **Bugles/whistles at Unsan UNSUPPORTED from fetched text** (Wikipedia mentions none; "Bugle Hill" is a map name). BUT Mossman documents bugles/whistles/horns repeatedly in the Second Phase: "At the sound of bugle signals, the Chinese in file changed formation for assaults" (EF ch. IV, 25-26 Nov, 23rd Infantry area); at Yudam-ni "bugle calls and whistle blasts" (ch. V); "A cacophony of whistle, horn, and bugle blasts preceded an attack on Company C about 2330" (ch. V, 5th/7th Div area). So use bugles for Nov 25-27, not specifically for Unsan unless Appleman is checked.
- US losses (Army.mil "Disaster at Unsan", search-snippet only, unverified): more than 1,000 men of the regiment initially missing; about 400 stragglers later returned; Chinese captured an estimated 200-300; the 3rd Battalion's organized resistance reportedly ended about 1600 on 6 Nov (this conflicts with Wikipedia's 4 Nov). Wikipedia infobox: US 449 killed, 1,149 total casualties (whole battle), 5th Cav relief attempt cost about 350 casualties. **Gemini "~1,000+ killed/missing/captured" is plausible for "missing, initially" but "killed" is overstated (449 killed in the infobox). Mark the 8th Cav total as DISPUTED; use "more than a thousand men missing at first" only if Army.mil is confirmed.**
- 5th Cavalry relief attempt failed; Gay ordered it to withdraw "after suffering 350 casualties" (Wikipedia). Maj. Gen. Hobart Gay commanded the 1st Cav Div: OK. "Forced to retreat" is fair. Milburn (I Corps) ordered the Unsan garrison out after the ROK 6th Div was destroyed on the right flank.
- Chinese disengagement: 5-6 Nov; Wikipedia: because of food and ammunition shortages; Mossman suggests Chinese caution / pause while the IX Army Group arrived. Chinese losses: UN estimated >600 at Unsan; Chinese estimate ~2,000 (Chinese Military Science Academy, as quoted on Wikipedia infobox); First Phase total 10,700 (Wikipedia). "Moderate/unknown" in Gemini is vague but OK.
- Terrain: Kuryong River flows past Unsan (Mossman: IX Corps "astride the Kuryong River toward Unsan and Onjong"); Task Force Dolvin recovered 30 men of the 8th Cav, captured at Unsan and released by the Chinese, 24 Nov (EF ch. IV): useful detail (Chinese released prisoners as part of their deception, also in Wikipedia). "Samtan River" UNSUPPORTED here. Gemini's "Kuryong River" OK.
- "UN intelligence believed Chinese were token volunteers incapable of a divisional offensive": DISPUTED/overstated. MacArthur's HQ downplayed it, but Eighth Army's own G-2 expected ~48,000 Chinese and a defence line at Unsan (EF ch. IV). The fair statement is that intelligence underestimated the scale by 3-4x.

## 5. Second Phase / Chongchon
- Eighth Army offensive step-off **Fri 24 Nov** (EF ch. IV: "A full resumption of the offensive was set for Friday, 24 November, to clear the remaining hundred miles or less ..."): OK. Naming it "Home by Christmas offensive" is a popular label (Wikipedia); Mossman does not use it.
- Chinese attack: **night of 25 Nov** (EF: "In opening the offensive on the night of the 25th, XIII Army Group commander ... had aimed his main attack at the IX Corps, in particular at the 2d Division, using units of the 39th and 40th Armies. In secondary attacks, forces of the 38th and 42d Armies had pushed the ROK II Corps out of position in the east while units of the 50th and 66th ... had launched assaults against the I Corps in the west.") CORRECTED: Gemini says Peng "explicitly targeted the ROK II Corps" as THE main effort; Mossman says the main attack was on the US IX Corps / 2d Division and the ROK II Corps was a secondary attack that then became the opening. (Wikipedia: 38th Army also struck the ROK 6th/7th/8th.)
- ROK II Corps (6th, 7th, 8th Divs): collapsed **near noon on 26 Nov**: "Thus, near noon of the 26th, the ROK II Corps front folded." (EF ch. IV, p. ~72-74). Gemini "Nov 26" OK. "30-mile hole" UNSUPPORTED (not found; Wikipedia says 30 mi was the later Eighth Army withdrawal distance from Kunu-ri).
- Wawon / Turkish Brigade: 27-29 Nov; Turks cut off, effectively destroyed as a fighting unit, ~20% casualties (Wikipedia, Battle of Wawon: 218 killed, 455 wounded, 94 missing); Mossman: Turks "in disorder" after the pass fight, Yasici later regathered "some thirty-five hundred of his original five thousand men" (EF ch. VIII, 2 Dec).
- 2d Infantry Division at Kunu-ri: 29-30 Nov. Mossman: 2d Div supply road ran Kunu-ri to Sunch'on; "the northern six miles" held all the division artillery, the division HQ sat six miles south of Kunu-ri; Chinese 112th and 113th Divs built "an elongated fire block" along the road: "As Peploe's 2d Battalion ... moved over the four-mile stretch below division headquarters, the two regiments of the 113th Division in the bordering heights began strengthening their elongated fire block. The heaviest fire fell in the pass" (EF ch. VI, p. 122). **"Gauntlet" length: Gemini 6 mi; Wikipedia (citing Appleman 1989, "Disaster in Korea") says the roadblock "grew to 6 mi in depth"** : acceptable as "about 6 miles" but attribute to Appleman/Wikipedia, not Mossman. Name: "the Gauntlet" from S.L.A. Marshall's *The River and the Gauntlet* (1953), used throughout the Mossman footnotes. NOTE: Mossman's "ten-mile gauntlet" is the Chosin Kot'o-ri road, not Kunu-ri; don't mix them.
- Machine-gun fire from the ridges: OK in substance ("small arms, machine gun, and mortar fire"); "50 yards up the slopes" UNSUPPORTED.
- **2d Division losses: CORRECTED.** EF p. 126-127: "A count rendered on 1 December listed 2d Division battle casualties at 4,940 for the last half of November. Of these, 90 percent, or about 4,500, had been incurred since the 25th ... one-third of the division's actual strength of 15,000 on 15 November ... In addition to hundreds of trucks and trailers, the major losses included **64 artillery pieces**, almost all of the 2d Engineer Combat Battalion's equipment, and between 20 and 40 percent of the signal equipment." Wikipedia: postwar estimate 4,037 casualties. Gemini "over 4,000" OK; "all its artillery" CORRECTED to 64 guns (the 38th and 503rd FA Battalions were effectively lost; 23rd Inf's 15th FA fired its ammunition and left howitzers). 
- The 23rd Infantry (Col. Freeman) escaped by the Sinanju-Sukch'on route instead and "wise choice": EF p. 125.
- Overall UN casualties: Wikipedia: "11,000+"; US 676 KIA / 3,034 WIA / 2,055 captured / 743 missing; Turkey 218/455/94. Gemini "approx. 11,000" OK.
- Chinese strength: **XIII Army Group ~200,000** (Mossman), Wikipedia infobox 230,000 (Roe et al.) with a note actual may have been as low as 150,000. Gemini "180-230k, Roe 180k, Blair 230k" cannot be verified; say "about 200,000" (EF). Wikipedia Chinese casualties: 45,000 infobox; Chinese claim 20,000 battle casualties. DISPUTED, don't put on screen as fact.
- Peng's plan: deception ("showing ourselves weak", released POWs, withdrew after First Phase); authorized the campaign on 22 Nov (Wikipedia). Peng claims "no fewer than 6,000 motor vehicles and well over 1,000 tanks and gun-carriers" lost by the enemy (PB p. 476): obvious exaggeration, UNSUPPORTED, do not use.
- Walker's decision: after the Tokyo conference of 28 Nov MacArthur ordered the withdrawal; Walker ordered withdrawal 29 Nov to Sunch'on line (Wikipedia). Pyongyang: Walker decided 2 Dec; "Much of the city was afire by 0730 on **5 December** when the rear guards destroyed the last bridges over the Taedong" (EF ch. VIII p. 154); 8-10,000 tons of supplies lost; Chinese had not made contact ("with almost no enemy contact"). OK.
- To the 38th parallel: by 23 Dec I Corps stood on the Han/Imjin, IX Corps "right at the 38th parallel" (EF ch. IX). Gemini "Dec 1-14" is about right as the main retreat; stable positions by 23 Dec; Eighth Army's official history count: 275 miles total. Distance to the parallel ~120 mi (Wikipedia). Gemini's "shattered ... abandons heavy equipment" see section 0 item 2.
- **Walker's death: OK, but "during the retreat" is imprecise.** 23 Dec 1950, jeep collided with a Korean civilian's 3/4-ton truck about **ten miles north of Uijongbu**; Walker was "pronounced dead of multiple head injuries" at the 24th Division clearing station (EF ch. IX, p. 177). He was driving to visit units; the retreat was already over. (Also a "ROK Ministry of National Defense" note on the driver being sentenced.)
- Ridgway: Collins informed him near midnight 22 Dec; he left Washington 23 Dec, reached Tokyo late 25 Dec, Taegu late **26 Dec**, Seoul at dawn 27 Dec (EF ch. IX). OK for "arrived in Korea 26 Dec". He found the army "a dispirited command".

## 6. Third Phase / Seoul
- Start: **31 Dec 1950**. Mossman: 116th Div (39th Army) crossed the Imjin "just after full dark" after a 30-minute artillery preparation; broadened around midnight (116th and 113th Div, 38th Army, hit the ROK 6th Div); two hours later (about 0200) the 66th Army hit ROK 5th Div, ~0500 ROK 2nd Div. Wikipedia: shelling began 16:30. Gemini "5:00 PM + snowstorms" PARTLY UNSUPPORTED (snow not found); "under cover of failing light" fair.
- Targets: Mossman: "Either by a decision to single out the interior ROK 1st and 6th Divisions or by the coincidence of their adjoining positions athwart Route 33, the Chinese concentrated on wedging through the South Koreans, particularly the 1st Division." The US 25th Div (I Corps left) "was not touched" and attacks on the 24th Div were "numerous, remained small and ineffective". So "aimed at the seams between US and ROK" is DISPUTED as intent: Mossman says deliberate OR coincidence. Also attacked: ROK III Corps (5th, 2nd Divs), on a 45-mile front. The seam between US I and IX Corps = the I-IX Corps boundary, where the ROK 1st and 6th sat; that part is right.
- Chinese strength: Mossman: Eighth Army G-2 estimate "174,000" for XIII Army Group (six armies of 29,000) plus 65,800 North Koreans at the front (EF ch. IX). Ridgway: a map "with a big red goose egg ... '174,000' scrawled in the middle" (ridgway, Soldier). Wikipedia: ~170,000 Chinese+KPA, UN 136,525 US + 12,269 UK/Aus. Gemini "170-300k, Roe 170k" DISPUTED; use "about 170,000" (Mossman 174,000 estimate).
- Ridgway on 1 Jan: **OK, confirmed.** "When he met trucks packed with South Korean troops. No weapons other than a few rifles were in sight. He sensed immediately that the South Koreans were running and jumped from his jeep to wave the trucks to a halt. But he 'might as well had tried to stop the flow of the Han.'" (EF ch. IX, pp. 189-190). He then established straggler points. Mossman adds the episode "had not signaled the collapse of the two ROK divisions."
- 1 Jan: Ridgway gave Milburn and Coulter **withdrawal orders at noon on 1 Jan** (EF ch. IX end), to the Seoul bridgehead (retreat from Line B). He ordered the final evacuation of Seoul at **about 1300 (an hour past noon) on 3 Jan**, IX Corps started noon 3 Jan, I Corps ordered out at 1600 (EF ch. X). Gemini "Jan 2-3 orders complete withdrawal across the Han": OK (Jan 3).
- Seoul fell **4 Jan**: last Han bridge demolished about 1400; Chinese patrols sighted inside Seoul within an hour; ROK 1st Div patrol saw Chinese raising a North Korean flag over city hall: "That flourish marked Seoul's third change of hands" (EF ch. X p. 208). Gemini "second time in the war" CORRECTED: it was the **third** change of hands (June 1950, Sept 1950, Jan 1951), or the second time it was lost to the Communists; "capital captured for the second time" is wrong wording. Also "Chinese forces captured it without street fight": OK. About 600,000 civilians (about half) left; Seoul had "few inhabitants" (EF).
- Troops: "close to seventy-five thousand troops" north of the Han (EF p. 198-199).
- Line D (Pyongtaek-Ansong-Changho-won-ni, then Wonju): withdrawal began 5 Jan, delayed by 100,000 refugees at Suwon; I and IX Corps completed it **7 Jan** (Wikipedia; EF ch. XI). By 7 Jan patrols failed to find the Chinese south of Seoul (EF: "Both towns were empty"). Gemini "Line D by ~7 Jan" OK.
- Chinese stop: Wikipedia: Peng halted the Third Phase by **7 Jan**, citing exhaustion and fear of an Inchon-style landing. Peng's memoir: "The strength of our forces had been reduced by nearly 50 per cent due to combat and non-combat losses ... Our troops badly needed reinforcements and rest and reorganization." Han Xianchu reported "only 8,500 battle casualties" but exhaustion and poor logistics (Wikipedia). OK for "stopped from exhaustion/supply". "Reached 37th parallel (Suwon)" per memoir.
- **Looting UN supply dumps: UNSUPPORTED.** Mossman says the UN burned or destroyed Inch'on, Kimpo and ASCOM City stocks (1.6M gal POL, 9,300 tons engineer material) and that Chinese reached Pyongyang's abandoned 8,000-10,000 tons only in Dec. No source for Chinese soldiers looting Seoul dumps. Cut.
- Casualties: Wikipedia infobox: US 481, UK 300, Australia 9; China ~5,800; NK ~2,700 (low-confidence). Gemini "thousands of ROK losses": ROK 6th Div lost half its men in disorganized withdrawal; no clean total. DISPUTED/UNSUPPORTED as a single figure.
- Ridgway's quote of Chinese tactics (3 Jan letter to Collins, EF ch. XI p. 209): "The Chinese probe on a wide front. When they strike resistance their overwhelming numbers immediately flow around both flanks and join in the rear." And "an enemy whose only advantage is sheer numbers, whose armament is far inferior ... who has no air support whatever, meager telecommunications and negligible armor." Safe, primary.
- **Peng vs Mao dispute: PARTLY SUPPORTED, details UNSUPPORTED.** Wikipedia: Peng told Mao on 7 Dec the PVA needed at least three months to replace casualties; Mao "finally permitted the PVA to rest for at least two to three months" (14 Jan telegram, planning "the last battle" in spring). Mao also pressed for another operation in late Jan: his 28-29 Jan 1951 telegram to Stalin says the troops are "deprived of the possibility of continuing rest and reformation" and must start the "fourth operation" (Wilson Center, Mao-Stalin telegrams, https://digitalarchive.wilsoncenter.org/node/88581). I could not read Peng's own 8 Jan telegram, so "Peng wanted to stop, Mao pushed" is a defensible shorthand but unverified in primary text. Mark as DISPUTED.
- Follow-up (one line): Ridgway's **Operation Thunderbolt began 25 Jan 1951** (Wikipedia); Peng's Fourth Phase (late Jan-Feb) and Fifth Phase (Apr-May) offensives failed; his memoir admits the Fifth Campaign losses "were the highest suffered by our forces" and one division of the 60th Army lost 3,000 men (PB p. 480).

## 7. Myths
- "Human wave" myth: Gemini's gist OK. Reputable wording: "Contrary to popular belief, Chinese forces rarely employed 'human wave' attacks ... Under cover of darkness, infantry units would infiltrate through these gaps or around the enemy's flanks ... while other elements outflanked and enveloped the enemy position ... The Chinese only employed true human wave attacks on occasion late in the Korean War." (WarHistory.org, "Chinese Operations in the Korean War, 1950-1953, Part I", https://warhistory.org/ja/article/chinese-operations-in-the-korean-war-1950-1953-part-i). Primary support in Mossman: Ridgway's own letter ("probe on a wide front ... flow around both flanks and join in the rear"); Mossman describing columns that "infiltrated the gaps between companies" at Chongchon (ch. IV, 25 Nov). Gemini's "highly sophisticated" is editorial; cite "infiltration, not mindless waves".
- Careful: Peng's own memoir admits the Chinese had little artillery/air cover and rarely annihilated whole regiments ("Only once did our troops wipe out an entire U.S. regiment", the 2nd Campaign), i.e., the Chinese were not as dominant as the legend; and the "night attack" worked because of UN air power (PB p. 481).
- "MacArthur surprised by weather" myth: this is not a documented myth; skip. What IS sourced: MacArthur's HQ wasn't ignorant of Chinese prisoners; the failure was assessing scale (see section 2).
- "Longest retreat" see section 0.
