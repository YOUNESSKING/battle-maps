# Peng Dehuai (#10): fact notes (corrections to research/GEMINI_PENG.md)
Checked 2026-10-12 against the US Army green books: Appleman, *South to the Naktong, North to the Yalu* (CMH Pub 20-2, full text
archive.org item CMHPub202) and Mossman, *Ebb and Flow* (archive.org DTIC_ADA232745); Peng's own *Memoirs of a Chinese Marshal*
(Beijing 1984); his 12 Sep 1953 report (*People's China* supplement, 1 Oct 1953); Wikipedia (Unsan, Ch'ongch'on, Third Battle of
Seoul, Peng Dehuai, Mao Anying, Onjong, Wawon) as cross-check. Raw notes + URLs: FACT_CHECK_RAW.md. Gemini page numbers ignored.

## Biography
- Born 24 Oct 1898 (Xiangtan, Hunan), died 29 Nov 1974 in custody: OK. Aged 51 when he crossed the Yalu, 52 from 24 Oct 1950.
- Poor childhood (mother died young, begging, coal mine), joined the CCP 1928 (Pingjiang), Long March (~18,000 at start -> ~3,000): OK (Wikipedia / memoir).
- Nickname: "Generalissimo Peng" UNSUPPORTED (he was a Marshal from 1955). Used: "Old Chief Peng" (Peng Laozong).
- Mao's first choice, Lin Biao, declined pleading ill health; Peng named PVA commander early Oct 1950: OK (Wikipedia).
- Death: "cancer" UNSUPPORTED. Script: "died in custody in 1974, denied proper medical care" (Domes / Shambaugh via Wikipedia). Rehabilitated Dec 1978.
- Purge: private letter criticising the Great Leap Forward, Lushan, July 1959: OK. Red Guards 1966, struggle sessions, beatings: OK.
  Accused in the Cultural Revolution of responsibility for Mao Anying's death: Wikipedia (Mao Anying, Chinese sources): keep as "his enemies even blamed him".

## Hook / crossing
- "300,000 crossed undetected": CORRECTED in detail. First wave = XIII Army Group, ~200,000 (Mossman ch. I); with the IX Army Group,
  "above 300,000" by 23 Nov (Mossman). Appleman: "The Chinese Communist Forces moved 300,000 men into position in October and November
  and none of them was ever discovered by the U.N. Command prior to actual contact" (ch. XXXVIII). Hook line: "300,000 ... marched into
  North Korea in secret, and not one unit was spotted from the air" = Appleman.
- Crossing: Peng crossed "at dusk on October 18, 1950" (memoir); Wikipedia 19 Oct. Script: Peng at dusk 18 Oct; Pyongyang entered 19 Oct (Appleman ch. XIX).
- March discipline (Appleman ch. XXXVIII, from a documented CCF march): march 1900-0300, everything concealed by 0530; if caught by day,
  "every man to stop in his tracks and remain motionless if aircraft appeared overhead"; officers could shoot violators. Appleman notes
  the documented case is early 1951 and the first wave "apparently" marched the same way: script says "a typical day's march".
  Gemini's quote "Hide by day, march by night ... every man must freeze" UNSUPPORTED as a quote: NOT used in quote marks.
- "Longest retreat in US military history (275 miles)": the phrase is the Eighth Army's own official history ("withdrawn a total of
  275 miles ... longest retreat in US military history"); 275 mi is the whole withdrawal into January, not Dec 1-14 (~120 mi to the
  parallel). Script: "in what the US Army itself calls the longest retreat in its history"; no mileage on screen.
- "Home by Christmas" CORRECTED: not a formal promise to the troops. MacArthur flew in 24 Nov; communiqué: the attack "should for all
  practical purposes end the war" (Mossman ch. IV); "home by Christmas" = troop/press talk (Mossman "speculation"; Appleman 1989 via
  Wikipedia). Script: quotes the communiqué; "There was talk among the troops of being home by Christmas."
- Hook "few trucks, few radios": XIII AG had two truck regiments (Mossman); Ridgway: "meager telecommunications". "Almost no trucks" softened
  to "few trucks". Shoes: Appleman: "laceless shoes ... of cloth, low-cut, rubber-soled" -> "thin cloth shoes". No air cover: OK.

## Quotes
- "The enemy is advancing in separate columns, and his flanks are exposed ..." (Peng to Mao): UNSUPPORTED, not used.
- Used instead, Peng's memoir (2nd campaign): "purposely showing ourselves to be weak, increasing the arrogance of the enemy, letting him
  run amuck, and luring him deep into our areas" (PB p. 476): on screen as a short card + paraphrased in the voice.
- 1953 report: REAL, Gemini wording is a paraphrase. Real text (12 Sep 1953): "the days have gone forever when a Western aggressor was
  able - as for several hundred years past - to occupy a country by merely setting up some cannons along a sea coast in the East".
  Script paraphrases it without quote marks ("Peng said that the days had gone forever ...").
- MacArthur at Wake Island, 15 Oct 1950 (Appleman ch. XXXVIII): chances of intervention "very little"; "Only 50,000 to 60,000 could be
  gotten across the Yalu River"; "if the Chinese tried to get down to Pyongyang there would be greatest slaughter": OK, used.
- Ridgway, 1 Jan 1951: met trucks of ROK troops, "might as well had tried to stop the flow of the Han" (Mossman ch. IX): paraphrased.

## Move 1: Unsan (First Phase)
- Dates: First Phase 25 Oct - 6 Nov (Chinese broke contact 6 Nov, Mossman); Unsan 1-4 Nov: OK.
- 25 Oct first Chinese prisoners near Unsan (ROK 1st Div): OK. ROK 6th Div's 7th Regiment reached the Yalu at Ch'osan (Appleman ch. XXXIII;
  26 Oct per standard accounts), then destroyed with the ROK 6th Div around Onjong (40th Army): OK. "the only unit that ever would"
  NOT used (US 7th Div reached the Yalu in the east on 21 Nov).
- Forces: Gemini "39th + 40th Armies, 50-60,000" UNSUPPORTED; Unsan attacked by the 39th Army (115th, 116th, 117th Divs), ~30,000 per army.
  Script: "the Chinese Thirty-Ninth Army" (no number).
- Bugles: CONFIRMED by Appleman ("With much sounding of bugles and whistles"; a column crossed the bridge at the 3rd Bn CP, mistaken for
  ROK troops, "one of its leaders sounded a bugle. This was the signal for a deadly surprise assault"; the 5th Cavalry named the ridge
  "Bugle Hill" because the Chinese "used bugles, horns, and whistles as signaling devices").
- 5th Cavalry relief attempt: ~350 casualties (Appleman). Gay at dusk 2 Nov: "the most difficult decision he was ever called on to make"
  - withdraw and leave the 3rd Bn "to its fate" (Appleman p. 705): used (paraphrased).
- Losses CORRECTED: "8th Cavalry virtually destroyed / ~1,000 killed" overstated. Appleman p. 707-708: "At first, more than 1,000 men of
  the 8th Cavalry Regiment were missing"; after stragglers returned "the losses were found to total about 600 men"; Chinese captured
  200-300. 1st + 2nd Bns escaped (equipment lost); the 3rd Bn was destroyed. Wikipedia infobox (whole battle): 449 US killed, 1,149 total.
  Card: OVER 1,000 MISSING AT FIRST · ~600 LOST. No Chinese loss figure (disputed: UN est. >600, Chinese ~2,000 at Unsan).
- Released prisoners: 22 Nov the Chinese freed 27 prisoners, 19 of them 8th Cav (Appleman p. 708); part of Peng's deception (Roe via Wikipedia).
- "UN intelligence thought the Chinese were token volunteers incapable of a division attack": overstated. Fair wording used: the UNC
  expected ~70,000 Chinese and acknowledged 12 divisions on 23 Nov when there were 30 (Mossman ch. I).

## Move 2: Chongchon / Kunu-ri (Second Phase)
- Eighth Army attack 24 Nov (10:00); Chinese attack night 25 Nov: OK.
- CORRECTED: the ROK II Corps was NOT the only/main target. Mossman: main attack on US IX Corps / 2nd Division (39th + 40th Armies);
  secondary attacks by the 38th + 42nd Armies "pushed the ROK II Corps out of position"; "near noon of the 26th, the ROK II Corps front
  folded". Script: heaviest blows on the Americans in the centre; the open flank decided it.
- "30-mile hole" UNSUPPORTED: not used. Chinese strength: ~200,000 XIII Army Group (Mossman); Wikipedia 230,000 (Roe). Script "some 200,000".
- "Eighth Army ~130,000 combat effectives" UNSUPPORTED: not used.
- Mao Anying (Mao's eldest son, on Peng's HQ staff) killed by a US air strike on the PVA HQ, 25 Nov 1950 (Wikipedia, Mao Anying): OK.
- Turkish Brigade at Wawon 27-29 Nov, pushed back with heavy losses (218 killed, 455 wounded, 94 missing): OK (Wikipedia). Script: no figure.
- Gauntlet 30 Nov, Kunu-ri -> Sunchon: Chinese 113th Div "elongated fire block", heaviest fire in the pass (Mossman ch. VI); ~6 miles
  deep (Appleman 1989 via Wikipedia). "50 yards up the slopes" UNSUPPORTED: not used. 23rd Infantry escaped west via Anju (Mossman). Name
  "the Gauntlet" from S.L.A. Marshall.
- 2nd Division losses CORRECTED: 4,940 battle casualties 15-30 Nov, ~4,500 since 25 Nov, a third of its 15,000 men; 64 artillery pieces
  lost (not "all its artillery"), hundreds of trucks (Mossman p. 126-127). Card uses these.
- Walker CORRECTED in detail: killed 23 Dec 1950 when his jeep collided with a truck ~10 miles north of Uijongbu (after the retreat, not
  "during" it). Ridgway reached Korea 26 Dec (Mossman ch. IX).
- Pyongyang left burning the morning of 5 Dec (Mossman ch. VIII); Eighth Army near the 38th parallel by mid/late Dec. "abandons all heavy
  equipment and flees" overstated: not used.
- Chinese losses: DISPUTED (Wikipedia 45,000 infobox vs Chinese claim 20,000 battle casualties): no number on screen; script says
  many were lost to the cold as well as firepower.

## Move 3: Third Phase / Seoul
- Start 31 Dec 1950 "just after full dark" behind a 30-minute barrage (Mossman; Wikipedia: shelling from 16:30): OK. "snowstorm" UNSUPPORTED.
- Targets: CORRECTED/DISPUTED. Mossman: "Either by a decision to single out the interior ROK 1st and 6th Divisions or by the coincidence of
  their adjoining positions ... the Chinese concentrated on wedging through the South Koreans"; US 25th Div "not touched". Wikipedia
  (Zhang 1995; Appleman 1990): the Chinese plan, limited by Mao after Peng's objections, ordered the 13th Army to destroy the ROK 1st and
  6th Divs and ROK III Corps, noting US units were not interspersed with them. Script: "the Chinese plan aimed squarely at the South
  Koreans" (plan, not "Peng alone"); the ROK 1st and 6th sat on the I/IX Corps boundary.
- CORRECTED: ROK 1st Div broke on 31 Dec / 1 Jan; the ROK 6th held on 1 Jan and fell apart withdrawing on 2 Jan. Script: "Before dawn,
  the South Korean First Division had been driven out of its positions."
- Peng argued against the offensive on 7 Dec (needed at least three months to recover); Mao limited its scope (Wikipedia, Zhang 1995):
  OK. "Peng vs Mao" duel card uses only this.
- Ridgway: withdrawal to the Seoul bridgehead ordered noon 1 Jan; Seoul abandoned ~13:00 3 Jan; Chinese in Seoul 4 Jan; Line D 7 Jan
  (Mossman ch. IX-XI): OK. ~600,000 civilians left (Mossman). British 29th Bde rear guard lost 250-300 men + 10 tanks (Mossman): "some
  three hundred". Seoul's THIRD change of hands (Gemini "second time" CORRECTED; line not in the script).
- "Chinese looted UN supply dumps" UNSUPPORTED: cut.
- Peng halted ~7-8 Jan: exhaustion, supply, fear of a landing behind him (Wikipedia, Zhang); memoir: strength "reduced by nearly 50 per
  cent due to combat and non-combat losses" (used as "by his own account ... nearly half their strength"). Kim Il Sung pushing on:
  UNVERIFIED, not used.
- No reliable casualty total for the Third Phase -> RESULT card.

## Ending
- Operation Thunderbolt 25 Jan 1951; UN retook Seoul 14-15 Mar 1951; Chinese spring (Fifth Phase) offensives Apr-May 1951 failed with
  heavy losses (Peng's memoir: the highest losses of the war); armistice 27 Jul 1953, Peng signed for the Chinese Volunteers: OK (standard).
- Minister of Defence 1954-59: OK (Wikipedia).

  Korea toward China's border. If nothing stopped them, North Korea would disappear, and American troops would be standing on the banks of
