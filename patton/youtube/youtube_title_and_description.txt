Battle of the Bulge: How Patton Turned His Army North and Reached Bastogne in a Week

In December 1944, Hitler's last great offensive trapped thousands of American paratroopers in the Belgian town of Bastogne. One American general, facing the wrong way, turned his whole army ninety degrees in the middle of a battle and broke through to them in a week.

In this video we break down George S. Patton's three greatest tactical moves: the Third Army's breakout through Avranches and the drive to Le Mans and Argentan in August 1944, where a bottleneck only a few miles long had to carry an army; the relief of Bastogne in December 1944, from the freezing conference room at Verdun to Abrams's dash through Assenois; and the night crossing of the Rhine at Nierstein and Oppenheim on 22-23 March 1945, a day before Montgomery's set-piece assault. We also cover the Argentan halt, the orders that stopped him, and the Sicily slapping incidents of 1943.
