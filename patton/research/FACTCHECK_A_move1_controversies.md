- **BP** = Blumenson, *Breakout and Pursuit* (US Army, CMH Pub 7-5-1), archive.org full text: https://archive.org/download/CMHPub751BreakoutAndPursuit/CMHPub751BreakoutAndPursuit_djvu.txt (chapter and printed page given where I could read them)

## Best vivid details that ARE verified (safe for the narrator)

- Third Army "came to life" at noon on 1 August 1944, as armored columns streamed past Pontaubault (Blumenson). Patton marked "the birthday of the Third Army" at high noon with a bottle of "alleged brandy" he and an aide could not drink (WAIKI).
- Pontaubault was "the most critical traffic point of all"; Task Force A was given three hours to move 3,500 men through the Avranches-Pontaubault bottleneck, to arrive at Avranches "precisely at 0200, 3 August, not before", and a division commander wrote "Still spending most of my time as a traffic cop" (Blumenson).
- The 4th Armored took the Pontaubault bridge intact on the afternoon of 31 July; German vehicles arriving from the west were too late (Blumenson).
- Two armored divisions got into Brittany in under 48 hours; bulldozers cleared lanes through the rubble of Avranches; Allied fighter-bombers and AA guns (more than a score of planes shot down around Avranches in the first week) covered it.
- XV Corps raced about 75 miles from the St. Hilaire-Fougeres line to Le Mans in four days (5-9 Aug) with light casualties; the 90th Div lost under 300 men in ten days and took over 1,500 prisoners.
- By 8 Aug, over 200 miles separated the 6th Armd at the gates of Brest from the XV Corps at Le Mans.
- Patton's warning to Haislip before the turn north: "Don't be surprised."
- The Argentan halt on 13 Aug: Canadians were still several miles north of Falaise; the Americans were stopped "just south of Argentan"; the gap was under 25 miles. Patton wrote that the halt "was a great mistake".
- Falaise pocket: about 50,000 prisoners, about 10,000 dead found; the 2d French Armd took 8,800 prisoners; the 90th Division took over 13,000 prisoners in four days.
- Kluge's farewell letter to Hitler ("make up your mind to end the war"), then cyanide on the road to Metz; Model took over at midnight 17-18 Aug.
- 79th Division crossed the Seine at Mantes on foot in single file over a dam in a torrential rainstorm the night of 19-20 Aug; the 79th also captured the Army Group B command post at La Roche-Guyon.
- Patton had Major Alexander Stiller, one of his own aides, ride with Task Force Baum "only because General Patton's son-in-law... was in the prison camp" (Abrams letter quoted by MacDonald).
- Task Force Baum: 10 medium + 6 light tanks, 27 half-tracks, 3 assault guns; destroyed a German locomotive and 12 vehicles at Lohr; Panzerfaust knocked out the lead tank 50 yards from the camp; Baum was wounded three times; Waters freed on 6 Apr by the 14th Armd (only 75 Americans left in camp).
- Fort Driant: 798 casualties, "the first publicized reverse" of Third Army; last troops left on the night of 12-13 Oct without a shot being fired by the enemy.
- Eisenhower's 17 Aug 1943 letter: "this does not excuse brutality, abuse of the sick, nor exhibition of uncontrollable temper in front of subordinates."

## Do not say

- "Pontlaubert" (it is Pontaubault); "single bridge"; "two-mile corridor"; "seven divisions / 100,000 men / 20,000 vehicles in 72 hours" as fact (secondary-only; 15,000 vehicles in the source, and the number is not in the official history).
- "Patton stood at the intersection directing traffic in his ivory-handled revolvers" as fact (only corps and division commanders are documented doing this; Patton himself is unsourced).
- The "another Dunkirk" line as a verbatim Patton quote, or any page citation for it.
- "90-degree left wheel" or "dual hook": the official history says right hook / shorter envelopment by XV Corps; Bradley, not Patton, proposed it.
- "Patton closed the Falaise pocket" or "annihilated two German armies": Bradley halted XV Corps on 13 Aug and a large part of the Germans escaped.
- Any Third Army August casualty or prisoner total (15,724 / 68,500): no source found.
- "Michelin maps" as a documented fact.
- "Collins punched the hole to Avranches" (VIII Corps took Avranches; Cobra was Bradley's plan; First Army).
- Driant as Patton overruling Walker's advice to bypass (the reverse: Walker pushed it; Irwin wanted to encircle).
- Hammelburg: "314 men / 57 vehicles / 32 killed or missing / 50 miles / 26-28 March / Patton called it his worst mistake".
- "Eisenhower forced Patton to apologize to every division" or "removed him from D-Day command because of the slap" as flat facts.
- "PTSD" for the 1943 soldiers (use "combat exhaustion"; Kuhl actually had malaria).
- Relief from Third Army on "October 2, 1945" (use "early October").
