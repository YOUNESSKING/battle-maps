## Best vivid details that ARE verified
1. Patton's own diary, Mar 24: he crossed the pontoon bridge with Eddy, "stopping in the middle to take a piss whew!! in the Rhine", then picked up dirt on the far side "in emulation of William the Conqueror." (LOC diary, folder 11.)
2. The first boat to touch the east bank at Nierstein carried 1st Lt. Irven Jacobs of K Company; seven stunned Germans surrendered and paddled themselves back across without an escort. (MacDonald.)
3. Patton's aide Maj. Al Stiller went in the first wave "at his own request" (Patton's diary, Mar 23 and 24).
4. Moonlight, "disturbing brightness"; the 5th Division was "conqueror of twenty-two rivers" and Patton called Oppenheim its 23rd river crossing. (MacDonald; diary.)
5. Eddy told Irwin only at midmorning on Mar 22 that the crossing was tonight; Irwin protested he could manage "some sort of bridgehead". Patton wanted nothing more. (MacDonald.)
6. About 500 boats, 7,500 engineers, and Navy LCVPs on trucks "as at Remagen"; boats hidden "two days in a bight of the river surrounded by trees" (Patton's diary note).
7. Patton wanted to airlift infantry across in about 100 artillery liaison "Cub" planes (idea from his artillery chief, Brig. Gen. E. T. Williams); Irwin objected; Patton even ordered a practice flight; German planes over the site killed the idea. (MacDonald; Patton diary Mar 21 confirms the idea.)
8. Felber's defense: only four organised divisions on 50 miles of Rhine; Oppenheim was one of three sites the Germans thought a likely crossing, but their divisions were far south. Kesselring and Hausser watched Felber's failed counterattack from Grossgerau. (MacDonald.)
9. Bradley's announcement said Third Army crossed "without even so much as an artillery preparation"; BBC replayed Churchill's pre-recorded speech praising the "first assault crossing of the Rhine", which everyone knew was Third Army's. (MacDonald.)
10. Cost comparison that is solid: 11th Inf = 20 casualties in the crossing, Patton's tally 28; 17th Airborne alone had 159 killed, 522 wounded and 840 missing on Varsity day one. (MacDonald; diary.)
11. Patton's Nov 7 1944 "Play ball" in the pouring rain, over Eddy's and Grow's objections at 1900 (diary), confirmed in the Army history. This is the genuine "Play ball" story.
12. Verdun Dec 19: Patton's diary: "I said, 'On December 22 with three divisions' ... it created quite a commotion." Ike's parting joke: "Every time I get a new star I get attack"; Patton: "And every time you get attacked I pull you out."

## Do not say
- Do not name Lt Col Lemmon, Company I, Sgt Chilson, or "748th Tank Battalion DD tanks" at Oppenheim.
- Do not say "8 killed, 20 wounded" (say "28 casualties, by Patton's count").
- Do not say "1,900 prisoners", "bridgehead 5 by 7 miles", "150 sorties", or "Patton shot down 33 planes" as fact (33 was Patton's boast; official claim 19).
- Do not say the crossing was "in total darkness"; it was moonlit. Do not say "zero air cover" or "zero smoke" (the Mainz feint used smoke).
- Do not say Hap Gay walked the bridge with Patton; the diary lists Codman, Stiller and Eddy.
- Do not put "See! I have taken the land in both my hands!" in Patton's mouth. Safe: he stumbled/scooped dirt "in emulation of William the Conqueror."
- Do not say "Wait till Monty hears about this" (not found) or quote Bradley's book by page.
- Do not say Patton promised Eisenhower "48 hours" or that a Dec 21 two-division offer was rejected. Say "the morning of December 22, with three divisions, about three days away".
- Do not say "Play ball" was the Verdun phone code. Use it for Nov 7 1944 only (Cole). Do not say Bradley visited or authorized the Nov 8 attack.
- Do not credit the "Paris" line to Patton's diary or to a specific page of Bradley. Say "reportedly".
- Do not say "Hausser was relieved before March": he commanded Army Group G until 2 April 1945.
- Do not present Cent/Nickel/Dime as "documented by Patton"; only Hymel names them.
