## Source for every new claim (patton/research/FACT_NOTES.md + FACTCHECK_*.md, Cole = The Ardennes: Battle of the Bulge)
| Claim | Source / verdict |
|---|---|
| "On the night of December twenty-first ... surrounded" | Ring clamped shut in the night of 20-21 Dec; the German commanders did not consider Bastogne surrounded until the evening of the 21st (Cole pp. 459-462, FACTCHECK_B row 8). |
| "thousands of American paratroopers" | 101st Airborne at nearly full strength plus CCB 10th Armd etc., over 10,000 (Cole; FACTCHECK_B row 9: no exact total, "thousands" is safe). |
| "hub where seven roads met" | Cole p. 551-ish ch. XIX: "Bastogne, with seven entrant roads, naturally dominates the road complex"; Eisenhower: "the key to the entire road net on the south side of the German Bulge" (Cole). |
| "the German drive toward the Meuse needed those roads" | Cole p. 465: Rundstedt wanted to "crush Bastogne and make this road center available for the build-up required to support the Fifth Panzer Army at or over the Meuse"; Luettwitz hoped to "give the armor of the 2d Panzer Division and Panzer Lehr a clear highway west" (read in the Cole full text 2026-11-01). |
| "turned his army north and broke through to them in a week" | Verdun 19 Dec -> link-up 26 Dec = 7 days (FACT_NOTES 10, script move2-8). "turned north": army attacking east in the Saar, ordered north (FACTCHECK_B). "90 degrees" removed. |
| 19 Dec, freezing barracks at Verdun, Eisenhower asked how soon; "the morning of the twenty-second, with three divisions" | Patton's diary 19 Dec: "Ike said, 'When can you attack?' I said, 'On December 22 with three divisions'" (FACTCHECK_C row 31); French barracks, freezing (War As I Knew It, FACTCHECK_B). |
| "His army was facing east, in another battle" | Third Army was in its Lorraine/Saar offensive facing east (FACTCHECK_B rows on 16-18 Dec; Patton misread the Bulge at first). |
| "Some thought he was boasting" | Patton's own words, "Some people thought I was boasting" (War As I Knew It; FACTCHECK_B best-details 2). |
| "Sixteen months earlier, in Sicily, he had slapped two hospitalised soldiers" | Slaps on 3 Aug (Kuhl) and 10 Aug 1943 (Bennett) at evacuation hospitals (FACTCHECK_A rows 28-30); Aug 1943 -> Dec 1944 = 16 months. "hospitalised", not "PTSD". |
| "the same Eisenhower had reprimanded him" | Eisenhower's letter of 17 Aug 1943: "this does not excuse brutality, abuse of the sick, nor exhibition of uncontrollable temper in front of subordinates" (FACTCHECK_A row 30). |
