## Best vivid details that ARE verified

1. **Patton's own mistake.** Night of Dec 16 he protested losing the 10th Armored: "to move the 10th Armored to the north would be playing into the hands of the Germans." On Dec 17 he had Eddy engage the 4th Armored so it would not be taken north, then wrote: "The fact that I did this shows how little I appreciated the seriousness of the enemy attack on that date." (WAKI)
2. **The Verdun room.** Dec 19, 1100, French barracks, freezing, coats on, coal stove too small. Eisenhower: "There will be only cheerful faces at this conference table." Patton promised three divisions by Dec 22; "it created a ripple of excitement. Some people thought I was boasting." (WAKI; HYMEL-WHN; American Heritage for the "cheerful faces" line, https://www.americanheritage.com/ikes-son-remembers-george-s-patton-jr)
3. **The three-plan code.** Cent / Nickel / Dime, on a slip of paper; after the meeting Patton tells Harkins: "Telephone Gay, give him the code name [Nickel], tell him to get started." The staff drew three plans between 0800 and 0915 on Dec 19 (WAKI: "a Staff meeting, planned three possible lines of attack, and made a simple code"). (HYMEL-WHN)
4. **Eisenhower's reaction (reported).** "Don't be fatuous, George... You will start on the twenty-second." (HistoryNet)
5. **The 4th Armored's rush.** 160 miles to the Ardennes, 21 Shermans short, 713 men short; the 8th Tank Bn alone lost 33 tanks to breakdowns on the road. (COLE p. 513)
6. **Patton on the phone, Dec 23.** "There is too much piddling around. Bypass these towns and clear them up later. Tanks can operate on this ground now." (COLE p. 527). And after the first day: "this was... a chance to win the war; the attack must be kept rolling through the night." Later he admitted the night attack was an error (WAKI: "I remember being surprised at the time at how long it took me to learn war.")
7. **The captured orders at Chaumont.** A CCB liaison officer carrying attack orders took a wrong turn into German lines, which may be why Kokott's assault guns were waiting. (COLE p. 528)
8. **Nuts, translated.** Colonel Harper, "hard pressed to translate the idiom, compromised on 'Go to Hell!'" (COLE p. 468). Army.mil adds Kinnard's advice: "What you said initially would be hard to beat."
9. **Dec 26 dash.** Abrams to Dwight: "It's the push!" Artillery code: "Concentration Number Nine, play it soft and sweet." Thirteen batteries, ten volleys, "not a hostile shot was fired as the tanks raced into the streets." Teller mines behind the first three tanks; a half-track exploded; Dwight's two tanks cleared the mines by hand. (COLE p. 554-555)
10. **After the link-up.** S/Sgt James Hendrix took on two 88mm crews with a rifle (Medal of Honor); that night 40 trucks and 70 ambulances got into Bastogne, with more than 200 vehicles waiting at Rossignol. (COLE p. 555-556)
11. **Patton's Christmas.** Every soldier got turkey, front-line men got turkey sandwiches (WAKI). Bradley told him that night that Montgomery wanted a withdrawal to the Saar-Vosges or Moselle; both found it "disgusting" (WAKI).
12. **Prayer scene.** "Sir, it's going to take a pretty thick rug for that kind of praying." Patton: "I don't care if it takes the flying carpet." And on the clear weather: "God damn! look at the weather. That O'Neill sure did some potent praying." (WAKI footnote)
13. **Schnee Eifel.** Two regiments, "at least seven thousand... probably closer to eight or nine thousand" lost Dec 19. (COLE p. 171)

## Do not say

- "250,000 Germans and nearly 1,000 tanks" (Cole: over 200,000 men, about 500 medium tanks on day one).
- "Hitler wanted a separate peace" as fact, or "30+ Allied divisions trapped" (the 30 were German divisions).
- "Largest mass surrender in the European Theater" (Cole: "most serious reverse"; 7,000 to 8-9,000).
- "133,178 vehicles / 4.5 million vehicle-miles / 419,581 tons" and "250,000 men moved" (no source found).
- "Patton ordered three plans on Dec 18 before anyone asked" or "Koch warned him Dec 17-18" (Koch's warning was Dec 9; Bradley summoned Patton on Dec 18; the three plans were drawn up the night of Dec 18-19 and the morning of Dec 19).
- "Dime was Luxembourg/Echternach" (Dime = the western tip of the Bulge).
- "Damp stone bunker" (barracks).
- "Patton promised in 48 hours" as fact. Safe: three divisions, morning of Dec 22 (about three days).
- "A rolling barrage by four batteries" (13 batteries, ten volleys, a time-on-target-style concentration).
- "Boggess shook hands with McAuliffe" (Abrams did, 20 minutes after the 1650 contact with the engineers).
- "400 yards" for Boggess's last run, the megaphone line, and the "MacAuliffe Rescued by Patton" caption (all unsourced).
- "Patton greeted at Bastogne on Dec 26" (he came Dec 30).
- "Weather prayer ordered Dec 22" (cards were issued Dec 22); "Bronze Star on Dec 23" (about Dec 24).
- "Dec 25 CCR shift to Remoifosse" (CCR moved overnight Dec 24-25; the Dec 26 route was Remichampagne-Clochimont-Assenois).
- Any precise 101st casualty figure or "~11,000 inside" without "about" (Cole gives no garrison total).
- "Gay was a Maj. Gen." in Dec 1944 (Cole: Brig. Gen.).
