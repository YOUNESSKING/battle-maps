# Collins video: raw fact check of GEMINI_COLLINS.md (2026-10-10)
Verdicts: OK / CORRECTED / DISPUTED / UNSUPPORTED. Page numbers in Gemini's notes were not trusted or checked.
Primary sources (US Army green books, mirrored on ibiblio HyperWar; history.army.mil returned 403 to the fetcher):
- CC = Harrison, Cross-Channel Attack, ch. 10: https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-E-XChannel/USA-E-XChannel-10.html (and ch. 9 for 15-18 June)
- UB = Utah Beach to Cherbourg (CMH), ch. 10-11: https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-A-Utah/USA-A-Utah-10.html and -11.html
- BP12/13/14 = Blumenson, Breakout and Pursuit, ch. 12-14: https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-E-Breakout/USA-E-Breakout-12.html (-13, -14)
- AR22 = Cole, The Ardennes, ch. 22: https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-E-Ardennes/USA-E-Ardennes-22.html
- Wikipedia pages were only used as secondary cross-check (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cherbourg, /Operation_Cobra, /J._Lawton_Collins).

## 0. Biggest corrections (read first)
1. Celles "unauthorized / ignored Montgomery" is WRONG. Montgomery and Hodges (First Army) agreed to shorten the line; VII Corps was told to go defensive and was "authorized" to fall back on its own judgment to Andenne-Hotton-Manhay. Collins himself decided to attack anyway (AR22).
2. Schlieben "radio broadcast refusal" is BACKWARDS. Collins OFFERED Schlieben the means of communication to order the garrison to surrender; Schlieben declined (UB ch. 11).
3. Cherbourg casualties (~2,800 killed / 13,500 wounded) are the WHOLE Cotentin campaign (6 Jun-1 Jul), and also include 5,700 missing; total over 22,000 (UB ch. 11).
4. Cherbourg "June 29 arsenal" is wrong: the arsenal surrendered 27 June; harbour forts 29 June; Cap de la Hague cleared 1 July.
5. Cobra 111 killed / 490 wounded is the 25 July strike. The 24 July aborted strike killed 25 and wounded 131 (30th Div). 3,300 tons = heavies only.
6. 26 July armour: 2nd Armored Div + 1st Inf Div with CCB of 3rd Armored. 3rd Armored (less CCB) was held back and went in on 27 July.
7. Celles loss figures (82 tanks, 83 guns) are Cole's total for the whole 2nd Armored Division operation, with 1,213 prisoners, not "~2,500 killed or captured" at Celles.
8. Harmon's quote wording and Collins's three quotes are not verifiable (see section 4).

## 1. Biography
- Born 1 May 1896 (New Orleans), died 12 Sep 1987 (Washington DC): OK. Aged 48 in 1944: OK (turned 49 on 1 May 1945). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Lawton_Collins
- Rank in 1944: Major General (since 26 May 1942); temporary Lt Gen only in April 1945. OK. Same URL.
- "Lightning Joe": DISPUTED origin. Linked to Guadalcanal / 25th Division ("Tropic Lightning"), but sources give competing explanations (his aggressive style; the division's codename; one claims a mispronunciation of "Lawton"). No contemporary source credits a named coiner. Safe line: "earned the nickname 'Lightning Joe' on Guadalcanal." Note the 25th Division's own nickname "Tropic Lightning" is a division name, not his. https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/louisiana-lightning-joe-collins ; https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/people_collins_j_lawton.html
- "Commanded VII Corps from the beaches to the Elbe": careful. He led VII Corps from Utah Beach (landed 6 June as corps commander) through the Rhine and Ruhr; do not claim the Elbe without checking.

## 2. Cherbourg
- 18 June "drove across the Cotentin": CORRECTED/OK. 9th Div's 60th and 47th Inf reached Barneville-sur-Mer on the evening of 17 June; the peninsula was cut by the morning of 18 June (CC ch. 9, p. 414-415). Collins announced the cut-the-peninsula order 15 June. Wikipedia says 9th Div reached the west coast on 18 June. https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-E-XChannel/USA-E-XChannel-9.html
- Divisions: OK. 9th, 79th, 4th "jump off abreast" (4th on right, 79th centre, 9th left); 90th took over blocking the west coast (CC). The corps had more than "3 divisions, 60,000 men" in the Cotentin overall (also 90th, 82nd Airborne earlier): "60,000" UNSUPPORTED.
- Ultimatum: broadcast night of 21 June (German, Polish, Russian, French), deadline 0900 on 22 June; Schlieben did not answer (CC; UB ch. 10 https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-A-Utah/USA-A-Utah-10.html).
- 22 June assault: CORRECTED detail. Air preparation began 1240 (Typhoons/Mustangs from H-80 min, 12 Ninth AF fighter-bomber groups from H-60, then light and medium bombers); H Hour 1400, 4th Div joined at 1430. Gemini's "12:40 PM launches three divisions" mixes the bombing start with the ground attack. Some friendly-fire hits on 47th/60th/22d Inf. CC ch. 10 p. 427-428.
- Fort du Roule: OK 25 June (not 26). 314th Inf (79th Div), preceded by a P-47 bombing that largely missed; fell that night/26th (the lower guns kept firing on 26 June). Cpl John D. Kelly (Co E, 314th) took a ten-foot pole charge up the slope three times: Medal of Honor. 1st Lt Carlos C. Ogden (Co K, 313th? Harrison places him in the 3d Bn zone) destroyed an 88 and MGs with a rifle grenade and hand grenades: Medal of Honor. Gemini's "satchel charges into ventilation shafts" is UNSUPPORTED; sources say pole charges and demolitions against the lower-level guns. CC ch. 10 p. 435; UB ch. 11.
- Schlieben: CORRECTED. 26 June. The 39th Inf learned from a prisoner he was in an underground shelter (tunnel) at St. Sauveur on the southern outskirts. A prisoner sent in with a demand was refused; tank destroyers fired into two of the three tunnel entrances; "a few rounds" brought them out. About 800 Germans, including Schlieben and Adm. Hennecke, surrendered to Gen. Eddy (9th Div). Schlieben's last radio message 1506: "Documents burned, codes destroyed." No loudspeakers brought him out (Gemini's PsyWar loudspeaker detail applies to the arsenal on 27 June). CC p. 437-438.
- Broadcast story: CORRECTED (reversed). He refused to order a general surrender, saying communications were too bad; "When General Collins offered to provide the means of communication von Schlieben still declined." So no "Collins refused to broadcast"; no "bloody piecemeal urban combat" consequence beyond what actually followed (400 men surrendered at city hall). UB ch. 11 https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-A-Utah/USA-A-Utah-11.html
- Arsenal: 27 June, Gen. Robert Sattler (deputy commander) surrendered about 400 men to Col. Smythe (47th Inf) after a PsyWar ultimatum, at about 0830. Wikipedia adds the Walsh navy party story (about 400 men at the arsenal; wording varies; Walsh claim is a side story). Harbour forts: 29 June. Gemini "June 29 arsenal" CORRECTED.
- Cap de la Hague: 9th Div fought 29 June-1 July; Col. Keil captured about midnight 30 June; more than 6,000 prisoners; organized resistance ended 1500 on 1 July (UB; CC).
- Garrison: Wikipedia says about 21,000 in the city, 40,000 in infobox; Gemini "40,000" UNSUPPORTED as garrison. UB: 39,000 Germans captured in the Cotentin campaign; over 10,000 prisoners taken in the day and a half to 27 June including 2,600 patients. Germany lost "an undetermined number" killed. Gemini "39,000 captured" is the campaign total, not the city.
- US casualties: campaign (VII Corps, 6 Jun-1 Jul) over 22,000: 2,800 killed, 5,700 missing, 13,500 wounded. No clean figure for the 22-27 June city assault. UB ch. 11.
- Port: CORRECTED. "Masterful" demolition, most complete in history (Col. Viney); Hitler gave Hennecke the Knight's Cross. Naples-based estimate was 3 days to open; in fact almost 3 weeks; first cargo over the Nouvelle Plage by DUKW on 16 July; western harbour minesweeping done 14 July; obstructions not cleared until end of September. Wikipedia: first ships late July, limited use mid-August. CC p. 440-441.
- "Collins realized garrison troops were brittle / avoided siege": plausible but this is sourced to Harrison's description of the plan to maximize demoralization (psychological and air shock); not a quote. OK as paraphrase.

## 3. Operation Cobra
- Hook claim "Collins executed Cobra, which made Patton's drive possible": OK in substance (VII Corps led the breakthrough, Bradley's plan, Collins contributed to it). Better wording: Collins's VII Corps made the hole; Patton's Third Army (activated 1 Aug) exploited it. Patton supervised VIII Corps from about 28 July (Bradley told him to supervise its exploitation). BP14 https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-E-Breakout/USA-E-Breakout-14.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cobra
- 24 July: H-Hour set for 1300; Leigh-Mallory postponed for cloud, but the message was late. About 300 bombers of the third formation dropped bombs, killing 25 and wounding 131 of the 30th Div. VII Corps then made a limited attack to the highway. BP12 https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-E-Breakout/USA-E-Breakout-12.html . (Wikipedia: "more than 25 killed, 130 wounded"; popular sources vary.)
- 25 July strike: OK-ish. More than 1,500 B-17/B-24 dropped more than 3,300 tons; more than 380 mediums dropped over 650 tons; more than 550 fighter-bombers dropped more than 200 tons plus napalm (BP12). Total therefore about 4,000+ tons, not 3,300. Wikipedia lists about 1,800 heavies; use Blumenson. Gemini's "09:38 AM" and "tiny 3-by-1.5-mile" box: Wikipedia gives box about 6,000 x 2,200 yards (3.4 x 1.3 mi); I did not find 7,000 x 2,500 in Blumenson text. Strike began shortly after 0936-0938 (commonly quoted; not confirmed in the fetched text). Panzer Lehr front about 3 miles along the Perier-St-Lo road.
- Short bombing: OK. "35 heavy bombers and 42 medium bombers dropped within American lines ... killed 111 ... wounded 490" (BP12). Gemini said "1,500 heavies" caused it, misleading: a small fraction did.
- McNair: Lt Gen, commander Army Ground Forces, pro tem commanding 1st US Army Group (the FORTITUDE decoy). Buried quietly; news suppressed until DeWitt arrived. "Highest-ranking American officer killed in the European theater": widely used wording, one Army account says "highest-ranking American officer killed by enemy or friendly fire in the European theater"; I did not find it in the green book; use "the highest-ranking US officer killed in Europe" with attribution to Army histories. https://www.army.mil/article/42658/operation_cobra_and_the_breakout_at_normandy
- "Collins ordered infantry to attack at 11:00 AM": UNSUPPORTED. Blumenson: the ground attack followed the bombing in the afternoon; VII Corps gained about 2,000 yards on 25 July. (Wikipedia: 2,000 yards.)
- Armour decision: CORRECTED. Blumenson: "To General Collins a decision either to commit or to withhold his mobile striking force was a gamble. The infantry had not secured the minimum objectives deemed prerequisite for commitment of the armor... Collins chose to move. During the afternoon of 25 July he decided to commit the armor on the following morning." So yes before a clean breakthrough, decided afternoon of 25 July; framed as a gamble, not "against doctrine" (it was against his own plan's prerequisite). BP12 end.
- 26 July: CORRECTED. 2nd Armored (Brooks; CCA under Brig. Gen. Maurice Rose through St-Gilles, with 22d Inf attached) and 1st Inf Div (Huebner, with CCB 3rd Armored). 3rd Armored (less CCB) was held till night/27 July. Marigny held out; infantry cleared it on 27 July. 2nd Armored's CCA "launched the exploitation phase... no doubt the German line had been penetrated" (BP13). Gemini "2nd and 3rd Armored on 26 July" CORRECTED.
- Bayerlein "annihilated": Blumenson says "a shocked Bayerlein reported Panzer Lehr 'finally annihilated'" after US tanks passed his command post at Dangy (27 July, not 25th). BP14. The famous "my front line looked like a death-valley, at least 70% of my men dead, crazed, wounded or numbed" is Bayerlein's post-war account (widely quoted, e.g., Belfield & Essame, Carlo D'Este); not in Blumenson. Treat as UNSUPPORTED here. Gemini's "tanks flipped upside down" appears in Bayerlein's account too; DISPUTED provenance.
- Panzer Lehr strength: Gemini "5,000 front-line troops" CORRECTED. Blumenson: troops facing VII Corps about 30,000, of which perhaps only 5,000 combat effectives between Taute and Vire; of these about 3,200 Panzer Lehr and attached units directly in the path; Panzer Lehr itself about 2,200 combat troops and perhaps 45 serviceable armored vehicles (full strength 7,000 combat and 200+ AFVs). Wikipedia: 12 Panzer IV + 16 Panther fit, about 1,000 casualties from the bombardment. Gemini's "approx. 5,000 casualties" UNSUPPORTED. BP12.
- Dates: Marigny cleared 27 July; Coutances taken about 28 July; Avranches reached 30 July (signal crew reported US troops approaching at dusk 30 July) and VIII Corps held it by 31 July. BP13-14. Gemini "27-28 July US armour burst out": OK in spirit.
- Third Army: operational 1 Aug. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cobra
- VII Corps losses for Cobra: not found; Wikipedia infobox gives about 18,450 US casualties for the whole operation. Gemini "~4,000 in VII Corps" UNSUPPORTED.
- "Patton not operational so Collins did it": OK but over-simplified.

## 4. Quotes
- "I have always felt that a commander's place is where the crisis of the battle is": UNSUPPORTED. Not found in any source; sounds like a paraphrase of Collins's known habit. Do not put in quotation marks.
- "The Germans in the Cotentin are beaten. It is only a matter of mopping them up.": UNSUPPORTED (single, uncheckable source).
- "If we can just get a hole, we'll go all the way.": UNSUPPORTED/legend.
- Bradley on Collins: "talks our language" (after Collins described always targeting the high ground): sourced in Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Lawton_Collins . Usable as an attributed line.

## 5. Celles / Bulge
- 2nd Panzer reached Foy-Notre-Dame about 23-24 Dec: OK. Cole: at daylight on 24 Dec the recon battalion and artillery column were near Foy-Notre-Dame, "only four or five miles from the bridges at Dinant"; Cochenhausen's main column halted in woods near Conneux; fuel nearly gone (Model reported 2nd Panzer short of fuel). AR22. Gemini "Celles, 3 miles" CORRECTED to 4-5 miles from Dinant. "Deepest penetration": OK. It is generally cited as the furthest-west German advance; phrase "the deepest German penetration of the Bulge, within sight of the Meuse" is common; "within sight" unsupported.
- Montgomery orders: CORRECTED. Evening 24 Dec: Col. Akers (First Army) brought word that "Montgomery and Hodges had agreed to shorten the First Army line"; VII Corps "was to go on the defensive and its commander was 'authorized' on his own judgment to drop back to the line Andenne-Hotton-Manhay." Hodges' chief of staff Kean's earlier call: "Roll with the punch." Gemini's "Montgomery ordered Collins" and "Collins ignored it/unauthorized" is not supported. Collins consulted his staff, none considered cancelling the attack, and he made the final decision himself. AR22.
- Who authorised Harmon: Harmon phoned mid-afternoon 24 Dec; Collins was away, Brig. Gen. Williston Palmer (corps artillery) took the call, told Harmon to wait (First Army strings); Kean's guarded call about "town A and town H"; Palmer misread it as a go; Collins at Harmon's CP finalised the plan late afternoon 24 Dec, told Harmon to "hold everything" when Palmer's second message arrived, but the attack on Christmas morning stayed on. Attack launched by CCB (Brig. Gen. Isaac White) at 0800, 25 Dec. Gemini's "24 Dec afternoon envelopment" CORRECTED to 25 Dec. AR22.
- 25 Dec: CCB in two task forces (TF A Hillyard to the high ground NW of Celles; TF B toward ridge 1,300 yds SE of Celles via Conjoux). Celles taken that afternoon/evening with about 200 prisoners. British 29th Armoured Brigade fought west of Foy-Notre-Dame and a few tanks of 3d Royal Tank Regiment helped TF A near Boisselles (AR22). Air: 370th Fighter Group (IX TAC) and RAF 83 Group; "twelve P-38s and an unknown number of British Typhoons"; the day was clear. OK. The pocket was squeezed over 25-26 Dec; many Germans escaped on foot (about 600 with Cochenhausen to Rochefort).
- Harmon quote: DISPUTED. HistoryNet gives "The bastards are in the bag... In the bag!" on Christmas Day (https://historynet.com/ernie-harmon-the-other-patton/). Gemini's "let me cut the string" is UNSUPPORTED (Cole only says "the string was drawn on the bag", narrative). Use "The bastards are in the bag!" attributed to Harmon, with "reportedly".
- Losses: Cole totals for the 2nd Armored Division's whole operation (Celles + Humain etc.): 1,213 prisoners, 82 tanks, 83 guns, 441 vehicles captured or destroyed; US lost 5 light and 22 medium tanks; 17 killed, 26 missing, 201 wounded. HistoryNet: 2nd Panzer lost 405 vehicles incl. 88 tanks/assault guns, all its artillery, about 2,500 killed or wounded and 1,200 captured (range). Gemini's "2,500 killed or captured" DISPUTED; "US losses light": OK. Cole, AR22 (ch. 22 end). Also: about 150 killed in one sweep, and 200 prisoners, 12 guns, 80 vehicles in the final woods squeeze.
- 2nd Armored was transferred from Ninth to First Army on 21 Dec (about 70 miles move); VII Corps had become involved in a defensive battle at Marche/Hotton. Gemini's "Lüttwitz Corps Commander" OK: XLVII Panzer Corps, Heinrich von Luettwitz; Fifth Panzer Army (Manteuffel) is the army, so "elements of the 5th Panzer Army" OK.
- Myth line "Bulge stopped entirely at Bastogne": fine as a myth; stress that Bastogne held out as a salient while the real high-water mark was Foy-Notre-Dame/Celles.

## 6. Gemini errors elsewhere
- "Bundesarchiv = Public Domain": it is CC BY-SA 3.0 DE; credit required (as noted in the file).
- "Highest tactical use of heavy bombers in history": hyperbole; "largest tactical use of heavy bombers to that date" is safer.
- "Bayerlein routed and division annihilated": Panzer Lehr was shattered on 25-27 July but pieces fought on (reported destroyed as a division 27 July).
