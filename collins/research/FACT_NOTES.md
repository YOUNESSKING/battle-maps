# Collins (#9): fact notes (corrections to research/GEMINI_COLLINS.md)
Checked 2026-10-10 against the US Army green books (Harrison, Cross-Channel Attack; Utah Beach to Cherbourg; Blumenson, Breakout and
Pursuit; Cole, The Ardennes: Battle of the Bulge; read via the HyperWar mirrors), Wikipedia, HistoryNet. Raw notes + URLs: FACT_CHECK_RAW.md.

## Biography
- Born 1 May 1896 (New Orleans), died 12 Sep 1987: OK. Maj. Gen. in 1944, commanding VII Corps (Utah Beach to the Elbe).
- "Lightning Joe": origin disputed (usually Guadalcanal, 25th "Tropic Lightning" Division). Script: "came out of the Pacific with a nickname".
- Quotes "a commander's place is where the crisis...", "The Germans in the Cotentin are beaten...", "If we can just get a hole...": ALL UNSUPPORTED. Not used.
- Later Army Chief of Staff 1949-53 (standard; double-check).

## Move 1: Cherbourg
- Cotentin cut at Barneville 17-18 June: OK (Gemini "18 June"). Divisions: 4th, 9th, 79th Infantry.
- 22 June: Collins's ultimatum expired 09:00; air preparation from 12:40; ground attack H-hour 14:00. Gemini "attack 12:40" CORRECTED.
- Fort du Roule, 25 June: Cpl John D. Kelly (pole charges) and Lt Carlos C. Ogden, both Medal of Honor. "Satchel charges down the ventilation shafts" UNSUPPORTED: not used.
- Schlieben: 26 June, ~800 men incl. Admiral Hennecke came out of the St-Sauveur tunnel command post to the 39th Infantry after tank destroyers fired into the entrances. CORRECTED: Collins OFFERED him a way to order a general surrender and Schlieben REFUSED (Gemini had it backwards). Loudspeakers = the arsenal surrender, 27 June (Gen. Sattler, ~400 men).
- Harbour forts 29 June; Cap de la Hague cleared 1 July (6,000+ prisoners).
- Casualties: CORRECTED. 2,800 killed / 13,500 wounded / 5,700 missing (22,000+) and ~39,000 German prisoners are the WHOLE Cotentin campaign (6 June - 1 July), not the city. Script + card say "Cotentin campaign".
- Port demolished: first cargo (DUKWs) 16 July; fully cleared only by end of September.

## Move 2: Operation Cobra
- 24 July: strike called off, but some bombs fell anyway: 25 killed, 131 wounded (30th Division).
- 25 July: 1,500+ heavy bombers dropped 3,300+ tons; plus 380+ mediums (~650 t) and 550+ fighter-bombers (~200 t): ~4,000 tons total. Target box ~6,000 x 2,200 yards (~3.4 x 1.25 miles). Script: "a strip of Normandy barely three and a half miles wide".
- Short bombs (35 heavies, 42 mediums): 111 killed, 490 wounded, incl. Lt. Gen. Lesley McNair (three-star). "Highest-ranking killed in Europe" wording not used.
- Panzer Lehr: ~2,200 combat troops, ~45 armoured vehicles in the line (Gemini "5,000" CORRECTED); ~1,000 casualties from the bombing; Bayerlein reported it "finally annihilated" on 27 July (Blumenson; paraphrased, no quote marks).
- Armour: CORRECTED. Collins decided on the afternoon of 25 July, before the infantry had secured its minimum objectives (Blumenson: a gamble), to commit armour next morning. 26 July: 2nd Armored + 1st Infantry with 3rd Armored's CCB; 27 July the rest of 3rd Armored. "Against doctrine" framing not used; "infantry attack 11:00" not used.
- Marigny cleared 27 July, Coutances ~28 July, Avranches reached 30 July (held 31). Patton supervised VIII Corps from ~28 July; Third Army operational 1 Aug and drove through the gap.
- VII Corps losses for Cobra: no clean figure found -> RESULT card (no casualty card).

## Move 3: Celles
- CORRECTED (the big one): NOT an "unauthorized attack ignoring Montgomery". Evening 24 Dec, First Army (Col. Akers) passed on that Montgomery and Hodges had agreed VII Corps would go on the defensive, and authorized it, on its own judgment, to fall back to the Andenne-Hotton-Manhay line. Collins and Harmon had already planned an attack; after weighing it, Collins chose to ATTACK on Christmas morning (Cole). Script says exactly this.
- Spearhead: 2nd Panzer at Foy-Notre-Dame / Celles, 4-5 miles from the Dinant bridges (Gemini "3 miles" CORRECTED), out of fuel. Deepest German penetration of the offensive: OK (standard).
- Attack: CCB (Brig. Gen. I. D. White) 08:00, 25 Dec (Gemini "afternoon of 24 Dec" CORRECTED), with British 29th Armoured Brigade / 3rd RTR tanks, P-38s and RAF Typhoons.
- Harmon: "The bastards are in the bag!" (HistoryNet, 25 Dec). Gemini's "let me cut the string" UNSUPPORTED. Script: "Harmon reportedly radioed".
- Losses (Cole, whole 2nd Armored operation 24-27 Dec): German 82 tanks, 83 guns, 441 vehicles, 1,213 prisoners; US 27 tanks, 17 killed, 26 missing, 201 wounded. HistoryNet gives 2nd Panzer ~2,500 killed/wounded + 1,200 captured (range). Card uses Cole.

## Added in script v1 (from memory, standard facts; double-check before publishing)
- 90th Division commander (Brig. Gen. Jay MacKelvie) relieved ~13 June 1944 after the division stalled; Collins went forward constantly.
- Sgt Curtis G. Culin's hedgerow cutter ("Rhino"), welded from German beach obstacles, fitted to hundreds of tanks before Cobra.
