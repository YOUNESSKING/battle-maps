# Generals On The Map: hit-vs-flop analysis brief

Prepared 2026-11-24 for an AI analyst. **Your task:** work out what makes a video on this channel a hit or a flop, and say what to do next.

## Sources and how reliable they are
- **Live views and dates:** vidIQ, pulled 2026-11-24. These are exact.
- **Studio analytics:** the owner's YouTube Studio screens and Studio AI answers, logged in the repo (`research/CHANNEL_LOG.md`, `HANDOVER.md`). Retention at 0:30 and 0:40 is approximate: Studio interpolates it, or we read it off a graph.
- **Competitor and keyword data:** vidIQ, yt-dlp public listings and the public RSS feeds. YouTube's bot check blocked per-video pages, and we did not try to get around it.
- **Owner reports:** a few numbers come only from what the owner told us in chat. They are marked "owner".
- **Gaps:** there is no Studio data for anything published after Arnold (3 Nov). The exceptions are the owner's CTR figures for Grant and Vicksburg and Marion's early pace.

### Format lifecycle (study of 17 Nov)
- **Supply flood:** format uploads went from 8 in September to 37 in October. Total views fell (2.98M → 1.91M).
- **Same-age medians collapsed:** videos at 3-17 days old dropped from 22k (Oct) to 456 (Nov), about 48x. Excluding War Table Atlas the drop is about 18x. Control channels outside the format dropped only about 3x.
- **Repeating a famous general did not hurt:** in October, repeat generals had a median of 21k vs 8k for first-time generals.
- **Momentum:**
  - A hit buys about a week. The next upload within 9 days got 1.5-1.7x the channel median; after 10+ days of silence it got 0.57x.
  - Consecutive uploads barely correlate overall (Spearman 0.09).
- **Our own curve:** our first 5 mature videos had a median of 40.5k, the next 5 694. The steepest decay of any channel, until Marion.
- **Owning one era helps:**
  - Channels that keep producing hits own one era: Ravelin (Revolution only: 31 hits of 72, 32k subs) and The Generals' Oath / Civil War Dose (Grant-Lee stories).
  - Mixed-era format channels decayed fastest.
- **Copycats:** they cloned our picks within days (Hannibal, Morgan, Marion, Rokossovsky).

### 3D and cinematic niche
- **Grand Maneuver** (1.2k subs at the time, Napoleon only, AI narration, about one 3D film a week):
  - "Napoleon's Last Masterpiece: 4 Battles in 5 Days | France 1814" got **109k** in 11 days; its median was 17k.
  - The winning title shape: count + time + place.
  - The thumbnail had no face: navy map, one giant word ("HE WON."), wax seal and arrow.
  - Paradox titles did 0.6-0.9x its median.

  - Vicksburg: Warhawk 159k, Biography 387k, Context History 106k.
  - Saratoga: Ravelin 365k.
  - Trenton: Epic History 500k.
- **Counter-example:** Ruse & Ruin makes long Revolution films with a median of 259 views.

### Top-20 war-history channels (study of 7 Nov, 457 videos)
- **Thumbnails:**
  - One focal subject, readable at 168×94 px.
  - About 2.6 words on average; 38 % of winners have no text at all.
  - A face on about two-thirds of winners. A map works only with ONE named place and ONE arrow.
  - One fixed brand template per channel. One hot accent colour (red).
- **Titles:**
  - About 54 characters, a named person or place plus a stake word.
  - "Part N" titles lose 15-50 %.
  - Famous Western topics beat niche ones by 5-60x within the same channel. Topic fame is the biggest driver.
- **Hooks:**
  - Winners open on one concrete moment, a hard number, or a hero named within 15 s.
  - Background-first openings work only on mega-topics.
  - Sponsor reads before the content are a common mistake.

## 5. What we concluded so far (hypotheses, with the evidence against them)
1. **YouTube judges in two stages.**
   - **Stage 1:** the size of the day-1/2 test batch depends on channel momentum. It was big right after a hit (Morgan 7.6k Browse impressions, Greene 6.3k) and small on a cold channel (Smith 164, Patton 766, Arnold 1,262).
   - **Stage 2:** watch time per Browse impression (CTR × average view duration) of about **18 s or more** and a Suggested CTR of about **3.5 % or more** led to expansion (Morgan, 2 Para). About 13-15 s held flat (Greene, Patton). About 8 s was cut (Gavin, Arnold). The ranking matched the outcome in all 5 cases with full data.
   - **Against:** Marion exploded right after the Yorktown flop, with no momentum. Its Studio data is not logged yet, and it is the key case to explain.
2. **The opening matters, but it is not sufficient.**
   - The two videos that left the crisis story for a biography in the first minute (Gavin, Collins) lost about 60 % of viewers by 0:42.
   - Hits stayed in one crisis story and brought the general in as its turning point (0:47-1:42).
   - **Against:** 0:40 retention did not decide reach on its own: 2 Para had 37 % and was a hit; Greene had 50 % and was flat.
3. **The title pattern is a big lever.** Thomas went from 0.9 % to 3.85 % CTR after the retitle.
   - **Against:** most format titles land at 3.8-4.7 % CTR whatever happens next, so CTR alone does not separate hits from flops.
4. **Era.** WWII is 0 for 3 and Korea 0 for 1. The Revolution has 2 hits and 2 flops; the Civil War has 1 hit and 2 middles.
   - Studio AI says "18th-19th-century American wars are our audience".
   - **Confound:** the WWII videos also had the bad openings (Gavin, Collins) or saturated sidebars (Patton).
5. **The commander must be the hook.** All 4 hits are commander-led "Top 3" videos. Both battle or campaign titles with no hero name flopped (Yorktown 583, Vicksburg 1,311), even with strong search demand and good retention.
6. **The topic gets burned by our own repeats.** Vicksburg came 11 days after Grant, whose move 2 was Vicksburg. Both got 3.1 % CTR.
7. **Thumbnail.** Arnold's dark, nameless thumbnail with an unlike face gave the lowest CTR (2.85 %).
   - Marion's "contradiction word" (HE RAN.) with two tested recipes is the current best guess.
   - Nothing has been A/B-proven: **no Test & Compare winner is logged for any of our videos.**
8. **Format fatigue.** Small "Top 3" channels fell about 18-48x in November. Yet Marion (a format video) did 89k in a week that month. So the format is not dead; generic copies of it are.

### Ruled out as single causes
Time of day, subscriber count at launch, a 2-day upload gap, end screens, topic crowding and video length (all ours are 13.5-19 min).
