# Judge 1 "Get the idea" verdict (2026-10-30)

**Verdict: pick E (with two small tweaks). A fails the owner's "never empty" rule; C fails "simplicity" and is not safe under counters/front lines/territory multiply.**

**What the owner wants:** a clean, premium documentary map (Tactical Genius / Kings and Generals: flat colour, big readable shapes, few labels) that still has believable geography. Look is locked to relief + territory + glowing lines + counters (STYLE_LOCK 2/2a). Detail may only be a quiet base layer, never a competitor of the units.

**A (current):** clean and in-lock, but on Pickett and Bentonville it is empty (grey-green mush; Bentonville has no visible geography at all). This is the owner's complaint.

**C:** accurate but busy. Every stream (Tunnel: dozens of hairlines), canopy mottle (grid-like green blocks on the Tunnel plain look like a pixel pattern, not forest), swamp wash plus outlines (Bentonville becomes a green carpet with outlined blotches). It also fights the multiply territory tint and the 54 px counters, and a modern land cover shows farm plots as "trees" in 1863 places. This is the "losing simplicity" case.

**E:** reads like a TG map. Tunnel: the Tennessee River, the one winding creek and the lake are crisp; the relief stays the hero. Bentonville: lakes and the river meander give the map identity (large swamp shapes are readable; the flat tan "clearings" are slightly odd but quiet). Pickett: only tiny ponds, which look like random blue dots and add nothing.

**Add value (keep):** main rivers (Tennessee, Chickamauga/named creeks), big lakes and big swamp areas, soft forest tint on ridges only (the ridge darkening gives the Missionary Ridge idea), one-colour water with a thin edge.
**Hurt simplicity (drop):** minor streams and drain lines, canopy mottle and crown dots (D), reed marks, small swamp specks, tiny ponds (Pickett dots), swamp outlines, farm-field squares read as forest.

**Tweaks to E:** (1) drop ponds below a size threshold (Pickett has ~40 specks; show none or 3-4 max); (2) keep the forest tint only where large and contiguous, lower its alpha a touch so the multiply territory colour stays strong. Where a map has no named water (Pickett) accept the relief alone, or add a named creek label, rather than inventing detail.

**3 reasons:** 1) E is the only variant that fixes emptiness without adding noise: the rivers and lakes are what a viewer notices. 2) C's clutter collides with units, labels and glowing front lines and breaks the premium look. 3) E stays inside the locked look (relief + territory + lines), D/C drift toward a satellite-photo style TG never uses.
