# Judge 3 (readability + viewer appeal): VERDICT: ADOPT FOR SOME SHOTS, conditionally. Do not adopt G3 as is, not for all maps.
Method: real Tunnel Hill (4:50) and Ringgold (8:30) frames from cleburne.mp4; our counters/fronts/flags pasted over G3 (saturation mask, 1920 and 360 px); CIEDE2000, WCAG and high-pass stats.
Findings:
1. Contrast stats, current vs G3: background luma std 16 vs 50 (3x); phone-size high-frequency energy 10.9 vs 28.5 (2.6x). Red/blue counters keep median dE 36-43 on G3, but 27-28% of G3 pixels have WCAG <1.5 against them (10-13% now), because dark orange-brown shadow sides (45,49,30) and bright sand (196,176,131) sit either side of the counters.
2. Union red on orange-brown relief is the weak pair (hue near). Blue counters read well. The red/blue fronts nearly vanish at 360 px; the glow washes out on sand.
3. Trees clutter: dense crowns pull the eye away from the counters at phone size. As ribbons along rivers they read as the film's look.
4. Palette clash: warm sand conflicts with the locked grey-green day grade (blue-muted = night). A warm day and muted-blue night would be a bigger swing; the look would have to follow the same grade rule.
5. Data: map texture is not a measured retention lever (CHANNEL_LOG, 4_meta). Risk is readability.
6. The owner's "balance of simplicity and a bit of detail" is met by the smooth rivers and ribbons, not by this relief strength.
Conditions: relief contrast cut to about 50% (luma std <= 25); tree ribbons only, capped; fronts and glow strength raised on warm; keep a dark halo on counters; night keeps the muted-blue grade; wide/option-1 shots first, close-ups with option-2 fronts stay on the current look until tested.
Measure after upload: retention 0:30 (>= 64%), 1:00, mid-video dip on map scenes, phone share, comments about "can't see".
