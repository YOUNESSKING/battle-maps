Use them only to place units and terrain correctly. Do not paste them into scenes. Licences listed so a credit can go in the description if one is ever shown.

## missionary_ridge_25nov1863_jespersen.png  (2400x4096)
Hal Jespersen, "Battle of Missionary Ridge, 25 Nov 1863" (Wikimedia Commons File:Missionary_Ridge.png; Jespersen maps are CC BY-SA / CC BY, credit "Map by Hal Jespersen, www.cwmaps.com"). North is up, Tennessee River top left, Chattanooga left, Georgia line at the bottom.
TUNNEL HILL (top right, the move-1 ground): the north end of Missionary Ridge, the railroad tunnel under it, Goat Hill (Union side) to its north. Blue (Sherman's wing): Ewing (Corse/Walcutt/Loomis/Matthies), J. Smith, M. Smith/Giles Smith, Howard/Buschbeck/Krzyzanowski, Baird, Davis on the far left; they attack from the north-west and from Goat Hill. Red at Tunnel Hill (labels as drawn): Cleburne, with Smith/Granbury, Govan and Lowrey/Polk-side brigades on the hill; Stevenson, Walker, Cheatham to the south; Brown, Pettus, Wilson, Gist, Maney, Walthall, Moore, Jackson, Vaughan, Deas, Manigault along the ridge; Hardee (red arrows east = counter-attacks), Bragg HQ and Breckinridge's line lower down. Blue arrows centre: Thomas's four divisions (Baird, Wood, Sheridan, Johnson: Phelps, Van Derveer, Turchin, Beatty, Willich, Hazen, Wagner, Harker, Sherman, Staughton, Carlin) assault straight up the ridge; Hooker (Geary, Cruft, Osterhaus) bottom left via Rossville Gap. Blue top (Sherman's wing): Ewing/Corse/Walcutt/Loomis/Matthies, J. Smith, M. Smith, Howard (Buschbeck, Krzyzanowski), Baird, Davis far left, attacking from the north-west.
Use the order of battle in research/FACT_NOTES.md for Cleburne's brigade names.

## ringgold_gap_jespersen_960.png  (960x1152, from 2251x2701 original; only the 960 thumbnail could be fetched)
Hal Jespersen, "Battle of Ringgold Gap" (Wikimedia Commons File:Battle_of_Ringgold_Gap.png, CC BY 4.0). Hand-checked layout (north up, 0.4 mile scale): Ringgold town and the depot at left (west, Union side), Western & Atlantic RR running north-south then east through the gap along South Chickamauga Creek; Taylor's Ridge bottom, White Oak Mountain top right. RED (Cleburne): Govan (6/7 AR, 16 AL) on Taylor's Ridge south of the gap; Goldthwaite's artillery + 5/13, 8/19, 6/7(-), 2/15/24 AR at the gap mouth; Granbury (6/10/15 TX, 17/18/24/25 TX, 7 TX) on the slope north of the gap; Lowrey (32/45 MS, 33 AL, 45 AL, 1 AR) on the ridge face with counter-attack arrows from the east; Polk (2 TN, 1 AR, 45 AL, 3/5 Conf.) at the north end. BLUE (Hooker): Geary and Cobham/Ireland by the creek (29/109/111 PA, 99 OH, 149/102/60/137 NY, 13 IL), Wood(s) (12, 3, 17, 29, 31 MO) centre, Osterhaus/Williamson (4, 9, 25, 26, 30 IA, 76 OH) north of centre, Creighton (147 PA, 7 OH, 28 PA, 66 OH) turning the red left on the north (large arrow), Davis arriving from the west.

## new_hope_church_cox_1882.png  (2400x2547)
Jacob D. Cox, "Operations near New Hope Church" (from *Atlanta*, 1882; public domain, Wikimedia Commons File:Cox_map_of_New_Hope_Church.jpg). Contemporary-style relief map: Dallas lower left, New Hope Church centre, Pickett's Mill upper right (at Pumpkin Vine Creek), Allatoona top right, Federal (solid) and Confederate (dashed) lines. Pickett's Mill sits on the Confederate right flank at the creek.

## loc_allatoona_new_hope_church_1864.jpg  (1200x1713)
Library of Congress map 99447162, "Map of Allatoona, New Hope Church and vicinity" (Western & Atlantic RR Co., Matthews-Northrup; no known restrictions). Roads, mills, "Battle of Pickett's Mill, May 27, 1864" marked north-east of New Hope Church; useful for the road network of the corridor map.

## loc_chattanooga_and_its_defences_1865.jpg  (2487x3097)
Library of Congress map 99448360, Charles Sholl, "Chattanooga and its defences" (1865; no known restrictions). Shows Chattanooga, Missionary Ridge, the Tennessee River, Chickamauga Station, Rossville, Tunnel/Taylor's Ridge and the Ringgold Gap (bottom right, "Held by Hooker" labelled) with the railroad running Chattanooga-Chickamauga-Ringgold-Dalton: the theatre view for `chatt`.
