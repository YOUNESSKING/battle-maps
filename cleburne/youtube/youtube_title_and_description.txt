Patrick Cleburne's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves | The Man Who Saved the Confederacy's Western Army

In November 1863, the Confederacy's western army was running for its life. Its last hope was a mountain gap called Ringgold. Four thousand men held it against three times their number, and saved the army.

In this video we break down Patrick Cleburne's three greatest tactical moves: Tunnel Hill at Missionary Ridge on 25 November 1863, where one division on the high ground broke Sherman's attacks one brigade at a time and counterattacked; the rear guard at Ringgold Gap two days later, with two guns hidden behind cut brush and a reserve that outran the Union flankers up Taylor's Ridge; and Pickett's Mill on 27 May 1864, where a Union flank march walked into a steep ravine. We also cover his Dalton memorandum of January 1864, which proposed freeing and arming enslaved men to save the Confederacy, and what it cost him.

Let them tire on the hill, then strike. Hide your strength until they are close. Let the ground do the killing.
