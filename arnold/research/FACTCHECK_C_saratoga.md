## Morgan overlap (what Morgan did; keep the camera on Arnold)

## (a) Alternatives, 2 lines each
- **Ridgefield, 27 Apr 1777**: Arnold organised the militia against the British force returning from Danbury, worked with Wooster and Silliman; Wooster died; Arnold was wounded again (left leg per WA, but this contradicts nothing); Ridgefield led to his promotion to major general without restored seniority. Map-friendly (one road, barricade), moderately sourced here (WA only). Rating: good #2/#3 candidate.
- **Fort Stanwix relief, Aug 1777**: Schuyler sent Arnold on 13 Aug with 900 men; the ruse with a messenger (Hon Yost Schuyler, name not confirmed in this pass) convinced St. Leger's Indian allies that a larger force came, and St. Leger raised the siege (WA). Best "trick" move, easy to show, but Arnold is a relief commander on a wide map. Rating: strong as an add-on; fewer hard numbers (UNVERIFIED).

## (b) Treason in ONE line
- Where: after the wound and the French alliance, as the closing twist, and NOT in the first two minutes. Line suggestion: "Three years later, in 1780, the man who saved the army at Saratoga tried to sell West Point to the British, and his name became a word for traitor." Keep to one sentence.
- Facts checked in WA: West Point command 3 Aug 1780; offered the fort in July 1780 for £20,000; met John André 21 Sep; the Vulture (Captain Andrew Sutherland) driven off 22 Sep, André returned overland; captured 23 Sep near Tarrytown by three militiamen; Arnold fled to the Vulture 24 Sep; André hanged at Tappan 2 Oct 1780. Washington: "Arnold has betrayed me. Whom can we trust now?" (WA reports "reportedly"; do not use as a verified quote).

## (c) Controversies, neutral
- **Debts**: about £16,000 in debt in the 1760s (WA); Congress inquiry found him ~£1,000 in arrears on Quebec expenses, mostly undocumented, papers lost in the retreat (WA).
- **Promotion**: Feb 1777 Congress passed him over for major general; Washington refused his resignation and wrote Congress warning of losing "two or three other very good officers" (WA). Resignation letter 11 Jul 1777, refused; ordered north.
- **Court-martial**: began 1 Jun 1779, delayed to Dec; 26 Jan 1780 cleared of all but two minor charges; Washington's formal rebuke early Apr 1780, "imprudent and improper" (WA).
- **Peggy Shippen**: met summer 1778; married 8 Apr 1779; son Edward born 19 Mar 1780; many historians credit her with helping the plot (Philbrick: "exerted a powerful influence on her husband") (WA). Her role is debated.

## (d) Best HOOK candidate (7 Oct 1777)
Stakes: Burgoyne's army, 6,600 men, 30 miles from Albany and the Hudson, which would cut New England off; a general with no command is told to stay in his tent. Place: Bemis Heights. Hard number: the British column ~6,600 (WS) OR "two redoubts, ~300 men in Breymann's" (WS). Draft: "October 1777. A British army of 6,600 is one battle from the Hudson and from cutting the colonies in two. The American general has taken the one officer who can break it out of command. He is in his tent at Bemis Heights, until the guns start." Check the claim "Gates took his command" against row 3 (DISPUTED): say "left him without orders".
