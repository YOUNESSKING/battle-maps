# FACTCHECK B: Valcour Island, 11-13 Oct 1776 (Benedict Arnold #17)

Method: primary texts fetched and read in full from archive.org: *Naval Documents of the American Revolution* vol. 6 (ND6; "navaldocumentsof06unit") and vol. 7 (ND7), and Mahan, *Major Operations of the Navies* (Gutenberg #16602; archive.org copy cross-checked). Wikipedia, American Heritage, Battlefields.org, Smithsonian Magazine used as pointers only. Saved texts: scratchpad `arnold/nd6c.txt`, `nd7.txt`, `mahan.txt`.
Founders Online blocked curl and WebFetch returned empty; the Varick (14 Oct) and Schuyler (16 Oct) letters were NOT read. Their content reached me only through search snippets: Varick enclosed Arnold's 12 Oct letter to Gates, and Arnold's letter to Schuyler is dated 15 Oct.
Short verbatim quotes below are from the ND6 transcription (original spelling kept).

## 2. Best vivid details that ARE verified (map and hook)
- Arnold 12 Oct to Gates: a letter written on Schuyler Island the day after, saying he pointed most of the guns himself on Congress; "hulled a doz times".
- Royal Savage lost her first blood: ran onto the south point, "all the Men were saved"; Arnold lost "All my Papers & most of my Cloaths" aboard her.
- Pellew on the bowsprit under musket fire, hauling the jib to swing the Carleton round (Mahan, from Douglas's own letter).
- Five broadsides from the Inflexible at dusk "silenced their whole line" (Douglas, via Mahan). Philadelphia sank an hour after the guns stopped.
- The British anchored across the south end of the sound "in line of battle", Burgoyne wrote "they must have surrendered this morning, or given us battle on our own terms"; then the fleet slipped through at 7 pm.
- Congress beached "ten miles from Crown Point on the East Side" and burned with four gondolas; 200 walked through the woods to Crown Point and "escaped the Savages who way laid the Road in two Hours after we passed".
- Douglas on British effort: the Inflexible sailed "twenty Eight Days after her keel was laid".
- Riedesel (via Mahan): "If we could have begun our expedition four weeks earlier, I am satisfied that everything would have been ended this year (1776)".
- Hagglund raised the gondola Philadelphia in 1935; a 24-lb ball hole is visible in her side; she sits at the Smithsonian.

## 3. Stakes (hook) with sources
- Carleton's 14 Oct report to Germain: "The Rebel Fleet upon Lake Champlain has been intirely defeated"; "only Three of Fifteen Sail... having escaped". 20 Oct to Howe: "I am in hopes however that our appearance on this side the lake will occation a diversion which may be favourable to your opperations." (ND6)
- Mahan p.28: the 1776 plan was for Howe to advance up the Hudson and join the Canada army to control the Hudson-Champlain valley. He writes that the control of "the valley of the Hudson, in connection with Lake Champlain, was, very properly, the chief object of the British government". That supports "cut off New England"; I did not find Germain's 1776 instructions text (UNVERIFIED).
- Same day as Valcour: Howe's army landed at Throg's Neck, 12 Oct (ND6: "Extract of a letter from Harlem, October 13": "yesterday morning about 4000 of the enemy landed at Fogg's or Frog's [Throg] Point"), and Washington "feeling obliged to let go the line of the Harlem" and cross into New Jersey (Mahan p.45). Washington had already lost New York city on 15 Sep (UNVERIFIED here).
- Hook line safe: "While Washington was losing New York, a ragged fleet of 15 boats stood between Carleton and Ticonderoga." (Arnold feared a joint fleet-army attack; Gates and St. Clair asked for 8-10,000 more militia.)
