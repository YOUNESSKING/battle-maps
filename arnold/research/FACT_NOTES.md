## Verified facts used in the script
HOOK / SARATOGA
- Burgoyne 1777 from Canada down Champlain/Hudson toward Albany (NPS; Mahan: Hudson-Champlain valley = chief British object).
- 7 Oct 1777: over 1,500 British/German troops moved out to probe the American left (NPS); Fraser mortally wounded (NPS/ABT; shooter "often identified as Timothy Murphy": not named in script).
- Arnold had no command (Wilkinson's printed papers; ATL); went to the fighting; Maj. Armstrong sent after him did not catch him until it was over (Luzader/Nickerson via WP: hedged "reportedly").
- How he got to the field is disputed (NPS: asked Gates for men "it is Late in the Day but Let me have men & we will have some Fun with them Before Sun Set"; Wilkinson: rode out). Script says accounts differ.
- Arnold's 22 Sep letter: Gates said he "did not know I was a Major-general or had any command in the army" (Wilkinson prints it).
- Light Infantry (Balcarres) redoubt held (NPS); gap held by Canadians/provincials around two log cabins, cleared by Learned's men (Wilkinson); Breymann's redoubt open at the rear (NPS); Breymann killed; Arnold wounded in the left leg, horse killed (NPS, Wilkinson); "dashed through the fire of the two lines" = Wilkinson hearsay -> "by several accounts".
- Gates 12 Oct report: "the Gallant General Arnold, whose Leg was fractured by a musket ball, as he forced the Enemy's Breast Work" (ATL/Luzader).
- Losses 7 Oct: British over 630, Americans about 150 (NPS). 19 Sep: British nearly 600, Americans 320 (NPS); Morgan's corps ~700; Arnold sent regiments in waves (NPS); Riedesel reinforcement at dusk.
- Burgoyne abandoned camp 8 Oct night, retreated to Saratoga, surrendered 17 Oct (NPS). France: Treaty of Alliance 6 Feb 1778 (standard; Wikipedia).
- Boot Monument: boot, epaulet; "does not mention Arnold by name" (NPS); 1887 de Peyster (stone header per WP).
- Treason: 1780, tried to hand West Point to the British (André captured 23 Sep 1780, Arnold fled 25 Sep to HMS Vulture). One line in the hook only.
- Promotion snub 19 Feb 1777: Congress promoted five brigadiers junior to him (Stirling, Mifflin, St. Clair, Stephen, Lincoln) (standard; Gemini; WP).
- Bemis Heights ~25 mi north of Albany (standard geography).
BACKGROUND
- May 1775 Ticonderoga taken (Allen and Arnold); Arnold a New Haven merchant/sea captain trading to Quebec (standard); Washington conceived the Quebec plan and chose Arnold (Codman).
MOVE 1
- ~1,100 men (Codman), ~200 bateaux (Colburn's yard; ~224), plan ~180 mi / ~20 days (Montresor map, Codman); actual ~350 mi; Fort Western 25 Sep -> Pointe-Lévy 9 Nov.
- Great Carrying Place ~12 mi of ponds and bog.
- Storm night of 21 Oct, trees down, river up 8-10 ft overnight, bateaux under water (Dearborn, Senter).
- Three rear companies turned back late Oct, ~450 with invalids, took more than their share of food (Dearborn, Stocking via Codman).
- Arnold ahead with a small party down the Chaudière to the first settlements (Sartigan), sent back cattle and flour (Codman).
- Dearborn: 1 Nov Goodrich's company "Kill'd my Dog ... and Eat them". Senter: shaving soap, pomatum, lip salve, shoe leather, cartridge boxes eaten.
- ~600 reached Pointe-Lévy 9 Nov (~500 fit); 13-14 Nov night crossing in ~35 canoes/dugouts past HMS Lizard and Hunter, to Wolfe's Cove (Codman).
- Montgomery arrived early Dec with ~300 (Codman); New England enlistments ended 31 Dec; paper in caps "Liberty or death!" (Codman); blizzard; Montgomery killed by grapeshot at Près-de-Ville; Arnold hit in the leg at the Sault-au-Matelot barricade; Morgan over the first barricade, stalled, sortie from Palace Gate behind; ~400 captured.
- Senter: Arnold "ordered his pistols loaded, with a sword on his bed, adding that he was determined to kill as many as possible if they came into the room."
- Siege/blockade kept up until British relief in May 1776 (standard).
MOVE 2
- Inflexible: 18 guns, taken apart and carried, sailed "twenty Eight Days after her keel was laid" (Douglas).
- Arnold: 15 vessels, ~700 men (Mahan); British ~25 armed vessels, ~700 seamen (Mahan, Douglas) + "Upwards of One thousand Men in Batteaus prepared for boarding" (Arnold).
- Skenesborough boatyard (standard).
- Arnold to Gates (28 Sep, via Mahan): if the enemy came up the lake it would be impossible for them to take advantage of his situation (paraphrase only).
- 11 Oct: British seen ~8 am, strong NE wind carried them past Valcour; attacked from leeward; Royal Savage ran onto the point of Valcour, men saved, burned; fight from ~11-noon to dusk; only Carleton + gunboats could close (Pringle "wind so unfavourable"); Arnold "obliged myself to Point Most of the Guns on board the Congress"; Pellew out on the bowsprit; Inflexible's five broadsides at dusk; Philadelphia sank about an hour after the fight.
- Losses 11 Oct ~60 killed and wounded; ammunition "Nearly three fourths spent" (Arnold).
- British line anchored across the south end; 7 pm Trumbull led, gondolas followed, Congress + Washington last (Arnold); Waterbury: went through "entirely undiscovered" (Mahan).
- 13 Oct ~noon Washington struck; Arnold fought Congress + 4 gondolas ~2.5 h, ran ashore "in a small Creek ten Miles from Crown Point on the East Side", burned them; Mahan: flags flying; ~200 walked to Crown Point and escaped an ambush on the road (Arnold).
- Total: ten or eleven of fifteen vessels lost, "Eighty odd" killed and wounded, ~110 captured; British under 40 (Douglas/Pringle).
- Carleton at Crown Point 14 Oct; Ticonderoga ~9,000 (Arnold); British left Crown Point for Canada 2 Nov, first snow (Digby).
- Mahan (verbatim): "That the Americans were strong enough to impose the capitulation of Saratoga was due to the invaluable year of delay secured to them in 1776 by their little navy on Lake Champlain, created by the indomitable energy, and handled with the indomitable courage, of the traitor, Benedict Arnold."
- Philadelphia raised 1935 (Hagglund), Smithsonian (not in the script).
