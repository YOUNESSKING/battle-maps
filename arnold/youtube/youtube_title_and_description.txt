Benedict Arnold's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves | The Man Who Turned the Tide at Saratoga

By 1777 the Revolution was going badly: the British held New York and Philadelphia, and a second British army came down from Canada. At Saratoga, on the Hudson, about six thousand of its soldiers laid down their arms, and the victory brought France into the war. The man who broke the British line that day had no command at all. Three years later his name would become another word for traitor. This is what he did before that.

In this video we break down Benedict Arnold's three greatest tactical moves: the march to Quebec in 1775, about 350 miles up the Kennebec and down the Chaudière through the Maine wilderness, and the assault on the city in a New Year's Eve blizzard; Valcour Island in October 1776, when a fleet built from fresh-cut trees fought the Royal Navy on Lake Champlain and slipped away in the dark; and Saratoga in 1777, from Freeman's Farm to the charge on Breymann's redoubt on 7 October.

Go where the enemy says no army can go. Let the ground and the weather fight for you. Lead from the front, at the point where it is decided.
