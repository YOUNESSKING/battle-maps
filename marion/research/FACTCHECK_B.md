## A. Biggest corrections to Gemini (read first)

1. James 1821 is Gutenberg #923. James never uses "Swamp Fox" (grep: only "d----d old fox"). The first "Swamp-fox" in print that I found is **Simms 1844** ("Popular tradition represents him as ... exclaiming ... this d--d Swamp-fox"). Gemini's "James 1821, p. 67 ... christened Swamp Fox" is wrong.
2. James reports the outburst as Tarleton's words but gives no witness, no source and no date: it is an anonymous oral tradition recorded 40 years later. Gemini's claim that James was a 16-year-old in the brigade in 1780 is also unchecked (James was Marion's militiaman and an eyewitness of other scenes, e.g. the dinner at the White Marsh, but not shown for the chase).
3. Gemini's "26 miles / seven hours" is a mix: "seven hours, through swamps and defiles" is **Tarleton's own phrase**; James says Tarleton pursued "about twenty-five miles" and Marion rode "thirty-five miles". The "26" comes from modern handouts (Georgetown museum, battlefields.org).
4. "Tarleton burned the plantation" is **C** (James), but the Gemini dossier's "lures him toward the Richardson plantation" is backwards: Marion saw a glow at the plantation and went toward it; Tarleton had lit fires at the plantation as bait (Georgetown handout, modern), and James says Tarleton burned house, outhouses, corn and fodder of Gen. Richardson's estate and that he "first dined in the house".
5. The famous "Look at this, sir. On such fare as this we fight and conquer..." quote is NOT in James and not in the Weems edition I read. Weems' blindfolded-officer scene is there, with different words. James's own sweet-potato dinner is a different event (see C-9). Gemini's claim that James "explicitly confirms the blindfolded officer's visit" is false.
6. The "Greene tribute" quote is mangled: the real text (Greene to Marion, Camp before Camden, 24 Apr 1781, printed in James) says: "History affords no instance wherein an officer has kept possession of a country under so many disadvantages as you have ... To fight the enemy bravely with the prospect of victory is nothing, but to fight with intrepidity under the constant impression of a defeat, and inspire irregular troops to do it, is a talent peculiar to yourself." "Doing so much with such little means" and "criterion of a great mind" are invented.
7. Cornwallis's 3 Dec 1780 "terror of his threats and cruelty of his punishments" is real (Ross vol 1 **p. 72**, also Tarleton 1787 appendix); Gemini's "Ross vol 1 p. 298" is wrong.
8. Marion's ankle: James says "dislocated", in a drinking party, when Lincoln's order (31 Jan 1780) expelled unfit officers; Gemini's "fractured", "March 1780", host "Alexander McQueen" and "jumping from a second-story window" are not in James (window/McQueen come from later tradition; UNV).
9. Oscar Marion "recognised by Presidential proclamation" is half right: a proclamation signed by President Bush was read at a **15 Dec 2006 ceremony at the Capitol** (HNN/WaPo). It was not an act of Congress.
10. Senate painting catalogue number is **33.00002.000** (painted 1836, given by the artist's son Octavius White 1899), not "21.00001".
11. Gemini file names on Commons are mostly wrong (Tarleton, Greene, Lee, Cornwallis, Rawdon, Moultrie flag, Marion portraits). Correct names in section G.
12. The "Gamecock" is Sumter; James writes "game cock, (meaning Sumter)".

## H. Cross-cutting notes

- Gemini's "Move 2 hook: 7 hours, 26 miles, 300-400 dragoons" should be: "a few hundred British horse and light infantry with two light guns" (James/handout), "seven hours" (Tarleton), "about twenty-five miles" (James), 8 Nov 1780.
- The strongest verified story beats: the false fire at Richardson's (James), Marion's "Now we are safe!" (James), 35 miles vs 25 miles (James), Cornwallis's recall express after Fishdam (Tarleton + Ross), Cornwallis 8 Nov "Marion is cautious and vigilant" (Ross), and 25 Nov "I wish your friend Marion was as quiet" (Ross).
- Unfixed: Rankin/Bass/Oller details (not accessible). Gemini's page numbers for them are not verified.
