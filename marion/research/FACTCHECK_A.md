# FACTCHECK_A: Gemini Marion dossier, hook + background + MOVE 1 (Aug-Oct 1780)

Checked 2026-11-14. Verdicts: C = CONFIRMED, FIX = CORRECTED, TRAD = TRADITION, UNV = UNVERIFIED.
Gemini's page numbers (S1 p. 52, 65-68 etc.) and its "52 horsemen", "sawed-off farm saws", "drunken dinner at McQueen's, Lincoln order" details were NOT found in any source I read. Ignore every Gemini page number.

## Exact verbatim quotes

**Cornwallis to Clinton, Camp at Wynnesborough, 3 Dec 1780** (Ross vol 1, p. 71, abridged, with the editor's ellipses):
"Colonel Marion had so wrought on the minds of the people, partly by the terror of his threats and cruelty of his punishments, and partly by the promise of plunder, that there was scarcely an inhabitant between the Santee and Pedee that was not in arms against us. I therefore sent Tarleton, who pursued Marion for several days, and by convincing the inhabitants that there was a power superior to Marion, who could likewise reward and punish, so far checked the insurrection, that the greatest part of them have not dared openly to appear in arms against us since his expedition."

**Same letter, full version** (Ross vol 1, Appendix No. V, p. 498; the extra sentence is the "gates" line): "Colonel Marion had so wrought on the minds of the people, partly by the terror of his threats and cruelty of his punishments, and partly by the promise of plunder, that there was scarce an inhabitant between the Santee and Pedee that was not in arms against us. Some parties had even crossed the Santee and carried terror to the gates of Charlestown. My first object was to reinstate matters in that quarter, without which Camden could receive no supplies. I therefore sent Tarleton, who pursued Marion for several days, obliged his corps to take to the swamps, and, by convincing the inhabitants that there was a power superior to Marion, who could likewise reward and punish, so far checked the insurrection, that the greatest part of them have not dared openly to appear in arms against us since his expedition."
(Tarleton 1787 Appendix prints "scarcely an inhabitant between the Sentee and Pedee ... carried terror to the gates of Charles town". Pick one spelling and say "Cornwallis to Clinton, 3 December 1780". Gemini's "terror ... to the gates of Charles Town" is real but is NOT the same sentence as "terror of his threats".)

**Cornwallis to Balfour, 25 Nov 1780** (Ross vol 1, ch. III, p. 70): "We have lost two great plagues in Sumpter and Clarke. I wish your friend Marion was as quiet." (Possible cliffhanger line for the end of Move 1/2; verified.)

**Cornwallis to Germain, Camden, 20 Aug 1780** (Ross vol 1, App. II, p. 490): "...the whole country between Pedee and Black River openly avowed the principles of rebellion, and collecting in parties commenced acts of hostility." Also: "...I therefore employed myself in fixing posts of troops from the Pedee to the Savannah Rivers, to awe the disaffected and encourage the loyal inhabitants".

**Marion to Gates, Port's Ferry, N. side Pedee, 29 Aug 1780** (as printed by ABT): "I have destroyed all the boats at the ferries, from Murray's to the mouth of the Santee, and drove the guards placed at each ferry. Hearing of some prisoners, which the enemy were carrying down the Charlestown by the way of Nelson's Ferry, I marched my party of seventy men and surprised them the 25th. Instant, at the Great Savanna, at Colonel Sumpter's house, near Nelson's ferry - killed two, wounded five, and took prisoners one Captain of the 63d, one Lieutenant of the Prince of Wales' regiment, and sixteen privates, and two tories doing duty with them. The whole guard, as the Captain informed me, consisted of forty regulars and twenty tories. Our loss was one man killed, and Captain benson wounded slightly; and I have retaken one hundred and fifty soldiers of the Maryland line ... I am certain that they will desert, to a man, without it." Also: "I have given to the two officers their servants, and to the wounded their parole" and "some days I have not more than a dozen with me".

**Otho Williams, Narrative (from Johnson 1822), at the Pee Dee crossing:** "Colonel Marion, a gentleman of South Carolina, had been with the army a few days, attended by a very few followers, distinguished by small black leather caps and the wretchedness of their attire; their number did not exceed twenty men and boys, some white, some black, and all mounted, but most of them miserably equipped; their appearance was in fact so burlesque that it was with much difficulty the diversion of the regular soldiery was restrained by the officers; and the general himself was glad of an opportunity of detaching Colonel Marion, at his own instance, towards the interior of South Carolina, with orders to watch the motions of the enemy and furnish intelligence."

**James 1821 on Marion at Lynch's Creek:** "He was rather below the middle stature of men, lean and swarthy. ... his knees and ankles were badly formed; and he still limped upon one leg. ... He was dressed in a close round bodied crimson jacket, of a coarse texture, and wore a leather cap, part of the uniform of the second regiment, with a silver crescent in front, inscribed with the words, 'Liberty or death.'"

**Wemyss to Cornwallis, Cheraw Court House, 20 Sept 1780** (as quoted by SCAR; Cornwallis Papers 2:215): "I have burnt and laid waste about 50 houses and Plantation, mostly belonging to People who have either broke their Paroles or Oath of Allegiance, and are now at arms against us." (Seen only second-hand; not the Saberton volume itself.)

**Cornwallis to Wemyss, 28 Aug 1780** (as quoted in the Clarendon murals, secondary; not verified in Ross): "I should advise your sweeping the country entirely from Kingstree bridge to Pedee, and returning by the Cheraws. I would have you disarm in the most rigid manner, all persons who cannot be depended on and punish the concealment of arms and ammunition with a total demolition of the plantation."

## Action detail blocks

Coordinates are MODERN approximations (my best estimates from gazetteer/Wikipedia points; no marker GPS could be opened). Certainty noted.

### A. Great Savannah / Sumter's house, near Nelson's Ferry
- **Date:** pre-dawn, 25 Aug 1780 (Marion's own letter; James/Simms say 20 Aug, unresolved, see #6).
- **Place:** Sumter's house at the Great Savannah, east side of the Santee, ~6 miles above Nelson's Ferry. Nelson's Ferry crossing (TopoQuest, historical crossing) ~33.431, -80.326 (fair certainty). Great Savannah itself: ~33.47, -80.25 (LOW certainty, within about 10 km; area now Santee NWR / Lake Marion).
- **Forces:** Marion ~70 militia (Hugh Horry's 16 as the road-blocking party; Capt. Benson in the main body). Guard: Capt. Jonathan Roberts, detachment of 63rd Foot, a lieutenant of the Prince of Wales' American Regiment, Tories, "forty regulars and twenty tories" per the captain (ATL: ~38 men); ~150 Maryland-line prisoners from Camden.
- **Terrain:** Santee River (low, swampy), the main Camden-Charleston road on the east bank, Horse Creek pass in the swamp, a planter's house with a yard.
- **Phases (with source):**
  1. Marion destroys the boats at the ferries Murray's to the Santee mouth and drives off ferry guards (ABT-letter).
  2. Scouts report a guard with prisoners resting at Sumter's house (James; ABT-letter).
  3. Night approach through the swamp, force split (James).
  4. Hugh Horry with 16 men sent to seize the road at Horse Creek pass; Marion with the main body goes round to hit the rear (James).
  5. Horry, in the dark, comes too near a sentinel, who fires (James).
  6. Marion's party rushes the house; British muskets are piled at the door and seized (James).
  7. Short fight: 2 guards killed, 5 wounded, a captain (63rd), a Prince of Wales lieutenant, 16 privates, 2 Tories taken (ABT-letter).
  8. 150 Maryland men freed; Americans lose 1 killed, Benson wounded (ABT-letter).
  9. News of Gates's defeat arrives and "struck such a damp on my men" (ABT-letter); freed men desert; Marion gives the officers their servants and paroles the wounded and withdraws to Port's Ferry (ABT-letter; James).

### B. Wemyss's raid and Marion's retreat (Sept 1780)
- **Dates:** orders 28 Aug; raid about 7-20 Sept; Marion in NC about early Sept to mid/late Sept (his 15 Sept letter from White Marsh, NC, via secondary).
- **Places:** Kingstree and Williamsburg Township (Black River, Indiantown Church area), Lynches Creek, Pee Dee, Cheraw Court House (Wemyss's report); Marion: Port's Ferry (Pee Dee, redoubt with two old iron guns), Little Pee Dee, Amis's Mill on Drowning Creek (~34.7 N, -79.3 W, LOW certainty, Lumber River, NC), White Marsh (Bladen Co.).
- **Forces:** Wemyss: his 63rd Foot (about 300, many malarial) + Harrison's Loyalists; James (J): Marion had ~150 men, then 60. Skirmish at Kingstree: Maj. John James reconnoitres, takes prisoners from the column's rear (James; casualty numbers are from McCrady via the murals; UNV).
- **Phases:** 1 Cornwallis orders Wemyss (28 Aug, Clarendon quote); 2 Marion hears of the 63rd above Kingstree, only 150 men, Maj. James counts them (James); 3 Marion meets Maj. James before day; council; retreat ordered; "a hollow groan" (James); 4 retreat to Port's Ferry, recrosses the Pee Dee, ~60 men; two guns hidden in a swamp (James); 5 night/day march to Amis's Mill (James); 6 Wemyss burns Whig houses (about 50, his own count) and hangs Adam Cusack (James; Wemyss letter); 7 Maj. James returns with the news; militia anger; Marion marches back "by a forced march ... sixty miles" in two days (James).

### C. Black Mingo
- **Date:** around midnight, 28-29 Sept 1780.
- **Place:** Dollard's "Red House" tavern at Shepherd's Ferry, south/west side of Black Mingo Creek near Willtown/Rhems; bridge about 1 mile upstream. Wikipedia point 33.62 N, -79.43 W (low, rounded; ~5 km). 
- **Forces:** Marion with Capts John James, Mouzon, Waties, Logan, Cols Hugh Horry/Hugh Giles; Ball's Loyalists ~46-50 (numbers UNV).
- **Phases (James):** 1 Marion learns at Lynches Creek that Capt. Ball is at Black Mingo, 15 miles below; 2 men are "unanimous for battle"; 3 he crosses a boggy causeway and "bridge of planks" a mile above the ferry, near midnight; 4 alarm gun heard in the Tory camp; 5 gallop to the road about 300 yards from the ferry, dismount; 6 Waties's supernumerary officers sent down the road against Dollard's house, Hugh Horry's two companies to the right, cavalry to the left; 7 Ball's men have left the house and are formed in an old field; their first fire is "so severe and unexpected" Horry's men fall back; 8 Capt. John James rallies them; Waties's flank attack, Tories break "after a few rounds" into the swamp; 9 losses "nearly one third" both sides; Logan killed, Mouzon and Scott disabled; 10 Marion takes Ball's horse (footnote) and baggage.

### D. Tearcoat Swamp
- **Date:** night of 24-25 Oct 1780 (attack after midnight).
- **Place:** Tynes's camp "at Tarcote, in the fork of Black river", old muster field. Wikipedia point 33.803, -80.125 (moderate certainty, a few km). Marion crossed the lower ford of the north branch of Black River at Nelson's plantation (James), after riding from Kingstree (Williamsburg) via Witherspoon's Ferry on Lynches Creek, after camping at Port's Ferry (Oct 12-23).
- **Forces:** Marion ~150 (some 7 companies under the Horrys and Maj. James plus Benton's Cheraws men); Tynes ~80-200 Loyalists.
- **Phases:** 1 Marion at Kingstree (24 Oct), officers want to go to Gates, Hugh Horry persuades him to stay (James; murals); 2 scouts report Tynes at Tearcoat (James); 3 hard day's ride, ford Black River at night; 4 scouts say the Loyalists are playing cards, lax guard (Wikipedia pointer); 5 delay to about midnight, three columns; 6 pistol signal, mounted charge; 7 Loyalists flee into the swamp, 6 killed 14 wounded 23 captured, Gaskens killed; 8 80+ muskets/horses taken, Marion loses no man; 9 Tynes's men later join Marion (James: "The most of Tynes' men, soon after joined Gen. Marion, and fought bravely").

- Whether Peter Horry took part at Great Savannah (James says Hugh Horry). 
- Reconciling 20 vs 25 Aug Great Savannah (Marion to P. Horry vs Marion to Gates letters; Marion Papers editors choose 25th). Check Marion Papers pp. 201, 205 or Oller pp. 55-56.
- Exact Black Mingo and Tearcoat troop numbers; Tearcoat marker's "three prongs at midnight" came from search summaries of the Turbeville marker (not opened).
- Date of Marion's march to NC and return (James 28 Aug is too early); Wemyss's 20 Sept letter seen only second-hand.
- Tarleton 1787 page numbers (OCR scrambled), including the line "waylaid by the inhabitants ... under the direction of one Horry" (T87, ch. 2) and the Appendix print of the 3 Dec letter.
- Bass, Rankin, Oller, Crawford not read; no Gemini page number from them was verified.
- Weather: nothing stated in any source; moon phases are my calculation only.
