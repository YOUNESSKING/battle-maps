# FACTCHECK_C: Marion Move 3 (April-Sept 1781: Fort Watson, Greene's letter, Fort Motte, Georgetown, Parker's Ferry, Eutaw Springs)

Checked 2026-11-14 against GEMINI_MARION.md ("S1/S7/S9 p. xx" page numbers in the dossier are NOT checkable and were not trusted). Verdicts: C = CONFIRMED, FIX = CORRECTED, TRAD = TRADITION, UNV = UNVERIFIED.

## Sources actually read (full text, not snippets)
- **Lee** = Henry Lee, *Memoirs of the War in the Southern Department* 1812, vol. II (archive.org `memoirsofwarin02leeh`, OCR; his "Marion's mate" Maham is spelled "Mayham", Fort Watson commander "M'Koy", Motte commander "M'Pherson"). Eyewitness of Fort Watson, Fort Motte and Eutaw.

Francis Marion* (1821), Project Gutenberg **#923** (NOT #9798: that number is "Clarissa Harlowe vol. 2"; the task brief's number is wrong). ## Phase lists (each with its source)

### 1. Fort Watson, 15-23 April 1781 (Americans ~ Marion's 80-150 men plus Lee's ~300 Legion; garrison 114 under Lt McKay; no artillery either side)
1. 14 Apr: Lee joins Marion at the Santee after a rapid march from Ramsay's Mill (8 days) (Marion letter).
2. 15 Apr evening: they invest the fort on its Indian mound beside Scott's Lake; riflemen and Continentals take post between fort and lake to cut the water (Marion, Lee).
3. Day 3 (about 17 Apr): the garrison has "sunk a well near the stockade" (Marion); they have no entrenching tools, and no tree is near enough to give cover (Marion).
4. The attackers despair (Lee: "despaired of success"); Maham proposes a log tower (Lee).
5. Night of 22-23 Apr: working parties cut logs, dragoons fetch axes from nearby farms, the "oblong pen" is raised and floored (Lee); finished before morning of 23 Apr.
6. Morning 23 Apr: riflemen take post in the tower; a musketry party under cover of the riflemen makes a lodgment, supported by Legion infantry with bayonets (Lee); Ensign Baker Johnson and volunteer Lee pull away the abatis (Marion).
7. McKay hangs out a white flag; capitulation signed by Lee and Marion; 114 taken; Americans 2 killed, 6 wounded (Marion; James). Greene hears on the 24th; prisoners reach him that day (Lee).
8. Marion and Lee then turn on Lt Col Watson, who evaded them (Lee; James).

### 2. Fort Motte, 8-12 May 1781 (Marion + Lee's Legion, six-pounder; garrison ~150-165 under McPherson)
1. 7-8 May: Rawdon, reinforced by Watson, comes out at Greene (Lee), Greene retires to Sawney's Creek then Cornal's Creek (Lee).
2. 8 May: Marion and Lee arrive; invest Motte's post: new mansion on a commanding hill, trench and a lofty parapet; Mrs. Motte moved to the old farmhouse (Lee).
3. Six-pounder battery in Marion's quarter, Lee breaks ground in the valley 400 yards off (Lee).
4. 10 May: summons refused; Rawdon evacuates Camden that day (Lee).
5. 11-12 May: Rawdon reaches the Santee hills; his fires seen the night before the surrender (Lee).
6. 12 May near noon: Lee tells Mrs. Motte the house must burn; she gives an Indian bow; second flag to McPherson refused (Lee).
7. Arrows light the roof; McPherson sends men to the loft, the six-pounder drives them down; white flag (Lee).
8. Prisoners taken, the loyalist Smith saved from Marion's men (Lee); a dinner with McPherson at Mrs. Motte's; officers paroled to Rawdon that evening (Lee).

### 3. Georgetown (brief, early June)
Marion marches about 3 June, breaks ground before the town on 6 June, the garrison evacuates by ship that night (James). Marion refits there (new uniform coat, mules).

### 4. Parker's Ferry, 30 August 1781 (Marion about 400 vs about 500-600; Edisto River)
1. Greene sends Marion to help Col. Harden south of the Edisto; Capt. Cooper's diversion near Dorchester and Monck's Corner (James).
2. 27-29 Aug: Marion shadows von Borcke's and Fraser's column (Hessians, British, Loyalists, 80 horse) from Godfrey's Savannah to Hayne Hall (Marion).
3. 30 Aug: the enemy heads for Parker's Ferry on the Edisto.
4. Marion forms an ambuscade within 40 yards of the road in a thick wood, Harden's 80 on the right, Cooper's 60 swordsmen to the rear (Marion).
5. At about sunset a Loyalist party meets his picket, shots, decoy horsemen draw Fraser (Marion; James).
6. Fraser charges down the causeway parallel to the ambush at 40-50 yards; buckshot and ball; horses and men heaped; Fraser's horse killed (James; Marion).
7. The British column with a field piece comes up; Marion holds the road three hours then withdraws two miles, men hungry (Marion).
8. Next morning a burial party is driven back by a superior force with field piece (Marion); Marion joins Greene at Burdell's plantation before Eutaw (James).

### 5. Eutaw Springs, 8 September 1781 (Greene ~2,300, Stewart ~2,000-2,300; very hot; Eutawville)
1. 8 Sep 4 AM: Greene advances in two columns from Burdell's, Lee in front (Lee).
2. ~8 AM: Lee's Legion meets Stewart's rooting parties four miles from the camp, takes ~40 prisoners (Lee; James).
3. Both armies form: American first line = NC militia (Malmedy) and SC militia (Marion and Pickens, on the right) (Lee; James); second line Continentals; Washington's horse and Kirkwood reserve; British Buffs, Cruger, 63rd and 64th in one line, Marjoribanks in the thicket, Coffin's cavalry (Lee; James).
4. First line advances, firing; Marion's marksmen "galling the enemy"; the NC militia give way and Sumner's Continentals fill the gap (Lee; James; Simms for "seventeen rounds").
5. The Marylanders and Virginians charge with bayonets; the British break; Americans enter the camp (Lee).
6. Washington's horse repulsed at the blackjack thicket; Sheridan takes the brick house (Lee; James).
7. Greene's guns fail against the house; he withdraws to water (Lee; James); Marion and Lee sent next day by a circuit to cut the road below Eutaw.
8. 9 Sep evening: Stewart burns stores, leaves ~70 wounded, retreats toward Monck's Corner; M'Arthur joins; 10 Sep reunited at Martin's Tavern; Marion and Lee fail to strike (Lee; James).
