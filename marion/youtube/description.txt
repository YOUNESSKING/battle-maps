In 1780, Britain's grip on the South hung on one road, across the Santee River at Nelson's Ferry. There, before dawn, seventy men on horseback set a hundred and fifty American prisoners free under the guns of their British guard.

This is how Francis Marion, the Swamp Fox, kept the war alive in South Carolina after Charleston and Camden, told on animated painted maps: the night raids from the Great Savannah to Black Mingo and Tearcoat Swamp, the escape from Banastre Tarleton's British Legion into the swamps in November 1780, and the campaign with Henry Lee that broke the British chain of forts, from Hezekiah Maham's log tower at Fort Watson to the burning roof of Fort Motte, Parker's Ferry and Eutaw Springs.

Strike in the dark. Make the enemy fight the swamp. Then hit the chain with the army.

Which was Marion's greatest move: the night raids, the escape from Tarleton, or the tower and the fire? Tell us in the comments, and tell us which commander we should cover next.

ABOUT THE PICTURES OF MARION
No portrait of Francis Marion made in his lifetime is known. Every image of him in this video, and on the thumbnail, is a later artist's work, painted or drawn long after his death.
