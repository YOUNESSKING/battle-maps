Nathanael Greene's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves | The Man Who Drove the British Out of the South

In the spring of 1780, the whole American army in the South was trapped inside one city, Charleston: five thousand soldiers surrendered in one day. Then one general lost almost every battle he fought in the South, and drove the British out anyway.

Nathanael Greene took command of 1,500 starving men against 8,000 British. In this video we break down his three greatest tactical moves on animated maps: the Race to the Dan, where he led Lord Cornwallis two hundred miles across North Carolina to a flooded river with no boats; Guilford Courthouse, where he let the British win a battle that cost them more than a quarter of their army; and the reconquest of the South, from Hobkirk's Hill and Ninety Six to Eutaw Springs. Seven months after Guilford, Cornwallis surrendered at Yorktown.

Make the land fight for you. Make every victory cost more than it's worth. And never lose the army.
