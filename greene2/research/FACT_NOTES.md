## Hook claims
- Hook v2 (owner 2026-10-27: "match the new rules"): line 1 "Five thousand American soldiers, surrendered in one day" = the Charleston capitulation, 12 May 1780 (siege 29 Mar - 12 May; the surrender itself was one day). Twist = odds as action: ~1,500 fit for duty (Buchanan: 1,482) vs ~8,000 British + loyalists in the South (Buchanan via Gemini); "many without shoes" (Greene's letters on his naked, barefoot army, Dec 1780) **(memory)**; "refused a commission because he limped" = Kentish Guards 1774 (below).
- "Two American armies, wiped out in three months": Charleston surrendered 12 May 1780 (Lincoln's army, ~5,000+ captured, often given as 5,266 incl. militia/sailors **(memory)**); Camden 16 Aug 1780, Gates's army routed. 12 May -> 16 Aug = ~3 months. "Wiped out" = captured / routed.
- "Two-hundred-mile chase": Gemini (Buchanan) "some two hundred miles" from the Catawba region to the Dan.
- "Cost them more than a quarter of their army": Guilford British losses 532 of ~1,924-2,000 (~27 %).
- "Seven months later, that army surrendered at Yorktown": Guilford 15 Mar 1781 -> Yorktown 19 Oct 1781 = 7 months. Cornwallis's Yorktown army included the Guilford regiments (Guards, 23rd, 33rd, 71st, Hessians von Bose).
- "Six years earlier, he was a private ... wouldn't make him an officer, because he limped" **(memory)**: Greene joined the Kentish Guards (East Greenwich, RI, chartered Oct 1774) and served as a private after members objected to his limp as an officer. Six years before his Dec 1780 command.
- "The Quakers had thrown him out" **(memory)**: in 1773 the East Greenwich Monthly Meeting **put him out of meeting** (suspended/disowned) after he attended a military parade / public gathering. "Thrown out" is the plain-words version.
- "Never seen a battle" (before 1775): true; his first combat was the 1776 New York campaign **(memory)**.

## Move 1 (Race to the Dan)
- Greene took command at Charlotte, 2-3 Dec 1780; ~1,482 fit for duty (Buchanan via Gemini) -> "about fifteen hundred".
- "More than eight thousand regulars and loyalists in the South": Gemini (Buchanan), theatre-wide figure.
- Split army: Morgan sent west (Dec 1780); Greene to Cheraw. Cowpens 17 Jan 1781.
- Ramsour's Mill baggage burning 25-27 Jan 1781, ~2,500 regulars after (Buchanan).
- Greene rode ~125 miles from Cheraw with a small escort to join Morgan (late Jan) **(memory: often "about 125 miles"; script says "a hundred miles")**.
- Cowan's Ford, 1 Feb 1781: British forced the Catawba; Brig. Gen. William Lee Davidson killed **(memory)**.
- Trading Ford, Yadkin, 2-3 Feb: Americans crossed by boat; river rose; British cannonade across the river **(memory)**.
- Reunion at Guilford Courthouse 7-9 Feb; council of war 9 Feb voted against battle **(memory)**; Williams's light corps ~700.
- Boats: Greene had Edward Carrington survey the Dan and collect boats (Dec 1780) **(memory)**; script says "officers".
- Last 24 h: light corps marched ~40 miles; "six hours' sleep in forty-eight" (Lee's memoirs, via Gemini/Buchanan).
- Crossing 13-14 Feb at Boyd's and Irwin's ferries; British reached the Dan 15 Feb; Cornwallis turned back to Hillsborough.
- O'Hara letter (to the Duke of Grafton, Apr 1781): "without baggage, necessaries, or provisions of any sort for officer or soldier, in the most barren, inhospitable, unhealthy part of North America" **(memory; script paraphrases, on-screen headline uses only the verbatim fragment)**.

## Move 2 (Guilford Courthouse, 15 Mar 1781)
- Forces: US ~4,400 (≈1,400-1,600 Continentals, rest militia); British ~1,900-2,100 (Babits & Howard / Buchanan via Gemini).
- New Garden morning clash, Lee vs Tarleton **(memory)**.
- Three lines: NC militia at the rail fence, VA militia in the woods, Continentals near the courthouse; ~300-500 yards apart (Gemini "roughly 400 yards"; script says "a few hundred yards").
- "Fire two volleys, then fall back": Greene's request, borrowed from Morgan at Cowpens.
- "One half of the Highlanders dropped on that spot": Capt. Dugald Stuart, 71st, later letter **(memory; script: "one British officer later wrote")**.
- Casualties: British 93 killed, 413 wounded, 26 missing = 532 (Cornwallis's return) **(memory, matches Gemini range 500-600)**; American 79 killed, 185 wounded (Greene's return) **(memory, matches Gemini 250-300)**, plus ~1,000 militia "missing" who went home.
- Wounded left at the New Garden meeting house when Cornwallis marched for Cross Creek / Wilmington **(memory)**.
- Fox in the Commons: "Another such victory would ruin the British army" (Gemini; widely quoted).

## Move 3 (Reconquest, Apr-Sep 1781)
- Hobkirk's Hill 25 Apr 1781: Rawdon attacked, Greene driven back.
- Fort Watson: Maham tower, surrendered 23 Apr (Marion + Lee) **(memory)**; Orangeburg 11 May; Fort Motte 12 May; Augusta 5 Jun; Camden evacuated 10 May **(memory)**.
- Ninety Six: siege 22 May - 19 Jun (28 days), assault 18 Jun failed, Greene withdrew as Rawdon's relief approached; British evacuated Ninety Six in July **(memory)**.
- "We fight, get beat, rise, and fight again": Greene to La Luzerne, 1781 (Gemini VERIFIED).
- Summer camp in the High Hills of Santee (Jul-Aug 1781) **(memory)**.
- Eutaw Springs 8 Sep 1781: US ~2,200 (often 2,092), British ~2,000 (Stewart) (Golway via Gemini).
- Rooting party digging sweet potatoes captured (Gemini, standard accounts).
- "Seventeen rounds": Greene's report praising the militia **(memory; script: "by Greene's own account")**.
- William Washington wounded and captured at the thicket.
- Casualties: American 139 killed, 375 wounded, 8 missing (≈522); British 85 killed, 351 wounded, 257 missing (≈693, Stewart's return) **(memory; Gemini: US ~500, British ~700-800)**.
- Stewart fell back toward Charleston (Monck's Corner) on 9 Sep.
- Yorktown 19 Oct 1781 = 6 weeks after Eutaw. By Dec 1781 (Wilmington evacuated Nov 1781) the British in the Deep South held only Charleston and Savannah.
