Method: Wikipedia pages fetched/searched (summaries by a fetch tool, so exact wording not read in full), plus my background knowledge. Items marked [NOT RE-VERIFIED] come from memory/secondary hits and still need a primary check (Glantz/House, Zetterling/Frankson, "A Soldier's Duty" text). Gemini's page numbers are unusable; ignore them.

## 1. Biography
- Birth "21 Dec 1896, Polish-born": DISPUTED. Wikipedia: born 21 Dec 1896 (his Soviet papers) but sources give Velikiye Luki, Warsaw or Telekhany; he himself claimed Velikiye Luki. 1894 appears in some documents. Safest line: "born around 1896 (some records say 1894), Polish family, Warsaw by most accounts". Gemini's "died 3 Aug 1968 (aged 44-47)" ok for death (3 Aug 1968), ages 44-47 ok if 1896.
- Arrest 17 Aug 1937, release 22 Mar 1940: OK. Wikipedia. Charge: spying for Poland/Japan. Released via Timoshenko's efforts (secondary).
- Torture: PARTLY SUPPORTED, WEAK SOURCING. Wikipedia: broken/denailed fingers, pulled teeth, cracked ribs, mock shootings at night. Great-granddaughter Ariadna Rokossovskaya (family tradition, via gw2ru.com article): front teeth knocked out, hammer on toes, ribs broken, twice taken out to be shot with blanks (1939). Fingernails: appears in Wikipedia's wording only; the family version lists teeth/ribs/toes. Script wording: "reportedly beaten, teeth and ribs broken, mock executions". Do not say "toes" and "fingernails" as fact. Gemini's first-person "I was so beaten..." quote: UNSUPPORTED as a verbatim quote (family retelling).
- "Released because Stalin ran out of generals": UNSUPPORTED/oversimplified; released Mar 1940, before the war, after Timoshenko/Soviet-Finnish-war era reappraisals; did not get an army until 1941.
- 9th Mech Corps June 1941 (35th, 20th Tank, 131st Motorised Divs), fought near Lutsk/Dubno-Rovno: OK (Wikipedia Rokossovsky). 
- 16th Army: CORRECTED. Second formation created 10 Aug 1941 (Yartsevo area) under Rokossovsky; Wikipedia's Rokossovsky page says he was appointed in Sept 1941 in Moscow context; he commanded it Aug 1941 - 15 Jul 1942 (then Bryansk Front). Use "summer 1941 (Smolensk/Yartsevo), then Volokolamsk Oct". 

## 2. Moscow 1941
- Dates: Gemini "Oct-Nov": CORRECTED. 16th Army defended the Volokolamsk highway mid-Oct to early Dec; main fighting 16-Nov onward (German 2nd-phase offensive began 15-16 Nov), Soviet counteroffensive from 5-6 Dec; Volokolamsk retaken 20 Jan 1942 [NOT RE-VERIFIED, Gemini/Brief "20 Dec" is wrong, as Istra/Solnechnogorsk fell ~11-12 Dec]. 
- Istra story: CORRECTED. Wikipedia Rokossovsky: on 18 Nov 1941 Rokossovsky asked Zhukov to withdraw behind the Istra reservoir, Zhukov refused; Rokossovsky appealed to Shaposhnikov, who approved; Zhukov revoked it (source = memoir). So not "mid-November secretly over Zhukov's head then forced back": he asked Zhukov first. Result: 16th Army stayed west; Germans crossed the frozen reservoir ~25 Nov (tankarchives/16th Army page); by late Nov army pushed back to Krasnaya Polyana - Kryukovo - Istra line (Wikipedia 16th Army) where it held. Gemini "stabilised at Kryukovo" ok. Gemini's "heavy losses due to cancelled retreat" is Rokossovsky's own framing; Zhukov's view differs. Istra dam blown by Soviets (flooding to slow Germans, worldwater.org), not Germans [NOT RE-VERIFIED in detail]. 
- Closest approach: a German recon battalion reached Khimki on 2 Dec, ~30 km from Kremlin (Wikipedia Battle of Moscow). Krasnaya Polyana ~ 25-30 km also cited.
- Forces: 16th Army "60-80k": UNSUPPORTED number (at Oct-Nov probably 30-45k with 316th Rifle, 18th, 78th (Belobrodov, Siberian) divisions, Dovator's 3rd Cav Corps, 1st Guards Tank / 4th Tank brigade of Katukov [NOT RE-VERIFIED]). Germans: 4th Panzer Group (Hoepner) with V Army Corps, XL, XLVI Panzer Corps, 2nd Panzer Div etc. against the highway (also 3rd Pz Group north); "100,000+ men, 400 tanks" UNSUPPORTED.
- Siberian divisions: PARTLY OK: 78th Rifle (Belobrodov) was Far Eastern/Siberian, arrived Oct-Nov in 16th Army; Gemini's "fresh Siberian reserves" counterattacking on 5 Dec is garbled; the Stavka moved 18+ divisions from Siberia/Far East overall (Wikipedia Battle of Moscow).
- Temperature: Gemini "-30F": CORRECTED. Wikipedia: Soviet records lowest Dec temp -28.8 C (-20 F); 130,000+ German frostbite cases. Colder than -30 C reported around 4-7 Dec; -30F is mild. Use C.
- Hoepner: CORRECTED. Dismissed 8 Jan 1942 for ordering withdrawal without Hitler's approval (Kluge reported him); stripped of pension. Not for the November halt. Gemini says "halted and dismissed" - fine if dated Jan 1942.
- Katukov: Mtsensk ambushes (Oct 1941) were by 4th Tank Brigade (became 1st Guards Tank Brigade 11 Nov), before joining 16th Army [NOT RE-VERIFIED here; widely documented]. 
- Panfilov's 28: Gemini broadly OK, with nuance: 1948 military prosecutor probe (Afanasyev) found it invented by Red Star journalists Koroteev/Krivitsky/Ortenberg, five of the "28" survived (e.g. Dobrobabin); Politburo buried findings; 2015 Russian archive release repeated it. Real fighting at Dubosekovo 16 Nov 1941 did occur. The 316th was in Rokossovsky's army. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Dubosekovo ; https://themoscowtimes.com/news/russian-archives-cast-doubt-on-legends-of-soviet-war-heroes-48026
- "Zhukov saved Moscow / Rokossovsky's elastic defence instrumental ... in defiance": DISPUTED/overstated; Rokossovsky was one of four army commanders; he mostly complied with Zhukov, and the "elastic" request was refused.

## 3. Kursk north (Ponyri)
- Central Front 711,575 men (510,983 combat) and 9th Army 335,000 (223,000 combat): OK (Glantz/House via search results; https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Kursk_salient and phdn.org review). Tanks 1,785 and German 730 tanks/assault guns [NOT RE-VERIFIED]; Gemini's figures plausible.
- Counter-preparation: CORRECTED. Wikipedia: ~02:00 on 5 July, Zhukov (Stavka rep) ordered it with Rokossovsky; Rokossovsky memoir/Russian sources: done "in agreement with Zhukov", ~10 min ahead of Germans. Germans began 05:00 (Gemini 05:30 wrong in minutes; north barrage 80 min). Effect: Wikipedia says it "failed to disrupt schedule or inflict substantial losses"; German assault delayed ~2.5 h only. So do not say "minutes before the German attack": it was hours (~3 h). Gemini's "from captured sappers" (Hungarian/German deserter intel) [NOT RE-VERIFIED].
- Belts: CORRECTED. Wikipedia: six belts on either side of Kursk, 130-150 km deep (whole system); Central Front "three heavily fortified belts" in its sector. "200 miles deep" = WRONG.
- Penetration: Wikipedia: on day 1 XLVII Panzer Corps penetrated ~9.7 km; later fighting 7-10 July over Ponyri/Olkhovatka; German advance 12-15 km max by 10-11 July. Gemini "10-12 km" OK. Olkhovatka (not just Ponyri) was Model's main target; 300 German tanks attacked on 10 Jul.
- Ponyri: 6-9/10 July see-saw (station, tractor depot, school, water tower); Germans held half the town; "Stalingrad of the Kursk salient": Paul Carell phrase (Wikipedia). Gemini "Ponyri 5-12 July" fine for whole northern phase.
- Ferdinands: 653rd Heavy Panzerjager Bn, 45 Ferdinands, "all but 12 immobilised by mines before 17:00" on 5 July (Wikipedia). 654th too. Tigers: 505th Heavy Tank Bn destroyed 69 Soviet tanks in 16th Tank Corps counterattack on 6 July.
- Operation Kutuzov 12 July (Orel): OK; Model halted by ~10-11 July and shifted forces.
- Casualties: CORRECTED. Central Front 33,897 (15,336 irrecoverable + 18,561 sanitary) per Krivosheev, Wikipedia Battle of Kursk. Gemini "33,859 killed + 59,000 wounded" WRONG: the 33,897 is total. 9th Army: ~20,000-23,000 for 5-11 July [NOT RE-VERIFIED]; one secondary source says ~10,000 casualties in 48 h. 
- Predicted the axis: broadly OK. Rokossovsky placed his main strength (13th Army, 70th, 2nd Tank Army) on the Olkhovatka-Ponyri axis; Soviet commanders (Rokossovsky vs Vatutin, Voronezh Front) differed; Rokossovsky correct on north, Vatutin less so. Not "exactly" - he put ~45-50% of resources there on the right guess.

## 4. Bagration
- Force: CORRECTED. Gemini "1.2M" UNSUPPORTED; Wikipedia gives 490-495k for the Bobruisk offensive proper; 1st BF total approx 1.07M [Glantz, NOT RE-VERIFIED]. Whole operation: ~1.67M personnel, 5,818 tanks, 7,799 aircraft. Germans: 9th Army (Jordan) ~90-100k at Bobruisk; Army Group Centre only 495 operational tanks/AGs.
- Dates: DISPUTED. Wikipedia: Bagration began 22 June (recon in force 21-22 Jun) on 3 fronts; Bobruisk offensive 23-28 June; Rokossovsky's main attacks 24 June [Glantz; NOT RE-VERIFIED]. Gemini "23 south, 24 north" muddled: both prongs (northern: 3rd & 48th Armies from Rogachev; southern: 65th & 28th Armies with Pliev's cavalry-mechanised group from Parichi/Ozarichi) attacked ~24 June, the south through marshes. Swamp crossing was done by Pliev's group, 1st Guards Tank Corps, infantry, not "tank armies erupting". Encirclement closed 27 June (Wikipedia). Bobruisk taken 29 June [NOT RE-VERIFIED].
- Pocket: Wikipedia: ~50,000 killed, 20,000 captured, 12,000 escaped; pocket held five divisions of XXXV Corps + elements of XXXXI Pz Corps and 20th Pz Div. Gemini "70,000 trapped" is plausible upper bound; typical "~40,000 trapped" also quoted. Say "tens of thousands (~30,000-70,000 depending on source)". Soviet losses in Bobruisk offensive: 7,061 killed (Wikipedia, probably partial).
- Busch relieved 28 June, Model replaced: OK. Jordan also relieved 28 June (Wikipedia Bobruysk), replaced by Nikolaus von Vormann. 
- Marshal promotion: 29 June 1944 [NOT RE-VERIFIED; Wikipedia says "June 1944"]. 
- Two blows: DISPUTED but mostly plausible. Wikipedia: Rokossovsky proposed Bobruisk AND Slutsk equally, stubbornly insisted; Stalin sent him out to "think it over" three times, then said "Your confidence speaks for your sound judgement." Wording "Two main blows, Comrade Stalin" is paraphrase, not documented verbatim. Zhukov (memoir/letter) disputed: "of the two strikes cannot both be the main ones", accused R of embellishment; Telegin and Bagramyan partly back R; Vasilevsky closer to Zhukov. Some historians (Glantz) point out the final plan from 22-23 May did include two blows on right wing. URL: https://community.timeghost.tv/t/rokossovskys-argument-did-it-happen-operation-bagration/9505 (forum summary: use only as pointer). Gemini "May 1944" ok (22-23 May). Gemini "repeated three times" OK (memoir).
- Corduroy/swamp shoes: plausible, widely cited (mokrostupy are not well documented in my fetches) [NOT RE-VERIFIED]. 
- Losses: Frieser 399,102 German (26,397 killed, 109,776 wounded, 262,929 missing) vs Glantz/House ~450,000 vs Zaloga 300-375k (Wikipedia Bagration). Gemini ~400k OK but for whole operation, not Rokossovsky alone. Soviet: Wikipedia lists Krivosheev 539,480 killed/missing/captured?? and Glantz/House 770,888 total (~550k combat), the commonly cited Krivosheev total is 178,507 irrecoverable + 587,308 sanitary = 765,815 [NOT RE-VERIFIED; the Wikipedia summary I got conflicts]. Gemini's "65k killed/150k wounded for the Front": UNSUPPORTED. 
- POW parade Moscow 17 July 1944: ~57,000 prisoners (Operation "Big Waltz"); not in my fetched text, [NOT RE-VERIFIED] but well known.
- Stalin using name-patronymic: UNSUPPORTED as claimed. His patronymic "Konstantinovich" was his own choice (real father Xavery/Ksawery); Stalin addressed senior generals formally anyway. No source ties it to Bagration. Drop it or say "Stalin addressed him by name and patronymic" only if from a memoir. (Wikipedia Rokossovsky notes real patronymic; real-life hero and tracesofwar pages say he adopted Konstantinovich.)
- "Southern prong vs Ukraine": German expectation of main attack in Ukraine: OK (Hitler/Busch), per standard histories.

## 5. Quotes
- "Two main blows, Comrade Stalin": paraphrase of memoir anecdote; memoir quote: "Making sure that I firmly insisted on our point of view, Stalin approved the plan." Use paraphrase.
- "The German army is a machine, and machines can be broken!": appears in Wikipedia's Rokossovsky quotes list, but unsourced to a date; treat as UNSUPPORTED/legend.
- Safer quotes (Wikipedia): Stalin to him: "Your confidence speaks for your sound judgement" (memoir); Smolensk: "Our activity apparently puzzled the enemy command, which encountered resistance where it was not expected."; Stalingrad report 2 Feb 1943.
- "Suvorov of the 20th century", "Kostya": UNSUPPORTED in my searches. Also "Zhukov brute-force vs Rokossovsky maneuver rivalry": interpretive.
