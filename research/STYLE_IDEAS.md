### 3. A re-hook every ~3 min: a 5-8 s cold open at the start of each move
- **Idea:** Every move opens with its own climax first: 5-8 s of the move's biggest beat (Sherman: Atlanta's ammunition trains exploding at 6:00; Fort McAllister stormed in 15 min at 10:50; the Bentonville swamp charge at 14:00), then "But four months earlier…" and the move's title card. Put one open-loop line inside each move as well ("He would call it the mistake of his life. Not yet.").
- **Why:** [SOURCE] Loewenstein's information-gap theory: curiosity is a felt gap between what we know and what we want to know, and it drives people to seek the answer (Psychological Bulletin 1994). Kang et al. (2009) found that curiosity raises attention and memory for the answer. The MrBeast guide plans a "re-engagement" about every 3 minutes. [DATA] Our dips sit at the seams: Morgan -4.7 points at 6:35, Thomas -2.5 at 7:25, both right at a move boundary, where viewers "feel a chapter break" (owner's report). [JUDGEMENT] TG's Patton and Zhukov videos make each act an escalation. A cold open makes that escalation visible.
- **Where:** Sherman 2:15 (move 1), 7:01 (move 2), 11:30 (move 3). Also right after the subscribe line (6:47-7:00), where the risk is highest.
- **Lock:** Adds a beat to the script formula (HANDOVER §1). Visuals are all existing kit (K.impact, stamps, night). The flash-forward has to reuse frames from that move's scene, so no new map.
- **Effort:** M (script lines + ~6 s of extra animation per move, or re-use of rendered frames with a "LATER" tag).
- **Test:** A 1-min clip of the move-2 seam (subscribe line → cold open → title card) against the current seam. After publishing, compare the dip size at each seam with Morgan/Thomas (target ≤ 1.5 points).

### 4. Static-stretch detector + "breathing camera"
- **Idea:** (a) A QA tool (~30 lines of Python, the same method used for this report: ffmpeg 2 fps, 96×54 greyscale, mean frame difference) that runs on any 540p preview or scene render and lists every stretch ≥ 5 s below a set level of motion, the way the snapshot sheet lists beats. (b) The default fix for a flagged hold is a slow push-in (5-8 % scale over the hold) or a slow lateral drift. Cards that hold more than 6 s (E.decide, RESULT, method card) get a subtle life sign: the map behind them keeps drifting, the counter ticks, the "TELL US" ticks pulse.
- **Why:** [SOURCE] Cutting et al. (Cornell, 150 films): attention follows the *rhythm* of change, not raw cutting speed. [JUDGEMENT] Our maps fail by holding still too long, not by moving too slowly. [DATA] The rule "never more than 4 s without a change" already exists (STYLE_LOCK 1a) but is only checked for the hook. Sherman has 9 nearly static stretches, all after 7:40. The Morgan/Thomas mid-video decline is about 1 point/min, and no-change holds are the cheapest place to win part of that back.
- **Where:** Sherman 7:41-7:53 (WHAT WOULD YOU DO?, 12 s frozen), 9:33-9:42 (bummers band), 9:56-10:13, 11:13-11:21 (SAVANNAH TAKEN card), 12:57-13:05.
- **Lock:** None. Camera moves and living-map motion are in the locked kit. Push-ins are already "Ken Burns" on archive.
- **Effort:** S (tool) + S per fix.
- **Test:** Run the tool on Sherman's move 2 and fix two holds. Show the owner a 1-min before/after clip of 7:40-8:40.

### 5. Archive punch-ins (3-6 s) inside the moves

Sherman candidates (public domain): Barnard's *Photographic Views of Sherman's Campaign* (1866): the Atlanta rail-yard ruins, Allatoona Pass, Columbia and Charleston ruins; Savannah street views; the Fort McAllister interior. - **Why:** [DATA] Sherman is 2.3 % archive against the plan's ~20 %, and all of it sits in one slot. [JUDGEMENT] A real photo every 2-3 minutes resets attention and proves "this happened", which maps alone cannot. Epic History's long-standing format mixes maps with archive images for the same reason (Military History Now, 2016).

## 2. Do NOT do (common advice that would hurt this niche)
1. **"Cut every 1-2 seconds" / jump-cut editing, zoom-punch on every sentence.** Our audience skews older and watches on TVs (Nielsen: 65+ viewing +96 % in two years), and our maps need time to read. Cutting's research says the *rhythm* matters, not a faster mean. Our rule is "a change every 2-4 s", not "a cut".
2. **TikTok-style burned-in word-by-word captions.** They clutter the maps, and Mayer's redundancy principle says full on-screen text that repeats the voice hurts understanding. Upload SRT instead (idea 7).
3. **"Remove the face from the thumbnail"** (a viral vidIQ claim). The 1of10 data says it depends on the niche, and every TG winner has the general's portrait. Test pictures, keep the face.
4. **Clickbait that the video doesn't pay within 30 s.** Sister channel: CTR 1.7 % when thumbnail and title disagreed. Thomas: the promise paid at 7:37 and viewers left. Test & compare also scores on watch time, so a misleading thumbnail loses the test anyway.
5. **AI-generated "photos" of real people or events, or AI-animated archive faces.** This is a credibility niche (comments check names and facts). It breaks the owner's "never a fake likeness" rule and raises synthetic-content disclosure questions.
6. **Louder music or more SFX "for energy".** Music at 0.35 was rejected, and whooshes are restricted to the 2-3 biggest moves. More layers mask the narration, which carries the story.
7. **Dropping the "What would you do?" card or the final question because "CTAs hurt retention".** The data shows the *subscribe* phrase costs points. The decision card is story, and the final question drives comments (TG Patton: 670 comments on 752K views).
8. **Padding to 20+ min for watch time.** TG's videos above ~20 min underperform (Manstein, MacArthur, Wellington). Aim for 16-19 min of real content (Sherman's 15:46 should grow with a human beat per move, not filler).
9. **Globe/3D tilt openings, flashy transitions, LUT "teal-orange" grading.** Already rejected or off-brand. The dark Frontlines grade is a known asset.
10. **Trusting single-vendor percentages** ("chapters +64 %", "faces +33 %"). Treat them as hypotheses and confirm on our own curves (the scorecard in HANDOVER §10).
