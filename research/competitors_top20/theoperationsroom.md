## 5. Format

Transcripts: Desert Storm Air War Day 1 (22:00, 14.0M views) and Midway (15:29, 7.5M).
- **Hook**: Desert Storm opens with a cold open that is a promise with a clock: "seven B-52s take off from Barksdale... in 35 hours when they return a war will have started and they will have set a record for the longest bombing raid" (0:00-0:16), then straight into ~100 s of background (oil prices, Kuwait, Desert Shield) before H-hour at ~3:00. Midway has no cold open: it starts with the strategic situation ("in June 1942 the war is going very well for Japan"), the plan and the codebreaking until the first PBY sighting at ~3:12. Both name the subject in the first sentence and the title promise is delivered by ~3 min.
- **Structure**: strictly chronological, clock-stamped ("2:38 am H-hour", "at 7:48 a.m. the next wave arrives"), built from 60-90 s self-contained mini-actions (an Apache strike, one F-15 kill, one Tornado crew captured). Numbers on almost every sentence (154 SAM sites, 16,000 missiles, 27 Hellfires, 4,000 cannon rounds). Ends with a hard results paragraph (sorties, losses) and a one-line teaser to the next video (ground war on 24 Feb). No spoken subscribe/CTA in either transcript.
- **Narration**: single neutral male voice, ~150-170 words/min (estimated from timestamps), sound-effects and music under it, no jokes, no first person.
- **Visuals**: the whole point of the channel: 3D/2D animated maps and unit models ("Animated" in every title), plus archive stills.
- **Length / cadence**: 2019-22 videos 7-23 min single-battle (the big winners); since 2023 mostly 30-48 min parts of 2-4 part series plus 1-4.5 h "Complete Animated Documentary" compilations (Iwo Jima 1h 4.55M, Leyte Gulf 2h 2.48M, Bulge 2h58 1.30M, Iraq 4h26 1.22M). Currently about one upload per week (Fri/Sat 16-20 UTC).

## 6. What works here

1. One recognisable subject + 1-5 words, channel name on every thumbnail.
2. Plain event title + year + `- Animated`: the format is the promise.
3. Clock-stamped chronology of short vignettes with numbers; hard results at the end.
4. Open with a concrete event + deadline ('in 35 hours a war will have started') rather than the backstory.
5. Famous Western-audience topics (Desert Storm, Midway, Black Hawk Down, Bismarck, Hiroshima) beat niche ones by 5-10x.


## 7. What fails here

- Multi-part series where part 2/3 repeats the thumbnail style: the audience drops 15-50 %.
- Obscure episodes (New Georgia, Bay of Pigs, weather report, UFO satellite) at 0.3-0.5x of median.
- Busy map thumbnails with 6-7 word captions and small icons.


## 8. Three things OUR channel can copy or must avoid

1. Copy the clock-stamped vignette structure: each of our 3 moves can be 4-6 time-stamped micro-actions (60-90 s) with one number each, ending on a hard results line, then a one-line teaser. Matches our casualty/result card rule.
2. Copy the cold-open-with-a-clock hook for move 1 ("in 35 hours..." style) but with OUR rule (stakes of this story, ending on the place name) rather than 100 s of background; Ops Room's 100 s background is the one thing the data does not defend.
3. Avoid splitting a general into Part 1/Part 2; keep ~16 min single video (Ops Room series show the 2nd part loses up to half the audience) and avoid obscure subjects when thumbnails must carry one image: test a single iconic frame (unit + commander portrait) with <=5 words before a busy map render.
