## 2. What drives views

**2a. Which general/era works.** The four winners (>= 1.9x) are Napoleon and three WW2 Eastern Front figures (Zhukov, Model, Manstein), average 598k. The rest: Rommel 250k, Suvorov 232k, Caesar 229k, Alexander 143k, Lee 43k (average 179k). Eastern Front WW2 is 3 of 3 hits, and it is where all the biggest comment counts sit (Zhukov 1,987 comments, 24k likes; Model 1,467 comments, 21k likes). Ancient generals are mid (Caesar 229k, Alexander 143k). Napoleon is the one outlier outside WW2, and he has by far the biggest search/suggest demand of any name.

**2b. Napoleon 815k vs Lee 43k (19x).** Honest reading, several causes stacked, we cannot isolate one:
1. Name demand: Napoleon is a top-3 history search name worldwide; Lee is a US-only Civil War niche and the Civil War audience on YouTube is older and already served by bigger channels.
2. Length: Napoleon is the shortest video (11:09), Lee is 20:38. Length vs views across the 9 has a correlation of -0.41 (short ones: Napoleon 815k, Rommel 250k; long ones: Lee 43k, Suvorov 232k). But Manstein (22:02) is a hit, so length alone is not it; topic and thumbnail matter more.
3. Thumbnail: Lee's is the weakest of the nine: dark navy/brown, low contrast, small face at the right edge, tiny flag icons, the headline "LINCOLN's NIGHTMARE" does not say what the video is (no "Top 3"). Napoleon's has a huge blue-glowing face left and clean blue/red arrows on a lighter map plus "Top 3 MOVES" in big letters.
4. Timing: Lee came out in April 2026 right after the Alexander/Caesar mid-performers, i.e. when the channel was not hot (see DATA_RULES). Model, two months later, got 497k from the same small channel, so the channel was able to be hot.
5. Lee's like ratio (4.0 % of views) is normal for the channel (Napoleon 1.8 %, Zhukov 4.1 %, Model 4.2 %, Manstein 1.2 %), so this is not a weak-viewer signal, simply too few impressions: vph 5.2 for Lee vs 82 for Napoleon.
(We do not have their impressions/CTR/retention; this is inferred from public numbers.)

**2c. Newest video and "views per day".** Manstein: 482k in 19 days = 25.4k/day average. The 2026-10-30 scan saw 395,628 at 11 days (36.0k/day), so days 11-19 added 86.5k = 10.8k/day: already decaying fast (about 3x lower per day). Expect roughly 600-700k at day 60 if the decay continues. The 25k/day figure is from the first week; the stable rate is ~5-10k/day.

**2d. What the "(No AI)" label does.** It is in the title of the 3 newest hits except Suvorov (Zhukov Dec 2025, Model Jun 2026, Manstein Oct 2026); Suvorov (Aug 2026) skipped it and fell to 0.93x. Average views with label 525k vs without 285k (but Napoleon 815k has no label). Evidence it is a response to the audience: the Zhukov video (first with the label) has the highest like count and comment count. The thumbnails do NOT carry the label or any other AI/no-AI text, so it works only as a title trust tag (what it asserts is unclear: probably narration and art made by a human; the channel's own art is colourised photos and game-style maps, and our owner-view of the three videos' visuals cannot be seen from transcripts). Caution: label and topic are confounded (all three are WW2 Eastern Front hits), so we cannot say the label itself added views. It does cost nothing and signals "real narrator" in a feed full of AI-voice war channels; **we use an AI voice (Kokoro) and should not copy the label unless we can truthfully say it**.

**2e. Length vs views.** Lengths 11-24 min; hits at 11:09 (815k), 15:45, 19:01, 22:02. No clean relation: short and long both work. The channel moved from 11 min (2025) to 19-24 min (2026) and views per video stayed in the 230-600k band. Total time watched per viewer is therefore much higher on the 22-minute Manstein video.

**2f. Upload cadence.** 9 long videos in 17 months, 1 every ~56 days on average (gaps 29-117 d; last four gaps 66, 60, 52, 83, 52 d). No shorts counted here. Slow cadence but each video is a full 15-24 min production; the channel is hot enough that each one still lands 230-600k. We upload faster than this.

## 3. Thumbnails (looked at the sheet)
Every thumbnail has the same recipe: **one big colourised/painted general (100 % face, always facing the map or the viewer), full-frame saturated map with big arrows and unit counters (100 % map), 2-6 big white or cream outlined capital words (100 % text, average 3.2 words)**. Winners vs rest:
- Winners (Napoleon, Zhukov, Model, Manstein): the face is large and left, close-up (3 of 4), a clear colourised photo; one or two strong arrows; colour pair red + green/olive or blue + red; text 2-5 words; two of the four carry a curiosity line ("ZHUKOV's BRILLIANCE", "Prolonged WW 2 by a YEAR?") and two carry the series tag "Top 3 MOVES". The Model thumbnail asks a question the viewer wants answered (did he really prolong the war a year?); the Zhukov thumbnail shows an encirclement arrow cutting Japanese flags (the first move) so the click promise is visible.
- Weaker (Rommel, Suvorov, Caesar, Alexander, Lee): paintings (Caesar, Alexander are AI-looking, on horses) or a small face, busier or darker maps, generic claims ("IMPOSSIBLE to BEAT", "RUSSIA's NAPOLEON"), Rommel's pale vintage map breaks the palette, Lee's dark navy is the lowest contrast.
- Palette: saturated red as the main hue in 6 of 9. Text weight: always heavy. Arrows: in all 9. Flags/icons: 7 of 9.
- Titles are identical across videos, so the thumbnail carries all the variety; the title adds the "Top 3" promise and the (No AI) tag that the thumbnail lacks.

## 4. Titles
`<Name>'s Top 3 Tactical Masterpieces [(No AI)]`, 37-52 characters, no number besides "3", no year, no question, no curiosity gap, no ALL CAPS. Model says "Defensive Masterpieces" (a small differentiation, still a hit). Same title for hits and flops, so the title is not the lever; the name is. Their format is a brand: viewers who want a given general search the name.

## 6. What works here / what fails here
Works: (1) a known general with Eastern Front or Napoleon demand; (2) one huge face + arrow map + 2-5 words, saturated red; (3) a sticky repeatable title brand; (4) a curiosity line in the thumbnail text instead of just "Top 3" (Model "Prolonged WW 2 by a YEAR?", Zhukov "BRILLIANCE"); (5) the 3 moves being three real battles with a result each; (6) the "(No AI)" trust tag during a period when audiences distrust AI channels.
Fails: American Civil War (Lee 43k), low-contrast thumbnails, generic claims, painting/AI-art face (Caesar, Alexander), vintage pale map that breaks the palette (Rommel), 83-117 day gaps that cool the channel.

## 7. How to beat them
**What they do better than us**
- Name demand: they chose Napoleon, Zhukov, Model, Manstein, Rommel, Caesar, Alexander (big search names); we chose Rokossovsky, Meade, Cleburne etc.
- Thumbnails: a ready recipe (huge colourised face + saturated arrow map + 2-5 words) that gives 3x outliers; 9 of 9 consistent, immediately readable at feed size.
- Brand-consistent titles plus one trust tag; a dependable audience of "Top 3 moves" searchers who comment 1-2k times.
- Plain, dense, factual narration with many dated unit details that reads as "serious"; short intro.
- Finishing on the Eastern Front: the era with the largest war-video audience on YouTube.

**What we do better than them**
- Hook: they open with a CV-style thesis in 12-70 s (the very thing our OPENING RULE forbids); we open with the stakes of one crisis, a number and a camera dive.
- Visuals: our Warhawk ink map, portraits, locked sound design and archive blocks against their animated game-style maps (as the thumbnails show); casualty and method cards give a ranking and a recap that they never give.
- Engagement beats: mid-video questions, result cards and an end-screen-ready final card; theirs ends abruptly and puts a 90 s sponsor read into move 1 of the newest video (watch-time risk).
- Cadence: we can publish more often than their 1 per 8 weeks.

**Generals they have NOT covered (our chances)**
Ours: Grant, Sherman, Jackson, Meade, Cleburne, Patton, Rokossovsky, Peng, Arnold, Collins. Others: Hannibal, Scipio, Frederick the Great, Wellington, Montgomery, Guderian, Konev, Eisenhower, Bradley, MacArthur, Nimitz, Yamamoto, Saladin, Genghis Khan, Belisarius, Gustavus Adolphus, Marlborough, Eugene of Savoy, Moltke. Their only American is Lee (43k): Civil War is proven for us (see CLAUDE.md data rules) and a weak spot for them, so Grant and Jackson are open ground, and Patton and Rokossovsky (the Eastern Front/WW2 audience that built their 3 hits) are the best pair: the audience that watched Zhukov/Model/Manstein will search Rokossovsky, Konev, Guderian, Patton next.

**Concrete actions**
1. Copy their thumbnail recipe for the next test: one big colourised face, big arrow, 2-5 words with a curiosity line ("Prolonged WW 2 by a YEAR?" style), saturated red; avoid dark low-contrast backgrounds. Test against our current style in Test & Compare.
2. Put Rokossovsky/Patton/Guderian/Konev ahead of lesser-known generals, and run the Eastern Front thumbnail + a title carrying the general's name first; do not wait for Rokossovsky to be forgotten.
3. Our video must beat their hook (stakes, number) and add the thing they lack: a counted ranking, end recap, no flat thank-you ending. Use the same title family but add the promise ("Legendary") that makes it ours; do not use "(No AI)" unless true.
4. Do not copy their 90 s sponsor inside move 1.
