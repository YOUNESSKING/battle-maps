## Format
Transcripts: D-Day From the German Perspective (10:52, 8.4M) and Vietnam War from the North Vietnamese Perspective (19:36, 6.2M).
- **Hook:** thesis/angle line within 5-20 s, no cold open; D-Day: "You're probably already familiar with the iconic Normandy landings, but chances are you're more familiar with the experiences of those who landed ... as opposed to that of the defenders" = the perspective is the hook; Vietnam: "mentioning the Vietnam War is one of the fastest ways to spark controversy... what is much less talked about is the perspective of the Vietnamese people". Topic named in the first sentence; sponsor read lands at 0:00-0:10 (Audible) or 0:49-1:36 (Conflict of Nations) BEFORE the content proper.
- **Structure:** context (1-3 min history), then the event, then human-voice quotes (a 17-year-old soldier, a staff sergeant's diary), closing reflection; no chapter cards, no recap. Second sponsor mid-roll (D-Day at 8:35), "subscribe and click the bell" + community-tab vote; ends with "I'll see you next Friday with our video on ...".
- **Narration:** host Griffin Johnson, conversational, ~160-190 wpm (estimate; fast), animated 2D cartoon with maps and unit icons; stock/archive minimal.
- **Length/cadence:** weekly (Fri/Sat); typical 11-27 min, full-documentary compilations 50-100 min do 0.4-1.8M.

## What works here
- The "other side's perspective" angle is a repeatable series formula (D-Day German 8.4M, Vietnam North 6.2M, Poland, India, American WW1).
- Hero character in cartoon: face close-up + emotion, little or no text.
- Evolution/uniform/tank explainers perform (2.5-5.4M) and cross-sell.
- Humanising first-person quotes keep a fast-talking video alive.

## What fails here
- Niche theatres or weapon histories (0.11-0.2M).
- Sponsor read before the content (0:00-1:36) at the start.
- Recent videos 5-10x below old winners; the channel owner announced retirement end of 2026 (video 677k) - not a safe model for the format's future.
