## What the study MISSED about the narration
1. **Hook lines replayed in the body (bookends):** "When night fell, the Russians were still there" [0:15] returns as [13:21] "Night fell. Both armies lit their fires... the Russians too were rejoicing over a victory"; "Moscow was burning" [0:31] returns in the Moscow chapter. The cold open is a trailer cut from the body's own sentences.
2. **Two-way callbacks:** the sun of Austerlitz is planted at 1:18 (a broken promise at Vitebsk) before the 6:25 myth check; "the Moskova" is named at 6:09 and paid off in the will at 17:28.
3. **The illness thread** explains the refusal without editorialising: [5:36] "That night, Napoleon fell ill. A fever, a dry cough, a burning thirst." -> [9:36] "ill with a heavy cold and fever".
4. **The ironic pay-off of the 5:36 prediction:** "I shall lose 20,000" vs [13:54] "The French had lost around 30,000"; the narrator never points it out.
5. **Kicker sentences:** almost every micro-scene ends on a 2-6-word line: "None came." "It never came." "Take the fool away." "Armand raised his hat and stayed." "He could catch them." Rhythm: medium sentence, medium, short punch.
6. **Triads and anaphora:** "All day... All day", "Again... Again", "Moscow, and Moscow was burning", "A fever, a dry cough, a burning thirst", "Austerlitz, Jena, and the Moskova", "in November, in the snow, on the banks of a river".
7. **Chapter transitions have no signposts:** no "but first", no "now", no recap; each chapter opens on a date/time or place phrase and the description chapters do the labelling. The battle clock rewinds sector by sector (late morning at 9:03 after "before noon" at 8:16).
8. **The attribution sits mid-sentence as a parenthetical** ("Next day, Ségur says, Napoleon asked...", "On the Russian side, Clausewitz wrote, the reserves...") so the action leads and the source follows; Clausewitz is a recurring CHARACTER (did the sums, was there, rode with the raid, heard the false Murat news, judged the result), not just a citation.
9. **Balanced point of view:** Russian human stories (Davydov, both Tuchkovs, Bagration, Kutaisov, the Guard squares, Kutuzov's roubles) match or outnumber French ones.
10. **A deliberate ~13 s narration pause** at 14:46-14:59 (after "past houses in ashes") before the verdict chapter: the only long silence in the film.
11. **Heavy number hedging** throughout ("some", "roughly", "about", "around", "nearly", "more than") on almost every figure.
12. **No explicit "why this matters" lines and no moral:** the paradox is never explained aloud; Clausewitz's "tip the scales a little" plus the Berezina line do the job.
