## 5. Format

Transcripts: 'Battle Of Britain: The 13-Hour Dogfight' (48:35, 8.2M) and 'The Falklands War: Remembered' (1h41, 7.5M; only the opening was inspected).
- **Hook**: 'one day' cold open: archive footage plus eyewitness soundbites, with a plain promise in the first sentence ("on one day in September 1940 the Battle of Britain reached its decisive moment") and, at 1:26-1:42, the exact frame "this is the story of that day, 15 September 1940, and 13 hours that saved Britain". Falklands: archive radio broadcast of the Argentine takeover of the Stanley radio station, then testimony.
- **Structure**: clock-stamped chapters ("6:34 a.m.", "8:02 a.m.", "11:50", ... "5:25 p.m.") alternating RAF operations room, civilians in Kent/London, pilots; roughly a third of the runtime is oral history of ordinary people (children collecting shrapnel, a wedding, a bombed greengrocer) = human detail. Ends with kill totals (79 vs 29 aircraft) and its meaning (Sea Lion postponed). No subscribe/CTA in narration.
- **Narration**: sparse narrator (~100-120 wpm effective because interviews and music take most of the time), archival audio, first-person recollection; licensed TV-quality production (Timeline = TV distributor), not reproducible by a one-person faceless channel.
- **Visuals**: archive film, photographs, some reconstruction; no maps shown in transcript.
- **Length / cadence**: 2-6 uploads a day; typical 26 min or 48-58 min; frequent 2-4 h compilations. Typical video length 54 min; long compilations bring the 5-12M accumulated views.

## 6. What works here

1. 'One day / minute-by-minute / 13 hours' framing: a promised time-box in the title gives an obvious structure (their 2026 hits: Minute-By-Minute 9/11, Che 'Inside the CIA Files').
2. Human details from ordinary people anchor a battle story.
3. 'Inside' / 'Complete Story Of' title words for the current algorithm audience.


## 7. What fails here

- Daily uploads of cheap 26-min edits: 17-40k views each (0.3-0.8x of an already tiny median); volume does not rescue weak topics.
- Text-free thumbnails with only a wordmark: works for a 5.9M-sub channel and for evergreen mysteries, not for a small channel.
- 3-hour compilations are not our format.


## 8. Three things OUR channel can copy or must avoid

1. Test a time-box frame in the title/hook for each move ('the 13 hours that saved Britain' style): "<General>'s 13 hours at <place>" gives the hook (1:26-1:42 in Timeline) and built-in time-stamped chapters.
2. Keep one human detail per move (kids collecting shrapnel, a bombed greengrocer) as the 'one human detail' rule in our opening.
3. Avoid copying their text-less thumbnails and the daily mass-upload cadence; their weak videos prove that volume without a hook produces 0.3x.
