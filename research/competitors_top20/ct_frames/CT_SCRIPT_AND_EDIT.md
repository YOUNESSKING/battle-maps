## B. The editing (AI-reported, Model + Napoleon)
- **Shot length** ~4-7 s; the camera always drifts (slow zoom/pan), never a dead still; arrows draw along curves; units slide.
- **Scale ladder every move:** continent/theatre map -> regional satellite map -> tactical close-up -> archive or painting of the result.
- **Colour code:** Germans yellow on the tactical maps (Model video), Soviets red, Western Allies blue; satellite terrain with bright blue rivers.
- **Map props and drawings:** mines, barbed wire, anti-tank obstacles, trench lattices, diamond strongpoints, dashed fire tracers with muzzle flashes, crossed swords at clashes, fog question marks, C-47 icons, parachute markers, bridge-blown icons.
- **People:** colourised cut-out portraits slide in at the frame edge when named; one speech bubble with a real quote; a round portrait icon at the general's HQ on the map.
- **Archive:** picture-in-picture in rounded frames with a red/blue glow over the map sector (Model, blocks of 10-40 s every ~1-2 min); full-screen archive montages at move ends; Ken Burns on photos and paintings; captions naming each painting (blue serif, top-left); photo insets of real objects (a canvas boat, a Jagdpanther).
- **Move cards:** full-screen greyscale photo (Model) or black (Napoleon), white serif title + red place/year, 4-12 s with music only.
- **Numbers on screen:** troop counters that add up (Napoleon: 38,000 vs 25,000 + 30,000 = 55,000).
- **Sound:** music bed throughout, changing per act; a sub-bass hit on cards; Model is sparse (artillery rumble); Napoleon has a sound for nearly every map action.
- **Film B-roll (Napoleon):** clips that look like feature films (copyright risk, not for us).
