## 2. Their craft (repeating devices)
- **Opening with the general's REAL world:** a famous speech (Model) or famous admirers (Napoleon) before any map; the general is named within ~35 s; move 1 starts at 0:32-1:06 (ours: ~1:30-2:00).
- **Move titles are NAMED like chapters with an emotional label** ("Corridor of Death", "The Perfect Trap") + place + year. Ours: "MOVE 1 · FORT HENRY · 6 FEBRUARY".
- **Scale ladder in every move:** continent/strategic map -> regional satellite map -> tactical close-up with custom props (mines, wire, anti-tank obstacles, canvas boats, crossed sabres) -> archive/painting/film of the result.
- **Visual metaphors drawn on the map:** the "checkerboard" defence as a lattice of trench lines and diamond strongpoints; funnels and kill zones with dashed fire lines; question marks in the fog over hidden French divisions (Austerlitz).
- **Numbers on screen:** troop counters that add/subtract (38,000 vs 55,000), casualty rates per hour.
- **People:** colourised cut-out portraits slide in when named (Zhukov, Stalin, Montgomery, Frost); a speech bubble with a real quote ("Monty, you're nuts!"); Frost's last radio message as text; Hitler-handshake photo + a Knight's Cross icon.
- **Archive/film B-roll between map beats** every ~30-60 s: archive film, colourised photos, period paintings, and (Napoleon) clips from feature films (Napoleon 2023, Waterloo 1970) — a licensing risk we cannot copy.
- **Pacing:** ~130-140 wpm (slower than ours at ~150-160), a change every 2-6 s, no static shot over ~5 s, continuous slow camera drift.
- **No sponsor/subscribe interruption** in either video; one outro ask with 2 questions.
