Channel list + ids: `channels.json`. Our baseline: `research/COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS.md` (Tactical Genius), `research/NICHE_ANALYSIS.md`.
**Caveats (read first):** (1) "outlier" = views / median of the last ~10-50 uploads, so 2018-2021 winners are inflated (years of views vs days): use for ranking and patterns, not as a forecast. (2) Words per minute and "% of winners with X" are agent estimates from ~12 thumbnails per channel (the agents' labels are not uniform); treat percentages as direction, not statistics. (3) 5 of the 20 are not comparable: Wartime (feature films), Johnny's War Stories (film clips), War Archives (dormant newsreels), Animarchy (1-3 h commentary), Timeline (non-war evergreens); History Hit is a presenter/documentary channel. Most comparable to us: HistoryMarche, Eastory, Operations Room, Kings and Generals, Armchair Historian, Epic History.

## What works: THUMBNAILS (patterns across the 20)
1. **One focal subject, readable at feed size.** Every winner reads in 168x94: ONE face, ONE iconic object or ONE map with one named place. Busy maps with 6-7 words and small icons are the common weak thumbnail (Operations Room, HistoryMarche weak videos, Epic's 12-word Tsarist Russia).
2. **Text: few words or none.** Winners average ~2.6 words (median 3); 38 % of winner thumbnails have no text at all (Mark Felton, Dark Docs 8/8, War Stories, Timeline, Wartime). When there is text it is 1-5 words in heavy caps, with ONE accent colour (red word, red bar, red ribbon).
3. **A face wins more often than a map, but maps win for battle topics.** About two thirds of winners carry a face (real photo, colourised portrait, painted or cartoon). Maps are ~1 in 5 of winners overall, but 75 % of HistoryMarche's best 8 and 7 of 8 of Eastory's: a map thumbnail works only when it shows ONE named place + ONE arrow/front line.
4. **One brand template per channel** (Epic cream/red, MHV red ribbon, History Explorer bottom bar, War Archives blue bar, Operations Room channel name bottom-left): recognisable in the sidebar. Variety is not rewarded.
5. **Emotion / mystery beats information:** wide-eyed or grim close-ups, a red circle on one odd detail (Dark Docs), a quote as text ("THE SOUND IS DEAFENING!").
6. **Colour:** muted base (grey-blue, olive, sepia, b&w) with ONE hot accent (red) dominates; cream/red/black is the Epic/MHV look.
7. **What the title adds:** the thumbnail names the subject, the title carries the angle, the number or the curiosity gap; the best pairs never repeat the same words.

## What works: TITLES
- Plain subject + year + "- Animated" (Operations Room, K&G "DOCUMENTARY") wins on famous topics; famous Western-audience topics beat niche ones by 5-10x (Desert Storm, Midway, D-Day, Caesar, Rome, Mongols).
- **Angle titles** repeat: "<Battle> from the <other side's> view" (Armchair 8.4M, History Explorer 2.2M), "How One <thing> Destroyed <empire>" (HistoryMarche 6.6x), "When <X> mistook a <Y> for a <Z>" (Liveth 13.6M), "The Most Terrifying Man..." (Dark Docs), superlative question ("The Greatest ...?", Epic).
- Median winning title ~54 characters, almost never caps, rarely a number; named person or place + one stake word.
- "Part N" / "All Parts" / series numbers lose 15-50 % (Operations Room, Eastory 0.7x).

## What works: FORMAT
- **Hook:** the winners with a strong hook open on ONE concrete moment (Epic: the Opis speech 16M from a 7:39 video; Dark Docs: cold open in action, hero named <15 s; History at War: one named witness + one quote + what was about to be lost; Operations Room: "in 35 hours a war will have started"; World History: dated scene with a hard number at 0:00). The weak openers are background-first (K&G sponsor at 0:10 then chronology; Ops Room 100 s background; Eastory flat dated opening; Liveth 45 s context): they win only on mega-topics.
- **Sponsor/channel-promo before the content (Armchair 0-1:36, K&G 0:10, History Explorer 3-4 min Patreon) = the competitor mistake. Keep asks late.**
- **Structure:** 3-7 self-contained acts; clock-stamped vignettes with one number each (Ops Room, Timeline); plan -> result -> decision-beat loop (Eastory); ending on a twist fact (Felton) or a cliffhanger + recap (War Stories, every ~11 min).
- **Length:** hits exist at every length. 7-20 min for single stories (Felton 7-15, Epic Opis 7:39, Armchair 20, Operations Room ~35-60); 1-3 h compilations draw clicks for K&G/Epic but are not our format. Our ~16 min Top-3 sits inside the proven band.
- **Series fatigue:** a second/third part of the same subject, or many near-identical videos in a row, falls 15-50 % (Ops Room, MHV 2026 StuG series, Eastory RCW parts).
- **Topic beats packaging:** the same channel's best and worst differ 5-60x mostly by topic fame (Caesar x3 in K&G's top 6; Desert Storm/Midway at Ops Room; obscure battles 0.3-0.5x of median). Lead our Top-3 with the most famous move.
- **Decline of the old winners:** 14 of the 20 channels' 2026 uploads sit at 0.1-0.5x of their old winners; the Armchair Historian owner announced retirement end of 2026. Evergreen back-catalogue carries many of these channels, new videos do not.

## What this means for us (to apply, in priority order)
1. **Thumbnail test set** (fits Test & Compare): (A) general's portrait + 1-3 caps words + ONE red accent; (B) our own Style W map frame with ONE named place + ONE arrow + 1-3 words (HistoryMarche/Eastory pattern, "HATTIN, 1187" style) — check it reads at 168x94; (C) a real archive face with NO text (Felton/Dark Docs) or a circled mystery detail. Use ONE brand template (colour + logo corner) on every video of the channel.
2. **Titles:** keep A (locked format), and make B/C angle titles: "How <X> ..." counter-intuitive claim, "when <army> walked into its own trap", "<Battle> from the <losing side's> view", "The Man Who ...". Every claim true and sourced.
3. **Hook:** our stakes-first hook is the differentiator; add the best competitor devices: a hard odds number in the first sentence (War Stories), name the hero by ~0:15-0:50 (Dark Docs, we have 0:50), one repeated signature line as the spine (Dark Docs), and a clock/deadline cold open for move 1 (Ops Room) — all inside the existing opening rule (no background before move 1).
4. **Move structure:** copy the Ops Room clock-stamped vignettes with one number each, close every move on a twist/result line (Felton) with a one-sentence recap into the next move (War Stories); this matches our casualty/result card rule and DATA RULES (minutes watched).
5. **Never:** sponsor/asks before the content, "Part N" or "All Parts", background-first openings, 8+ word thumbnails, shock/gore or Nazi-leader gimmicks (monetisation and brand risk), SS insignia in thumbnails.
6. **Topic order:** put the most famous battle/move first; famous Western-audience topics beat niche ones by 5-10x. Do not repeat a winning subject family until the audience is still there (Liveth lost ~90 % after its pivot).
7. **Measure:** log which test thumbnail/title wins and the day 1-3 impressions per traffic source (CHANNEL_LOG), separate from retention.
