## Findings
**What works here (winners, 2019-2022):** extremely consistent thumbnail: a real, clean, modern press photo (ship, soldier, tank) with ONE bold white-on-dark-red label bar (2-6 words, mixed case), plus unit crests/badges in the corner (SAS, SBS, Spanish Navy, US flag). Faces: only 4 of 8 visible, none a recognisable named person; no maps. Title formula: '<hook phrase or Operation name> | <Month Year>' with superlatives ('Longest', 'Only', 'When Pirates Mistook a Warship for a Cargo Ship'). The top two (13.6M, 8.3M) are record/irony stories with one sentence of curiosity: 'longest sniper kill', 'pirates attack a warship by mistake'.
**What fails (recent weak):** colourised WW2 portraits, crest + long caption (up to 8 words), obscure raids ('Operations Deep Cut & Chopper', 9k). The label bar and crest system is unchanged, but the TOPIC moved from record-breaking modern incidents to small-unit WW2 stories, and views dropped to ~34k median (the older winners are 30-400x that, so ranking only).
**Typical length / cadence:** 5-8 min (modern winners) -> 6-13 min now; about 1 video every 2-3 weeks.
**Title patterns:** length 40-95 characters; ' | <Month Year>' suffix on every title; named operations; superlatives; 'When...' and 'The Only...' curiosity gaps beat plain place names.
## Format (transcripts: Pirates mistook a Warship, Longest Sniper Shot)
- **Hook:** neither opens with stakes. Pirates video starts with 'In November of 2011 the Spanish Navy replenishment ship Patino departed from Spain for a five-month deployment...' (45 s of context; the irony of pirates attacking a warship is only revealed at 2:00). Sniper video starts with the Mosul offensive date and context for 1:44 before the Canadian team appears. The packaging (title + thumbnail) does the hook; the video is a slow-burn account. Topic named in the first sentence.
- **Structure:** chronological briefing: context, unit, the incident, outcome, epilogue; no chapters. 5-6 min, ~650-700 words, ~130-140 wpm narration over stock/press photos and agency video, with a direct quote from an officer in the middle (Pirates: commander Cabero's quote at 1:47).
- **Visuals/music:** photos, public footage, ambient music; no animated maps seen in the thumbnails; no on-screen quotes counted.
- **Asks:** none in the narration (final 1:20 is music-only credits).
- **Ending:** outcome sentence, then silent music tail (~70 s of music in both videos).
