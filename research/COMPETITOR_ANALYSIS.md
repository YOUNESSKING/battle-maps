## What the winners have in common (from 10 transcripts analyzed: Ridgway, Patton, Zhukov, Manstein, Guderian, Slim, Montgomery, Wellington, Yamashita, Rommel)

**Cold-open hook (first ~30–60s), always the same shape:** establish that the general's side is losing badly / considered doomed or contemptible by the enemy ("the war had become a catastrophe," "the German High Command looked… and saw amateurs," "Singapore… the men holding it did not believe it could fall" — used in reverse for the enemy general). Then introduce the general by his soldier-given **nickname** ("Old Iron Tits," "Old Blood and Guts," "the Desert Fox," "Uncle Bill," "Fast Heinz," "the Tiger of Malaya"), plant a rhetorical question ("But what exactly set him apart from every other general?"), then the title card line: "Let's take a closer look at [Name]'s three greatest tactical moves."

**Structure:** exactly three acts, each a self-contained battle, each ending with a one-line "signature" recap (three parallel short phrases, e.g. "the careful eye, the concealed strength, the timed counterstroke") that is repeated almost verbatim at the very end of the video. Acts escalate in stakes and scale.

**Mid-roll subscribe ask:** placed at the *end of Act 1*, folded into a teaser for Act 2 ("there is a lot more like this ahead… because next, this general would face X"). Never a separate ad-break-style interruption.

**Numbers-driven narration:** exact dates, exact troop/tank/gun counts, casualty ratios stated as arithmetic ("more than 80 Chinese casualties for every hour the perimeter held," "one panzer destroyed every 2 minutes"). This is a heavy signature of the writing — it reads like a data brief, not a story.

**Cross-references to other generals** ("one of the very few commanders who fought as relentlessly from the front as Ridgway did was Rommel…") — deliberately builds a shared universe that rewards binge-watching the channel, and doubles as a soft trailer for future videos.

**Ending:** always the same two-part CTA — "What is your favorite tactical move by [Name]? And which commander should I cover next? Let me know in the comments. And if you enjoy the content, why not subscribe?"

**Winning era/subject:** WW2 European and Eastern-front commanders tied to a **comeback-from-disaster** or **myth-breaking** narrative (an army/side thought beaten or invincible, and the general inverts that). Ridgway (Korea reversed in 100 days), Zhukov (breaks the "Japanese/German invincibility" myth twice), Patton (breaks the "Americans can't fight" myth). These are the three outliers above 5x median.

**Winning subtitle formula:** "**The Man Who [verb] [famous rival/event]**" (Ridgway: "The Man Who Turned Korea Around"; Zhukov: "The Man Who Broke Hitler") clearly outperforms a plain nickname subtitle or "The Victor of X" — both of which sit near or below the median (Montgomery, Yamashita).

## What flopped, and why

- **Wellington** (0.06x) and **Yamashita** (0.05x) are the two worst performers despite strong tactical stories and famous rival hooks ("The Man Who Beat Napoleon," "The Tiger of Malaya"). Both sit **outside the WW2-Europe/Pacific-Allied core** this audience has been trained on: Wellington is Napoleonic era, Yamashita is a Japanese antagonist general with a shorter, dimmer nickname-only subtitle. The channel's audience appears to reward the WW2 Allied/Axis-in-Europe frame far more than earlier eras or Axis-in-Pacific figures.
- **Richard O'Connor** (0.17x) and **Rommel** (0.22x) — O'Connor is a much less famous name with no viral rival-hook strength; Rommel is the single most over-covered general in the entire genre across YouTube (WW2 channels, "Kings and Generals," etc.), so a generic Rommel video has weak differentiation even with Churchill's quote as a hook.
- **MacArthur** (0.27x) is a partial flop for a very famous name — likely because the video ran long (27:27, the longest in the catalog) on a figure whose reputation is already "known" rather than a discovery, diluting the myth-breaking hook.
- Pattern: length above ~20 minutes (Manstein 26:10, MacArthur 27:27, Wellington 20:45, Bradley 20:14) correlates with weaker-than-Ridgway/Patton/Zhukov performance (16–19 min); the three biggest hits are all in the 16:38–18:59 band. 15–19 minutes looks like the performance sweet spot; anything past ~20 minutes needs an unusually strong hook to justify it.

## Actionable rules for our channel

1. **Cold open in ≤45 seconds:** establish the disaster/contempt the general's side faced, name the nickname, ask "what set him apart," cut to "top 3 legendary tactical moves."
2. **Prioritize the comeback/myth-breaking angle** over a pure "great general" biography — audience is buying the reversal, not the résumé.
3. **Subtitle formula: "The Man/General Who [verb] [named rival or catastrophe]"** — beats a bare nickname or "The Victor of X."
4. **Target 15–19 minutes.** Don't pad past 20 without a very strong justification.
5. **Mid-roll subscribe ask goes at the Act 1→Act 2 seam**, phrased as a teaser for what's coming, never as a break.
6. **Load the script with hard numbers and ratios** — this channel's narration reads like a stat sheet and it works; keep ours equally dense.
7. **Cross-link to other generals** by name in the narration — cheap, builds a shared universe, and is a free trailer for future videos (and for us, a chance to plug our own back catalog, e.g. Hannibal).
8. **Avoid** the two eras that have already underperformed for this competitor (Napoleonic-era British generals as lead subject, and Japanese Pacific antagonists) unless the angle is materially different (rivalry format, flip-POV, or an underdog with almost no existing coverage).
9. **End every video with the same two-part comment bait**: favorite move + "who next" — cheap, proven engagement driver (Patton's video: 670 comments on 752K views, one of the best engagement ratios in the set).
