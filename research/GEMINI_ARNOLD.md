## Move selection
Keep Quebec, Valcour, Saratoga. Fort Stanwix (ruse, no battle) works as a ~15 s setup inside Move 3; Ridgefield is a small delaying skirmish.

## Cold-open candidates
- **A (Gemini's pick): 7 Oct 1777, Bemis Heights.** Burgoyne's reconnaissance to break the line and reach Albany; Gates in HQ; Arnold, stripped of command after a feud, leaps onto a borrowed brown horse ("no man shall keep me in my tent"), outraces the officer sent to call him back (Maj. John Armstrong), takes over regiments and charges the British line. Stakes: Burgoyne through to Albany = New England cut off, France stays out, Revolution likely doomed. Number: 5,791 British and German troops surrendered ten days later.
- **B: 11 Oct 1776, Valcour Island.** Washington routed from New York; Carleton sails down Champlain with a fleet carried in pieces from Europe/rebuilt at Saint-Jean to split the colonies; British round the south tip of Valcour and find 15 green-timber boats behind the island, forcing them to fight against the wind. Number: 88 British guns vs 83 American; broadside ~1,032 lb vs ~545 lb.
- **C: late Oct 1775, Dead River.** Hurricane floods the river 8 ft overnight; powder ruined, food lost, a third turn back, ~600 boil moccasins, cartridge boxes and dogs; Arnold goes ahead to buy cattle from French-Canadian farmers. Number: 350 miles in 45 days (vs 180 miles / 20 days estimated), ~450 of 1,050 turned back or died.

## Treason in one line
At the climax of move 3 (Breymann's redoubt, the leg wound) or right after the name: "had the ball struck higher he would have died the greatest hero of the Revolution — instead ... crippled ... grievances and debts that led him in 1780 to sell the plans of West Point to the British for £20,000." Echo: 1781 Virginia anecdote (captured American captain: they'd bury the wounded leg with honours and hang the rest).

## Background (max 20 s)
May 1775: Arnold (34, New Haven merchant, sea captain trading to Quebec, Connecticut militia captain) helps Ethan Allen take Ticonderoga. Knowing Carleton had fewer than 700 regulars in Canada, he convinces Washington at Cambridge to give him ~1,050 volunteers: Montgomery via Champlain to Montreal, Arnold through the Maine wilderness to Quebec.

## The five checks
- Same left leg at Quebec (31 Dec 1775, ricochet into the lower left leg/calf; Senter, Dearborn, Stocking; Arnold's letters) and Saratoga (7 Oct 1777, left thigh broken above the knee, horse killed and fell on him; Dearborn, Wilkinson, Dr Potts). Refused amputation; leg ~2 in shorter.
- Gates: Sept 22 report to Congress omitted Arnold; Morgan's corps detached to report to Gates; Sept 22 shouting match (Wilkinson, Varick, Clarkson letters to Schuyler); Arnold asked for a pass to Philadelphia; Gates "officially stripped" him on Sept 23 (Gemini); officers' petition, Arnold stayed with no command on Oct 7. Never formally arrested.
- Boot Monument: 1887, John Watts de Peyster, at Breymann's redoubt (Stop 7); boot, epaulet, wreath; inscription "In memory of the 'most brilliant soldier' of the Continental Army who was desperately wounded on this spot, the sally port of BORGOYNES GREAT WESTERN REDOUBT 7th October, 1777 winning for his countrymen the Decisive Battle of the American Revolution and for himself the rank of Major General." No name.
- Valcour "saved the Revolution": Mahan (1898 in Clowes / 1913 book): "That the Americans were strong enough to impose the capitulation of Saratoga was due to the invaluable year of delay secured to them in 1776 by their little navy on Lake Champlain, created by the indomitable energy, and handled with the indomitable courage, of the traitor, Benedict Arnold." Nelson, Martin endorse.
- Morgan overlap: 19 Sep Arnold got Gates to send Morgan (~380-500) + Dearborn (~300) forward, then fed 7 regiments of Poor's and Learned's brigades; Arnold's presence on the field 19 Sep DISPUTED (Wilkinson says Gates kept him at HQ). 7 Oct: Arnold told Morgan to dispose of Fraser ("That man on the grey horse is a host in himself and must be disposed of" — SINGLE SOURCE); Timothy Murphy shot Fraser. In this video: 19 Sep + Fraser brief; 80 % on Arnold's charge at Balcarres, the ride between the lines, the rear of Breymann's.

## Hook / stakes material
Champlain–Hudson corridor ~300 miles: British aim 1776 and 1777 = reach Albany, cut New England off. Vergennes waited for proof Americans could beat a British field army. Enemy run of success: Trois-Rivières 8 Jun 1776 / Americans out of Canada; Long Island 27 Aug 1776, New York 15 Sep 1776; Ticonderoga 5-6 Jul 1777 (Mount Defiance); Brandywine 11 Sep 1777, Philadelphia 26 Sep 1777. Nicknames "American Hannibal" (late 1775/76), "The Fighting General" (Gemini, VERIFIED?). Quotes: "No man shall keep me in my tent today..." (TRADITION); Fraser line (SINGLE SOURCE); "I wish to God the ball had passed through my heart instead of my leg" (Gemini says VERIFIED, Dearborn/Varick); deathbed uniform line = APOCRYPHAL. Born 14 Jan 1741, died 14 Jun 1801; 34 at Quebec (colonel), 35 Valcour (brig. gen.), 36 Saratoga (maj. gen.).

## Battle 1: march to Quebec + assault (Sep-Dec 1775)
- Opening: New Year's Eve blizzard; white paper slips on hats ("Liberty or Death" written on them); enlistments expiring at midnight.
- Dates: march 19 Sep (Newburyport) / 25 Sep (Fort Western) – 9 Nov (Point Lévis); assault 31 Dec 1775 ~04:00-10:00.
- Coords: Height of Land 45.5333,-70.8667; Sault-au-Matelot 46.8161,-71.2017; Fort Western 44.31,-69.77; Great Carrying Place 45.09,-69.99; Lake Mégantic 45.58,-70.88; Point Lévis 46.80,-71.18.
- Forces: start ~1,050-1,100 (two battalions Greene, Enos + three rifle companies ~250 under Morgan); ~600-675 reach the St Lawrence (~100 died/sick; Enos took ~300 back on 25 Oct). Assault ~800-900 (Arnold ~550-600, Montgomery ~300 from Montreal arrived 2 Dec, Livingston ~150-200 Canadians). Quebec garrison ~1,800 (70 of 7th Foot, ~230 Royal Highland Emigrants under Maclean, ~400 sailors/marines from HMS Lizard + Hunter, ~330 British militia, ~550 French-Canadian militia), 100+ guns.
- Commanders: Arnold (wounded), Montgomery (killed by grapeshot at the first barricade at Près-de-Ville), Morgan (took over, captured); Carleton, Maclean, Capt. Laws (sortie from Palace Gate behind Morgan).
- Terrain: Kennebec rapids; Great Carrying Place 12-mile portage, three ponds; Dead River under Bigelow Mountain; Height of Land 4.5-mile portage; Chaudière rapids; Point Lévis on the mile-wide St Lawrence. Quebec: Cape Diamond ~350 ft cliff, Upper Town walls, Lower Town strip; Près-de-Ville path (south), Sault-au-Matelot street with two barricades (north).
- Phases: 25 Sep-11 Oct ~220 green-pine bateaux (~400 lb), leaking, food spoiled; 11-16 Oct Great Carrying Place, 65 tons of gear; 19-20 Oct hurricane, Dead River rises 8 ft overnight, columns lost; 25 Oct Enos turns back with ~300 and 3-4 days flour left; Arnold ahead over Height of Land, Chaudière rapids (six boats smashed), reaches Sartigan 30 Oct, sends back cattle and oatmeal; 9 Nov ~600 at Point Lévis; 350 miles in 45 days vs 180 miles on the Montresor map. 13-14 Nov: ~500 men in ~35 canoes past two British warships to Wolfe's Cove, Plains of Abraham, then withdraw to Pointe-aux-Trembles; Montgomery arrives 2 Dec with ~300. 31 Dec: snowstorm, enlistments expire; Livingston feint on Upper Town; Montgomery ~300 via Près-de-Ville, killed by blockhouse grapeshot (~50 defenders, four 3-pdrs), Campbell retreats; Arnold ~600 from Saint-Roch along the docks (6-pdr on a sled), wounded in the left leg at the first barricade, Morgan takes it (30 prisoners); delay at the second barricade (wet powder, waiting for rear), Carleton reinforces and sends Laws out of Palace Gate behind them; ~400 trapped and surrender ~10:00. Arnold in hospital with loaded pistols; ~350 keep up the blockade until spring.
- Belief vs insight: Carleton's attention on the Champlain/Montreal approach, Maine judged impassable; Arnold (traded at Quebec) saw the back door; blockade kept Carleton inside all winter.
- Result: British held; blockade until May 1776. Americans 48-60 killed, 34-42 wounded, 372-426 captured (Desjardin 48/34/372; Carleton 426 prisoners); British 5 killed, 14 wounded.
- Vivid: Dearborn's Newfoundland dog eaten (Morison, Senter), shaved moose-hide, boiled leather, tallow candles, lip-salve; white paper with "Liberty or Death"; pistols on the hospital bed.

## Battle 2: Valcour Island (11-13 Oct 1776)
- Opening: outgunned 2:1 by a fleet carried in pieces, hides green boats in a wind-shadowed cove; night escape with muffled oars past the British line.
- Place: Valcour Sound (between Valcour Island and the NY shore, 5 mi S of Plattsburgh) 44.6214,-73.4317; Ferris Bay / Arnold's Bay (Panton VT) 44.1944,-73.3611; Schuyler Island 44.52,-73.41; Split Rock 44.27,-73.33; Crown Point 44.03,-73.43; Ticonderoga 43.84,-73.39; Skenesborough (Whitehall) 43.55,-73.40.
- US: 15 vessels: sloop Enterprise (12), schooners Royal Savage (12), Revenge (8); galleys Congress, Washington, Trumbull (~8-10), Lee (6); 8 gondolas (Spitfire, Boston, Providence, New Haven, Connecticut, Jersey, Success, Philadelphia; 1x12 + 2x9 pdr). ~750-800 men (soldiers), 83 guns, broadside ~545-605 lb.
- UK: 25 armed vessels (+ longboats): Inflexible (18x12 pdr, taken apart and rebuilt at Saint-Jean in 28 days), schooners Maria (14, Carleton's flagship) and Carleton (12), radeau Thunderer (6x24, 6x12, 2 howitzers), gondola Loyal Convert (7), 20 gunboats (one gun each, 9-24 pdr). ~670-700 seamen + soldiers + ~650 Native warriors; 88 guns, broadside ~1,032 lb.
- Commanders: Arnold (Royal Savage then Congress), Waterbury (Washington, captured 13 Oct), Wigglesworth (Trumbull); Carleton, Pringle (Maria), Schank, Dacres (Carleton, wounded), Midshipman Edward Pellew (took over Carleton).
- Terrain: lake ~120 mi; Valcour Island ~2 x 1 mi; sound ~800-900 yd wide, shoals at the north entrance; bluffs hid the ships.
- Phases: Jul-Sep shipbuilding race (Skenesborough vs Saint-Jean; Carleton halts ~12 weeks); early Oct Arnold anchors a crescent across the sound against Waterbury's advice; 11 Oct ~08:00-10:30 British sail south past the island on a north wind, Arnold sends Royal Savage + galleys out as bait, British must beat back upwind; Royal Savage runs aground on the south tip (later burned); Inflexible/Maria/Thunderer cannot get up into the sound; 12:30-17:00 Carleton schooner + 17-20 gunboats fight at ~350 yd; Arnold aims guns himself on Congress; Carleton schooner battered (Pellew), one gunboat blown up; Congress hulled 12 times, Washington shattered, Philadelphia sinks ~18:00; dusk Inflexible fires five broadsides; British anchor across the south exit; ~60 dead/wounded, ammunition 3/4 gone; night, fog, muffled oars, a shaded lantern in each stern, single file between the British left and the NY shore. 12 Oct Carleton finds the sound empty; Americans at Schuyler Island scuttle Providence, New Jersey, abandon Spitfire; 13 Oct ~11:00 near Split Rock Washington surrenders, Arnold fights 2.5 h with Congress + four gondolas, beaches and burns them in Ferris Bay with flags flying, ~200 men walk to Crown Point / Ticonderoga.
- Belief vs insight: Carleton expected a fight in the open southern lake; Arnold = fleet-in-being, narrow strait where the north wind keeps the heavy ships out.
- Result: 11 of 15 vessels lost (Enterprise, Trumbull, Revenge, Gates? reached Ticonderoga); ~60-80 killed/wounded, ~110-120 captured (paroled); British 1 gunboat sunk, ~40 killed/wounded. Carleton takes Crown Point 14 Oct, looks at Ticonderoga (~12,000 garrison) ~20 Oct, withdraws to Canada ~2 Nov.
- Vivid: Arnold aiming the guns; Congress hulled; flags nailed on burning hulls, Arnold last off; Philadelphia raised 1935 by Lorenzo Hagglund with a 24-lb shot in her hull, now at the Smithsonian.

## Battle 3: Saratoga (19 Sep + 7 Oct 1777)
- Opening: stripped of command, rides out, outruns the messenger, rides between the lines, leads them into the rear of the enemy's strongest work.
- Coords: Freeman's Farm / Balcarres redoubt 42.9992,-73.6267; Breymann's redoubt 43.0053,-73.6358; Bemis Heights 42.980,-73.615; Barber's wheatfield 42.992,-73.628.
- Forces 19 Sep: Americans ~8,500-9,000 in camp, ~3,000-4,000 engaged (Morgan ~400, Dearborn ~300, 7 regiments Poor + Learned); British ~6,000-7,200, ~2,500-3,000 engaged in the centre column (9th, 20th, 21st, 62nd) + Riedesel ~550-800 Brunswickers at dusk. 7 Oct: Americans ~11,000-13,000 in camp, ~6,000-8,000 engaged; British ~5,000 effectives, 1,700 + 10 guns in the reconnaissance (Balcarres light infantry right, Riedesel centre, Acland grenadiers left), ~500 in Balcarres redoubt, ~200 Germans in Breymann's.
- Commanders: Gates (HQ both days), Arnold, Morgan, Poor, Learned, Lt Col John Brooks (8th Mass.); Burgoyne, Fraser (mortally wounded, died 8 Oct), Riedesel, Breymann (killed 7 Oct), Balcarres.
- Terrain: Kosciuszko's fortified camp on Bemis Heights over the river road defile; wooded ravine-cut plateau to the west; Barber's wheatfield (~15 acres); Balcarres redoubt ~500 yd long, 12-14 ft, 8 guns; Breymann's ~400 yd NW, ~200 yd long, 7 ft logs, open at the rear; gap with two log cabins held by Canadians.
- Phases: Aug 1777 Stanwix relief (950 men; Hon Yost Schuyler, brother hostage, bullet holes in his coat, "as numerous as the leaves"; St Leger leaves 22 Aug). 19 Sep: Arnold urges fighting in the woods, Morgan + Dearborn forward, 7 regiments fed in; Riedesel at dusk; Burgoyne loses ~600. 22-23 Sep feud, relieved. 7 Oct: 1,700 + 10 guns into the wheatfield; ~14:30 Gates sends Poor (800) at Acland, Morgan/Dearborn (600) at the British right; Arnold rides out ~1.5 mi, outrunning Armstrong; Learned at the German centre; Fraser shot by Murphy; British back into Balcarres; Arnold sends Paterson's and Glover's brigades at Balcarres (repulsed, pins it); gallops across ~300 yd of crossfire to Learned; clears the cabins (~300 Canadians); leads Brooks's 8th Mass. (+ Wesson, Livingston) into the open rear of Breymann's; German soldier kills his horse, ball breaks his left thigh; "Rush on, my brave boys"; stops a soldier bayoneting the German ("he's a fine fellow and only did his duty"); Breymann killed; Armstrong catches up only as he is carried off. Burgoyne abandons camp 8 Oct, Saratoga (Schuylerville), surrenders 17 Oct.
- Belief vs insight: British thought the day was over once behind the redoubts; Arnold saw the 400-yd gap and the open back of Breymann's.
- Result: French treaty 6 Feb 1778. 19 Sep: US ~319 (65-80 k, 218 w, 22 m), UK ~556-600 (~160 k, ~364 w, ~42 m). 7 Oct: US ~130-150 (~30 k, ~100 w), UK ~600-894 (176 k incl. Fraser and Breymann, ~250 w, ~240-468 captured, all 10 field guns). 17 Oct: 5,791 surrendered (+ ~1,856 already prisoners/sick, 37 brass cannon).
- Vivid: Gates to Armstrong "he will do some rash thing"; Arnold hitting officers with the flat of his sword; refused amputation, fracture box in Albany, Gates got the credit.

## Method (Gemini)
1 Conquer "impossible" geography to pick the arena; 2 use weather and terrain to cancel superior firepower; 3 lead from the front and hit the seam before the enemy recovers. Alt: "Turn the wilderness into a weapon, use narrow terrain and foul weather to equalise firepower, lead the decisive charge from the front."

## Controversies
- 19 Feb 1777 Congress promoted five juniors (Stirling, Mifflin, St Clair, Stephen, Lincoln) over him (state quota; Connecticut had two); Washington's letter; promoted after Ridgefield (27 Apr 1777), seniority restored Nov 1777 (Gemini).
- Unreimbursed expenses, lost receipts; Montreal goods accusations (Brown, Hazen); Board of War cleared him May 1777 ("cruel and groundless").
- Philadelphia commander Jun 1778; married Peggy Shippen 8 Apr 1779; Joseph Reed's eight charges Feb 1779; court-martial Dec 1779-Jan 1780 convicted on two minor counts (pass for the Charming Nancy; army wagons for private goods); Washington's reprimand published 6 Apr 1780.
- Treason: correspondence with André from May 1779 ("Monk"/"Gustavus"); West Point command 3 Aug 1780; £20,000 (£10,000 if it failed), received £6,315 + brigadier's commission; André captured 23 Sep 1780 near Tarrytown with papers in his stocking; Arnold fled 25 Sep to HMS Vulture; André hanged 2 Oct 1780; Arnold later burned Richmond and New London.
