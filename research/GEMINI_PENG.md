### 3. The general's method

**Peng's 3-Part Method:**
1. **Neutralize Technological Superiority:** March exclusively at night, freeze under aircraft, and utilize rugged mountain terrain to render enemy air power and heavy artillery useless. `[S2, p. 150]`
2. **Lure and Ambush:** Fake a retreat to bait an overconfident, road-bound enemy into overextending their supply lines into an invisible double-envelopment. `[S5, p. 120]`
3. **Target the Seams:** Never attack the enemy's strongest points; smash the weakest allied links in their coalition (the ROK units) to unhinge the entire line and force a systemic collapse. `[S2, p. 315]`

*Alternative phrasing 1:* Blind the enemy's air force, bait them into the deep mountains, and shatter the weakest links in their armor.
*Alternative phrasing 2:* Weaponize the night, use tactical retreats as massive strategic traps, and exploit the boundary seams of multinational coalitions.

### 6. Common myths and errors to avoid
- **Myth:** The Chinese won their battles strictly by launching mindless "human wave" attacks and drowning the Americans in bodies.
  - **Correction:** This is an enduring Western myth used to excuse the massive defeat. Peng Dehuai utilized highly sophisticated light-infantry infiltration tactics, precise night coordination, and masterful strategic deception. While their attacks were massed, they were specifically targeted flanking maneuvers, not mindless frontal rushes. **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 405]`
- **Myth:** MacArthur was caught by surprise purely because of bad weather.
  - **Correction:** MacArthur was caught by surprise due to his own profound arrogance and a massive intelligence failure; he explicitly ignored repeated ground reports of Chinese prisoners being captured prior to the massive offensives. **[VERIFIED]** `[S1, p. 45]`
