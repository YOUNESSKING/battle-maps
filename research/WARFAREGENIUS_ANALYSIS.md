## 4. Format versus Tactical Genius (from 4 transcripts: Guderian, Eisenhower, Patton, Rommel)

- **Template is TG's.** Same title formula, the same bridge line ("what set him apart from every other general of his age? Let's take a closer look at...") and the same closing CTA (favorite move + who next). Four of its six topics (Patton, Guderian, Rommel, Montgomery) are TG topics. A shared operator or template is plausible but unverified.
- **Hook differs.** TG opens on the general's side facing disaster or contempt. WG opens on a dated personal scene of the general's downfall or irony: Guderian refusing a hold order and being fired, Eisenhower missing WWI by seven days, Patton's hospital slaps, Rommel's forced suicide and state funeral. It then swings to "and yet he was the man who...". Hook length is roughly 2-3 minutes, longer than TG's 30-60 seconds.
- **Length: 18:41 to 21:19, average 19:52**, which is about 2 minutes longer than TG's sweet spot. The two best videos are the two shortest (under 19:30). The three longest are the three weakest or middling.
- **Structure.** Three moves, each ending on a one-line "creed/principle". Before Move 1 there is a backstory beat (Guderian the radio man, Rommel at Caporetto). WG adds a closing "honest ledger" section (memoir spin, war-crime caveats) and a verdict question, which TG lacks.
- **Subscribe ask** sits at the Act 1 to Act 2 seam, exactly as at TG.
- **CTA.** Same two-part comment bait; two of four drop the final subscribe line.
- **Cross-references.** Eisenhower points back to the Washington episode, Patton teases Grant at Vicksburg (likely next topic), Rommel is compared to Hannibal. Same shared-universe trick as TG.
- **Pacing/numbers.** Same stat-sheet narration with ratios (57 hours, 37 prisoners per casualty, 20 casualties for the Rhine crossing).
- **Engagement.** Weaker per view than TG: Guderian 0.58% likes and 0.33 comments per 1K views, against TG Patton 1.54% and 0.77. Its reach looks like broad suggested-feed traffic.

## 5. Title and thumbnail patterns

**Thumbnails** are one fixed template: AI-rendered aerial map diorama with blue versus red block armies, place-name tags, a portrait on one side and a red brush-stroke with a single quoted word.

| Result | Thumbnail word | Subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| Winners | "PUNISHED" (656K), "HOPELESS" (261K), "A DECOY" (112K) | injustice or paradox about the man: "Fired for Being Right", "Never Saw Combat" (Patton has none) |
| Losers | "BETRAYED" (41K), "OVERRATED?" (12K), NO WAY OUT (9K, no quote marks) | long two-clause, insult or trope: "Loved to Hate", "Admired by His Enemies, Destroyed by His Own", "Lost Almost Every Battle..." |

Key finding: **the same topic with different packaging gave very different results.** WG Guderian (656K) beat TG Guderian (112K, "The Father of Blitzkrieg") by 5.9x with near-identical content. The subtitle states a wrong done to the general, not a nickname. This extends our rule 3 from "The Man Who [verb] [rival]" to "stated injustice or paradox." Reverse case: TG Patton (897K) beat WG Patton (112K) 8x, so the hook of the opening 30 seconds and the thumbnail word still matter. Disparaging or skeptical framing ("Overrated?", "Loved to Hate") flopped. The "lost every battle, won the war" trope flopped here on Washington.
