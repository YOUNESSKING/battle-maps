### The stakes (channel rule since 2026-10-28)
**What was about to be lost or won.** At stake were the physical survival of the Confederate Army of Tennessee (the Confederacy's primary western army, ~40,000 men) and the gateway to the Deep South. The places were Chattanooga, Tennessee, and Ringgold Gap, Georgia (along East Chickamauga Creek at Taylor's Ridge).

Bragg's center on Missionary Ridge collapsed suddenly on November 25, 1863. Ulysses S. Grant's victorious Union armies (~60,000+ men under Thomas, Sherman, and Hooker) then surged south. They meant to capture Bragg's bogged-down artillery and wagon trains and annihilate the fractured Confederate army before it reached winter quarters at Dalton, Georgia.

Historians Peter Cozzens and Craig Symonds agree on what failure would have meant. Had Cleburne failed to hold Tunnel Hill on November 25 or Ringgold Gap on November 27, the Army of Tennessee would have been destroyed as a fighting force in late 1863, laying Atlanta open six months early. [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 205-216; S2, p. 345-368]

## 2. The three battles
Note on move selection: Tunnel Hill at Missionary Ridge, the rearguard at Ringgold Gap, and Pickett's Mill are universally agreed by historians to be Cleburne's three greatest independent tactical masterpieces.

### Battle 1: Holding Tunnel Hill at Missionary Ridge
**Date and place:** November 25, 1863. Northern end of Missionary Ridge ("Tunnel Hill"), Chattanooga, Tennessee. Coordinates 35.0631 N, 85.2433 W (Sherman Reservation / Tunnel Hill, Chickamauga and Chattanooga National Military Park), via NPS / Wikipedia. [VERIFIED] [S7]

**Forces:**
- **Confederate** (Cleburne's Division + attached brigades on the northern flank under Hardee):
  - Cleburne's core division: ~4,100 to 4,500 effectives. Four brigades (Polk's, Lowrey's, Govan's Arkansas and Granbury's Texas), plus Semple's, Swett's and Calvert's artillery batteries.
  - Reinforced on his flanks during the afternoon by Cumming's, Maney's and Stevenson's brigades, for a total sector force of ~9,000 to 10,000 under corps commander Lt. Gen. William J. Hardee (Cozzens [S2, p. 220, 405]; Symonds [S1, p. 195]). [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 195; S2, p. 220, 405]
- **Union** (Sherman's wing: Army of the Tennessee + XI Corps detachment):
  - ~24,000 to 26,000 troops available on the field across four divisions: Ewing's, John E. Smith's and Morgan L. Smith's of XV Corps, Jeff C. Davis's XIV Corps division, and Howard's XI Corps.
  - Of these, Sherman actually committed ~6,000 to 9,000 in piecemeal frontal assaults directly against Tunnel Hill (Cozzens [S2, p. 222-268]). [VERIFIED] [S2, p. 222-268]

**Commanders:**
- **Confederate:** Maj. Gen. Patrick R. Cleburne (division commander at Tunnel Hill, victorious); Lt. Gen. William J. Hardee (right wing / corps commander, victorious on his flank); Gen. Braxton Bragg (overall army commander, routed in the center). [VERIFIED] [S2, p. 218-220]
- **Union:** Maj. Gen. William T. Sherman (wing commander, repulsed). Assault commanders, all repulsed: Brig. Gen. Hugh Ewing, Brig. Gen. John E. Smith, Brig. Gen. Matthias Loonies [sic: Loomis] and Brig. Gen. John M. Corse (Corse severely wounded). [VERIFIED] [S2, p. 225-250]

- **Missionary Ridge, northern end:** the northern terminus of a steep, broken 400-foot ridge running north-south east of Chattanooga.
- **Billy Goat Hill** (35.068 N, 85.241 W): the northernmost detached knob, seized by Sherman by mistake on Nov 24.
- **The Saddle / Ravine:** a deep, 200-foot drop separating Billy Goat Hill on the north from Tunnel Hill (35.063 N, 85.243 W) to its south.
- **The Railroad Tunnel:** the Chetoogeta Mountain brick tunnel of the East Tennessee & Georgia Railroad, running east-west directly underneath Cleburne's position on Tunnel Hill.
- **The Switzer House Ridge:** a lower spur just west of Tunnel Hill, with an open meadow creating a deadly crossfire zone.

**What the enemy believed vs. what the general saw (key insight):**
- **Enemy belief:** on Nov 24, Sherman believed Billy Goat Hill was the main crest of Missionary Ridge. On Nov 25, he believed brute-force infantry charges up the narrow spine and western face would collapse the Confederate right [S2, p. 206, 223].
- **Key insight:** Bragg placed his main trenches in a line along the military crest in the center, where artillery couldn't depress its barrels to hit climbers. Cleburne instead sited his defenses in a convex "horseshoe" around Tunnel Hill, with intersecting fields of artillery fire across the approaches. He used the steep dead-space ledges as traps from which he launched downhill bayonet countercharges [S1, p. 196-200]. [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 196-200; S2, p. 218]

**Result as hard numbers:**
- **Tactical and strategic result:** complete Confederate tactical victory on the northern flank (Cleburne held the field entirely). It was negated strategically by the collapse of Bragg's center 2 miles south, but Cleburne's stand prevented Sherman from cutting off the army's sole retreat bridges over Chickamauga Creek [S1, p. 203].
- **Casualties at Tunnel Hill, Nov 25, 1863 (killed + wounded + missing):**

- **Location:** 2 miles northeast of New Hope Church (33.961 N, 84.788 W), on the extreme right (eastern) flank of Joseph E. Johnston's Dallas-New Hope Church line.
- **Pickett's Mill Creek Ravine:** a deep, V-shaped gorge with very steep walls, choked with tangled briars, vines, fallen timber and dense second-growth oak and pine woods. A small wheatfield clearing sits on its northern lip.
- **The Spur / Ridge:** an L-shaped, steep-sided high ridge overlooking the ravine from the south and west. Cleburne placed his trenches here, with Key's 2-gun section of 12-pounder howitzers at the bend to fire straight down the length of the gorge.
- [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 279-281; S3, p. 234-236; S7]

On May 26 they shift Cleburne's division to the extreme right flank behind Pickett's Mill Creek, where Cleburne's men dig L-shaped log breastworks along the steep ridge lip above the ravine.    - Meanwhile, warned by Kelly's cavalry skirmishers on the right, Cleburne shifts Granbury's Texas Brigade and two 12-pounder howitzers of Key's Arkansas Battery directly onto the ridge lip overlooking the ravine. **What the enemy believed vs. what the general saw (key insight):**
- **Enemy belief:** Sherman and Howard believed they had marched entirely past the Confederate right wing and would roll up Johnston's army from an undefended flank [S3, p. 235].
- **Key insight:** Cleburne used his cavalry skirmishers (Kelly) as "tripwires" in the dense forest to track Howard's exact march direction. He held his main infantry brigades out of sight until Howard committed to a blind downhill charge into the V-shaped ravine. Then he slid Granbury and Lowrey sideways behind the ridge to catch three Union brigades, one by one, in an inescapable crossfire [S1, p. 280-282]. [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 280-282; S3, p. 236]

**Result as hard numbers:**
- **Tactical and strategic result:** complete Confederate tactical and operational victory. Cleburne held the field, defeated Sherman's flanking maneuver at New Hope Church, and inflicted one of the most lopsided casualty ratios of the Atlanta Campaign [S3, p. 242].
- **Casualties at Pickett's Mill, May 27, 1864 (killed + wounded + missing):**
  - Confederate (Cleburne's official report): 448 total (85 killed, 363 wounded) [S5, Vol. 38, Pt. 3, p. 726].
  - Union: ~1,600 to 1,662 total. That is 1,457 in Wood's division alone (Hazen lost 467, Gibson 681, Knefler 309 [S3, p. 241; S4, p. 158]), plus ~150-200 in Scribner's and McLean's supporting brigades.
  - On the morning of May 28, Cleburne's men counted over 500 dead Union soldiers lying in a few acres of the ravine floor.
  - Oddly, William T. Sherman left the disaster at Pickett's Mill out of his Memoirs completely.
  - [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 284; S3, p. 241-243; S5, Vol. 38, Pt. 3, p. 726]

**Vivid, sourced details:**
1. First Lieutenant Ambrose Bierce, later the famous American author, served as topographical engineer on Brig. Gen. William B. Hazen's staff during the charge into Pickett's Mill ravine. He survived the slaughter and wrote a haunting essay, "The Crime at Pickett's Mill". He described how Cleburne's sheet of fire stripped all the bark and leaves off the saplings, and how dead Union soldiers piled up in the creek bed of what he called "the Dead Angle." [VERIFIED] [S3, p. 238, 243; S4, p. 145-155]
2. During the 45-minute firefight at the wheatfield and ravine lip, Granbury's Texans had no time to dig trenches before Hazen struck. They stood or knelt in the open scrub oaks, firing so rapidly that their rifle barrels became too hot to touch. Several Texans fought until their ammunition boxes were completely empty before Lowrey's men relieved them. [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 282; S5, Vol. 38, Pt. 3, p. 725]

## 3. The general's method
**Cleburne's 3-part method:**
1. **Personal reconnaissance of micro-terrain:** never deploy off a paper map. Personally ride every yard of the ridge crest and ravine floor before the enemy arrives, and site the artillery in concealed "keyhole" positions where two guns can do the work of twenty with point-blank double canister. [S1, p. 196, 207]
2. **Fire discipline at 50 yards:** camouflage the line with cut brush, and forbid all long-range skirmish firing that reveals your strength. Hold every musket and cannon silent until the attacking column is trapped inside a steep funnel in the terrain, within 30 to 50 paces. [S1, p. 208; S5, Vol. 31, Pt. 2, p. 754]
3. **The mobile reserve and the downhill bayonet counterstroke:** always hold one full brigade out of the line as a mobile "fire brigade" to outrace enemy flanking moves. The instant the enemy wave wavers under point-blank fire, launch a sudden downhill bayonet charge to turn a repulse into a rout. [S1, p. 200, 210, 283]

**Alternative phrasings:**
- Hide the guns in the gap, hold your fire until fifty paces, and countercharge down the slope with the bayonet.
- Master the micro-terrain, turn ravines and ridges into hidden kill-sacks, and keep a fast reserve ready to strike the enemy's flank.

## 6. Common myths and errors to avoid
- **Myth:** all of Bragg's army panicked and fled from Missionary Ridge on November 25, 1863.
  - **Accurate version:** only Bragg's center (Breckinridge's corps and part of Hindman's/Cheatham's commands) collapsed against George Thomas's afternoon charge.
  - On the northern end of the ridge at Tunnel Hill, Cleburne's division (under Hardee's wing) never lost a single foot of ground. It decisively defeated Sherman's main assault and captured 8 flags and 500 prisoners.
  - It withdrew only after dark, when ordered, because the center had given way two miles south.
  - [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 195-204; S2, p. 255-265]
- **Myth:** Sherman was never tactically defeated in the Atlanta Campaign outside Kennesaw Mountain.
  - **Accurate version:** at Pickett's Mill on May 27, 1864, Sherman ordered Howard's 14,000-man flanking column to turn the Confederate right near New Hope Church. The result was a catastrophic tactical defeat at Cleburne's hands (~1,600 Union casualties vs. 448 Confederate).
  - Sherman was so embarrassed by the lopsided defeat that he left any mention of the battle out of his two-volume Memoirs.
  - [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 284; S3, p. 242-243]
- **Myth:** Cleburne was passed over for corps command simply because he was Irish-born.
  - **Accurate version:** being foreign-born didn't help, but Symonds and Castel showed from Confederate War Department correspondence that the decisive reason was his January 2, 1864 Dalton proposal to emancipate and arm enslaved Black men.
  - Jefferson Davis passed him over for corps command repeatedly in 1864, promoting men like Stephen D. Lee and A.P. Stewart instead.
  - Maj. Gen. W.H.T. Walker secretly sent a copy of the proposal to Davis, calling it "incendiary". Davis ordered the document suppressed and never promoted Cleburne again.
  - [VERIFIED] [S1, p. 225-246]

## Fact-check list (Gemini)
1. **Missionary Ridge, Nov 25, 1863.** While Bragg's center collapsed, Cleburne's division (~4,100-4,500 at the point of attack) held Tunnel Hill undefeated against Sherman's 25,000-man wing. It captured ~500 prisoners and 8 flags while suffering only 222 casualties (42 killed, 178 wounded, 2 missing), against ~1,700-1,986 Union casualties.
2. **Rolled shells.** When the gunners were shot down and the barrels could not be depressed far enough, Texans manned the guns and rolled lit shells and boulders down the slope by hand.
3. **Ringgold Gap, Nov 27, 1863: the order.** Cleburne was ordered to hold the gap "at all hazards" and deployed 4,157 men and 2 cannons against ~12,000-15,000 men under Hooker.
4. **Ringgold Gap: the tactics.** Goldthwaite's two 12-pounders were camouflaged with pine branches until the enemy was within 150 yards. Lowrey's reserve outraced the Union flankers up the 400-foot crest of Taylor's Ridge.
5. **Ringgold Gap: the result.** He held 5 hours until the trains were safe, inflicting 509 to ~800+ casualties for 221 (20 killed, 190 wounded, 11 missing). He earned the Thanks of the Confederate Congress, and Grant halted the pursuit.
6. **Nicknames and praise.** Davis dubbed him "The Stonewall of the West"; Lee called him "a meteor shining from a clouded sky".
7. **The Dalton memorandum, Jan 2, 1864.** It proposed to emancipate and arm enslaved Black men. Davis ordered it suppressed, and Cleburne was passed over for corps command.
8. **Quote:** "As between the loss of independence and the loss of slavery..." (Dalton memorandum, OR Vol. 52 Pt. 2 p. 589).
9. **Quote (single source, staff memoir):** "If this cause that is so dear to my heart is doomed to fail..."
10. **Pickett's Mill, May 27, 1864: the trap.** Cavalry skirmishers tracked Howard's 14,000-man flanking column. Granbury and Key's two howitzers were shifted above the ravine.
11. **Pickett's Mill: the result.** ~6,500 Confederates shattered three Union brigade assaults, then made a 10 PM night bayonet charge. Union losses were ~1,600 (over 500 killed), Confederate losses 448 (85 killed, 363 wounded).
12. **Bierce and Sherman.** Bierce survived, called the ravine "the Dead Angle" and wrote "The Crime at Pickett's Mill". Sherman left the battle out of his Memoirs.
13. **Biography.** KLEE-burn; born in County Cork; a corporal in the British 41st Foot before emigrating to Helena, Arkansas.

## Main-session notes (2026-10-29): red flags for the fact-check
- **"The Dead Angle"** is the name of the salient at Cheatham Hill, Kennesaw Mountain (27 June 1864), not Pickett's Mill. Check whether Bierce used the phrase for Pickett's Mill before keeping it. Bierce's essay title "The Crime at Pickett's Mill" is real.
- **"Follow me, boys! Wait till they get within fifty yards..."** looks invented. Do not use it unless it is found verbatim in the OR or Buck.
- **"A meteor shining from a clouded sky":** check the attribution (commonly given as Lee, possibly after Cleburne's death). Use it only with a source.
- **"Matthias Loonies" is a Gemini typo:** it is Col. John M. Loomis.
- **The numbers to recheck against the OR and Cozzens/Symonds/Castel:** Union strength at Ringgold Gap (Hooker's engaged force), the casualty figures, the "8 flags", and "Sherman omitted Pickett's Mill from his Memoirs".
- **Flags:** use the owner's A1 rule from Jackson/Meade/Sherman, i.e. the US 35-star flag (from 4 July 1863) + the Confederate First National, and never the battle flag. The Second National (from May 1863) has the battle-flag canton, so don't use it either. Cleburne's divisional "blue flag with white moon" is NOT the Southern Cross, but ask the owner in the flags sheet before using it.
- **Sensitivity:** this is the channel's first Confederate hero. Keep it tactical and factual. The Dalton memorandum must be described accurately: it proposed freeing and arming enslaved men to save the Confederacy, not for abolitionist reasons. Never romanticise the Lost Cause.
