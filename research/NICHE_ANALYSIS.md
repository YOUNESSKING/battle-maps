## Topic clusters — repeated demand across ≥2 channels

1. **American Revolutionary War generals/tactics** — by far the strongest new cluster. Ravelin (a small, ~29K-sub faceless channel) has built an entire library on this and racks up 4-24x multiples routinely, plus a completely independent 269-subscriber channel hit 123x on Daniel Morgan/Cowpens. Zero coverage from Tactical Genius, Kings and Generals rarely, Epic History rarely. Nathanael Greene ("lost every battle, won the war") is a ready-made comeback-format general with three real, map-friendly battles (Cowpens is Morgan's, but Greene's Guilford Courthouse/Southern campaign fits our exact format).
2. **"The general who never lost a battle"** — a recurring, cross-era, cross-channel trope: George Thomas (2 independent small channels), Khalid ibn al-Walid (existing idea #11, now confirmed by a 3rd-party 40x hit on a channel with under 500 subs), Marlborough (274K-sub channel). Confirms this exact subtitle phrasing is a proven bankable hook, not just a Tactical-Genius pattern.
3. **Underrated/overlooked Civil War generals** — George Thomas, George Meade both get "why you've never heard of him" framing with strong multiples; Real History's Chickamauga video is the single biggest outlier found (180x). US Civil War is underused by our current list (only Sherman/Grant).
4. **Mongol invasions (not necessarily Subutai by name)** — demand is real and repeats across independent channels (History Vanguard x2, Thrilling True Stories, Historical Club at 582x) but centers on the *invaded* side's resistance (samurai, Rus knights) as much as the Mongol commander. Supports keeping Subutai but suggests a stronger hook is "the invincible horde that failed here."
5. **Byzantine (Belisarius)** — real but modest demand; most "Belisarius" search traffic is actually epic/phonk *music*, not documentary content — a warning that the keyword looks bigger than the documentary audience actually is.
6. **Tactic-*mechanism* explainers** (Battle Logic) — not our format, but the single highest multiples in the whole dataset (100-135x) come from explaining *how a tactic physically works* before naming who used it. Worth borrowing as a narration technique inside our Act structure, not as a new video type.
7. **Modern/Cold War gaps** — Falklands (Goose Green, 9.8x on Real History), Cold War Africa (Viljoen, SADF), and "how did this colonel become an army commander by the end of the war" WW2 rapid-promotion angle are all real, low-competition, and completely unclaimed by any channel in this scan.

## Underperforming / lower-priority signals

- **Napoleonic-era rival generals (Kutuzov, Davout, Blücher)**: no independent outlier hits found under their own names; all Napoleonic search volume is captured by Napoleon himself (movie clips, biography) or by "Napoleon vs X" framing. Keep Kutuzov but frame the title around beating *Napoleon* explicitly, not around Kutuzov's name recognition.
- **Rommel, Wellington, Yamashita** (already flagged in COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS.md as flops) — no evidence anywhere in this wider scan that a plain triumph-format video on these names would work better on any other channel either.
- **Belisarius by name** — real risk of confusing search traffic (dominated by music) with documentary demand; don't over-index keyword-volume tools alone for this one.
- **Off-niche outliers to ignore**: Real History's biggest hits are Tudor/true-crime/royal content, not military — don't over-read "Real History is a military channel" from its view counts.

## What to avoid

- Repeating Tactical Genius's exact 13 generals as straight "greatest moves" biography (Ridgway, Patton, Zhukov, Manstein, Guderian, Slim, Montgomery, MacArthur, Rommel, O'Connor, Bradley, Wellington, Yamashita) or our own Hannibal.
- Leading a video on a name whose search demand is mostly non-historical (Belisarius = music, Napoleon Hill = self-help) without a very sharp military-specific hook.
- Videos over ~20 minutes — nothing in this wider scan contradicts the competitor's own 15-19 minute sweet spot.
