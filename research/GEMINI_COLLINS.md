### 2. For EACH of the three battles

#### Battle 1: The Capture of Cherbourg
- **Date and place:** June 22–29, 1944. Cherbourg, Normandy, France. **Latitude/Longitude:** 49.633° N, 1.616° W. **[VERIFIED]** `[S5]`
- **Forces:**
  - *United States:* VII Corps (approx. 3 divisions, roughly 60,000 men). **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 140]`
  - *Germany:* Approx. 40,000 mixed garrison troops and naval personnel. **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 142]`
- **Commanders:**
  - *US:* Maj. Gen. J. Lawton Collins (Commander, victorious). **[VERIFIED]** `[S1, p. 220]`
  - *Germany:* Lt. Gen. Karl von Schlieben (Commander, surrendered). **[VERIFIED]** `[S4, p. 215]`
- **Terrain:** A heavily fortified deep-water port city, dominated by steep ridges to the south and anchored by the massive concrete bunkers of Fort du Roule carved directly into the rock. **[VERIFIED]** `[S4, p. 210]`
- **Phase-by-phase movement list:**
  1. **June 18:** Collins rapidly drives his corps completely across the Cotentin Peninsula to the sea, intentionally cutting off Cherbourg from German reinforcement. **[PLANNED]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 145]`
  2. **June 22, 12:40 PM:** Following a massive Allied air bombardment, Collins launches three infantry divisions simultaneously against the city's outer perimeter. **[PLANNED]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 150]`
  3. **June 25, Morning:** US troops assault Fort du Roule. Engineers scale the cliffs under fire and drop explosive satchel charges directly into the bunker ventilation shafts. **[PLANNED]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S4, p. 218]`
  4. **June 26, Afternoon:** US psychological warfare units blast surrender demands in German over loudspeakers; von Schlieben and 800 men emerge from his underground command post and surrender to US forces. **[PLANNED]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 160]`
  5. **June 29:** The final German holdouts in the harbor arsenal surrender, completing the capture of the city. **[PLANNED]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 165]`
- **What the enemy believed:** Hitler and the German high command believed the concrete fortifications of Cherbourg could easily withstand a siege for months, denying the Allies a vital port. **[VERIFIED]** `[S4, p. 212]`
- **What the general saw differently (Key Insight):** Collins realized that garrison troops trapped with their backs to the sea were psychologically brittle; instead of a slow, methodical siege, a relentless, non-stop infantry assault would collapse their morale before they could dig in. **[VERIFIED]** `[S1, p. 230]`
- **Result:** Tactical and strategic US victory (Cherbourg captured, though the port facilities were severely sabotaged by the Germans). Casualties: US ~2,800 killed, ~13,500 wounded; German ~39,000 captured, unknown killed. **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 170]`
- **Vivid details:** Upon surrendering, the German commander von Schlieben requested that the Americans broadcast his capitulation via radio to his men, but Collins flatly refused, forcing the Germans to surrender piecemeal in bloody urban combat. **[VERIFIED]** `[S1, p. 235]`

#### Battle 2: Operation Cobra (The Breakout)
- **Date and place:** July 25–31, 1944. Marigny/Saint-Lô sector, Normandy. **Latitude/Longitude:** 49.116° N, 1.083° W. **[VERIFIED]** `[S5]`
- **Forces:**
  - *United States:* VII Corps (Over 100,000 men and hundreds of Sherman tanks). **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 215]`
  - *Germany:* Panzer Lehr Division (Depleted to roughly 5,000 front-line troops in the direct bombardment zone). **[VERIFIED]** `[S4, p. 308]`
- **Commanders:**
  - *US:* Maj. Gen. J. Lawton Collins (Commander, overwhelmingly victorious). **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 210]`
  - *Germany:* Gen. Fritz Bayerlein (Commander, routed and division annihilated). **[VERIFIED]** `[S4, p. 309]`
- **Terrain:** The notorious Norman *bocage*—ancient, impenetrable hedgerows that heavily favored defense—opening slightly to the south toward the plains of Brittany. **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 220]`
- **Phase-by-phase movement list:**
  1. **July 25, 09:38 AM:** 1,500 US heavy bombers drop 3,300 tons of bombs on a tiny 3-by-1.5-mile rectangle of German positions. Due to smoke drift, bombs fall short, killing 111 American soldiers, including Lt. Gen. Lesley McNair. **[ACCIDENTAL friendly fire]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 230]`
  2. **July 25, 11:00 AM:** Despite the friendly fire chaos, Collins immediately orders his infantry to attack into the cratered lunar landscape. **[PLANNED]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 235]`
  3. **July 25, Evening:** The infantry advance is slow, struggling through massive bomb craters, and commanders urge caution. **[UNPLANNED delay]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 240]`
  4. **July 26, Morning:** Defying standard doctrine to wait for a clean breakthrough, Collins commits his heavy armor reserves (2nd and 3rd Armored Divisions) directly into the messy, congested front lines. **[PLANNED tactical gamble]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S1, p. 260]`
  5. **July 27–28:** The US armor shatters the disorganized German remnants at Marigny and bursts into the open countryside, advancing miles in hours. **[PLANNED outcome]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 265]`
- **What the enemy believed:** The Germans believed the hedgerow terrain would allow them to endlessly bleed the Americans in a static war of attrition, much like World War I. **[VERIFIED]** `[S4, p. 300]`
- **What the general saw differently (Key Insight):** Collins realized that after carpet bombing, the German lines were physically shattered but not yet broken; committing his armor early, before the infantry fully cleared the way, was the only way to induce total operational collapse before the Germans recovered. **[VERIFIED]** `[S1, p. 262]`
- **Result:** Complete tactical and strategic US victory (the Normandy breakout). Casualties: US ~4,000 casualties in VII Corps during the initial breakout; the German Panzer Lehr Division was virtually annihilated (approx. 5,000 casualties in the sector). **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 280]`
- **Vivid details:** German General Bayerlein described the carpet bombing as hellish, stating that his tanks were flipped upside down by the blasts and his infantry simply vanished into the dust; the friendly-fire death of Gen. McNair made him the highest-ranking American general killed in Europe. **[VERIFIED]** `[S4, p. 308; S2, p. 235]`

#### Battle 3: Stopping the Spearhead at Celles
- **Date and place:** December 23–25, 1944. Celles, near Dinant, Belgium. **Latitude/Longitude:** 50.233° N, 5.000° E. **[VERIFIED]** `[S5]`
- **Forces:**
  - *United States:* 2nd Armored Division (under Collins's VII Corps). **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 435]`
  - *Germany:* 2nd Panzer Division (Elements of the 5th Panzer Army). **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 438]`
- **Commanders:**
  - *US:* Maj. Gen. J. Lawton Collins (Corps Commander); Maj. Gen. Ernest Harmon (Division Commander, victorious). **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 430]`
  - *Germany:* Gen. Heinrich von Lüttwitz (Corps Commander, defeated). **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 440]`
- **Terrain:** Snowy, heavily forested hills of the Ardennes, featuring narrow, icy roads leading down toward the critical bridges over the Meuse River. **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 435]`
- **Phase-by-phase movement list:**
  1. **Dec 23:** The German 2nd Panzer Division reaches the village of Celles, just a few miles from the Meuse River, representing the absolute deepest penetration of the Battle of the Bulge. **[PLANNED by Germans]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 445]`
  2. **Dec 24, Morning:** British Field Marshal Montgomery orders Collins to keep VII Corps in a purely defensive line. Gen. Harmon begs Collins for permission to attack the overextended Germans. Collins approves the attack. **[PLANNED defiance]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 448]`
  3. **Dec 24, Afternoon:** US 2nd Armored Division executes a sweeping double envelopment around the village of Celles, physically trapping the German tanks, which are completely out of gasoline. **[PLANNED]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 450]`
  4. **Dec 25 (Christmas Day):** The weather finally clears. Allied P-38 and Typhoon fighter-bombers relentlessly strafe the trapped, immobilized German panzers. **[ACCIDENTAL weather clearance, PLANNED airstrikes]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 455]`
  5. **Dec 25, Evening:** The German 2nd Panzer Division is utterly crushed, effectively ending the German offensive threat to the Meuse. **[PLANNED]** **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 460]`
- **What the enemy believed:** The Germans believed that capturing the Meuse River bridges would crack the Allied coalition in half and allow them to drive all the way to Antwerp. **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 442]`
- **What the general saw differently (Key Insight):** While Montgomery saw a crisis requiring conservative defense, Collins and Harmon saw an exhausted, overextended enemy out of fuel; attacking an armored spearhead with counter-armor while they were immobile was safer than waiting for them to resupply. **[VERIFIED]** `[S1, p. 310]`
- **Result:** Tactical and strategic US victory (the high-water mark of the Bulge was defeated). Casualties: US losses light; German 2nd Panzer Division lost 82 tanks, 83 guns, and approx. 2,500 men killed or captured. **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 462]`
- **Vivid details:** The German panzers at Celles literally ran out of gas within sight of the Meuse River; when Harmon called Collins to ask if he could attack against Montgomery's wishes, the phrase often cited was "The bastards are in the bag, let me cut the string!" **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 448]`

### 3. The general's method
**Collins's 3-Part Method:**
1. **Relentless Momentum:** Never allow a retreating or pinned enemy time to dig in; maintain offensive pressure until their operational structure collapses entirely. `[S1, p. 230]`
2. **Commit Armor Early:** Ignore conservative doctrine; push heavy armored reserves directly into messy, contested front lines to exploit the shock of initial bombardments. `[S1, p. 260]`
3. **Exploit the Overextension:** Recognize when the enemy's spearhead has outrun its supply lines, and immediately transition from defense to a crushing envelopment. `[S1, p. 310]`

*Alternative phrasing 1:* Cut off their escape, break the line with brute force, and turn the enemy's deepest advance into a lethal trap.
*Alternative phrasing 2:* Attack before the enemy is ready, commit the tanks to the chaos, and ignore conservative orders when you spot a weakness.

### 6. Common myths and errors to avoid
- **Myth:** Patton orchestrated the breakout from Normandy. **Correction:** Patton's Third Army was not operational during the initial breakout; Collins's VII Corps executed Operation Cobra. **[VERIFIED]** `[S2, p. 235]`
- **Myth:** The German advance in the Bulge was stopped entirely at Bastogne. **Correction:** the furthest penetration was at Celles, destroyed by VII Corps / 2nd Armored Division. **[VERIFIED]** `[S3, p. 445]`
