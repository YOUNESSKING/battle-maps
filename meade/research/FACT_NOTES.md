# George G. Meade (#12): fact notes (corrections to research/GEMINI_MEADE.md)
Checked 2026-10-18 against: Official Records Series I vol. 21 (Fredericksburg: reports of Meade, Gibbon, A.P. Hill, Lane; revised
casualty return pp. 139-140) and vol. 27 pts 1 + 3 (Gettysburg: Meade's report pp. 114-119, his 4 July telegram, General Orders No. 68,
Halleck/Meade/Lincoln dispatches of 6 and 14 July), *The Life and Letters of George Gordon Meade* (1913, 2 vols, archive.org; Meade's
own letters to his wife + the family's narrative), *Battles and Leaders* vol. III (Gibbon "The Council of War on the Second Day" with
Butterfield's minutes; Hunt "The Third Day at Gettysburg"), Fremantle *Three Months in the Southern States* (1863), Haskell *The
Battle of Gettysburg*, Lincoln's unsent letter of 14 July 1863 (ABT primary-source page), NPS (FRSP "Prospect Hill", "Union
Breakthrough", auto-tour stop 5 + wayside; Gettysburg "The Angle", "Meade and Lee at Gettysburg", NPS historical handbook), American
Battlefield Trust (Fredericksburg, Gettysburg, July 2, July 3, Pickett's Charge, Falling Waters, Vicksburg pages), Lydia Leister's
1865 account (Trowbridge, via Civil War Times). Sears *Gettysburg* only second-hand (Cleveland CWRT). Wikipedia as cross-check only.

## Biography
- Born 31 Dec 1815, Cádiz, Spain (American parents living there): OK (Life and Letters, NPS). Raised in Philadelphia.
- West Point 1835, 19th of 56: OK (L&L, NPS). Resigned 1836, re-entered 1842 (NPS). Engineer: Brandywine Shoal light (Delaware Bay),
  Carysfort Reef light (Florida, from 1851) (L&L); Great Lakes survey from 1857 (WP only; double-check). Mexican War: standard (not re-read).
- Glendale, 30 Jun 1862: wounded in the forearm and a ball through the right side, exiting near the spine (L&L, his own letter: "a wound
  in the arm and back"): OK. Script: "shot through the body".
- Ages: 46 at Fredericksburg (47 on 31 Dec 1862), 47 at Gettysburg. Died 6 Nov 1872, Philadelphia (WP; cause pneumonia from memory:
  double-check).
- "Old Snapping Turtle": OK as a nickname (NPS: short temper "garnering him the nickname Old Snapping Turtle"); no 1863 source found;
  "goggle-eyed" UNSUPPORTED (search summary only). Script: "people called him the Old Snapping Turtle" (not "his soldiers").
- MYTH (Gemini hook fact 3): "most underrated and forgotten victorious general in American history" = opinion, UNSUPPORTED as a fact;
  the forgotten/underrated angle is also banned for titles (HANDOVER §10). NOT used.
- Gemini "explicitly countered Jackson's and Cleburne's flanking manoeuvres" (shared universe): UNSUPPORTED (Jackson died May 1863,
  Cleburne was in the West). NOT used. Callback used instead: Meade broke Jackson's line at Fredericksburg (true, Jackson #11 universe).

## Quotes
- "I am a dead man": UNSUPPORTED. Not in Life and Letters (full text searched). NOT used.
- Meade's real words (letter to his wife, 29 Jun 1863): "Yesterday morning, at 3 A. M., I was aroused from my sleep by an officer from
  Washington entering my tent, and after waking me up, saying he had come to give me trouble. At first I thought that it was either to
  relieve or arrest me": VERBATIM (L&L vol. 2). Officer = Col. James A. Hardie of Halleck's staff (L&L). Used.
- "Such men as you and I, dear, must be content ... without expecting justice": UNSUPPORTED (not in L&L; looks invented). NOT used.
  Nearest real letter (12 Apr 1865): "I have the consciousness that I have fully performed my duty" (not needed).
- "If Lee attacks tomorrow, it will be in your front": CORRECTED wording = Gibbon's own account (B&L III, 1887): "If Lee attacks
  to-morrow, it will be in your front." + Meade's reason: "Because he has made attacks on both our flanks and failed, and if he
  concludes to try it again it will be on our center." A recollection ~24 years later: script says "as Gibbon remembered it".
- Council: Meade "Such then is the decision" (Gibbon, B&L). Butterfield's three questions VERBATIM in B&L (minutes). Used in substance.
- Meade on Fredericksburg (letter 16 Dec 1862): "my men went in beautifully, carried everything before them, and drove the enemy for
  nearly half a mile, but finding themselves unsupported on either right or left ... they were checked and finally driven back": VERBATIM. Used.
- Lee after the charge (Fremantle, eyewitness diary, to Gen. Wilcox): "Never mind, General, all this has been my fault": VERBATIM.
  CORRECTED from "It's all my fault". Used with "a British observer wrote down".
- Hunt (B&L III), of the massed Confederate guns: "Never before had such a sight been witnessed on this continent, and rarely, if
  ever, abroad." VERBATIM. Used on a card.
- Lincoln's unsent letter (14 Jul 1863): "He was within your easy grasp, and to have closed upon him would, in connection with our
  other late successes, have ended the war. As it is, the war will be prolonged indefinitely." VERBATIM; endorsed "never sent, or
  signed". Used, said as unsent.
- Halleck to Meade 14 Jul: "the escape of Lee's army without another battle has created great dissatisfaction in the mind of the
  President". Meade the same day asked "to be immediately relieved". VERBATIM (OR). Used in substance.
- Lincoln "the whole country is our soil" (Hay diary): secondary only; NOT used. Lincoln's real 6 Jul complaint about "drive the
  invaders from our soil" (OR): not needed.

## Hook
- "Woken in the middle of the night ... three days before Gettysburg": OK. 3 a.m., 28 Jun 1863, near Frederick, Maryland (Meade's letter,
  NPS). Battle began 1 Jul. L&L: "in command but three days before the battle".
- "Greatest defensive victory of the Civil War": UNSUPPORTED superlative. NOT used. Used instead: ABT "As many as 51,000 soldiers from
  both armies are killed, wounded, captured or missing in the three-day battle" (hook line 1 = 51,000).
- Gemini hook fact 2 "the ONLY Union unit to break through Jackson's lines": CORRECTED, see Move 1 (Gibbon's division also took the
  railroad embankment beside Meade). Not in the hook.
- Pickett's Charge payoff numbers: see Move 3 (12,500; about half lost; less than an hour).

## Move 1: Fredericksburg, Prospect Hill (13 Dec 1862)
- Meade's division = Third Division, First Corps (Reynolds), Franklin's Left Grand Division; Pennsylvania Reserves; brigades Sinclair
  (wounded), Magilton, C. Feger Jackson (killed): OK (OR 21, Meade's report; his letter of 20 Dec).
- Strength "~4,500": OK as Meade's own "liberal estimate" (OR) and NPS; NPS wayside says 3,800. Script: "about forty-five hundred".
- Franklin ~60,000 men on that flank; Burnside's order said "a division at least"; Franklin sent Meade's division to spearhead: OK (NPS
  Prospect Hill). Sears (via CWRT): Franklin had 27 of the army's 51 brigades. Script: "some sixty thousand men ... one division".
- Jackson's corps ~38,000 (NPS Union Breakthrough): OK.
- Gemini "~10,000 in the immediate sector": UNSUPPORTED, not used.
- Fog: OK, A.P. Hill (OR): "About 10 o'clock ... the lifting of the fog". Morning fog on the map, lifting ~10 am: supported.
- Pelham's two guns on the Richmond Stage Road held up the advance for about an hour; then an hour-long artillery duel: OK (NPS, A.P. Hill).
- TIMES CORRECTED: Gemini "1:00 pm order / 1:30 discovery / 1:45 / 2:15 / 2:30" = invented minute-by-minute. Sources: moved out in the
  mist ~8:30 am (NPS), column formed 9-10 (Meade), infantry attack after the guns were silenced; hit the woods "around 1:00 pm" (NPS
  place page) / 1:30 pm (wayside); "for more than an hour they struggled" (wayside). Script: "a little after one o'clock"; no minutes.
- The gap: OK = about 600 yards between Lane's and Archer's brigades (Lane's own OR report: "an open space between us of about 600
  yards"; NPS "a 600-yard gap"); boggy wooded tongue of land "considered impassable", covered by "only a thin line of skirmishers"
  (NPS wayside); Gregg's South Carolina brigade 500-600 yards behind on the Military Road (Lane), surprised; Gregg mortally wounded (NPS).
- "Enemy believed the bog impassable and left it unguarded": OK (NPS: Hill "left undefended, thinking it was impassible"). But
  CORRECTED nuance: Lane reported the gap to Archer's brigade, Gregg and Hill BEFORE the battle; Hill left it.
- "Meade realised impassable terrain is the safest route / Meade saw the gap": CORRECTED. NPS: his men took the point of woods as their
  reference point and "Meade's troops accidentally discovered" the gap. What Meade did: pick the wooded tongue as the aiming point, push
  through when it was found empty, and call for support. Script says the men found it empty (honest) and the move = drive into the gap.
- "Only Union unit to break through": CORRECTED wording. NPS auto tour: "It was the only place that Union forces broke the Confederate
  line during the 1862 battle"; ABT "briefly pierces Jackson's line". Gibbon's division on Meade's right also took and held the
  railroad embankment for a time, capturing 180 prisoners (Gibbon, OR). Script: "with Gibbon's division fighting on its right ... the
  only break in Lee's line all day" (the place, not the unit), Gibbon credited.
- Prisoners: Meade "Over 300 prisoners were taken and several standards" (OR): OK. Penetration "over half a mile" (Meade report): OK.
- Reinforcements: Meade sent staff to Gibbon and Birney (OR). Birney refused twice, then Meade rode back "in high temper" and "lit into
  Birney in full voice" that a bystander said almost made "the stones creep" (Sears via CWRT, secondary); Birney sent two regiments,
  too late (Sears) / a brigade as Meade's men came out of the wood (Meade's OR report). Bullet through Meade's hat (Sears).
  "Waving his sword": UNSUPPORTED, NOT used. Script: "Meade rode back himself and, by one witness's account, cursed Birney so hard ..."
- "Reinforcements inexplicably withheld by Franklin": CORRECTED nuance: Burnside's vague order ("a division at least") read literally
  by Franklin; Doubleday's division idle, Reynolds did not call it (Sears). Script: "the orders above him were vague, and nobody sent
  the men".
- Jackson's counterattack (Early and others) drove Meade "out of the forest, across the railroad, and through the fields to the
  Richmond Stage Road" (NPS): OK.
- Casualties: CORRECTED/DISPUTED. Meade's report: 179 k, 1,082 w, 509 m = 1,770 = "40 per cent" of 4,500. Revised return (OR p. 140,
  OCR): brigades 510 + 632 + 681 = 1,823 + four batteries ~30 = ~1,853 (so Gemini's 1,853 is probably the revised total; recheck page
  image). Meade's letter: "eighteen hundred killed and wounded ... besides some four hundred missing" (early count). Script/card:
  "about eighteen hundred, four men in every ten". Gibbon's division 1,249 (OR).
- Battle totals: Union 12,653 (OR; Meade's L&L footnote); Confederate ~5,300 (Lee's return, from memory: double-check) / ABT ~6,000.
  Script: "more than twelve and a half thousand ... about twice Lee's losses" (ABT 12,500 vs 6,000).
- Result: Meade given command of the Fifth Corps (order ~23-24 Dec 1862, his letter of 26 Dec): OK.

## Move 2: Gettysburg, 2 July 1863
- Appointment 28 Jun near Frederick (see Hook). Lee's invasion of Pennsylvania (June 1863): standard. Reynolds killed 1 Jul (standard,
  not re-read: double-check). Meade arrived on the field at 1 a.m. on 2 Jul (Meade's OR report): OK. HQ = Lydia Leister's house
  behind Cemetery Ridge (Gibbon, Hunt, Leister): OK.
- Strengths: CORRECTED: ABT 93,921 Union vs 71,699 Confederate (Gemini "~90,000 vs ~70,000" roughly right). Script: "some
  ninety-four thousand ... seventy-two thousand".
- Fishhook: OK (ABT: "Culp's Hill, curving around Cemetery Hill and proceeding down Cemetery Ridge"). Interior lines: OK in substance
  (Meade's report: the enemy massed "on an exterior ridge, distant ... from 1 mile to 1 1/2 miles"; NPS handbook: Lee saw the position
  "strong if held in sufficient numbers to utilize the advantage of interior lines"). Line lengths in miles UNSUPPORTED: no miles in the
  script or on screen; shown as geometry (short inner chord vs long outer arc).
- Sickles: CORRECTED wording. Gemini "blatantly disobeys Meade's orders ... a mile forward". ABT: "advanced west, without orders";
  Meade (OR): Sickles, "not fully apprehending the instructions", advanced "some half a mile or three-quarters of a mile in front of the
  line of the Second Corps". Script: "moved his whole corps forward, without orders, up to three-quarters of a mile"; "Meade later
  wrote that Sickles had not fully understood his instructions". Time ~2 pm (secondary); Meade rode out ~3 pm and was talking to
  Sickles when the Confederate guns opened (Meade OR): OK.
- Longstreet's attack "after 4:00 p.m." (ABT): OK. Lee's plan = coordinated attacks on both flanks, Longstreet's main attack on the
  Union left (ABT). "En echelon" wording NOT found in the fetched sources: standard in Pfanz/Sears but NOT used in the voice (and no PRON
  needed); script says "brigade after brigade, from south to north".
- Meade's shifts: OK. V Corps (Sykes) sent to the left (Meade OR, ABT); Warren sent by Meade to Little Round Top, found only signal men
  there (L&L narrative; ABT); Vincent's brigade took it without waiting for orders (ABT); Caldwell's division from II Corps (L&L);
  Willard's brigade (L&L); 1st Minnesota charge, 262 men, 47 unhurt, 82 % (ABT); XII Corps (Williams: Ruger's division + Lockwood's
  brigade) pulled off Culp's Hill to the left (Meade OR, L&L); VI Corps arrived 2 pm "after a march of 32 miles" (Meade OR; L&L says 34).
  Script: "thirty-two miles".
- "Meade personally rode into the fire near the Wheatfield": CORRECTED. Family narrative (L&L, compiled by his son and grandson): Meade
  rode at the head of Lockwood's brigade "through and beyond McGilvery's guns" (the Plum Run line, north of the Wheatfield), "in person
  bringing up and placing reinforcements" and "his faithful old horse Baldy shot under him". Meade's own letter (8 Jul): the ball
  "passed within half an inch of my thigh, passed through the saddle and entered Baldy's stomach" (day not stated in the letter; the
  narrative places it on 2 Jul). Script: "by his family's account, he rode at the head of one brigade himself"; Baldy said plainly.
- "Plugs the holes at the Wheatfield, the Peach Orchard and Little Round Top": CORRECTED: the Peach Orchard and the Wheatfield were LOST
  (Sickles's line driven back); the line held on Little Round Top, Plum Run and Cemetery Ridge (Meade OR: enemy "retired in confusion
  and disorder about sunset"). Script says the forward line was lost and the main line held.
- Day 2 casualties "~20,000": UNSUPPORTED (no NPS/ABT figure; HistoryNet ~8,750 U + ~6,500 C). NOT used.
- Culp's Hill weakened when XII Corps left (Meade OR: "the line on the extreme right was held by a very much reduced force"; Geary):
  OK (used in one line).

## Move 3: Gettysburg, 3 July 1863
- Council of war, night of 2 Jul, Leister House: OK. Twelve officers in a room "not more than ten or twelve feet square" (Gibbon); three
  written questions (Butterfield's minutes): 1. remain or "retire to another nearer its base of supplies"; 2. attack or wait; 3. how
  long; all answered stay (Slocum "Stay and fight it out"); Meade "Such then is the decision" (Gibbon). Lasted ~2 hours, ended near
  midnight (Haskell). Decision card = question 1.
- "Meade predicted the attack on the centre": OK as Gibbon's recollection (see Quotes) + ABT July 3: "Meade had predicted a strike on his
  center". Script: "as Gibbon remembered it".
- Bombardment: CORRECTED. ~1 pm (Meade OR, ABT). Confederate guns: "over one hundred and twenty-five" (Meade report) / "about 150" (Meade
  telegram 4 Jul) / "some 150" (ABT); "nearly two hours" (ABT; Meade "over two hours"). "Largest artillery bombardment in North American
  history" UNSUPPORTED as stated; ABT: "ostensibly the largest such duel of the war"; Hunt: "Never before had such a sight been witnessed
  on this continent". Script: "about a hundred and fifty guns", Hunt's line on a card. On the II Corps front: "77 guns to oppose nearly
  150" (Hunt): used.
- Overshooting: OK (Hunt: "Most of the enemy's projectiles passed overhead"; ABT "consistently overshooting"). Leister House shot through,
  horses dead around it (Haskell, Hunt); Lydia Leister: "There was seventeen dead horses on my land" (her 1865 account); Butterfield
  wounded (Skelly via CWT). Gemini "dozens of staff horses" CORRECTED -> seventeen (her count). "Meade calmly stood outside while shells
  exploded": UNSUPPORTED as told; NOT used (script: the house was hit, horses killed, the staff moved; no claim about Meade's pose).
- Hunt's cease-fire: CORRECTED. Purpose (Hunt, B&L): fire "slowly, so that when the enemy's ammunition was exhausted, we should have
  sufficient left to meet the assault"; he then ordered the fire to cease; the enemy stopped too, "under the mistaken impression that he
  had silenced our guns"; Meade's aides were already bringing orders to "cease firing" ("I had only anticipated his wishes"); Meade (OR):
  "our guns, in obedience to my orders, failing to make any reply". So: conserve ammunition first, the deception was the effect. Script:
  "to save ammunition for the attack ... and the Confederates took the silence to mean the guns were finished". Hancock's II Corps guns
  (Hazard) fired out their long-range rounds (Hunt's complaint): one line.
- Pickett's Charge: ~12,500 (ABT) / "nearly 13,000" (NPS): script "some twelve and a half thousand". ~3 pm (ABT). Distance: "three-
  quarters of a mile of open field" (ABT July 3) vs "one mile" (ABT PC page): script "three-quarters of a mile". Union defenders at the
  point of attack (~6,500): not confirmed in fetched sources -> NOT given as a number; Hunt's "77 guns" used instead.
- Stannard's Vermont brigade hit the flank (Meade OR: "Stannard's brigade of the First Corps"): OK. Armistead over the wall with ~200
  (NPS) / ~250 (ABT), mortally wounded: OK. Hancock and Gibbon wounded (Meade OR): OK. "In less than one hour" (NPS): OK.
- Losses: DISPUTED. ABT 6,555 Confederate (Pickett 2,655, Pettigrew 2,700, Trimble 885), "about half"; NPS ~5,500 of nearly 13,000;
  NPS articles 40-60 %. Union ~1,500 (ABT) / nearly 1,700 (NPS). Card: CONFEDERATE ~6,000 (ABT 6,555 / NPS 5,500, ABOUT HALF) ·
  UNION ~1,500-1,700. Voice: "about half of them".
- Battle totals: Union 23,049 (OR revised return; ABT); Confederate 28,063 (ABT; other counts ~23,000): card uses ABT both, labelled ABT.
- Meade's 4 Jul telegram: "nearly 3,000 prisoners" from the charge, Armistead among them: not used.

## Ending
- Lee stayed on Seminary Ridge 4 Jul and began retreating that night (NPS); swollen Potomac; Meade's council on 12 Jul advised against
  attacking Lee's lines; Lee crossed after dark on 13 Jul at Williamsport / Falling Waters; Heth's rearguard lost 500+ prisoners on
  14 Jul (ABT Falling Waters): OK.
- Lincoln's anger: unsent letter + Halleck's telegram + Meade's request to be relieved, 14 Jul (refused): OK (see Quotes).
- Thanks of Congress, joint resolution approved 28 Jan 1864 (plaque text via a secondary site): OK (double-check list).
- MYTH Grant at Gettysburg: OK as Gemini says: Grant took Vicksburg (surrender 4 Jul 1863, ABT), a thousand miles away (approx.: say
  "far away on the Mississippi"). Meade kept the Army of the Potomac to the end of the war; Grant travelled with it from spring 1864
  (WP; standard): used, worded so as not to diminish either.
- "Meade won by luck": handled by the content (moves 2-3), no claim about "modern historians" in the voice.
- Gemini "Meade was solely responsible for the victory": CORRECTED (Hunt, Hancock, Warren, Gibbon etc. named in the script).
