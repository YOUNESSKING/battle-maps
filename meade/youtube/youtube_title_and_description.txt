George Meade's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves | The Man Who Beat Lee at Gettysburg

Robert E. Lee had beaten the Union army again and again. Then he marched into Pennsylvania, to a small town called Gettysburg. Fifty-one thousand men were killed, wounded, captured or missing in three days, and the general who had to stop him had been in command for just three days. On the last day, twelve and a half thousand of Lee's men marched across open fields straight at his centre. In less than an hour, about half of them were killed, wounded or captured.

In this video we break down George Gordon Meade's three greatest tactical moves: the breakthrough at Fredericksburg on 13 December 1862, where his Pennsylvania division found the one gap in Stonewall Jackson's line; the second day at Gettysburg, 2 July 1863, when Meade held a breaking line together by moving troops across the inside of his fishhook position; and the third day, 3 July 1863, when he told John Gibbon the night before exactly where Lee would strike, and Pickett's Charge broke against the Union centre.
