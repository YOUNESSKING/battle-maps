## Test

[MAP: e-1 | Europe, 5 June 1944: dark terrain, crimson occupied Europe, stamped emblem, white-box labels; Britain blue] June, nineteen forty-four. For four years, Hitler's empire has held the coast of Europe. Now, from England, the Allies strike back.

[MAP: e-2 | push into Normandy: transport planes cross the Channel to the Cotentin's west coast at night] Hours before the landings, about thirteen thousand American paratroopers take off in more than eight hundred planes. Their mission: jump behind the beaches in the dark, and seize the roads.

[ARCHIVE: a-1 | headline card; film in the projector frame; date card 6 JUNE 1944 · NORMANDY, FRANCE] Cloud and anti-aircraft fire break the formations apart. Most men land miles from their drop zones.

[ARCHIVE: a-2 | pathfinders photo (3D depth), film] Some fall straight into the town of Sainte-Mère-Église. One man's parachute catches on the church steeple. He hangs there for about two hours, playing dead.

[MAP: n-1 | Normandy: scattered chutes; small groups gather; blue grows around Sainte-Mere-Eglise; the road from Cherbourg is cut] But small groups find each other. Before dawn, a battalion of the five hundred and fifth takes the town, and cuts the main road to Cherbourg.

[MAP: n-2 | the town turns blue; flag; end card] Sainte-Mère-Église is often called the first town freed in France. D-Day has begun.
