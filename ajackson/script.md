# Andrew Jackson's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves (script v1, 2026-11-23)
Format: Top 3 commander film (hook + 20 s background + 3 moves + short ending), ~13-14 min, owner voice clone.
Look: 3D Vicksburg engine, line-block troops, muzzle flashes on every shot, 3 cloud dives (S01 Chalmette, S03 Horseshoe Bend, S05 Villeré night).
Facts: `research/FACT_NOTES.md` (every number, date and quote below is logged there). Tags: [Sxx | shot]. No "]" inside a tag. Nothing on screen before it is said.
Hook (1a + 1c): stakes of THIS story (the river and the city that fed half of America), one continuous dive onto the plain below New Orleans, the number + result by ~15 s, the twist (peace already signed, not yet ratified), back into the danger, the general enters as the turn with ONE human detail (the British sword scar), name slam + 3 snap cuts.
Devices (1d): each move opens on the enemy plan + "in other words"; "But... instead" twist; set-up -> pay-off: the ladders (S06 -> S07) and the scar (S01 -> S08); day-by-day dates in moves 2-3; cliffhanger at each move end.
Engagement (1b): subscribe bridge after move 1; ONE "What would you do?" on 23 Dec (S05); final 1-2-3 question + "which commander next" (19th century: no family line).
Sensitivity: Horseshoe Bend is told respectfully, both sides named (Red Stick Creeks; Jackson's Cherokee and Lower Creek allies), the cost and the Treaty of Fort Jackson said plainly; Jackson's later record (Indian removal) said honestly in the ending. No hero worship.
Sides: Americans BLUE (with their Cherokee and Lower Creek allies), British RED, Red Stick Creeks RED. Flags: US 15-star 15-stripe flag (1795-1818); British Union Flag (1801); Red Sticks: no flag (banner with a plain red pennant and no portrait).
Method card ("THE OLD HICKORY METHOD"): 1. CLOSE EVERY DOOR · 2. STRIKE FIRST · 3. MAKE THEM COME TO YOU.

## S01 HOOK (0:00 - ~1:00)

[S01 | gulf wide from altitude, late light: the British fleet (ships) moving north across the Gulf toward the Mississippi mouth, the river glowing as one line up to NEW ORLEANS; the camera pushes in on the river; NEW ORLEANS label on its word]
In November eighteen fourteen, a British fleet sailed for New Orleans. The city guarded the mouth of the Mississippi, the main way to the sea for the whole American West.

[S01 | cloud dive 1 down onto the plain below the city: emerge over the river, the plantations, the cypress swamp; red line-blocks in long lines on the open fields facing a thin brown ditch line; date plate 8 JANUARY 1815 on its words]
On the eighth of January, eighteen fifteen, more than five thousand British regulars attacked a ditch and a wall of mud below the city.

[S01 | the red lines advance; the whole American line flashes with muzzle fire (each shot with its sound), cannon smoke; red blocks break and fall back; number card 2,000+ BRITISH on its words, ticks; then 71 AMERICAN CASUALTIES under it]
In about half an hour, more than two thousand of them were killed, wounded or captured. The Americans lost seventy-one, killed, wounded or missing.

[S01 | hard cut, dark parchment overlay: two wax seals, GHENT on its word; stamp SIGNED on "signed"; caption NOT YET APPROVED on its words]
And the strangest part? Two weeks earlier, in Ghent, both sides had signed a peace treaty. America had not yet ratified it, so the war went on, and no one in New Orleans knew.

[S01 | back to the plain: date plate 23 DECEMBER 1814; red blocks walk out of the cypress swamp onto the river fields; the city label far up the river; distance ruler 9 MILES on its words]
Two weeks before the battle, it had looked hopeless. A British army had come out of the swamps unseen, about nine miles from the city.

[S01 | the general enters: banner with no portrait yet riding down into the city; a dark card on "sword": a thin red scar line drawn across it, AGE 14 and BY HIS OWN ACCOUNT small]
The general sent to save it was worn down by illness and painfully thin. And he carried a scar from a British officer's sword, from when he was a boy of fourteen.

[S01 | NAME SLAM: ANDREW JACKSON (gold 3D title on its words), the banner portrait revealed; then 3 snap cuts, each on its words: Horseshoe Bend from above (river bend + wall), the night attack (muzzle flashes in the dark, the schooner firing), the mud line with smoke]
His name was Andrew Jackson. He would trap an enemy fortress inside a river bend. He would attack a British army in the dark, on the night it landed. And he would stop a veteran British army behind a wall of mud. These are Andrew Jackson's three greatest tactical moves.

## S02 BACKGROUND: THE CREEK WAR (~1:00 - ~1:40)

[S02 | gulf wide, 1813: the Creek country on the Coosa and Tallapoosa rivers, Tennessee to the north, the Gulf ports; Jackson banner in the north; date plate 1813]
In eighteen thirteen, Jackson was a general of the Tennessee militia. That spring, on a hard march home, his men had started calling him Old Hickory, because he was as tough as the wood.

[S02 | Fort Mims marked on the Alabama River (on its word), a red pulse; Red Stick markers across the Creek towns]
That same year, a civil war had split the Creek Nation. One faction, the Red Sticks, rose against the Americans and against the Creeks who sided with them. That August, they stormed Fort Mims, north of Mobile, and killed hundreds of people inside.

[S02 | blue arrow from Tennessee south into the Creek country; the camera eases toward the Tallapoosa; the bend appears small; label TOHOPEKA on its word]
Jackson marched south. By the spring of eighteen fourteen, about a thousand Red Stick warriors had fortified a town called Tohopeka, inside a great loop of the Tallapoosa River that the Americans called the Horseshoe.

## S03 MOVE 1: HORSESHOE BEND (~2:15 - ~4:45)

[S03 | cloud dive 2 down onto the bend: emerge over the Tallapoosa loop, the wooded hills, the town on the low ground at the inner end, the log wall across the neck; date plate 27 MARCH 1814]
The Red Stick plan was simple. The river guarded the town on three sides. On the fourth, across the neck of the bend, they had built a wall of logs nearly four hundred yards long, taller than a man, and pierced with two rows of firing holes. Canoes were drawn up on the riverbank behind the town, ready.

[S03 | the camera rises to show the whole bend and the single neck; the wall pulses; canoes along the inner bank glow]
In other words, an army that charged the wall would be shot down in a zigzag of crossfire. And if the wall did fall, the defenders could still slip away across the river in their canoes.

[S03 | Jackson's blue blocks arrive from the north and halt before the neck; two guns unlimber on the small hill; banners JACKSON and the Red Stick banner]
On the twenty-seventh of March, eighteen fourteen, Jackson arrived with about three thousand men, among them about five hundred Cherokee and a hundred Lower Creek allies.

[S03 | the twist: blue mounted blocks + allied blocks curve out east and south across the river and line the far bank all around the bend (wide arrow); the canoes highlighted]
But he did not throw his army at the wall. Instead, he closed the back door first. He sent General John Coffee, with the mounted men and the allies, across the river to line the far bank all around the bend.

[S03 | close on the neck: the two guns fire at the wall (flash, smoke, sound each shot); impacts on the logs; little damage]
Then, at about half past ten in the morning, Jackson's two small cannon opened fire on the wall. For two hours they pounded it. The logs held.

[S03 | across the river: small allied blocks wade and swim; canoes taken; canoes cross to the town side; fire and smoke rise from the town (fires)]
On the far bank, the Cherokee did not wait. Some of them swam the river under fire, took the Red Stick canoes, and paddled them back. Then they crossed in force, landed behind the town, and set it on fire.

[S03 | Jackson sees the smoke: blue lines step forward to the wall; drum; muzzle flashes along the wall (each with its sound); blue blocks cross the wall]
Jackson saw the smoke rising behind the wall, and ordered the charge. The Thirty-ninth United States Infantry went over first. Its major was shot dead on the parapet. A young officer named Sam Houston took an arrow in the thigh and kept going.

[S03 | inside the bend: red blocks driven back toward the river; the far bank lined with blue; no way out; smoke over the town]
The Red Sticks fought on through the afternoon, behind logs and brush in the bend. They did not surrender. When they ran for the river, Coffee's men were waiting on the other side.

[S03 | dimmed map; result card on its words: ABOUT 800 RED STICK WARRIORS KILLED; JACKSON'S ARMY: 49 KILLED, 154 WOUNDED (incl. allies); source: Jackson's report, 31 March 1814]
About eight hundred Red Stick warriors were killed, out of about a thousand, and some three hundred and fifty women and children were taken prisoner. Jackson's army lost forty-nine killed, eighteen of them Cherokee.

[S03 | treaty card: TREATY OF FORT JACKSON, 9 AUGUST 1814; the Creek lands shaded and taken away on the gulf map, including the allied lands]
It broke the power of the Red Sticks. And that summer, in the Treaty of Fort Jackson, Jackson forced the whole Creek Nation to give up some twenty-two million acres, about half of its land, taking it even from the Creeks who had fought beside him.

[S03 | method card: THE OLD HICKORY METHOD, 1. CLOSE EVERY DOOR on its words]
That was the first part of his method. Before you hit the front door, close every other door.

[S03 | gulf wide: Jackson banner moves south to Mobile; a red arrow of ships appears in the Gulf from the south-east on its word]
Jackson was made a major general in the United States Army, and given the defence of the whole Gulf coast. And in the autumn of eighteen fourteen, the British were coming.

[S03 | the bend fades; label NEXT: THE NIGHT ATTACK; subscribe button clicks on "subscribing"]
Horseshoe Bend was only the beginning. So how do you stop an army of British regulars who have already landed nine miles from your city? That's the kind of question we answer here, and subscribing is how you catch the next one. Here's how Jackson did it.

## S04 THE BRITISH COME (~4:45 - ~6:00)

[S04 | gulf wide: British fleet at Jamaica arrow -> Pensacola -> Mobile; Jackson banner at Mobile then Pensacola stamp on its word; then the fleet arrow to Lake Borgne]
The British plan was bold. A fleet from Jamaica would land an army near New Orleans, take the city, and with it the mouth of the Mississippi. Jackson guessed they would strike at Mobile first. In November he marched on Pensacola, in Spanish Florida, where the British had landed, and drove them out.

[S04 | push in to the New Orleans wide area: Lake Borgne, the city, the river; Jackson banner arrives in the city on 1 DECEMBER]
On the first of December, he reached New Orleans. He found a city with few soldiers, old forts, and a dozen ways in through the bayous and the lakes.

[S04 | Lake Borgne: five small blue gunboats in a line; British boats (many) row in; flashes; the gunboats turn grey (captured) on 14 DECEMBER; then MARTIAL LAW stamp over the city on its words]
On the fourteenth of December, the British rowed their boats across Lake Borgne and captured the five American gunboats guarding it. Now Jackson was blind on the lake. He put the city under martial law, and called for every man who could carry a gun.

[S04 | the route: red boats along Bayou Bienvenue through the marsh; red blocks file through the swamp onto Villeré plantation; date plate 23 DECEMBER; a small figure (marker) escapes out of a window and runs]
In other words, the British could land wherever they liked. They found a bayou no one had blocked, rowed up it through the marsh, and late on the morning of the twenty-third of December, their advance guard walked out of the cypress swamp onto a sugar plantation on the river. They captured the owner's son. He slipped out of a window, crossed the river, and raced to warn Jackson.

## S05 MOVE 2: THE NIGHT ATTACK, 23 DECEMBER (~6:00 - ~8:15)

[S05 | the plain by day: red line-blocks camped in the fields of Villeré plantation, the river beside them, the city far upriver; distance ruler]
The British plan was to rest, bring up the rest of the army, and march on the city. Their advance guard, some seventeen hundred men, were tired from days in open boats. They lit fires and settled in for the night.

[S05 | the city: Jackson banner; quote card on its words: "GENTLEMEN, THE BRITISH ARE BELOW. WE MUST FIGHT THEM TONIGHT." small caption AS LATER TOLD]
In the city, Jackson heard the news in the early afternoon. He is said to have told his officers: Gentlemen, the British are below. We must fight them tonight.

[S05 | decision card on the dimmed map: WHAT WOULD YOU DO? A: DIG IN AND WAIT FOR THEM  B: ATTACK TONIGHT; TELL US IN THE COMMENTS]
Now stop for a moment, and put yourself in Jackson's place. Your army is militia, volunteers, and a few regulars. The enemy are British regulars, and more of them are landing every hour. Do you dig in before the city and wait for them? Or do you attack tonight, before they are ready? Tell us in the comments what you would have done. And then listen to what Jackson did.

[S05 | cloud dive 3 into the night plain: moonless, campfires along the British lines; the blue schooner CAROLINA slides silently down the river and anchors opposite the camp; date plate 23 DECEMBER]
He did not wait. Instead, he marched his army down the river road that same evening. And first, he sent the schooner Carolina drifting down the river in the dark, until she anchored right beside the British camp.

[S05 | the Carolina's broadside: flashes from the river (each shot with its sound), impacts in the camp, fires scatter; red blocks fall back from the riverbank toward the levee]
At about half past seven, her guns opened fire on the campfires. The British threw themselves down behind the levee, and their fires went out.

[S05 | blue blocks advance along the river road from the north; Coffee's blue mounted blocks swing out wide toward the swamp side and hit the British flank; muzzle flashes along both lines in the dark]
Then Jackson attacked. His regulars and the city's volunteers came down the river road, while Coffee's Tennesseans swung out toward the swamp and struck the British from the side.

[S05 | close and confused: night smoke rolling over the fields, blocks of both colours mixed in small groups, flashes everywhere, the camera low]
In the dark and the smoke, the battle broke into a hundred small fights. Men could not tell friend from enemy. They fought with muskets and bayonets among the sugar cane stubble, for hours, in the dark.

[S05 | dawn grey: blue blocks pull back north up the river road to a thin ditch line (the Rodriguez Canal, label on its word); red blocks hold their camp; result card 23 DECEMBER: BRITISH 277 KILLED, WOUNDED OR MISSING / AMERICANS 213]
Neither side broke. Each lost between two and three hundred men. Before dawn, Jackson pulled back two miles to an old canal called the Rodriguez.

[S05 | the British side: Keane's banner; red blocks halted; the city label far up river; a stamp HALTED on its word]
But the British had been stung. They had been attacked in their own camp, at night, by an enemy they could not count. Their commander decided to wait for the rest of the army before he moved. That night bought Jackson what he needed most: time.

[S05 | method card: 2. STRIKE FIRST]
That was the second part of his method. When the enemy lands, hit him first, before he finds his feet.

[S05 | the British reinforcements arrive: many red blocks file out of the swamp; Pakenham banner on his name; a big number on its words]
On Christmas Day, the army's new commander arrived. Sir Edward Pakenham was Wellington's brother-in-law, famed for his charge at Salamanca in Spain. By early January, with fresh regiments, he had about eight thousand men. Now he would attack.

## S06 THE MUD LINE (~8:15 - ~9:45)

[S06 | day, the Rodriguez Canal from above: blue blocks digging along the line from the river to the swamp; the rampart rising (works grow); label RODRIGUEZ CANAL]
Jackson's men dug. Along the Rodriguez Canal, from the river to the cypress swamp, about a mile long, they piled up a wall of earth and mud, only about five feet high but in places twenty feet thick, with the canal as a ditch in front of it.

[S06 | batteries placed along the line one by one, guns appear (art units); one battery highlighted, label BARATARIANS on its word]
Along it, Jackson placed eight batteries of guns. One of them was manned by the Baratarians, the smugglers and privateers of Jean Lafitte, who had chosen to fight for the city rather than against it.

[S06 | the defenders file in: blue blocks of different groups take places along the wall: regulars, Louisiana militia, free men of colour, Tennessee and Kentucky riflemen, Choctaw scouts at the swamp edge; labels small, each on its word]
Behind the wall stood regulars, Louisiana militia, two battalions of free men of colour, Tennessee and Kentucky riflemen, and Choctaw warriors at the edge of the swamp. It was one of the strangest armies in American history.

[S06 | across the river: the schooner Carolina burns (fire on the water) on 27 DECEMBER; then 28 DECEMBER red columns advance, blue guns fire, red columns pull back]
The British tested it. On the twenty-seventh of December their guns set the Carolina on fire. The next day they advanced on the line, and the American guns, with the ship Louisiana firing from the river, drove them back.

[S06 | 1 JANUARY 1815: British batteries in the field built of sugar barrels; an artillery duel along the line (flashes, impacts both sides, each with its sound); the British batteries go silent]
On New Year's Day, Pakenham tried his guns. His gunners built batteries in the night, partly out of barrels of sugar. When the fog lifted, they opened fire. Within a few hours, the American guns had shot most of them to pieces.

[S06 | Pakenham's banner over a map of the plan: arrow 1 (west bank, boats on the river), arrow 2 (main column near the swamp), arrow 3 (river column); ladders + fascines icons on the main arrow; stamp LADDERS on its word]
Pakenham had one choice left: storm the wall. His plan had three parts. Across the river, a force would land on the west bank and turn the American guns there on Jackson's line. On the east bank, one column would attack along the river. And the main column would hit the line near the swamp. To cross the ditch and climb the wall, they would carry bundles of sugar cane and ladders. Remember those ladders.

## S07 MOVE 3: 8 JANUARY 1815 (~9:45 - ~12:15)

[S07 | before dawn, fog: the plain grey; red blocks forming in the fields; boats stuck in a narrow canal by the river (west bank force late); date plate 8 JANUARY 1815]
Before dawn on the eighth of January, the plan began to go wrong. The canal dug to bring the boats to the river had partly collapsed. The west bank force was hours late, and got across with only a fraction of its men.

[S07 | the twist: the American line behind the wall, silent; blue blocks waiting; the camera along the wall at eye level]
Jackson's answer to all of it was simple. He would not attack. Instead, he would let them come to him, across several hundred yards of open ground, into his guns.

[S07 | the signal rocket rises (flash in the sky); red lines advance in the fog; the fog begins to lift (fog fades); the red lines are suddenly in full view of the wall]
At first light, a rocket went up, and the British advanced through a thick fog. Then, as they crossed the field, the fog lifted. The British columns were in plain view, about five hundred yards from the guns.

[S07 | the American guns fire down the line (flashes + smoke, each with its sound), impacts in the red lines; then the riflemen: muzzle flashes ripple along the wall (volleys, each with its sound)]
The American batteries opened fire. Historians believe those guns did most of the killing. Then, as the British came closer, the men behind the wall opened fire, rank after rank. A sheet of fire ran along the parapet.

[S07 | the main red column near the swamp reaches the ditch: no ladders (stamp NO LADDERS on its words); red blocks bunch at the ditch, then break back]
And the ladders? The regiment that was supposed to carry them had left them behind. The main column reached the ditch with almost nothing to cross it. Men stood in the canal and tried to climb the wall with their hands.

[S07 | Pakenham's banner rides forward into the fire; the banner falls (fades grey); Gibbs, Keane cards small on their words]
Pakenham rode forward to rally them. He was hit, and hit again, and killed. The general leading the main column was mortally wounded, and a third general was badly wounded crossing the field.

[S07 | the Highlanders: a red block crosses the field obliquely toward the column, under fire, then halts and stands; label 93RD on its word; the block shrinks]
The Ninety-third Highlanders marched across the field to help, and halted in front of the guns. They stood there under grapeshot, waiting for orders that never came. Their colonel was killed where he stood.

[S07 | by the river: a red column storms the small redoubt at the river end of the line, takes it for a moment, then is wiped out; the redoubt back to blue]
By the river, one British column did storm an advanced battery in front of the line. Its officers were shot down inside it, and the survivors were driven out.

[S07 | the field: red blocks streaming back; the wall smoking; stamp HALF AN HOUR on its words]
The main attack was broken in about half an hour. The last British general on his feet called it off.

[S07 | across the river: the small west bank force breaks the American line there (red arrow), then halts; it is ordered back]
Across the river, the small force that had finally landed broke through the weak American line on the west bank. It was too late. With his generals down, the British commander ordered it back.

[S07 | result card on the dimmed field: BRITISH 2,037 KILLED, WOUNDED OR MISSING (3 generals); 71 AMERICAN CASUALTIES (BOTH BANKS); source line]
The British had lost more than two thousand men, killed, wounded or captured, three generals among them. Jackson's army lost seventy-one killed, wounded or missing, on both banks of the river. Behind the wall itself, six men were killed.

[S07 | method card: 3. MAKE THEM COME TO YOU, completing the method]
That was the third part of his method. Never fight the enemy where he is strongest. Choose the ground, and make him come to you.

## S08 ENDING (~12:15 - ~13:30)

[S08 | gulf wide: the British fleet sails away south on 18-27 JANUARY; date plate rolls to 16 FEBRUARY 1815; the treaty card returns: APPROVED on its word]
Ten days later the British army slipped away to its ships. On the sixteenth of February, the United States Senate approved the Treaty of Ghent. The battle did not change a word of it.

[S08 | Jackson banner over New Orleans; a soft push; then the gulf map with the Creek lands shaded and small arrows west across the Mississippi on "removal", muted]
But it made Andrew Jackson the most famous man in America, and it carried him, in time, to the White House. There, he signed the Indian Removal Act. Under it, tens of thousands of Native Americans were forced from their lands in the South, among them the Cherokee, who had fought for him at Horseshoe Bend.

[S08 | callback: the scar card from the hook returns briefly over the field of 8 January]
The boy who had been cut by a British sword at fourteen had beaten a British army. The method had three parts: close every door, strike first, and make them come to you.
