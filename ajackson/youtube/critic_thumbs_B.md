# Critic B: Jackson thumbnails vs the field (feed_mock.jpg)

Competition (vidIQ "andrew jackson battle of new orleans"): Uncovered "Jackson's Masterpiece" (705k, Jackson portrait left + blue/white map + 1815 + yellow tag), Kings and Generals (film strip, redcoat + American), ABT (navy, painted soldiers), Project Past, Mr Beat reaction, In4. All plain "BATTLE OF NEW ORLEANS" documentaries; most are painted soldiers or generic title text. Second search (top 3 tactical moves) returned only noise, so no direct format rivals.

## Scores (click appeal vs this field)
- A MAIN: 6/10. B: 7.5/10. B wins the feed.

## A MAIN
- Layout is the SAME idea as Uncovered (portrait right/left + map + stamp). At 168x94 it blends in; only the red band stands out. Word reads, but small and bottom-left.
- The painted aerial with ships and sunset is the prettiest picture in the row, yet low contrast; the unit blocks are tiny and read as texture.
- Story problem: "HE DUG." but no dug line is visible (a hairline at best). The arrow runs from the red British toward the American line, so it reads as the British attack, not Jackson's move.
- Cheap tells: tan glow/rim and soft dark gradient around the sepia portrait, sad flat face; tags are fine. Ships on the river are decor, not wrong, but unverified for 8 Jan 1815.
- Accuracy: British in six dense columns with a gap before the line is plausible; check New Orleans is placed downriver-correct (city is up-river from Chalmette; the tag is at the far side, ok).

## B
- Huge cream "HE DUG." is the only word-led thumbnail in the field; reads at 168x94. Colour portrait is clean, no halo, no sticker boxes.
- Weakness: the payoff icon (mud wall, cannons, cotton bales) is small and the lower-right is busy and dim at feed size; the seal-to-wall arrow explains little. "THEN THE BRITISH ATTACKED" is small, no British visible.
- Accuracy: cotton bales on the wall are a known myth-prone detail (bales were in the line early, then removed after they caught fire/shattered under fire); palisade/cannon smoke ok. Drop bales or the label "THE MUD WALL" is fine without them.
- Word test: "HE DUG." is mundane rather than a contradiction. Viewers believe Jackson "won", not that he "fought in the open". It intrigues only with the title (after the peace) and is weaker than "HE WON." / "HE RAN."

## Ordered fixes
1. Make B the lead test variant (or at least equal); it stands out. Keep A as the alternate.
2. A: show the dig. Draw a visible dark ditch/earthwork line with a shadow along the blue line, make blue blocks larger, drop half the red columns.
3. A: reverse/relabel the arrow: either remove it or make it the hero's move (Jackson's line, sappers digging), not a British charge. Enlarge "HE DUG." about 25 percent and lift it off the arrow tail.
4. B: enlarge the mud wall icon (about 1.4x), brighten it, remove cotton bales; add a few small red British blocks or a red arrow pointing at it so "then the British attacked" is visible.
5. Portrait: both A and B use the same worried face; crop tighter on B, remove A's glow rim, use a firmer expression if a better period portrait exists. Optionally test the word "HE WAITED." or "HE DUG IN." against "HE DUG."
