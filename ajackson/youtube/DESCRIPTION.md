On the eighth of January, 1815, more than five thousand British regulars attacked a ditch and a wall of mud below New Orleans. In about half an hour, roughly two thousand of them were killed, wounded or captured. The Americans lost seventy-one, killed, wounded or missing, on both banks of the river. And the strangest part: a peace treaty had already been signed in Ghent two weeks earlier. America had not yet ratified it, so the war went on, and no one in New Orleans knew.

This is how Andrew Jackson, Old Hickory, won his three greatest battles, told on animated 3D maps: the trap at Horseshoe Bend, where he shut the Red Stick Creek fortress inside a river bend; the night attack on the British camp of 23 December 1814, on the night the army landed; and the wall of mud on the Rodriguez Canal, where he made a veteran British army come to him.

Close every door. Strike first. Make them come to you.

The treaty was signed on 24 December 1814 and approved by the US Senate on 16 February 1815; the battle did not change a word of it. The video also says plainly what Horseshoe Bend cost the Creek nation, and where Jackson's career led: the Indian Removal Act.
