## 1. Treaty of Ghent

1. Signed Ghent, **24 Dec 1814** by the British and American commissioners. SOURCE: WP-GHENT; NPS Jean Lafitte "The Treaty of Ghent" page `nps.gov/jela/learn/historyculture/the-treaty-of-ghent.htm`.
2. Arrived at the Foreign Office 26 Dec; **ratified by the Prince Regent at Carlton House on 27 Dec 1814** (WP-GHENT citing Updyke 1915 p.360; the NPS Saint-Gaudens page also says Britain ratified 27 Dec). Wikipedia's lead says the Regent "signed it into law 30 Dec"; that is the formal assent, a different step. SAY "ratified by Britain on 27 December".
3. Copies of the treaty reached **New York 11 Feb 1815** (HMS Favorite), Washington 14 Feb. **US Senate approved unanimously 16 Feb 1815**; Madison exchanged ratifications with the British envoy **17 Feb**; proclaimed 18 Feb. SOURCES: WP-GHENT; US Senate page `senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/treaties/senate-approves-treaty-of-ghent.htm`; NPS "short-term-memory" article. (Some NPS pages say 15 or 18 Feb; Senate's own page and Updyke give 16/17.)
4. Official confirmation reached Jackson **13 Mar 1815** (Latour p.219: general orders that day revoked martial law and ordered hostilities to cease). The NPS Chalmette timeline gives 15 Mar. Jackson had written to Lambert on 6 Mar that he "had reason to believe" the treaty was ratified (Latour p.218). SAY "mid-March".
5. **Exact wording advice:** the battle was fought after the treaty was *signed* (24 Dec) and *ratified by Britain* (27 Dec) but **before the US ratified (16-17 Feb)**, and before either side at New Orleans knew. "Signed but not yet ratified" is correct but incomplete: it was not in force until the exchange of ratifications on 17 Feb. Better: "the peace treaty had been signed 15 days earlier, but nobody in Louisiana knew, and it did not take effect until February."
6. Bathurst's secret orders to Pakenham (24 Oct 1814) told him to keep fighting even if peace rumours arrived, because the US might not ratify (Grodzinski 2011 p.1 via WP-BNO). Historians (Carr, Latimer, Davis) reject the old claim that Britain planned to keep Louisiana: the Prime Minister's letter of 23 Dec 1814 shows London wanted peace quickly (WP-BNO "Background", "Aftermath").

## 2. British force and aims

7. Aim: seize the mouth of the Mississippi / New Orleans, win bargaining strength; Cochrane convinced the Admiralty (Aug 1814). Bathurst did not intend permanent occupation. SOURCE: NPS-HH29 (d); WP-BNO.
8. Commanders: Vice-Adm. Sir Alexander **Cochrane** (fleet); Maj.-Gen. Sir Edward **Pakenham** (36, Wellington's brother-in-law, replaced Ross who died at Baltimore; **arrived on the field 25 Dec**); Maj.-Gen. John **Keane** (led the vanguard, 1st Bde); Maj.-Gen. Samuel **Gibbs** (2nd Bde); Maj.-Gen. John **Lambert** (3rd Bde/reserve; landed 5 or 6 Jan; took command when Pakenham fell); Col. William **Thornton** (85th; west-bank force); Lt.-Col. Thomas **Mullins** (44th Foot, the fascines and ladders); Col. Alexander **Dickson** (artillery). SOURCE: WP-BNO; NPS-HH29 (d, m).
9. **Numbers (they conflict because some count headcount, some effectives, some include WI regiments or sailors):**
   - 25 Dec: 6,660 on the rolls, **5,933 effective** (Monthly Return Dec 1814, Bamford 2014, via WP-BNO).
   - Keane's vanguard 23 Dec: ~1,800 (Remini 1999) / ~1,680 present when fighting began (NPS-HH29 h).
   - With Lambert's 7th (≈681-750) and 43rd (≈785-820), Jan 5-6: "little short of **8,000**" (Gleig 1827 p.320, includes sailors/marines).
   - Assault force 8 Jan: about **5,400** (NPS-HH29 m); WP-OOB-GB sums ~5,208 for line infantry + dragoons with 8 Jan strengths.
   - Lake Borgne boat force: ~980 sailors/marines (WP-LB). 
   SAY "about 8,000 British landed over two weeks; about 5,000-5,400 in the 8 January assault".
10. Regiments at the 8 Jan assault (WP-OOB-GB, Pringle letter strengths): 4th (747), 7th Fusiliers (750), 21st (800), 43rd (820), 44th (427), 85th (298 on west bank), 93rd Highlanders (775), 95th Rifles (5 companies, 3rd Bn, 296), 1st & 5th West India Regiments (755 / 570 effective, 25 Dec), 14th Light Dragoons (295 dismounted), Royal Artillery, Engineers, Navy brigade, Marines.
11. **"Wellington's veterans" caution.** NPS-HH29 (d) says only "his troops included veterans of the wars against Napoleon." I could **not** find any source giving a count of Peninsular War veterans. The NPS/other pages describe the 4th, 21st, 44th and 85th as veterans of the **1814 Washington/Baltimore (Chesapeake) campaign**. WP-BNO states the 7th and 43rd were "veterans of Wellington's Peninsular War army" and that Pakenham kept them in reserve. The 95th Rifles were a Peninsular rifle battalion (general knowledge; not confirmed in fetched text). The 93rd Highlanders is described in the same sources as a crack unit; **do not claim Peninsular service for it** (unverified either way). SAY "veteran British regiments, some of them Wellington's Peninsular veterans, led by his brother-in-law." Never "Wellington's veterans" for the whole army and never a head count.
12. Wellington on the failure (WP-BNO quoting his letter): "I cannot but regret that he was ever employed on such a service or with such a colleague", blaming Cochrane. The 21st and 44th were thought less reliable by Pakenham's aide Wylly.

## 3. Jackson and the defenders

13. **Pensacola:** Jackson attacked Spanish-held Pensacola, where the British (Col. Edward Nicolls) had landed, **7-9 Nov 1814**; British withdrew. SOURCE: Wikipedia "Battle of Pensacola (1814)" (30.4107,-87.2112); NPS-HH29 (e) says "in November". Fort Bowyer (Mobile) had beaten off a British attack in Sept 1814 (NPS-HH29 e).
14. **Arrived in New Orleans 1 Dec 1814** (NPS "Andrew Jackson declares martial law" story; Battlefields.org). Proclamation to free men of colour issued from Mobile **21 Sept 1814** (Battlefields.org primary source; it led to Daquin's and Lacoste's battalions).
15. **Martial law:** most sources 15-16 Dec 1814 after the Louisiana legislature hesitated and Lake Borgne was lost (NPS says 16 Dec; WP-LB says 15 Dec; Latour p.70 gives no date, Appendix 21). SAY "mid-December". Lifted 13 Mar 1815 (Latour p.219).
16. **Defenders (Latour's roster for 8 Jan, pp.150-152; his figures are the best primary):** 7th US Infantry 430 (Maj. Peire); 44th US Infantry 240 (Capt. Baker); Marines 50 (Lt Bellevue); Plauché's uniform battalion 289; Lacoste's Louisiana free men of colour 280; Daquin's St Domingo free men of colour 150; New Orleans Rifles ~30 (Capt. Beale); Carroll's Tennesseans plus Adair's Kentuckians ~1,600 on the left; Coffee's Tennessee brigade ~500 (plus "a very small number of Indians", Latour p.97); reserve cavalry ~230; Baratarian gunners at Battery 3. **About 4,000 men in all, only 3,200 actually on the line.**
   - Other totals: 4,698 on the left bank + 546 on the right bank (Roosevelt 1900 via WP-BNO); 3,300 on 23 Dec and 3,961 on 1 Jan (Davis 2019 via WP-BNO). WP-OOB-US right bank: Arnaud's 2nd Louisiana brigade 640 + Gray's Kentuckians 400 (250 armed) + Patterson's ~30.
   - Kentucky militia: ~2,250 arrived 4 Jan, only ~550 armed (NPS-HH29 l); Jackson himself said a third were armed (Davis 2019 p.197).
   - Choctaw under Pierre Jugeat: small (no sourced head count found; Latour "very small"). Say "a handful of Choctaw".
17. **Baratarians:** Jean Lafitte offered his services; **Dominique You and Renato Beluche** commanded Battery 3 (two 24-pdrs, served by French mariners) (Latour p.148; NPS-HH29 e). Jackson's 21 Jan orders thanked them and the Lafittes (WP-BNO). Jean Lafitte also advised extending the line into the swamp (NPS-HH29 l).
18. **Ships:** schooner **Carolina** (Capt. Henley, Cdre Patterson aboard) and ship **Louisiana**. Carolina was blown up by British hot shot **27 Dec**; her crews and the 32-pounder (Battery 4) went to the line (Latour; NPS-HH29 i; WP-BNO). Louisiana retired upriver, fired ~800 rounds in the 28 Dec cannonade (Roosevelt via WP-BNO).
19. Free men of colour, Choctaw, Baratarians, Marines, regulars, Tennessee/Kentucky/Louisiana militia: all on the 8 Jan line (NPS-HH29 l). No source supports "pirates won the battle"; see Myths.

## 4. Lake Borgne, 14 Dec 1814

20. Lt. **Thomas ap Catesby Jones**, 5 gunboats (Nos. 156, 163, 5, 23, 162) plus tender Alligator and schooner Sea Horse (scuttled 13 Dec). Capt. **Nicholas Lockyer**, ~980 sailors and marines in **42 rowboats** (40 with carronades). Action 10:39 am-12:30 pm, 14 Dec. All 5 gunboats captured; US 6 killed, 35 wounded (41 killed/wounded), crews ~182-225 captured; British 17 killed, 77 wounded. SOURCE: WP-LB (Jones 12 Mar 1815, Lockyer 18 Dec); NPS-HH29 g. Cost: Jackson lost his "eyes"; New Orleans panicked.
21. Anonymous warning letter from Pensacola, dated 5 Dec, to Patterson (not to Jackson), received 7 Dec (WP-LB; NPS-HH29 g).

## 5. The British route and the distance

22. Fleet at Cat Island/Ship Island anchorage; troops rowed ~60 miles to **Pea Island (Isle aux Pois)** at the Pearl River mouth, ~30 miles east of the city (Brown 1969 via WP-BNO); ferrying took **6 days and nights**, ~10 hours per transit; boats could carry only ~2,000 men at a time (Gleig/Reilly via WP-BNO).
23. Three routes weighed: Rigolets (Fort Petites Coquilles) into Pontchartrain; Chef Menteur Road (narrow, Jackson guarded it); **Bayou Bienvenue - Bayou Mazant - Villeré's canal** (taken). SOURCE: Daughan 2011 p.381 via WP-BNO. Keane's failure to take the Chef Menteur road is called a missed chance (Lambert 2012 p.344).
24. The Americans' picket at the mouth of Bayou Bienvenue was captured ~midnight 22 Dec (NPS-HH29 h). The advance reached the end of **Villeré's canal about 4 a.m. 23 Dec**, rested, marched ~10 a.m.; **one mile to the wood, nearly two more to the river**; the advance reached Villeré's house **about 11:30 a.m.**; **Gabriel Villeré escaped through a window** and crossed the river by pirogue (Latour pp.86-87). Col. Denis de la Ronde also escaped. Jackson learned in force about **1 p.m.** (NPS-HH29 h).
25. **Distance:** Latour p.95 "within nine miles of New Orleans". Remini 1999 "9 miles"; Daughan "seven miles south of the city". NPS-HH29 i: the Rodriguez Canal is **6 miles from the centre of New Orleans** and 2 miles up the road from the night-battle site. (WP-BNO says the canal is "4 miles south", Patterson's letter heads the Marine battery "five miles below".) SAY: "about nine miles from the city at the Villeré plantation; the Rodriguez Canal was about five or six miles out."

## 6. The night attack, 23 Dec 1814

26. **Jackson's words.** The line "By the Eternal, they shall not sleep on our soil!" followed by "Gentlemen, the British are below. We must fight them tonight." is quoted in NPS-HH29 h as "an oft-repeated story" with "an eye of fire and an emphatic blow on the table"; Langguth (*Union 1812*) and a Parton-based 1888 reprint repeat it. **Neither phrase is in Latour** (searched the full text: no hit) and I found no contemporaneous source. Status: traditional, **secondhand, unverifiable**. SAY: never as a verified quote. If used, on screen "Jackson is said to have told his staff: 'Gentlemen, the British are below. We must fight them tonight.'" (the second sentence is the better-attested and more plausible).
27. **Time:** about **7:00 p.m.** Carolina anchored "on the brink of Villeré's batture ... within musket-shot"; at **7:30 p.m.** she opened fire, ten minutes of "dreadful fire"; she killed or wounded ">100" British in Latour's estimate (pp.95; NPS-HH29 h "about 7:30 p.m."). Fog later ended her firing (NPS-HH29 h). Jackson waited and attacked about half an hour after Carolina's fire began (WP-BNO via Davis).
28. **Attack plan:** Jackson on the road along the levee (artillery on the road, Marines on the river bank, then the 7th and 44th in line, Plauché's and Daquin's battalions and a few Choctaw on the left); **Coffee** (part of his Tennessee brigade, the New Orleans Rifles, Hinds' Mississippi dragoons) circled left behind De la Ronde's plantation, guided by Col. De la Ronde, dismounted and advanced on the British right/rear, pushing into Lacoste's and Villeré's grounds (Latour pp.96-99; NPS-HH29 h). Captain Beale's rifle company wandered into a British party in the dark.
29. **Fog and smoke:** darkness "increased by the fog, and by the smoke" blown into the Americans' faces by a southern breeze; the fight dissolved into groups; both wings withdrew near De la Ronde's house to wait for daylight (Latour p.97; NPS-HH29 h).
30. **Numbers engaged:** US ~**2,131** (Quimby 1997 via WP-BNO; NPS says "some 2,000"). British ~**1,680** present at the start with reinforcements (21st, 93rd, 4th) arriving during the fight (NPS-HH29 h; WP-BNO). British commander Keane reported his own figure as 1,800 vanguard (Remini). 
31. **Casualties 23 Dec:** US **24 killed, 115 wounded, 74 missing = 213** (Adjutant-General Butler, 16 Jan 1815, in Latour App. p.lviii-lix). British (Keane): **46 killed, 167 wounded, 64 missing = 277** (WP-BNO via Keane despatch; NPS-HH29 h). Jackson took ~66 prisoners (Hayne). Most American missing were Coffee's men lost in the dark (Latour App.).
32. **Outcome:** tactical draw. Quimby: a British "tactical victory, which enabled them to maintain their position" but they "were disabused of their expectation of an easy conquest" (WP-BNO). Keane stayed in camp at Lacoste/Villeré for reinforcements instead of marching up the road (Remini 1977 p.262). Jackson **withdrew at 4 a.m. 24 Dec** to the Rodriguez Canal "about two miles behind the field of battle" (Latour p.112) and began the earthwork. SAY "the British were stopped five or six miles from the city; Jackson bought time."

## 7. The Rodriguez Canal line

33. **What it was:** a disused millrace between the Macarty and Chalmette plantations, river to cypress swamp. Latour p.145-146: silted, banks fallen, grass-bottomed, "rather the appearance of an old draining ditch." Dimensions: **10-20 ft wide, 4-8 ft deep** (NPS-HH29 l); Wikipedia "Rodriguez Canal" says "about four feet deep by twenty feet wide" (Cowan 2001).
34. **Length:** **815 toises ≈ 1.6 km, "about a mile"**, "somewhat more than half" from the river to the wood, the rest bent back into the cypress swamp (Latour p.147). The straight river-to-wood stretch is therefore ~0.5-0.6 mile; "0.6 mi" in the brief is the river-to-swamp figure. SAY "about a mile of earthwork, from the river to the swamp".
35. **Rampart:** earth thrown carelessly up from the rear onto the canal bank; fence pales lined it; "in some places twenty feet thick at the top, though hardly five feet high"; raised in stages by different units in rain and cold; by 8 Jan proof against cannon to the wood; in the wood only a double log wall filled with earth (Latour pp.146-147). Whole position beyond the wood extended at a right angle into the swamp (Lafitte's advice, NPS-HH29 l). Enslaved labourers and townspeople were drafted for tools and digging (NPS-HH29 i; Latour on Boisgervais line). Built from the morning of 24 Dec; "semblance of a field fortification" by that night (NPS-HH29 i).
36. **Cotton bales: partly a myth.** Latour p.134: "The cheeks of the embrasures of our batteries were formed of bales of cotton, which the enemy's balls struck and made fly in all directions." NPS-HH29 k: bales were placed **around the gun openings**, and on **1 Jan** were "blown out of place and set on fire". Latour p.159 and others: the rampart itself was **earth, not cotton**. The 1892 engraving of a cotton-bale wall is wrong (HMDb marker 97183). Whether all bales were removed afterwards I could not confirm (NPS HRS Part I has a section "the question of cotton bales" that I could not open). SAY "mud and earth; cotton bales only in places around the gun embrasures".
37. **Eight batteries (Latour pp.147-148, distances in yards from the previous battery):** 
   - No.1 on the road inside the levee, 70 ft from the river: 2 brass 12-pdrs + 6-inch howitzer (Capt. Humphreys, US Artillery);
   - No.2 (+90 yd): one 24-pdr (Lt Norris, USN; Carolina crew);
   - **No.3 (+50 yd): two 24-pdrs, Capts. Dominique (You) and Beluche, French mariners**;
   - No.4 (+20 yd): the Carolina's **32-pdr** (Lt Crawley, USN);
   - No.5 (+190 yd): two 6-pdrs (Col. Perry, Lt Kerr);
   - No.6 (+36 yd): 12-pdr (Gen. Garrigues de Flaujac, Lt Bertel of the Francs); fired the first shot on 8 Jan;
   - No.7 (+190 yd): 18-pdr culverin + 6-pdr (Lts Spotts and Chauveau, US Artillery); this battery ripped Gibbs' column;
   - No.8 (+60 yd): small brass carronade (corporal of artillery, Carroll's militia).
   Total per WP-BNO: one 32, three 24s, one 18, three 12s, three 6s, one 6-inch howitzer. Beyond No.8 the line formed "a receding elbow" and ran knee-deep in swamp mud to the cypress swamp, held by Carroll's and Coffee's men (Latour p.149).
38. **Redoubt:** an unfinished advanced redoubt on the river in front of the line's right end, held by a company of the 7th (Lt Ross) with a 44th detachment serving the guns (Latour p.150).
39. **Dec 28, reconnaissance in force:** Pakenham's two columns, one on the road under the guns of Louisiana, one by the swamp; Louisiana's enfilading fire and the Carolina-gun line stopped the road column; the swamp column nearly broke the militia on the line's left, then withdrew. British **16 killed, 38 wounded, 2 missing** (Stovin/Gleig via WP-BNO). American: **Butler's return says 9 killed, 8 wounded = 17** (Latour App.); WP-BNO says 7 killed, 8 wounded. SAY "a probe that failed".
40. **1 Jan 1815 artillery duel:** foggy morning; British guns, fully manned from the fleet's artillery, opened after hogshead-of-sugar breastworks were built (NPS-HH29 k; the sugar ran into the mud when balls pierced the barrels). First targets: Macarty house (Jackson's HQ, shelled while officers breakfasted). US guns answered; British ammunition and carriages gave out; an afternoon probe by Rennie's fusiliers was beaten off. Duration **~3 hours** (WP-BNO) / British fire ceased 1 p.m. (NPS-HH29 k). British 32 killed, 44 wounded, 2 missing; US **11 killed, 23 wounded** (Butler, Latour App.). Pakenham decided to wait for his whole force.

## 8. 8 January 1815

41. **Plan** (Pakenham's memorandum; Forrest 1961 via WP-BNO): Thornton (85th, seamen, marines, planned ~1,400-1,500 men) crosses the Mississippi at night, storms the west-bank battery (Patterson's Marine Battery), and turns its guns on Jackson's line. Gibbs (2nd Bde: 4th, 21st, 44th) = main attack on the **right of the British line, by the swamp** end near Coffee/Carroll; Keane (1st Bde: 93rd, West India regiments) with Rennie's light companies along the river; Jones' light infantry on the far right by the swamp; Lambert in reserve. Columns started about **650 yd** from the line (Reilly 1976 p.286). **Attack at first light.**
42. **West-bank delay:** the boat canal (Villeré to river) caved in and the dam failed; sailors dragged boats through mud; Thornton crossed **8 hours late by his own despatch (assessed 12 h late by Patterson 2008)** with only enough boats for **~560 men** (298 of the 85th + 200 seamen/marines per Thornton; "about 600" per Cochrane; 460-560 elsewhere) = **a third** of the intended force. The frontal attack was **not postponed**; Pakenham hoped the west-bank force would still divert fire. SOURCE: WP-BNO citing Thornton, Gleig 1827 p.323, Brown 1969, Hickey 1989. Reilly blames Pakenham's staff for not waking him to tell him the west-bank landing had failed.
43. **Signal and fog:** Congreve rocket (Latour: from the wood's edge toward the river; WP-BNO: **6:20 a.m.**, Reilly p.289) was the signal; Battery No.6 fired the first shot "at the same instant" (Latour p.154). NPS-HH29 m: "fog covered the field and lasted longer near the river." WP-BNO: "began in darkness and heavy fog, which lifted as the British neared the main American line." **No source gave a clock time for the lift.** SAY "the fog lifted as they closed to about 500 yards".
44. **44th's fascines and ladders:** Mullins ignored Gibbs' written order to locate them on the 7th, halted at an advanced battery ~800 yd from the line instead of the redoubt where the materials lay; sent ~300 of his 427 men back at the double; the advance guard arrived in disorder without them (Davis 2019; WP-BNO). Mullins was found "half a mile to the rear when he was needed at the front." NPS-HH29 m: "led his men past the redoubt where they were stored."
45. **Gibbs' main column:** came on in a column of ~60 men front, "obliquing to the left" to avoid Battery 7, **broke after ~25 minutes of fire** (Latour p.155; NPS-HH29 m "about 25 minutes"); rallied and tried again; broke again. **Pakenham** was hit while trying to rally the 44th (grapeshot), mortally on the second hit, helped to mount a horse; **Gibbs mortally wounded**; **Keane wounded** crossing the field with the 93rd; Lt.-Col. Timothy **Jones** mortally wounded in the swamp-edge flank attack; Col. **Dale** (93rd) killed. SOURCES: WP-BNO; NPS-HH29 m; Latour p.156.
46. **Rennie's column:** pushed forward by the road/levee, drove in the pickets and **seized the advanced redoubt** over the parapet and through the embrasures; Rennie ("The enemy's works are ours" - NPS-HH29 m) killed climbing the main breastwork, shot down by Beale's riflemen and the 7th Infantry (Latour pp.157-158). All its men dead or captured within about half an hour (WP-BNO).
47. **93rd Highlanders:** ordered by Keane to leave the river column and cross the open field obliquely to join Gibbs' right; stood in the open under grape at ~100-300 yd with "no orders to advance further or retreat" (WP-BNO). Column lost its Colonel and more than 60 killed, 101 wounded (WP-OOB-GB: 63 k, 101 w).
48. **Lambert** in reserve took command when Pakenham, Gibbs and Keane fell; saw the battle as hopeless and ordered the withdrawal (NPS-HH29 m; WP-BNO). Reserve (650 yd from the line) took 182 casualties (Ritchie 1969 via WP-BNO). British fire stopped ~9 a.m.; Latour: "By half after eight in the morning the fire of the musketry had ceased" (p.159); American batteries ceased at 2 p.m.
49. **Duration:** the main infantry assault on the left lasted **~25-30 minutes**; the whole engagement on the east bank ~**two hours** (Latour p.157: "the whole hour that the attack lasted"; WP-BNO "two hours, main assault 30 minutes", Owsley 2000). SAY "about half an hour of assault; less than five minutes within musket range" (Dickson via WP-BNO).
50. **American range of fire:** cannon opened at ~500 yd, riflemen at 300, muskets at 100 (WP-BNO). Latour: Plauché, Daquin, Lacoste and three-quarters of the 44th "did not fire at all" because the attack never reached their sector.
51. **West bank:** Morgan (Louisiana militia) had been reinforced by ~200 Kentuckians (arrived 4 a.m.); Morgan split ~700 men between two positions about a mile apart. Thornton's force overran them and the Marine Battery. Patterson spiked his guns. At ~10 a.m. Lambert learned the west bank was taken but Dickson reported it would need 2,000 men to hold; Lambert **evacuated it**. US court of inquiry (Carroll): the militia's conduct "not reprehensible"; Reilly blames Morgan. Thornton badly wounded; 85th lost 2 killed, 41 wounded, 1 captured; Navy 2 dead (incl. Capt. Rowland Money) + 18 wounded; Marines 2 dead + 16 wounded. SOURCE: WP-BNO (Thornton despatch; Reilly 1976 pp.293-307; Latour App. Patterson letter).

## 9. Casualties and what "71" means

52. **British, 8 Jan:** "**291 killed, 1,262 wounded, 484 missing/captured = 2,037**" (Remini 1999, Davis 2019, via WP-BNO's Lambert/Stovin returns; commonly 2,037, sometimes 2,036/2,042). WP-OOB-GB summing line infantry returns gives 288 k, 1,055 w, 417 missing; that excludes the west-bank, artillery, navy, WI regiments. NPS-HH29 m: "nearly 2,000 killed, wounded and missing"; Jackson's contemporary estimates were far higher (1,500 then 2,600 via Hayne; Latour: "not less than three thousand"). SAY "about 2,000 (2,037 by the British return)".
53. **American, 8 Jan, from the primary return (Adjutant-General Butler, 16 Jan 1815, printed in Latour Appendix p.lix-lx):** header "ACTION ON BOTH SIDES THE RIVER, 8th JANUARY": **13 killed, 39 wounded, 19 missing = 71.** Footnote in the return itself: "Of the killed, wounded and missing on this day, but **six killed and seven wounded in the action on the east bank of the river**, the residue in a sortie after the action, and in the action on the west bank." So:
   - **71 = whole day, BOTH banks, including 19 "missing" (mostly Morgan's/Kentucky militia who fled on the west bank) and a sortie after the action.** It does not mean 71 dead.
   - The **main fight on the east bank (the Rodriguez line itself) cost the Americans 6 killed + 7 wounded = 13 casualties.**
   - Jackson's first despatch (9 Jan) says "seven killed and six wounded" (Latour App.; NPS-HH29 m "7 killed and 6 wounded"). Also see Jackson's "not exceeded ten killed and as many wounded".
   - WP-BNO adds "left bank 11 killed, 23 wounded" (Tatum 1922) and "right bank 2 dead, 11 wounded, 19 missing"; those two sets do not reconcile exactly with Butler's note (e.g., wounded 23+11=34, not 39). Treat as a different count.
   - Danger: "13" shows up twice with different meanings (13 killed on both banks vs. 13 killed+wounded in the east-bank fight).
54. **Thumbnail "71" claim:** defensible only as **"71 American casualties (killed, wounded, missing) on 8 January, both banks"** against **2,037 British**. It is **not** defensible as "71 killed" or as the east-bank figure, and "13/39/19" must not be read as "13 killed on the Rodriguez line". Safer for impact: "6 killed" is true for the east-bank main fight but needs context; best simple on-screen pairing: "**About 2,000 British casualties. 71 American** (13 killed)."
55. **Campaign totals (official returns, same sources):** US **333** (55 killed, 185 wounded, 93 missing; Butler); British **2,459** (386 killed, 1,516-1,521 wounded, 552 missing; Gazette/Stovin returns summed; James 1818 gives 385/1,516/591 incl. 39 dragoons missing in a boat). Per engagement: 23 Dec US 213 / GB 277; 28 Dec US 17 (or 15) / GB 56; 1 Jan US 34 / GB 78; 8 Jan US 71 / GB 2,037. Note the British effective strength fell from 5,933 (25 Dec) to 4,868 original troops (Jan monthly return) (WP-BNO).

## 10. Aftermath

56. **British withdrawal:** council of war three days after the battle; troops marched out of Villeré's camp the night of **18-19 Jan**, boats by Jan 19; Jackson's despatch of 19 Jan: enemy "precipitately decamped" at midnight, leaving ~80 wounded and 14 pieces of heavy artillery; he did not pursue in force. Final troops re-embarked **27 Jan**; fleet left Cat Island 4-7 Feb for Dauphin Island/Mobile. Pakenham and Gibbs were shipped home. SOURCE: WP-BNO (Jackson 19 Jan despatch; Gleig; James).
57. **Fort St. Philip:** two bomb vessels with four mortars bombarded it from **9 Jan** until about **18 Jan** and withdrew (WP-BNO; Roosevelt's "a week or two"); Jackson counted the failure to pass the fort a second relief (Jan 19 despatch).
58. **Fort Bowyer** (Mobile Point) captured by the British **12 Feb 1815** (WP-BNO); HMS Brazen arrived there **13 Feb** with word of the treaty (WP-GHENT, Tucker 2012 p.250). Official peace news reached New Orleans **13 Mar** (Latour; NPS says 15 Mar).
59. **What it meant (honest):** it did **not** change the treaty; Ghent restored the status quo, so a British victory would not by itself have transferred Louisiana (NPS Jean Lafitte treaty page: "the treaty returned captured land to its original owners"). It mattered for ratification politics only atmospherically: news of the victory reached the US and boosted morale at about the time the Senate unanimously ratified (16 Feb); no historian cited here claims it caused ratification, so avoid any causal claim. It made Jackson a national hero, helped ruin the Federalists after the Hartford Convention, and set up the "Era of Good Feelings" (WP-BNO "Political outcomes"). The 8th of January was a US federal holiday 1828-1861 (Stoltz 2012 via WP-BNO). A weaker, often-repeated line, "Britain would have kept Louisiana", is rejected by Carr/Latimer/Davis (item 6).

## Myths to avoid

1. **"After the war had ended"**: the treaty was signed but not ratified; the war was still on. Say "signed" and "in force 17 February".
2. **Cotton-bale rampart:** the wall was mud and earth with fence pales and logs; cotton bales only around some embrasures, blown out and burnt on 1 Jan (items 36).
3. **"71 killed" or "only 13 Americans died"**: 71 = all casualties on both banks incl. 19 missing; 13 killed is both banks; the east-bank fight alone was 6 killed + 7 wounded (item 53).
4. **"Wellington's veterans" as a headcount**: no source gives one; the 1814 Chesapeake veterans (4th, 21st, 44th, 85th) are not Peninsular men; do not claim the 93rd was (item 11).
5. **"Jackson said 'By the Eternal...'"** as established fact: tradition only (item 26).
6. **Lafitte's personal heroics:** Lafitte offered ships, flints and advice (extend the line into the swamp; a 19th-century tribute quoted in WP-BNO (Brannan 1823/Bunner 1855): "pirate though he was"); **Dominique You and Beluche** commanded Battery 3; no source puts Jean Lafitte at the guns or credits him with the victory. Jackson's 21 Jan orders praised them; the Baratarian share of the artillery is two 24-pounders of eight batteries.
7. **"Pakenham was shot by a Tennessee sharpshooter" / "Kentucky long rifles killed 2,000":** Reilly (via WP-BNO) argues artillery did most of the killing: the riflemen (95th) lost only 11 killed because they were hard to hit with cannon; Dickson says the British were within musket range under five minutes. Avoid "the rifles won".
8. **"Everyone in the line fought":** Latour says Plauché, Daquin, Lacoste and three-quarters of the 44th did not fire at all (item 50). The decisive fire came from the batteries on the line's right/centre and Carroll's Tennesseans/Kentuckians by the swamp.
9. **"The British were slaughtered in one charge":** there were two main attempts by Gibbs' column, a third small effort under Maj. Wilkinson (21st), and the Rennie column took the redoubt first; the west-bank force *won* (item 51).
10. **"Pea Island to Villeré in one move":** it took 6 days to ferry them and ~6 hours more from the canal head to the river; do not imply a surprise "the same day".
11. **"Jackson saved the Louisiana Purchase":** the British government did not plan to keep it (item 6); say "saved New Orleans" or "stopped the invasion".
12. **Casualty ratio inflations:** Jackson's own despatches claimed 1,500-3,000 British casualties; the British return gives 2,037. Use the return.
13. **"The monument is on the line":** the Chalmette Monument stands behind (west of) Line Jackson; the National Cemetery is on the British side.
