# FACTCHECK: ajackson/script.md (v1, 2026-11-23)

Method: every spoken line and every on-screen number/text checked against FACT_NOTES_neworleans.md and FACT_NOTES_horseshoe.md, plus web checks this pass (Bladensburg/New Orleans regiments, Mississippi trade, Jackson's illness, Junaluska/removal, Fort Mims and Fort Jackson acreage). Items marked "notes only" rest on the notes' primary sources (Latour 1816, Jackson's letters in NPS-CLR, NPS HH29), not re-fetched. Houston's arrow wound and Montgomery's death were taken from NPS-CLR p.21 via the notes; the Handbook of Texas was not re-fetched.

Verdicts: OK = keep. SOFTEN = true but over-stated or unsupported in detail; use replacement. FIX = wrong, contradictory or misleading; must change.

## Additions to FACT_NOTES

- **N / stakes line**: Jefferson to Livingston, 18 Apr 1802: New Orleans is "the one single spot" whose possessor is America's "natural and habitual enemy" (Founders Online). Right of deposit (Treaty of San Lorenzo 1795), withdrawn by the Spanish intendant 18 Oct 1802, caused an uproar in the West (NPS Jefferson NEM page; Madison Papers). SAY "the main outlet to the sea for the whole American West". Never "half of America".
- **N / regiments**: Ross's Bladensburg-Washington force (24 Aug 1814) had the 4th, 21st, 44th and 85th (light companies); the 4th, 21st, 44th were in Gibbs's column on 8 Jan, the 85th on the west bank. SAY "veterans of the army that burned Washington". The 7th, 43rd and 93rd were not in Maryland.
- **N / Jackson's health**: arrived 1 Dec 1814 weakened by dysentery, gaunt and sallow (Remini). Do not use "could barely stand".
- **N / martial law**: declared 15-16 Dec, after Lake Borgne; put after the 14 Dec line in the script.
- **N / Keane's expectation**: no source found for "expected militia to run"; use Quimby's "expectation of an easy conquest".
- **N / distance of assault**: columns started about 650 yards out; "half a mile" is wrong.
- **N / Senate**: unanimous approval 16 Feb, exchange of ratifications 17 Feb; the treaty took effect 17 Feb.
- **H / Fort Mims**: EoA: about 700 attackers, about 250 killed; other estimates higher; 30 Aug 1813.
- **H / Treaty acreage**: nearly 22 million (NPS), 23 million (LoC), over 21 million (EoA); about 8 million were allied lands.
- **H / Junaluska**: the story that he saved Jackson at Horseshoe Bend is a disputed legend; fact: a Cherokee warrior in the battle, held in a stockade in 1838, later returned and was granted NC citizenship and 337 acres in 1847 (NCpedia; NC DNCR). Do not repeat the legend or his alleged remark to Jackson as fact.
- **H / Removal Act**: signed 28 May 1830; Cherokee removal 1838-39 (Van Buren's term) after the Treaty of New Echota (1835); about 4,000 Cherokee died; about 60,000 across the Five Tribes were removed in the 1830s (NPS Trail of Tears).
- **H / Old Hickory**: origin is the early 1813 Natchez Trace march home, so the nickname predates the Creek War battles.
