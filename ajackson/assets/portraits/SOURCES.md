## Not obtained
- **John Keane**: the only Commons file ("Lord Keane.png", 237x338) is a modern CC BY-SA 4.0 drawing by "Gaelicbow", not usable under our public-domain rule. Look for a 19th-century engraving (NPG London or Commons "Keane, John, 1st Baron Keane" by William Salter?) before use [verify].
- **Sam Houston, young**: no public-domain portrait of Houston at age 21 (1814) was found. The earliest widely used ones are 1830s-50s (e.g. Commons "Samuel Houston by Henry Dexter", the 1858 Buttre engraving "Samuel_houston.jpg"). Use a map banner/silhouette for 1814, or a clearly captioned later portrait.
- **Jean Lafitte**: NO authentic portrait exists (none from life is documented). The "portrait" in the Rosenberg Library, Galveston (Commons "Anonymous portrait of Jean Lafitte, early 19th century", disputed) is of doubtful identity; do not label it as a likeness. Show a silhouette or a period map banner.
- **William Weatherford (Red Eagle)**: no authentic portrait from life is known; Commons "Chief Red Eagle.jpg" is a later imagined image.

## Period flags (for map markers; draw, do not invent)
- United States 1814: the 15-star, 15-stripe flag (Flag Act of 1795, in use until 1818); the Star-Spangled Banner flag at Fort McHenry (Sept 1814) is the same design.
- Britain: the Union Flag of 1801 (crosses of St George, St Andrew and St Patrick); regimental colours were a king's colour (Union Flag) and a regimental colour in the regiment's facing colour [general knowledge].
- Red Sticks: no documented flag or banner; the name comes from red-painted war clubs (NPS/Battlefields Trust). Do not invent one; use a plain neutral marker. Allies wore white deer tails to be told apart (NPS).
