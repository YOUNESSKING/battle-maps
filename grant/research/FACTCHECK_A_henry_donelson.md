## Best vivid details that ARE verified
1. Jan 1862: Halleck cut Grant off in St. Louis; Grant "returned to Cairo very much crestfallen". Then on 28 Jan he wired "if permitted, I could take and hold Fort Henry on the Tennessee", backed by Foote. (ME Ch. XXI)
2. Fort Henry stood partly "two feet deep in water", and Tilghman had already marched out nearly all his men; the navy took it with ~90 men. (ME Ch. XXI)
3. Grant on Pillow: "I had known General Pillow in Mexico, and judged that with any force, no matter how small, I could march up to within gunshot of any intrenchments he was given to hold." (ME Ch. XXII)
4. "I felt that 15,000 men on the 8th would be more effective than 50,000 a month later." (ME Ch. XXII)
5. Foote's flagship St. Louis was hit about 60 times; the pilot was killed, the wheel shot away; Foote wounded; two ironclads drifted back helpless; the Confederates telegraphed a great victory to Richmond. (ME Ch. XXII)
6. Grant was away at Foote's flagship when the breakout hit; he returned to "men standing in knots", muskets but no ammunition "while there were tons of it close at hand". (ME Ch. XXII)
7. The Webster line and "Fill your cartridge-boxes, quick, and get into line" which "acted like a charm". (ME Ch. XXII)
8. C. F. Smith "going in advance himself" through the abatis. (ME Ch. XXII)
9. Confederate regiments marched out at 2:30-4 a.m. with "three days' rations" in haversacks. (OR7 Cheairs and Palmer)
10. Floyd (ex-Secretary of War of Buchanan) feared a treason trial; Floyd and Pillow left on steamers while "the authorities at the capital were reading" Pillow's dispatch of great success. (ME Ch. XXII)
11. Grant to Halleck 16 Feb: "We have taken Fort Donelson and from 12,000 to 15,000 prisoners ... 48 pieces of artillery, 17 heavy guns". (OR7)
12. Buckner's reply: "ungenerous and unchivalrous terms" and Grant's meeting: Buckner said he "would not have got up to Donelson as easily" had he commanded; Grant replied he would not have tried it that way. (ME Ch. XXII)

## Do not say
- Do not quote "In every battle there comes a time when both armies are nearly exhausted ... turning-point" as Grant's or as Memoirs vol. 1 pp. 299-300: not in the Memoirs.
- Do not say "the enemy is as afraid of you as you are of him" belongs to Donelson or is verbatim Grant; his Harris-at-Florida (1861) lesson says Harris "had been as much afraid of me as I had been of him".
- Do not say Grant "spotted three days of rations in knapsacks" and then counterattacked "across both flanks"; he heard men say haversacks were filled with rations, and Smith attacked on one flank.
- Do not say "12 miles" as Grant's number (he says eleven, garrisons seven), or "12-mile neck".
- Do not say "sleet" or that men froze to death; say snow, cold, no tents, thrown-away blankets.
- Do not say Cincinnati was at Donelson, or that Halleck authorised Donelson.
- Do not give a single exact Union casualty figure as the Official Records number: OR7 says 2,832; Wikipedia 2,691.
- Do not say Grant coined "unconditional surrender": Foote and C. F. Smith used the words first; the nickname came from the Northern press (the "U.S." pun).
- Do not give a precise Confederate strength; Buckner says 12,000, Grant says 21,000.
