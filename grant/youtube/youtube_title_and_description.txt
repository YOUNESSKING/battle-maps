Ulysses S. Grant's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves | The Man Who Accepted Three Confederate Surrenders

In February 1862, two rivers ran like open doors into the heart of the Confederacy, and two forts held them shut. Within a week, more than twelve thousand Confederates had surrendered at Fort Donelson to a general almost nobody in the North had heard of. It was the first major Union victory of the war, and it began as a disaster.

In this video we break down Ulysses S. Grant's three greatest tactical moves: the capture of Fort Henry and Fort Donelson in February 1862, from the gunboats on the Tennessee River to the breakout on the morning of the fifteenth and the demand for "an unconditional and immediate surrender"; the Vicksburg campaign of 1863, from the night Porter's gunboats ran the batteries to the decision to cut loose from his base and the five battles in eighteen days; and the crossing of the James River in June 1864, when the Army of the Potomac slipped away from Cold Harbor and a pontoon bridge more than two thousand feet long carried it toward Petersburg. We also look at what went wrong: the missed chance at Petersburg on 15 June 1864.

Attack while the enemy is still deciding. Go where he says you cannot. Never give him time to catch his breath.
