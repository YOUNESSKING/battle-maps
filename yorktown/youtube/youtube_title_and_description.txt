Britain Thought Yorktown Was a Safe Harbor. Washington Turned It Into a Trap
By 1781 the American Revolution was broke, its army unpaid and close to mutiny. At Yorktown, in three weeks, a safe harbor became a trap.

About eight thousand British soldiers dug in at Yorktown, Virginia, sure the Royal Navy would keep them safe. This is how Washington and Rochambeau turned it into a trap, told on animated painted maps: the march south that fooled the British in New York, the Battle of the Capes where de Grasse's French fleet shut the Chesapeake, and the siege, from the first trench dug in the rain to the night attack on Redoubts 9 and 10, Cornwallis's failed escape in a storm, and the surrender on 19 October 1781.

Fool the enemy where he is looking. Close the sea. Then close the land.
