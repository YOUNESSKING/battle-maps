Stonewall Jackson's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves | The Man Who Terrified Washington

More than fifty thousand Union soldiers, three separate armies, sent into one Virginia valley to crush a force of seventeen thousand men. Instead, that small force came out from behind a mountain, smashed a Union army at Winchester and chased it across the Potomac, and Washington braced for an attack.

In this video we break down Thomas "Stonewall" Jackson's three greatest tactical moves: the Shenandoah Valley Campaign of 1862, where he used the Massanutten Mountain to hide his army and beat the armies of Banks, Frémont and Shields one at a time; the flank march around John Pope's army to Manassas Junction and the Second Battle of Bull Run in August 1862; and the march across Joseph Hooker's front and the flank attack at Chancellorsville on 2 May 1863, the night he was shot by his own men.
