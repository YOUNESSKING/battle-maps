## Biography
- Born 21 Jan 1824, Clarksburg, (West) Virginia; father d. 1826, mother d. 1831 (orphan at 7): OK (EVA, Henderson).
- West Point 1846, 17th of 59: OK (EVA). Mexican War, brevet major (Chapultepec): OK. VMI from 1851, professor of natural and
  experimental philosophy (physics) + artillery: OK (EVA). Ages: 38 in 1862, 39 at Chancellorsville.
- "Tom Fool": OK (EVA: cadets). "Old Blue Light": origin DISPUTED (glowing eyes vs a jibe at his piety): NOT used. "Old Jack" (his
  soldiers, EVA): usable.
- "Stonewall" (Bee, First Bull Run, 21 Jul 1861): DISPUTED wording; first printed Charleston Mercury 25 Jul 1861 ("There is JACKSON
  standing like a stone-wall"); Bee died the next day; one 1862 paper credited Beauregard. Script: "General Barnard Bee is said to have
  shouted... like a stone wall" (no quote marks in the voice; the card shows the Mercury text as "first printed version").
- MYTH (Gemini): "Stonewall = defensive commander": correct as Gemini says; used as the background's myth-breaker.
- Slavery (ending line, OWNER TO APPROVE WORDING): the Confederacy seceded to protect slavery (secession declarations); Jackson himself
  owned enslaved people (EVA biography; Robertson). From memory/EVA, not re-read in this check: on the double-check list.

## Quotes
- "Always mystify, mislead, and surprise the enemy, if possible; and when you strike and overcome him, never let up in the pursuit...":
  VERBATIM in Imboden, *Battles and Leaders* II p. 297, as Jackson's words in conversation "in the early part of the war", recalled
  ~25 years later. Script: "as his fellow officer John Imboden later remembered it". Same passage: "hurl your own force on only a part,
  and that the weakest part, of your enemy and crush it" (method line 3 comes from it).
- Lee: "He has lost his left arm; but I have lost my right arm." DISPUTED wording / reported: message given to chaplain B.T. Lacy
  (Dabney 1866; Mary Anna Jackson 1892 has a comma). Script: "Lee sent word through Jackson's chaplain..." + the words, said as
  reported; on screen with "(as reported by Jackson's chaplain)".
- Last words: "Let us cross over the river, and rest under the shade of the trees." OK = McGuire's account (via Mary Anna Jackson,
  Henderson). Dabney has "pass over", EVA "in the shade": use McGuire's text, credited "recorded by his doctor, Hunter McGuire".
- Pope "bag the whole crowd": two printed versions (NPS vs Henderson); only the phrase is quoted. Pope's dawn dispatch to Halleck,
  30 Aug: "the enemy is retreating towards the mountains" (Henderson's OR text; on the double-check list for the OR page).
- Hooker's HQ 9:30 am 2 May to Howard: "We have good reason to suppose the enemy is moving to our right." (OR via WP): OK.
- 4:10 pm 2 May: "We know that the enemy is fleeing, trying to save his trains." VERBATIM (Henderson's OR text) but signed by aide
  J.H. Van Alen for Hooker, to Butterfield for Sedgwick: CORRECTED attribution -> "a dispatch from Hooker's headquarters".
- "Are you ready, General Rodes?" / "You can go forward then.": NPS booklet (recollection). Used with "according to the story".
- Stanton: "There is no doubt that the enemy in great force are marching on Washington" (to the Governor of Massachusetts, quoted by
  Henderson): paraphrased in the voice (no quote marks); double-check list.

## Hook
- "17,000 vs 60,000": CORRECTED. Jackson ~17,000 at his peak (with Ewell, late May; Henderson "nearly 17,000", NPS, ABT): OK.
  Union: "over 50,000" (ABT "no less than 50,000"; WP 52,000); 60,000 only in WP's Jackson page; 64,000 nowhere. Script: "more than
  fifty thousand".
- "600 miles in 48 days": CORRECTED. "646 miles in 48 days" is only in Wikipedia; 48 = days of MARCHING, not elapsed time
  (23 Mar-9 Jun = 78 days). History.com "more than 650". Script: "some six hundred and fifty miles" (no "48 days" on screen).
- "Won 5 battles against 3 armies": CORRECTED: six battles, Kernstown (23 Mar) was a Jackson defeat. Script says so.
- Panic: Henderson: Stanton wrote "the enemy in great force are marching on Washington"; "the Governors of thirteen States were calling
  on their militia to march to the defence of Washington": used (paraphrase + "thirteen states").
- Banks "driven completely out of the Valley and across the Potomac": OK; Banks fled 35 miles on 25 May and crossed at Williamsport
  that night / 26 May (date not pinned): script says "across the Potomac" without a date.
- Lincoln's order 24 May (~5 pm) to McDowell: "laying aside for the present the movement on Richmond, to put 20,000 men in motion at
  once for the Shenandoah": OK. 40,000 = McDowell's whole corps kept from McClellan (since 3 Apr, after Kernstown): CORRECTED (Gemini
  mixed them). Script: 20,000 pulled off the Richmond move; ~40,000 never reached McClellan.
- "54 miles in 36 hours" (Manassas march): CORRECTED: only HistoryNet. NPS/ABT: ~55 miles in two days (NPS series: 56 miles in 48 h;
  "over 50 miles in 34 hours"). Script: "fifty-five miles in two days".
- "Canned lobster and Rhine wine": OK with source: unnamed eyewitness quoted by NPS: "To see a starving man eating lobster salad & drinking
  rhine wine, barefooted & in tatters was curious". Worsham (21st Va.) lists "potted ham, lobster, tongue, candy, cakes...". "Millions of
  dollars": one chaplain's guess ("some million dollars worth", Sheeran): NOT used as a figure.
- "12-mile march": OK: "more than 12 miles" (NPS FRSP, EVA, WP); Hotchkiss: 14 miles. Script: "more than twelve miles".
- "Deer and rabbits": OK as park-historian text (NPS booklet "rabbits and deer"; marker "deer, rabbits, and turkeys"); no named
  eyewitness: script says "the park's historians tell how...". Not "literally... moments before".
- "Shot three times by his own men": OK (two bullets left arm, one right hand; North Carolina troops of Lane's brigade, 18th NC).

## Move 1: Valley Campaign (Mar-Jun 1862)
- Battles/dates: Kernstown 23 Mar (defeat), McDowell 8 May, Front Royal 23 May, First Winchester 25 May, Cross Keys 8 Jun, Port
  Republic 9 Jun: OK.
- Kernstown result: led Lincoln to keep McDowell's ~40,000 near Washington (3 Apr countermand, EVA): OK.
- Massanutten ~50 miles, one road over it New Market-Luray; Banks's Strasburg works faced south: OK (Henderson, NPS). "New Market Gap"
  name not seen in sources: script says "the single road over the mountain".
- Front Royal 23 May: garrison CORRECTED 900-1,063 (1st Maryland US, Col. John Kenly); Union losses 773 (WP, 691 captured) to 904 (NPS).
  Script: "about a thousand men... most of them captured".
- Winchester 25 May: Jackson ~16,000 vs Banks ~6,500 (NPS/ABT; EVA 3,500 outlier): OK. "Commissary Banks" (ABT: 500,000 rounds,
  15,000 lb bacon left behind): OK.
- 1 Jun: Jackson slipped south through Strasburg between Frémont (west) and Shields (east): OK (standard; Henderson).
- Cross Keys 8 Jun: Ewell ~5,800 vs Frémont ~11,500: OK. Port Republic 9 Jun: vs Tyler's ~3,000-3,500 of Shields's division: OK.
- Bridges over the South Fork burned so Shields (east of the Massanutten) could not join Frémont: standard (Henderson); double-check.
- Casualties: CORRECTED. Gemini "C ~2,500 / U ~7,000". Sum of the NPS battle boxes (6 battles, k+w+m): Confederate 2,775, Union 5,435;
  WP 2,677 / ~7,000; Henderson 2,500 k+w. Card: UNION ~5,400 · CONFEDERATE ~2,800 (NPS, six battles).
- "Foot cavalry": OK (in the press by July 1862; no single coiner).
- Gemini "May 8 McDowell prevented them joining Banks": OK in substance (Frémont's advance under Milroy/Schenck turned back).
- "What the enemy believed: Jackson trapped and fleeing": partly OK (June); Banks believed Jackson was still south/west of Strasburg.

## Move 2: Second Manassas (25-30 Aug 1862)
- Forces: DISPUTED ranges. Jackson's wing ~24,000 (NPS); Pope ~51,000 Army of Virginia, ~70,000-77,000 with McClellan's arriving
  corps (WP, ABT); Lee ~50,000-55,000. Script: "about twenty-four thousand"; Pope "more than seventy thousand once McClellan's men
  arrived" -> said as "some seventy thousand".
- Route: Jeffersonton - Amissville - Orlean - Salem (night 25) - Thoroughfare Gap - Gainesville - Bristoe Station (sunset 26): OK.
- Manassas Junction taken early 27 Aug by Trimble's two regiments + Stuart's cavalry, ~300 prisoners: OK. Burned night 27 Aug: OK.
- Unfinished railroad / Stony Ridge position near Groveton 28 Aug: OK.
- Brawner's Farm 28 Aug evening: Jackson attacked King's column ON PURPOSE (NPS): OK. Losses ~1,150 U / ~1,250 C; "nearly one-third of
  the 7,000 engaged" (NPS): script uses the NPS phrase. Ewell lost his left leg: OK.
- 29 Aug piecemeal Union attacks; Longstreet deployed on Jackson's right by about noon: OK. "Unnoticed by Pope" OK; Porter and McDowell
  knew (Buford's report not forwarded): CORRECTED nuance -> script: "nobody told Pope".
- 30 Aug: Porter's attack at the Deep Cut ~3 pm, 6,000-8,000 men (NPS): OK. Rocks: Stafford's and Bradley Johnson's brigades out of
  ammunition (NPS booklet, NPS marker): OK (date 30 Aug, not "rocks to break charges" in general).
- Longstreet ~25,000-28,000 ("nearly 30,000" NPS panel) at ~4 pm, "the largest simultaneous mass assault of the war" = NPS wording:
  attributed in the script ("what the Park Service calls"). Pope retreated over Bull Run after dark: OK.
- Casualties CORRECTED: Union 14,462 (1,747 k, 8,452 w, 4,263 m; OR return 16 Aug-2 Sep, so the whole campaign); Confederate 7,387
  (ABT) / 7,298 (WP, k+w only). Gemini 13,800 / 8,300 UNSUPPORTED. Card: UNION ~14,500 · CONFEDERATE ~7,400 (campaign, ABT).
- Gemini "Jackson understood severing the railroad would force Pope to retreat blindly": interpretation; script keeps it as "Lee's plan".

## Move 3: Chancellorsville (2 May 1863)
- Hooker ~130,000-134,000 vs Lee ~60,000-61,000; Longstreet with Hood + Pickett near Suffolk: OK.
- Night 1 May: Stuart reports the Union right flank "in the air"; before dawn 2 May (~3:30 am, Hotchkiss) Lee and Jackson on cracker
  boxes; "With my whole corps" (Hotchkiss via Henderson, recollection): OK, said as "according to Jackson's mapmaker".
- Column ~28,000 (NPS booklet, WP) to ~30,000 (NPS FRSP, ABT): CORRECTED range; script "about twenty-eight thousand". Lee left with
  ~13,000-15,000: OK.
- Route: Catharine Furnace, then Brock Road north to the Orange Turnpike (NOT via the Plank Road): CORRECTED. Start 7-8 am, deployed ~3 pm:
  OK. Attack between 5:15 and 6:00 pm (NPS): script "a little after five".
- Seen at Hazel Grove; Hooker warned Howard 9:30 am; Sickles attacked the tail, captured most of the 23rd Georgia, drew ~20,000 men away:
  OK. Hooker came to believe Lee was retreating: OK.
- XI Corps ~11,000 (marker; NPS ~2,500 casualties = 25 %); Gemini 12,000 CORRECTED. Only two small regiments + two guns faced west: OK.
- Advance more than two miles; halted ~7:15 pm about a mile west of Chancellorsville: OK.
- Wounding ~9-9:30 pm (NPS 9:00, EVA 9:30), North Carolina troops (18th NC) of Lane's brigade, three bullets: OK. Litter "dropped twice":
  CORRECTED: a bearer was hit and Jackson fell once (McGuire, Henderson, NPS); twice only in HistoryNet. Script: "one of the bearers was
  hit, and Jackson was dropped to the ground". Amputation ~2 am 3 May by McGuire (2 in. below the shoulder): OK. A.P. Hill wounded,
  Stuart took command: OK.
- Died Sunday 10 May 1863, 3:15 pm, Fairfield (Chandler plantation office), Guinea Station, pneumonia: OK.
- Casualties: Union 17,304; Confederate 13,460 (ABT, campaign incl. Salem Church) = ~22 % of Lee's army: OK. Card: these two numbers.
- Gemini "28,000 vs 12,000" (column vs XI Corps): column 28,000-30,000 OK, XI Corps ~11,000.
