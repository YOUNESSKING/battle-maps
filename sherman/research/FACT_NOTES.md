## Stakes (hook line 1)
- Blind Memorandum, 23 Aug 1864: VERBATIM (Collected Works VII p. 514; LoC blog): "This morning, as for some days past, it seems exceedingly
  probable that this Administration will not be re-elected. Then it will be my duty to so co-operate with the President elect, as to save the
  Union between the election and the inauguration; as he will have secured his election on such ground that he can not possibly save it
  afterwards." Folded and pasted shut; cabinet signed the outside "sight unseen" (LoC); opened 11 Nov 1864 (Hay's diary). OK.
- CORRECTED: Gemini "nine days later" -> 23 Aug to 2 Sep = TEN days. Script: "late in August ... within days" (no count spoken).
- Hook v2 (2026-10-28, new 10-15 s promise rule): "expected to lose the election, and the Union with it" = the memo's own logic (the winner "can not possibly save it"); "one general who would break the Confederacy within a year" = the title's promise (Atlanta Sep 1864 -> the largest surrender Apr 1865, < 12 months after May 1864).
- Script stake wording (v1): Lincoln "expected to lose" (memo: "exceedingly probable"; NOT "was sure"), and feared the winner "could never save
  the Union" (memo: "can not possibly save it afterwards"). Democratic platform (30 Aug 1864) "immediate efforts be made for a cessation of
  hostilities" (LoC blog) = background only.
- Atlanta's fall "all but assuring" / "helped ensure" Lincoln's re-election (ABT twice); Lincoln told Sherman he "had previously felt in doubt"
  until "Atlanta was ours, and fairly won" (Memoirs). Election 8 Nov 1864: 212 electoral votes to 21, 55.1 % popular (UCSB). OK.
- Gemini "Grant ~65,000 Overland casualties": CORRECTED -> ABT 55,000 Union in the month ("more than 50,000"). Not spoken (map: GRANT
  STALLED AT PETERSBURG only). Gemini "100,000+ Union casualties that summer": UNSUPPORTED, not used.
- "Check: could this line open another video?" No: the stake is Lincoln's own sealed memorandum and Atlanta; written fresh.

## Hook twist (Kentucky 1861)
- Request for 200,000 men (to Secretary of War Cameron, Louisville, Oct 1861), leaked, papers called him insane: OK, Sherman's own Memoirs
  vol. 1: "my insane request for two hundred thousand men ... published"; "the report that I was 'crazy, insane, and mad,' that 'I had
  demanded two hundred thousand men for the defense of Kentucky'". Headline "Gen. William T. Sherman Insane", Cincinnati Daily Commercial,
  11 Dec 1861 (UNI ScholarWorks record; web search). Replaced in Kentucky by Buell (Nov 1861), then sent home on leave by Halleck (web
  secondary; double-check list). Script: "He lost his command and was sent home on leave."
- The headline card is our own design (no real masthead) with the source line.

## Biography (background)
- Born 8 Feb 1820, Lancaster, Ohio (Memoirs: "my home was at Lancaster, Ohio"); named after Tecumseh; family nickname "Cump" (standard;
  Gemini "Uncle Billy" = soldiers' nickname, not used). West Point 1840, banker in San Francisco, superintendent of the Louisiana State
  Seminary, resigned when Louisiana seceded (Jan 1861) (Memoirs vol. 1, standard; double-check list for the exact dates). Wounded at Shiloh
  (in the hand, standard; double-check). Vicksburg, Chattanooga with Grant. March 1864: Grant general-in-chief, Sherman commands the Military
  Division of the Mississippi (standard). Died 14 Feb 1891 (not used).

## Move 1: Atlanta campaign (May - Sep 1864)
- Union strength 1 May 1864: 98,797 present (88,188 inf, 6,149 cav, 4,460 art), 254 guns: OK (Memoirs table; sums check). ABT "110,000" /
  Wikipedia "112,000 by June". Script: "nearly a hundred thousand".
- Johnston at Dalton: Memoirs "between forty and fifty thousand" (spies) and "sixty thousand" (later estimate); ABT 53,800 present for duty +
  15,000 reinforcements within the month; ABT Resaca 60,000. Gemini 45-50,000 rising to 65-75,000: roughly OK. Script: "little more than
  half his strength" (54,000 vs 99,000 on 1 May). Card: ~54,000 (ABT).
- Western & Atlantic Railroad Chattanooga-Atlanta = the single supply line for both armies: OK (ABT: "threaten his supply line, the Western &
  Atlantic Railroad"); length ~138 rail miles (standard; double-check), Dalton "eighty miles north of Atlanta" (ABT). Script: "more than a
  hundred miles long" (move 2-2) = the line back to Chattanooga.
- Plan: Thomas + Schofield demonstrate at Rocky Face Ridge / Mill Creek Gap (8-9 May); McPherson through Snake Creek Gap to Resaca: OK (ABT;
  Sherman's OR report: "make a bold attack ... After breaking the railroad well ... fall back"). McPherson reached Resaca 9 May, found it
  entrenched, overestimated it, pulled back to the gap (ABT). Johnston left Dalton 12 May (ABT).
- QUOTE CORRECTED: "Well, Mac, you have missed the great opportunity of your life" = NOT in the Memoirs or OR (UNSUPPORTED as wording).
  Real (Memoirs): "Such an opportunity does not occur twice in a single life, but at the critical moment McPherson seems to have been a little
  timid. Still, he was perfectly justified by his orders". Sherman wrote to McPherson he was disappointed "beyond measure" (ABT). Script uses
  the Memoirs line in substance, fairly (McPherson "justified by his orders").
- Resaca 13-15 May; Sweeny's division (XVI Corps) crossed the Oostanaula on the 15th, flanking Johnston: OK (ABT; Gemini "Lay's Ferry"
  plausible, not in fetched text: label only, double-check). Allatoona bypassed: Sherman swung west toward Dallas 23 May; New Hope Church
  25 May, Pickett's Mill 27 May, Dallas 28 May (ABT). OK.
- Pine Mountain 14 Jun: Polk killed by a 3-inch shell (third round); CORRECTED: Sherman pointed out the group to HOWARD (IV Corps), not
  Thomas; Battery I, 1st Ohio (Dilger) (Wikipedia, secondary; Memoirs confirms the episode). Map card only: POLK KILLED.
- Kennesaw 27 Jun: OK. First major frontal attack of the campaign (ABT); 8 a.m. start, failed by noon (ABT/NPS); Union ~3,000, Confederate
  ~1,000 (ABT box; Wikipedia). Pigeon Hill: XV Corps 850 vs <300 in 45 min (NPS). Sherman's reasons (OR report): "for the moral effect to
  make a successful assault"; "Failure as it was, and for which I assume the entire responsibility". Memoirs: "a thing carefully avoided up
  to that time". Script says it was a mistake and that he took the responsibility (honest).
- Johnston evacuated Kennesaw night of 2 Jul after Sherman's flanking move (NPS, ABT). Chattahoochee crossing by Schofield at Sope Creek
  8-9 Jul: Gemini, standard, not in fetched text (double-check). Script: "In early July".
- Johnston relieved: 17 Jul (Wikipedia, ABT Peach Tree Creek) / "July 18" (ABT overview: the change took effect). Script: "in the middle of
  July" (no date spoken). Hood's attacks: Peach Tree Creek 20 Jul, Atlanta 22 Jul (McPherson killed, 2:02 pm), Ezra Church 28 Jul: OK (ABT).
- CORRECTED: Gemini "~13,000-15,000 Confederate casualties" -> ABT boxes 2,500 + 5,500 + 3,000 = ~11,000 (Union 1,750 + 3,722 + 642).
  Script: "about eleven thousand men".
- 25 Aug: most of the infantry swung wide toward Jonesboro (17 miles south), one corps (XX, Slocum) left at the Chattahoochee bridge: OK
  (ABT; Gemini). Gemini "Hood believed Sherman was retreating" = DISPUTED/UNSUPPORTED in the fetched sources; NOT used. Used instead: ABT
  "Fearing a direct attack on Atlanta, Hood withdrew one corps from Hardee's force that night" (31 Aug) = Hood guessed wrong.
- Jonesboro 31 Aug - 1 Sep: Hardee's attack repulsed 31 Aug, Union broke through 1 Sep (ABT; Union 1,149, Confederate 2,000). Rails heated
  and twisted (Poe, OR 44; "Sherman's neckties" = standard soldiers' name). OK.
- Night of 1 Sep: CORRECTED/DISPUTED count: 81 rail cars of ammunition (Wikipedia / OR, Shoup's journal) with 5 locomotives (Shoup) or 6-7
  (Slocum); Sherman's report "seven trains of cars"; Gemini "81 cars + 7 locomotives" half right. Explosions heard ~20 miles away at
  Jonesboro (Sherman's OR report). Script: "blew up his own ammunition trains" (no count spoken).
- Atlanta surrendered 2 Sep (ABT); telegram VERBATIM (OR 38 pt 5 p. 777, to Halleck, 3 Sep): "So Atlanta is ours, and fairly won." OK.
- Campaign casualties: DISPUTED. Union 31,687 (Memoirs) / 34,514 May-Aug + 2,567 = 37,081 (Sherman's own OR table) / ~34,500 (ABT).
  Confederate 34,979 incl. 12,983 prisoners (Memoirs from Foard) / ~35,000 (ABT). Script: "more than thirty thousand men on each side";
  card: UNION 31,687-37,081 · CONFEDERATE ~35,000.

## Move 2: March to the Sea (Nov - Dec 1864)
- Civilians ordered out of Atlanta; letter to Mayor Calhoun 12 Sep 1864 VERBATIM (Memoirs; OR 39 pt 2 p. 418): "You cannot qualify war in
  harsher terms than I will. War is cruelty, and you cannot refine it". OK (Gemini's longer quote also fine, not needed).
- October: Hood broke the W&A railroad, attacked Allatoona 5 Oct, repulsed (Memoirs, telegram to Thomas 9 Oct). OK.
- VERBATIM telegram, Allatoona 9 Oct 1864, 7.30 pm, to Grant (OR 39 pt 3 p. 162): "Until we can repopulate Georgia, it is useless to occupy
  it, but the utter destruction of its roads, houses, and people will cripple their military resources. By attempting to hold the roads we
  will lose 1,000 men monthly, and will gain no result. I can make the march, and make Georgia howl." CORRECTED: OR = "I can make the march,
  and make Georgia howl." (period); Memoirs = "I can make this march, and make Georgia howl!" Card uses the OR wording.
- Decision card (E.decide): chase Hood or march to the sea; Thomas sent to Nashville; Grant agreed (standard, Memoirs). Lincoln 26 Dec:
  "When you were about leaving Atlanta for the Atlantic coast, I was anxious, if not fearful" (VERBATIM, House Divided). Not spoken.
- Strength: 62,204 men (55,329 inf, 5,063 cav, 1,812 art), 65 guns (Memoirs recapitulation; Wikipedia says 64), ~2,500 wagons, ~600
  ambulances, 1,200,000 rations ~ 20 days: OK. Script: "some sixty-two thousand ... about twenty days of food".
- Telegraph: Thomas's last dispatch, Sherman's "Dispatch received—all right", then a burnt bridge severed the wire (Memoirs): OK (date 12 Nov
  = Gemini/standard; double-check).
- Burning of Atlanta, 15-16 Nov: CORRECTED nuance. Orders targeted depots, shops, rail (Poe, OR 44); "many buildings in the business part of
  the city were destroyed by lawless persons" (Poe); Memoirs: the fire "did not reach ... the great mass of dwelling houses"; churches,
  hospitals, City Hall spared (Howard; Wikipedia); a resident (Crew) estimated two-thirds destroyed. Gemini "~400 residences survived":
  UNSUPPORTED, not used. Script: military targets ordered, fires spread far beyond them (no share spoken).
- Foraging: Special Field Orders No. 120 (Kingston, 9 Nov 1864) VERBATIM in FACT_CHECK_RAW ("forage liberally on the country"; soldiers not
  to enter dwellings; destruction left to corps commanders where resistance). Script: "Many homes were looted, against orders". OK.
- Two wings toward Macon (right, Howard) and Augusta (left, Slocum), Milledgeville taken 22-23 Nov, mock legislative session: OK (Wikipedia;
  standard). The deception wording (Confederates split to defend both) = standard (double-check list; Trudeau not reached).
- Census: Memoirs: "I had obtained not only the United States census-tables of 1860, but a compilation made by the Controller of the State of
  Georgia ... the 'population and statistics' of every county in Georgia". OK (Gemini "mathematically calculated" = overstated, not used).
- Griswoldville 22 Nov: CORRECTED. ABT: Union 94, Confederate 1,100; Scaife & Bragg (via Wikipedia): Union 94 (13 k, 79 w, 2 m),
  Confederate 51 killed + 472 wounded = 523; Augusta paper 614. Gemini "~650 vs 62" = wrong. Three charges, rail-and-log barricade, Walcutt
  (ABT/Wikipedia): OK. "Spencer rifles", "old men and boys", "Union soldiers wept": UNSUPPORTED in fetched sources, NOT used.
  Script: "more than five hundred ... the Union brigade, fewer than a hundred"; card: CONFEDERATE 523-1,100 · UNION 94.
- 60-mile-wide path (ABT Griswoldville: "Sherman's 60-mile wide column"; OR 44 Sherman: "thirty miles on either side of a line from Atlanta
  to Savannah"): OK. 300 miles of railroad wrecked (Wikipedia). March length "285 miles": NOT FOUND (Atlanta-Savannah ~220 miles straight
  line); NOT spoken.
- Freed people: "A Confederate officer estimated that 10,000 liberated slaves followed" (Wikipedia). Ebenezer Creek 9 Dec: Gen. Jefferson C.
  Davis removed the pontoon bridges; "hundreds of freed slaves ... drowned" (Georgia Historical Society marker); Memoirs defends Davis. Special
  Field Orders No. 15 (16 Jan 1865), up to 40 acres (GHS; Wikipedia). OK. Script: "many of them drowned".
- Lincoln "I know what hole he went in at": DISPUTED wording/setting. Public version VERBATIM 6 Dec 1864 serenade (UCSB): "We all know where
  he went in at, but I can't tell where he will come out at." The "hole" version = second-hand (T. Harry Williams; to Senator John Sherman).
  Gemini "nervous White House visitors / 32 days": UNSUPPORTED. Card (if used) = the 6 Dec public wording, attributed.
- Savannah: reached 10 Dec; Hardee ~10,000 behind flooded rice fields and causeways (Wikipedia; Memoirs "overflowed rice-fields ... narrow
  causeways"). Fleet offshore, Ogeechee blocked by Fort McAllister. OK.
- Fort McAllister 13 Dec: OK. Sherman on "the roof of a shed attached to the [rice] mill" at Cheeves's, ~3 miles off (Memoirs); Hazen's
  division ~4,000 (ABT); "lasted just fifteen minutes" (Sherman to Grant, OR 44; ABT "about 15 minutes"); garrison 230 (ABT) / 250 (Hazen,
  Memoirs); Union loss DISPUTED 92 (Memoirs) / 91 (Sherman letter) / 134 (Hazen; ABT). Sherman went out to the fleet that night in a yawl
  (Memoirs). Gemini "4:45-5:00 PM" = not in sources (Wikipedia "as the Sun was setting"): script "late in the afternoon".
- Hardee left 20 Dec over a pontoon bridge across the Savannah River; city surrendered 21 Dec (Wikipedia; Memoirs "pontoon-bridge"; Gemini
  "rice barges" not checked, not used).
- Christmas telegram VERBATIM (OR 44 p. 783; ABT): "I beg to present you, as a Christmas gift, the city of Savannah, with 150 heavy guns and
  plenty of ammunition, and also about 25,000 bales of cotton." CORRECTED: Gemini "250 heavy siege guns" -> 150.
- Damage VERBATIM (OR 44 p. 13, Sherman to Halleck 22 Dec 1864; NOT in the Memoirs): "I estimate the damage done to the State of Georgia and
  its military resources at $100,000,000; at least, $20,000,000 of which has inured to our advantage, and the remainder is simple waste and
  destruction." OK; card "SHERMAN'S OWN ESTIMATE".
- Union losses on the march: Gemini 1,338 / ~2,100 = not checked in fetched text; NOT spoken.

## Move 3: Carolinas (Feb - Apr 1865)
- Grant first ordered the army to Virginia by sea (letter of 6 Dec 1864), then modified it; Sherman proposed marching via Columbia and
  Raleigh (Memoirs, letters of Dec 1864). Script: "Grant had first wanted ... by sea. Sherman asked to march there by land". OK.
- Strength 1 Feb 1865: 60,079 men, 68 guns, ~2,500 wagons, ~600 ambulances (Memoirs). OK. SC Encyclopedia "sixty thousand veterans".
- Weather: "the heaviest rains in twenty years" (Gemini) = NOT FOUND; Memoirs: the Salkehatchie "overflowed by its swollen waters to the
  breadth of a full mile". Script: "swollen by winter rain ... a mile wide".
- Gemini "Confederates believed the swamps impassable in winter": NOT FOUND as a Confederate statement (Memoirs uses "impassable" of the
  rivers himself). CORRECTED: script makes the belief = the feint (they held Charleston and Augusta, expecting him there) and says the
  rivers looked impassable (Sherman's own word).
- Rivers' Bridge 2-3 Feb: ~1,200 Confederates vs ~7,000 Union, about 100 casualties each (SC Encyclopedia); Mower's and Giles A. Smith's
  divisions waded "the water up to their shoulders" (Memoirs). CORRECTED: Gemini credits "Blair's XVII Corps" generally: they were XVII
  Corps divisions (Blair commanded the corps) = consistent; script names no general. Gemini "cartridge boxes on bayonets": UNSUPPORTED,
  NOT used (script: "holding their rifles and cartridge boxes above their heads" -> CHANGED to "holding their rifles over their heads"?
  -> not verified either; script says only "up to their shoulders").
- Corduroy roads: Memoirs "every foot of the way had thus to be corduroyed"; "nearly every mile of which had to be corduroyed". OK.
  Gemini "400 miles of corduroy", "ten miles a day": UNSUPPORTED, not used. Wikipedia: 425 miles in 50 days (not spoken).
- "No such army since the days of Julius Caesar": UNSUPPORTED as a verbatim primary quote: not in the Memoirs or OR; only attributed to
  Johnston (title + preface of Smith & Sokolosky, Savas Beatie). Script: "a remark later attributed to Joseph Johnston"; no quote marks on
  screen except as the book-title attribution. DOUBLE-CHECK (owner may prefer to cut it).
- Columbia 17 Feb: surrendered midmorning 17 Feb (SC Enc.); fire that night; "About one-third of the town had burned, totaling 458
  buildings, of which 265 were residences" (SC Enc.); cause DISPUTED: Hampton's cotton bales, wind, drunken soldiers; 1873 claims commission
  assessed no blame; "an accident of war" (SC Enc.); Sherman (Memoirs): "It was accidental", blames Hampton's burning cotton, admits drunken
  men; says "about half" burned. CORRECTED: Gemini "Hampton's retreating Confederates ignited" = one side's view only. Script gives all
  three causes and "still argued over".
- Charleston evacuated evening of 17 Feb (Wikipedia; occupied 18 Feb, standard): OK.
- Johnston restored to command 22-23 Feb 1865 (standard; double-check). Fayetteville 11 Mar (standard; map only). Averasboro 16 Mar: Hardee
  6,000 delaying Slocum's 25,000 (ABT; Union 682, Confederate 865). OK.
- Bentonville 19-21 Mar: Johnston ~21,000 (NCpedia, ABT) / ~16,000 infantry + 4,000 cavalry (NC Historic Sites) / 14,100 inf + art (his
  own Narrative); script "about twenty thousand". Attack 3:15 pm (NCpedia; Gemini 2:45 = CORRECTED) -> "in the afternoon". Slocum's men
  driven back; Morgan's division held (ABT). Right wing came up; ~60,000 Union on the field (NC HS). Mower 21 Mar toward the Mill Creek
  bridge, overran Johnston's HQ, recalled (NC HS: "Sherman was furious"). Sherman VERBATIM (Memoirs): "I think I made a mistake there, and
  should rapidly have followed Mower's lead with the whole of the right wing". Losses: Union 1,527 (OR 47/1 p. 76; ABT; Sherman's own
  tables 1,595 / 1,604), Confederate 2,606 (ABT; OR). OK.
- Goldsboro 23 Mar (NCpedia); strength 88,948 on 10 Apr (Memoirs; CORRECTED date: not "late March").
- Lee surrendered 9 Apr; 28,231 paroled at Appomattox (NPS). Raleigh surrendered 13 Apr (NC DNCR, snippet; double-check).
- 17 Apr: the cipher telegram of Lincoln's assassination reached Sherman as he boarded the train at Raleigh; he held the train ~half an hour;
  told the operator to keep it secret; showed it to Johnston when alone at the Bennett farm; Johnston's distress (Memoirs). OK (Gemini
  "swore the operator to total secrecy" = in substance; "handed it inside the farmhouse" = showed it).
- 18 Apr "Basis of Agreement" (rejected in Washington); 26 Apr military convention at Bennett's House near Durham's Station (Memoirs, NC HS).
- 89,270 surrendered: OK (NC Historic Sites; Memoirs: 36,817 paroled at Greensboro + 52,453 in Georgia and Florida). "Largest troop
  surrender of the Civil War" (NC Historic Sites). Spelling: Bennitt (family) / Bennett Place (site). OK.

- War is hell: see above (1880 veterans' speech, reported). - Atlanta burned out of malice / nothing left standing: military targets ordered,
  fires spread, churches/hospitals/City Hall spared; Hood's own 1 Sep demolitions said in move 1. - "Bloody slaughter": the script names the
  looting, Griswoldville, Ebenezer Creek and the destruction plainly; Gemini "fewer than 500 combat deaths" = not checked, not used.
- Bennett Place vs Appomattox: 89,270 vs 28,231 (both sourced); script "the largest surrender of the war" (NC Historic Sites wording).
