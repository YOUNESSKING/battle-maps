William T. Sherman's Top 3 Legendary Tactical Moves | The Man Who Broke the Confederacy

In August 1864, Abraham Lincoln expected to lose the election, and the Union with it. Everything hung on Atlanta. Nearly a hundred thousand Union soldiers, and one general who would break the Confederacy within a year.

In this video we break down William Tecumseh Sherman's three greatest tactical moves: the Atlanta campaign of 1864, where he outflanked Joseph Johnston ridge after ridge and finally swung his army around Atlanta onto its last railroad at Jonesboro; the March to the Sea, where two wings threatened Macon and Augusta at once and the army crossed Georgia with no supply line to Savannah and Fort McAllister; and the Carolinas campaign of 1865, through flooded swamps on corduroy roads, past Columbia and Charleston to Bentonville and the surrender at Bennett Place. We also cover his mistake at Kennesaw Mountain, and the cost of "hard war" for the people of Georgia and the Carolinas.

Hit the railroad, not the wall. Threaten two places at once. Go where they say no army can.
