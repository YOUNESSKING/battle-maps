## Biazza Ridge, 11 July 1943

**Main source:** Gavin's own account, excerpted in American Heritage, "Jump into Sicily" (americanheritage.com/jump-sicily).
**Cross-checks:** HistoryNet "Jim Gavin: the general who jumped first"; Armchair General "A Journey to WWII Battlefield part 5: Biazza Ridge"; tiger1.info "Biazzo Ridge"; battledetective.com battle study 18; uswarmemorials.org / maritimequest (Ponte Dirillo memorial).

| Gemini claim | Finding | Use in script |
|---|---|---|
| Ridge at 36.993 N, 14.444 E | That point is on the open plateau about 2.5 km SSE of the ridge. The Ponte Dirillo memorial is at 37.0318 N, 14.4044 E (uswarmemorials). Biazza Ridge is the scarp on the east/south-east side of the Acate (Dirillo) valley, overlooking the bridge and Route 115. The terrain shows it at about 37.012 N, 14.41-14.43 E (the edge rises from 30 m to 90-150 m). | The map uses the terrain scarp above Ponte Dirillo. |
| Gavin and 250 men | Gavin started 11 July with about 8 troopers. About 250 men of the 3rd Bn 505th were found resting in a tomato field and joined him, along with the engineers (Lt. Wechsler's B Coy, 307th Engineers), 456th PFA gunners, 180th Infantry men and sailors. The force grew through the day. | "a few hundred men" |
| Ridge taken 10:00 | The attack began about 08:30 (Gavin, American Heritage; Armchair General). | 08:30 |
| Tigers counterattacked at 11:00 | The German attack came in the afternoon (Armchair General). No exact time is given. | "that afternoon" |
| ~700 infantry + a company of Panzer III, IV and Tiger tanks | Armchair General gives "at least 700 infantry, an armored artillery battalion, and a company of Tiger tanks". The Tigers were 2./s.Pz.Abt. 504, attached to the Hermann Göring Division (tiger1.info). No source was found for Panzer IIIs or IVs. | 700 infantry + Tigers; no Panzer IV |
| Gavin personally fired a bazooka and directed a captured 75 mm gun | Not supported. Bazooka teams fired at the Tigers' front plates and made only finger-sized holes (Gavin). 75 mm **pack howitzers** (456th PFA) fired at the tanks over open sights. Only Armchair General says "captured". Gavin's own account calls it "our 75". | "two small pack howitzers"; no "captured", no "Gavin fired a bazooka" |
| Navy gunfire in the evening | A young ensign with a radio called for a trial round, which landed where the Tiger had disappeared, then called for a concentration. In Gavin's words, "from then on the battle seemed to change." The ship's name and the time are unknown. The ensign may be Ens. Augustus T. Allen Jr. (Navy Cross, 45th Div shore fire control party), but this is unconfirmed. | "a navy ensign with a radio", no ship or officer named |
| Six Shermans in the evening | About 6 p.m. Lt. Swingler arrived with "half a dozen of our own Sherman tanks" (Gavin). Others say about 7 p.m. Gavin then counterattacked with everyone who could carry a rifle, including clerks, cooks and truck drivers. They overran machine guns, captured twelve heavy mortars, and the Germans fled. | "about six in the evening, six Shermans"; counterattack; Germans fall back |
| US ~50 killed, ~100 wounded | Gavin: buried "fifty bodies", "more than one hundred wounded". The Ponte Dirillo memorial lists 39 names; that is a different count. | "about 50 killed, 100+ wounded" (Gavin's figures) |
| German losses | Unknown in total. tiger1.info: three Tigers reported knocked out. Gavin: one Tiger captured later, plus twelve mortars. | Casualty card: German column "?", plus "12 mortars captured" |
| Conrath commanded | Gen. Paul Conrath commanded the Hermann Göring Division. He was not at the ridge in person. | Not used in the test |
| Gavin wounded in the leg, refused evacuation | A mortar-fragment wound appears in HistoryNet only. Nothing found on the leg or on refusing evacuation. | Not used |
| Men fell asleep in foxholes | No source found. | Not used |
| "We're staying on this goddamned ridge, no matter what happens" | Found only in secondary sources (HistoryNet, Armchair General). | Paraphrased without quote marks |
| Gavin's age and rank | Colonel, commanding the 505th PIR. Born 22 March 1907, so he was 36. He received the Distinguished Service Cross for the action. | colonel, 36 |

## Europe control map (hook-3 test, owner request 2026-10: territory + flags)
Borders: aourednik/historical-basemaps world_1938 (Germany incl. Austria). Fronts are approximate, drawn from standard WWII situation maps; they read at a glance, not to the km.
- **Jul 1943 (Sicily, 10 Jul):** Axis holds France (all occupied since Nov 1942), Low Countries, Denmark, Norway, Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Greece, Italy incl. Sicily/Sardinia/Corsica; Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Finland are Axis allies. Allies hold North Africa (Tunisia fell 13 May 1943). Eastern front just before/at Kursk: Leningrad besieged, Orel and Kursk salients, Mius river; Crimea German. Neutral: Spain, Portugal, Switzerland, Sweden, Turkey, Ireland.
- **Sep 1943 (Salerno, 9 Sep):** Sicily taken (17 Aug); Allies in Calabria (3 Sep), Salerno and Taranto (9 Sep). Italy's armistice announced 8 Sep 1943; Germany occupies the rest of Italy. Soviets retook Orel (5 Aug), Kharkov (23 Aug), Stalino (8 Sep).
- **Jun 1944 (Normandy, 6 Jun):** Rome fell 4 Jun; Sardinia and Corsica Allied (Sep/Oct 1943). Leningrad relieved (Jan 1944), Crimea retaken (May 1944); front from Narva past Vitebsk (the Belarus bulge before Bagration, 22 Jun) to the Romanian border at Iasi and the Dniester.
- **Sep 1944 (Holland, 17 Sep):** France and Belgium largely liberated (Paris 25 Aug, Brussels 3 Sep, Antwerp 4 Sep), front near the German border; Italy at the Gothic Line (Pisa 2 Sep, Rimini fell 21 Sep). Romania switched sides 23 Aug; Bulgaria 9 Sep; Finland ceasefire 4 Sep (shown neutral). Soviets on the Vistula at Warsaw and in the Carpathians; Greece and Yugoslavia still German-held (Athens freed Oct 1944).
