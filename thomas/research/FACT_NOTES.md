# Fact notes: where the script departs from the Gemini research

| Research claim | What the script says instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "The only major Union commander who never lost a battle" | "It is often said he never lost a battle in which he held command" | Popular claim, not a clean fact (he was a subordinate at Chickamauga, a lost battle). Hedged. |
| "Slow Trot" came from a back injury | Cadets at West Point (he was cavalry instructor, 1851-54) named him for forbidding them to gallop the academy's worn-out horses | The back injury came in 1860 (fall from a train platform), years after the nickname. |
| Missionary Ridge casualties Union 2,457 / Confederate 3,840 | Chattanooga battles (Nov 23-25) totals: Union ~5,800, Confederate ~6,700 incl. 4,000+ captured; ~40 guns taken | Research figures not traceable; campaign totals are the standard NPS/ABT numbers. |
| Thomas "single-handedly fought off the entire Confederate force" | Thomas held with roughly half the army plus Granger's Reserve Corps (Steedman), who marched to the guns without orders | Accuracy; Steedman's arrival (~1:30 pm) saved the right of Horseshoe Ridge. |
| Vivid detail: women with lanterns screaming among the dead | Not used | Could not verify. |
| "Time and history will do me justice" | Not used | Attribution uncertain. |
| Rosecrans "fled in panic" | Swept back to Chattanooga with a third of the army, believing all was lost | Neutral wording; Garfield (his chief of staff, future president) rode back to Thomas. |
| Nashville "the only army destroyed in a single battle" | "One of the most complete victories of the war"; Army of Tennessee never again a real threat | Superlative is contested. |

Added (well documented):
- Wood's division pulled out of line after Rosecrans's order to "close up on Reynolds"; Brannan was already there, so a quarter-mile gap opened at ~11:00.
- Garfield's dispatch: Thomas "standing like a rock", origin of the nickname.
- Thomas at Chattanooga (Oct 1863): "We will hold the town till we starve."
- Grant: "Thomas, who ordered those men up the ridge?" Thomas: "I don't know; I did not." Granger: "When those fellows get started, all hell can't stop them."
- Confederate trenches on the topographical crest, not the military crest: dead ground on the slope; retreating men from the base masked the fire.
- Nashville: Grant's order relieving Thomas (Dec 9, suspended); Logan sent Dec 13; Thomas: "I will submit without a murmur." Steedman's feint included US Colored Troops brigades. Shy's Hill stormed ~4 pm Dec 16 by McArthur's division while Wilson's dismounted cavalry got into Hood's rear.
- His Virginia sisters turned his portrait to the wall and never spoke to him again. Died in San Francisco, March 28, 1870.
