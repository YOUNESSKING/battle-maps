# Media Credits — George H. Thomas video (Part B: archive stills)

Sources: mostly the Library of Congress Prints & Photographs Division
(`loc.gov` item JSON API, `tile.loc.gov` service-tier JPEGs, largest
available up to 1024px on the long side — LoC's own standard web-delivery
size, used after Wikimedia Commons' `action=query` API started
rate-limiting this session's IP with repeated 429s), plus two items from
Wikimedia Commons (`Special:FilePath` thumbnail redirects, which were not
rate-limited) for the Thomas Circle 1900 stereoview and the Oakwood
Cemetery grave photo. All images are Public domain (pre-1929 U.S.
photographs, engravings and chromolithographs, or federal LoC works) or,
for the one 3-D-object photo (the grave monument), CC BY-SA 3.0. No
NC/ND-licensed material was used.

## Notes on method

- LoC item pages were fetched as JSON (`?fo=json`) and the largest
  service-tier JPEG under `resources[0].files` (typically the `...v.jpg`
  tier, ~1024px on the long side) was downloaded from `tile.loc.gov`.
  Requests were made with a descriptive User-Agent and ~1s pacing.
- Two Commons files (the 1900 Thomas Circle stereoview and the Oakwood
  Cemetery grave photo) were fetched via `Special:FilePath` redirects,
  which serve standard-width thumbnails and were not affected by the
  `action=query` API's rate limiting.
- `a04_b.jpg` and `a05_c.jpg` intentionally reuse the same source
  photographs as `assets/media/grant_head.png` and
  `assets/media/granger_head.png` (full uncropped frame here vs. the
  background-removed cut-out there) rather than searching out further
  alternates, given the Commons rate-limit pressure; this is consistent
  with the Daniel Morgan video's precedent of reusing a source image
  across an archive slot and a different context when a good alternate
  wasn't readily available.
- No confirmed photo of the Brown's Ferry pontoon bridge (the "Cracker
  Line") was found on LoC or Commons; `a04_c.jpg` (Barnard's Lookout
  Mountain/river camp photo, Chattanooga 1864) was used as the brief's
  named fallback ("Tennessee River at Chattanooga 1864").
- No dated 1861 photo of the Virginia secession convention itself was
  found; `a01_b.jpg` (an 1865 photo of the Virginia State Capitol, where
  the convention met) was used as a stand-in, per the brief's own
  "Richmond / secession convention image" alternative.
